## In short

**Vaṁśīvaṭa Bābājī** was a Gauḍīya sādhu who lived at **Svarūpa Gañj** (Navadvīpa) in the days of Śrīla **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī**'s mission. Bhaktisiddhānta respected him and saw that he was given grains and other supplies by brahmacārīs of the Gauḍīya Maṭha, whom he sent to Svarūpa Gañj from the **Yoga-pīṭha** temple. He is not **Vaṁśī dāsa Bābājī** (#1617), the avadhūta of Navadvīpa town.

## The fish and the Deities

One day a woman selling fish offered him some choice catch, and for a moment his mind was drawn to taste it. At once he checked himself and became furious. He shouted at the woman to go away, and then cried out: "How could this happen? How could this happen? I have surrendered my life to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, I am under Their protection, and still this happens! Why are You not protecting me?"

He stormed into his bhajana-kuṭīra and, while people gathered, fumed at his Deities inside. Then he came out with the Deities tied by a rope and threw Them into the Gaṅgā, keeping the end of the rope under his foot. When a man asked why he was doing this, the Bābā threw a rock at him.

## "You cannot understand this bhāva"

When the news reached the Gauḍīya Maṭha, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta called his brahmacārīs together and forbade them to visit the Bābā again, except for one who would only deliver the supplies. A few days later he called them again and said: "Some of you think I have stopped you from visiting Bābājī Mahārāja because he is in māyā. That is not so. But you are not able to understand this bhāva. So stay away."

His reception had always been unpredictable: sometimes he welcomed the brahmacārī, sometimes took the gifts without a word, sometimes just sat and stared, sometimes angrily threw the gifts into the river. Afterwards, when that one brahmacārī came, he threw a stone at him and said: "If you want to please me, never come here again!"
