## In short

**Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī of Raṇabāḍī** — born **Kṛṣṇaprasāda Caṭṭopādhyāya** at **Mahammadpura**, the capital of Rājā Silārām in Bankura — ran away to Vṛndāvana as a boy and spent some fifty years in bhajana at **Raṇabāḍī** in Vraja. After taking the tapta-mudrā at Dvārakā, against Śrī Rādhā's word in a dream, he gave up food and water in repentance, and after three months the fire of separation burned his body (about **1865**). He was a close friend of the **Siddha Bābā of Govardhana**; his tomb at Raṇabāḍī is still honoured by the Vrajavāsīs.

## To Vraja

His father, Gokulacandra Caṭṭopādhyāya, was a servant of Śrī Hare Kṛṣṇa Rāya, and Kṛṣṇaprasāda too served the Deity as a child. When a marriage was proposed for him he left home late at night and went to Vṛndāvana. After a few days of sevā at the **Madana-mohana** temple he moved to Raṇabāḍī, then surrounded by dense forest, built a kuṭīra and lived in bhajana on mādhukarī. The families he begged from loved him so much that they asked him to come every day; whatever was left over after he kept enough for himself, he fed to the cows on his way back.

## The pilgrimage

Having come to Vraja so young, he had never been on pilgrimage, and after fifty years he wished to visit the four dhāmas. **Śrī Priyājī** came to him in a dream: "You have taken shelter of My lotus feet in Vṛndāvana. Do not leave Vraja; stay here, go on with your bhajana and you will reach your goal." Thinking the dream his own imagination, he set out. At **Dvārakā** he took the signs of the **tapta-mudrā**, as the four Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas do there and as the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa allows — a custom not approved by the rāgānugā Vaiṣṇavas of Vṛndāvana — but he felt disturbed within, lost his enthusiasm and returned to Vṛndāvana.

That night Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī said to him in a dream: "By taking the tapta-mudrā at Dvārakā you have become a devotee of Satyabhāmā. You are no longer fit to live in Vraja; go back to Dvārakā." The **Siddha Bābā** of Vraja and others all echoed Priyājī's words. When he went to see his close friend, the **Siddha Bābā of Govardhana**, who embraced him and asked where he had been, he opened his palm to show the mudrā. The Siddha Bābā drew back and sighed: "What luck! From today I have lost the right to touch you. You are the servant of the Supreme King and Queen, while I remain the attendant of a milkmaid!" Speechless, Kṛṣṇadāsa offered obeisances and left.

## The fire of separation

Back at Raṇabāḍī he gave up all food and drink in repentance, suffering from his act and from separation from Priyājī. After three months the fire within began to show outwardly, and for three days his body slowly burned. **Haridāsa dāsa** heard from **Bihārī dāsa Vrajavāsī**, the constant attendant of **Siddha Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī**, that Jagannātha dāsa was at Raṇabāḍī that night. Told that the Raṇabāḍī Bābā was burning in his hut, he cried, "What a pity! It is the fire of viraha!", rode on Bihārī dāsa's shoulders to the spot and broke into the room. The fire had reached the Bābā's throat and risen no higher; Jagannātha dāsa twisted some cotton into a wick and placed it on his head, and the flame leaped up and swallowed his whole body. Even as it reached his chest the Bābā went on chanting the holy name. Lifting his arms, he told the helpless Vrajavāsīs: "There will never be any suffering in your village; even if famine and epidemic rage everywhere else, your village will remain untouched" — words that, the record says, have proved true to this day.

A Muslim police officer and a Hindu tahsildār came; it is said the fire died down when the officer approached and leaped up again when he moved away, and the tahsildār had much ghee brought and poured on the body. When the ashes had cooled, his godbrother **Premadāsa Bābājī** arrived: "My brother, why did you not accept the wood I brought for you? Now I shall offer it" — and as he laid it on the ashes, the flame rose again. His tomb stands in the posture in which his body burned, and the Vrajavāsīs believe that a wish placed there is surely fulfilled.

## What he left

He told the local Vrajavāsīs to keep the ekādaśī of the bright fortnight of Phālguna and stay awake on Harivāsara; they still do, children too, with music and plays of Bhagavān's līlā. On his death anniversary, the **amāvasyā of Pauṣa**, the Vrajavāsīs raise funds and feed the Vaiṣṇavas of the eighty-four krośas.
