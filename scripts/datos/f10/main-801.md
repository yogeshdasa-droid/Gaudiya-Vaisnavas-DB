## In short

**Kṛṣṇasundara Rāya** («Rāya Prabhu»), of a Vārendra kāyastha family of **Udhumā** (Pabna), left a high post in the royal estate of Kākinā to live for the Lord. A disciple of **Vaikuṇṭha Gosvāmī** of Advaita Prabhu's family, of Mahiṣāḍerā (Nadia), he lived in solitude, mostly in kīrtana; **Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya** of Tāḍāś sought his guidance. He passed away chanting the holy name on the night after the Vaiśākha ekādaśī of **1300 BS (1893)**.

## Detachment

After a lecture on the passing nature of this world he became detached, and looked on his wealth and dear ones as the Lord's. When his only son, **Śyāmasundara**, died young, he did not shed a tear: he left the body for cremation and went on with kīrtana, playing the drum. "Lord Caitanya sent someone to play the mṛdaṅga with me," he told the astonished people, "and now He has taken him back. What can I do?"

## His bhajana

He took dīkṣā from **Vaikuṇṭha Gosvāmī** and lived in solitude, meditating on the Lord and chanting the name. No one ever saw him counting japa on beads, and no one was allowed into his bhajana-kuṭīra during his rituals and japa. His bhajana was mostly kīrtana — nāma-kīrtana and līlā-kīrtana were his great joy.

## Rājarṣi Vanamālī

When **Rājarṣi** Vanamālī Rāya, the renowned zamindār of Tāḍāś, asked him for spiritual guidance, Kṛṣṇasundara went to his capital; but seeing that the Rājarṣi's mind had first to be cleansed, he said: "Vanamālī, let me go now — but before I go, let me embrace you once." At his embrace the Rājarṣi was overwhelmed — tears fell and his body trembled — and he begged him to stay; Kṛṣṇasundara stayed a few days more to teach him.

## His last day

On the night of the Vaiśākha ekādaśī of 1300 (1893) he scolded the devotees who came late to the Harivāsara kīrtana: "You need not come to Harivāsara any more. Better live happily at home with your loved ones." They did not understand. The next day, after the offering to **Rādhā-vinoda**, they took prasāda, and in the afternoon he read from the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* the deliverance of Jagāi and Mādhāi, sighing: "Alas, such a sinner as I cannot be delivered." That night, after his meal and a short rest, he called his son **Gaura-bābu**: "Gaura, bring all the devotees and let them begin kīrtana; my last hour is near." He began his japa and asked to be carried into the courtyard facing Rādhā-vinoda; his body became paralysed, but his tongue went on chanting, and in the second quarter of the night, without any of the pangs of death, he passed away chanting the holy name.
