## In short

**Jagadānanda Ṭhākura** (born between 1698 and 1708; died 1780), a vaidya padakartā of the family of **Mukunda Sarakāra of Śrīkhaṇḍa**, the intimate devotee of Lord Caitanya, installed Deities of **Lord Gaurāṅga** at **Jofalāi** (Birbhum) and at **Āmalālā Sunurī**, a village given him by the king of Pañcakoṭa. His songs were gathered in the *Jagadānandera Padāvalī*.

## Family and homes

His father was **Nityānanda** and his grandfather **Paramānanda**; he was the second of four brothers, with **Sarvānanda**, **Kṛṣṇānanda** and **Saccidānanda**. His family home was at Śrīkhaṇḍa, but he lived for a time at **Āgaraḍihi Dakṣiṇakhaṇḍa**, near Ondal station, and moved from there to **Jofalāi**, where he installed Lord Gaurāṅga; Gopīnāthajīu (without Śrī Rādhā) is also installed in that temple. He passed away there on the **5th of Āśvina, 1780**.

## Gaurāṅgera Sāyara

He was hospitable and served visitors every day. Once some sādhus from a western state came to him tired and thirsty, wanting water only from a well dug by a brāhmaṇa — and there was no such well at Jofalāi. Remembering Lord Gaurāṅga, Jagadānanda dug into the earth with an iron rod, and water gushed out as a spring; the pond it formed is called **Gaurāṅgera Sāyara**.

## Āmalālā Sunurī

He also stayed at Āmalālā Sunurī, a village of the ruler of Pañcakoṭa. In the middle of a lake there was an island where he went every day to chant Harināma — walking across the water in his wooden sandals. Seeing this, the king of Pañcakoṭa gave him the village in the presence of his court. Jagadānanda installed Lord Gaurāṅga there, and the sevāits of the Deity still enjoy the village's revenue.

## His works

Kālidāsa Nātha and Dhīrānanda Ṭhākura published his songs as the *Jagadānandera Padāvalī*. He also translated the *Gīta-govinda* into Bengali (Burdwan Sāhitya-sabhā ms. 185) and wrote the *Citrapada-kāvya* (Gaura-pada-taraṅgiṇī, p. 10).
