## In short

**Dhruva Gosvāmī** was a sannyāsī of **Kāmyavana** in Vraja who, fleeing the tyranny of the Muslims, brought the Deities **Śrī Śyāmacāṅd** and **Śrī Balarāma** — and twelve Gopāla Deities — to Bengal, carrying them on his head. At **Maṅgaladihi** (Birbhum) he left Śyāmacāṅd and Balarāma in the care of his friend **Gopāla**, son of Mansukha; when he tried to take Them back, the Lord told him in a dream to return Him to Maṅgaladihi. The story is told in verse in **Jagadānanda**'s *Śrī Śyāmacandrodaya*.

## Bhāṇḍīravana

With the twelve Gopālas he reached Bengal and stayed for a time at the village of **Bhāṇḍīravana**. He left it after a tragedy at the Dola-mañca: a young widow of the royal family of Khaṭaṅgā, near Bhāṇḍīravana, had a secret love affair with the family's brāhmaṇa cook; the furious king ordered the cook beheaded, and though he fled to Dhruva Gosvāmī's āśrama, the king's guards seized him there and killed him.

## The twelfth Gopāla

Dhruva moved on with the twelve Gopālas and came to the **Mayūrākṣī**, which, though Caitra is usually dry, was flooding with an early monsoon. He placed the Deities one by one in the boat, but the twelfth would not move; so he gave that Gopāla to a poor brāhmaṇa beggar and went on. The brāhmaṇa carried Him at his heart to the house of Nandadulāla Ghoṣāl at **Noādihi** and left Him there; long afterwards a generous brāhmaṇa, Rāmanātha Bhāduḍī, built a temple at Bhāṇḍīravana where that Gopāla was installed.

## Śyāmacāṅd at Maṅgaladihi

At last Dhruva reached **Maṅgaladihi** and stayed in the house of a paṇḍita. **Gopāla**, a pious Vaiṣṇava of the village, came to meet him, and they quickly became friends; Dhruva told him his past and the story of Śyāmacāṅd. Leaving Śyāmacāṅd and Balarāma with Gopāla, he went on pilgrimage to Jagannātha Purī, and Gopāla, his wife Lakṣmīpriyā and his sister Mādhavīlatā joyfully served Them. After four years Dhruva returned and took the Deities away, leaving the three servants in grief — but before he reached the edge of the village the Lord appeared to him in a dream, in His form as **Viśvambhara-mūrti**, and ordered him to bring Him back to Maṅgaladihi.
