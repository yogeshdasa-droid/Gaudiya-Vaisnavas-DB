## In short

**Gauragovindānanda Bhāgavata Svāmī** was a renunciant of the first half of the twentieth century who, almost without schooling, became a master of all the śāstras by the blessing of a dying Vaiṣṇava saint and through dreams; he lived seven years in a dark cave at **Kāmyavana**, preached in Śrīhaṭṭa, Tripura, Silchar, Dhaka and Mymensingh, wrote four *Kusumāñjalis*, and passed away on **8 Jyaiṣṭha 1349 BS, the śukla saptamī** (May–June 1942).

## Childhood

He was born in a high-caste brāhmaṇa family of a prosperous village in the **Jessore** district (Bangladesh). His parents died when he was very young, and he was brought up by his maternal grandfather, **Sānyāl Mahāśaya**, a hospitable professor. One day, when his grandfather was out, an old brahmacārī with a long rosary and a tulasī garland came to rest; the boy seated him, gave him water and fanned him, and after he left ran after him in the afternoon sun: "Ṭhākura, I forgot to bow at your feet!" — "You have given me much more than your obeisances. Go home, son; maybe some other time…" Soon after his grandfather's death, at thirteen or fourteen, he left home with a brahmacārī, wearing Vaiṣṇava dress though he had no dīkṣā. The brahmacārī's guru, a Tāntrika, wished to make him a renunciant, and one evening, after his songs to Kālī, embraced him: "Your guide and guru is waiting to deliver you." He then studied bhakti-śāstra in a Vaiṣṇava householder's āśrama in Navadvīpa, and left it when a widow tried to seduce him.

## The dying saint

Wandering as an udāsīna, he met again the brahmacārī of his childhood, **Nitāi dāsa**, who took him to his own guru's ākhaḍā. The old Vaiṣṇava, very ill, cried on seeing him, "Oh! So this is him! Is he Gauracandra?" and kept him as his guest. While Nitāi went to fetch the guru's wife, the young Svāmī nursed him day and night, holding him on his lap, giving medicine and massaging him with ghee. Bathing in a pond full of women, he chose a weedy ghāṭa and came up with a water snake coiled round his neck; chanting "Jaya Nitāi" he dived again and it was gone. The guru's wife, an old ascetic, told him: "You worship Abhayā; when snakes and tigers make way for you, why fear human beings who are your mothers and sisters?" That night the guru, dying, made him sit beside Nitāi — "my Gaurāṅga and my Nitāicānd" — begged his wife's forgiveness, stretched out his feet to the Svāmī, "Take everything I possess," and at dawn, when he raised his cupped hand, the Svāmī held it and felt a powerful current pass through his body; when he came to, the guru had left. "Hold carefully what you have received from the mahātmā," said the old mother, "and use it with judgement."

## Knowledge in dreams

At Nutan Cara in Navadvīpa, **Nikhilānanda Gosvāmī** of Vāṇī (Mymensingh), amazed by his learning, asked where he had studied: apart from a little grammar as a child, he said, the śāstras had been revealed to him in dreams by that saint's blessing — he would hear the *Rāsa-pañcādhyāyī* in a dream and recite it all in the morning; so he knew the Bhāgavatam and its commentaries, the ten Upaniṣads, Nyāya, Mīmāṁsā, Yoga and Vedānta. About **1903** he studied Vedānta at Kāśī under **Paramānanda Tīrtha**, dressed as a brahmacārī with tilaka and tulasī.

## Devagiri Muni

In **1905–06**, keeping a vow to live a month in Mathurā, he stayed at an āśrama on **Kṛṣṇagaṅgā**, next to an old saint whom he begged for kṛpā: "Kṛpā, kṛpā — what kṛpā? All right, I shall see." Remembering *Bhāgavatam* 1.5.25, he took up removing the devotees' leaf plates and sweeping, until the saint asked him where he had learned such things, and had him stopped; the Svāmī lay weeping at his door, until he heard the saint recite 1.5.24 — *cakruḥ kṛpāṁ yadyapi tulya-darśanāḥ* — and was taken in his arms: "From now on you shall read the Bhāgavatam to me." One day, overwhelmed, the saint said, "Come, take kṛpā," and put his foot on his head. He told him to bow at the Lord's feet in Vṛndāvana, keep a year's silence at **Govardhana** and relish the Bhāgavatam; then he would know kṛpā entirely. This paramahaṁsa, a godbrother of the famous Bhāskarānanda Svāmī of Kāśī, was **Devagiri Muni**.

## Later life and books

He lived seven years in a dark cave at Kāmyavana in intense bhajana; ill with a bleeding nose, he went to Vṛndāvana, then preached in **Śrīhaṭṭa, Tripura, Silchar, Dhaka and Mymensingh**. His books: *Kṛpā-kusumāñjali* (1935), *Sādhana-kusumāñjali* (1936), *Śrī Guru-vaiṣṇava-bhakti-kusumāñjali* (1940) and *Śrī Līlā-tattva-kusumāñjali* (1941); in the first he tells of the saints he met — Paramānanda Tīrtha of Kāśī, Bholānanda Giri of Hardwar, Kṛṣṇaprasāda dāsa Bābājī ("Guḍhi Bābā") of Vṛndāvana, Siddha Jagadīśa Bābā and Rāmadāsa Kāṭhiyā Bābā.
