## In short

**Rādhāramaṇa Ghoṣa Bhāgavatabhūṣaṇa** of **Kāgojīṭolā** was the private secretary of **Mahārājā Vīracandra Māṇikya** of Tripura, who became his guide to Vaiṣṇavism; after a vision of Śyāmasundara in Vṛndāvana he spent his life reading the Bhāgavatam to Kṛṣṇa, and with his help and a gift of a lakh of rupees **Rāmanārāyaṇa Vidyāratna** of Berhampore published the Bengali Bhāgavatam with four commentaries.

## The king and the monkey

He was the son of a great devotee, **Kṛṣṇagovinda Ghoṣa**, a high officer in charge of government records. After graduating he managed the zamindari of Maharṣi Debendranāth Ṭhākura, then became tutor and finally private secretary to Sir **Vīracandra Māṇikya Bahādur**, King of Tripura, a great lover of Lord Caitanya, who valued him above all his court for his honesty and austerity. Not yet a Vaiṣṇava, he kept some Vaiṣṇava rites for his work and went with the king on his many visits to Vṛndāvana, where the king lived in his own kuñja. One day a monkey ran off with a costly shawl from the veranda and tore it up in a tree while the courtiers tried everything to get it back; the king watched smiling, his face wet with tears. That evening Rādhāramaṇa asked why. "Today the monkey taught me a great lesson: one should come to Vṛndāvana like a mendicant. I came with mountains of goods; this great soul in a monkey's form showed me that one must not bring worldly things to Vṛndāvana. It is very hard to be a true Vaiṣṇava; the spirit of Vaiṣṇavism is humility, and Gaurāṅga was humility itself — will He ever be merciful to me, a king?" Alone in his room, Rādhāramaṇa decided: the religion that makes a man so soft and humble is the best; Vaiṣṇavism is the supreme religion and Gaurāṅga the greatest avatāra. So the king may be called the guru who showed him the path.

## The tamāla tree

In Vṛndāvana he began to walk alone or with sādhus. One day he found a renounced Vaiṣṇava reading the Bhāgavatam under a **tamāla** tree with no listeners, explaining it wonderfully to Kṛṣṇa in the form of the tree. Day after day he sat silently behind him, until the saint saw him and called him to listen to the end. On the last day the trunk split open and showed Śyāmasundara with His flute in a bright blue light; Rādhāramaṇa fainted and woke with his head on the saint's lap. "Rādhāramaṇa, my Kṛṣṇa has mercifully given you His darśana. Now read the Bhāgavata to Kṛṣṇa, who is not different from it, and you will have its full fruit."

## The reader

He read the Bhāgavatam at home and with devotees for the rest of his life, explaining every verse in the line of Śrī Gaurāṅga; his intimate devotee Dr **Satyanārāyaṇa dāsa** said that Jīva Gosvāmī and Viśvanātha Cakravartī entered him as he read. He would read two or three verses of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, close his eyes and explain them so that even confirmed agnostics were drawn to Mahāprabhu — to five or six listeners, in a broken-down room or on the grass under the trees. His help enabled Rāmanārāyaṇa Vidyāratna to publish the Bengali Bhāgavatam with four commentaries, and he gave a lakh of rupees for it to be distributed free; many other Vaiṣṇava books were published under his editorship. A loving father and husband, a model householder, he mixed freely with great and small, high and low, rich and poor. When smallpox broke out he caught it nursing a neighbour, and passed away at a ripe old age.
