## In short

Rāmakṛṣṇa Ācārya (also called Rāmakṛṣṇa Cakravartī) was a brāhmaṇa disciple of **Narottama Ṭhākura**, whose Śrīpāṭa was at **Goyās**, where the Ganges and the Padmā meet. His elder brother was **Harirāma Ācārya**, and their father **Śivānanda (Śivāi) Ācārya**, a staunch Śākta.

## The goats for Durgā

At the time of Durgā-pūjā their father sent the two brothers to buy goats and a buffalo for the sacrifice. On the way, at the Padmā, they met **Narottama Ṭhākura** and **Rāmacandra Kavirāja**; Narottama, seeing them, told Rāmacandra that these two were fit to worship Kṛṣṇa, and spoke to them of the great offence of killing animals. They let the animals go, wept, bathed in the Padmā, and took dīkṣā: Rāmakṛṣṇa, the younger, from Narottama, and Harirāma, the elder, from Rāmacandra (*Narottama-vilāsa* 10). They stayed at the house of the devotee Balarāma Kavirāja. The Durgā-pūjā went without its sacrifice; their father, furious that a kāyastha had initiated his brāhmaṇa sons, brought the paṇḍita **Murāri** from Mithilā to debate them, but the brothers defeated him with the conclusions of the Bhāgavatam, and Durgā rebuked Śivānanda in a dream.

## Life

Rāmakṛṣṇa brought **Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī** to Narottama's feet. When Narottama entered the Gaṅgā at Gambhilā at the end of his life, Rāmakṛṣṇa and Gaṅgānārāyaṇa led the two kīrtanas and poured water over him.

His wife was **Kanakalatikā Devī**. His elder son **Rādhākṛṣṇa** died young; the younger, **Kṛṣṇacaraṇa**, was adopted by Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī (others say by Harirāma Ācārya), and was the teacher (śikṣā guru) of **Viśvanātha Cakravartī**. His descendants live at **Saidābād** (Murshidabad), and the king of **Manipur** is their disciple (*Prema-vilāsa* 20; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 15.121–122).
