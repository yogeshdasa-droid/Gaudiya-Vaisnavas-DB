## In short

Rasikānanda Prabhu (**Rasika Murāri**, Rasikānanda Deva Gosvāmī) was the foremost disciple of **Śyāmānanda Prabhu**, a prince of **Rohiṇī (Rayaṇī)** on the Subarṇarekhā who spread the faith of Gaura-Nityānanda through Orissa and western Bengal, served Śrī **Rādhā-Govinda** at **Gopīvallabhapura**, and at the end entered the Deity of **Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha** at Remuṇā. "The best branches of Śyāmānanda are Rasikānanda and Śrī Murāri, whose glories all Utkala sings; Śyāmānanda's two dear disciples, at Rayaṇī on the bank of the Subarṇarekhā" (*Prema-vilāsa* 20).

## Birth and youth

His father **Acyutadeva** (Acyutānanda), zamindar and king of Rohiṇī in Mallabhūmi, had long been without a son; by the mercy of Lord Jagannātha the boy was born in **1512 Śaka (1590 CE)**, on the night of **Dīpāvalī** — the śukla pratipat of Kārtika (another text: 18 Kārtika). His mother was **Bhavānī Devī**. He was called Rasikānanda and Murāri. He mastered the scriptures very young and was devoted to his parents; he was married early to **Śyāmadāsī** of Ghāṭaśilā on the Subarṇarekhā, where the Pāṇḍavas once lived in exile.

## Śyāmānanda

Longing for a guru, he heard a voice from the sky: "Do not worry; you will become the disciple of Śyāmānanda." When Śyāmānanda came from Dhārendā-Bāhādurpur to Rohiṇī with his devotees, japa-mālā in hand and eyes full of tears, Rasika knew him at once, fell at his feet, brought him home and worshipped him, offering himself, his family and his kingdom. Śyāmānanda initiated him and his wife. He travelled with his guru, became his most intimate disciple, and was given the service of **Śrī Rādhā-Govinda at Gopīvallabhapura**.

## Preaching

"By his preaching many rogues, robbers and atheists were delivered; he gave the jewel of bhakti even to Muslims, going from village to village with his disciples; the wild elephant sent to kill him he made his disciple and engaged in the service of Kṛṣṇa" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 15.81–86). The elephant was named **Gopāla dāsa**; two jungle tigers gave up their ferocity. Kings took shelter of him — **Vaidyanātha Bhañja** of Mayurbhañj, **Gajapati** of Pāṭaspur, **Candrabhānu** of Moyna — and also the cruel zamindar **Bhīma**, the Muslim governor **Ahmad Beg** and the atheist **Śrīkara**. He preached for about forty-six years by his guru's order (one text: sixteen).

**The Ratha-yātrā.** One year he was preaching far away and hurried to Purī, but the festival had begun. Lord Jagannātha stopped the chariot; not even the king's elephants could move it, until the Lord told the king He was waiting for His devotee Rasika. When Rasikānanda arrived with offerings of silk and the king asked him to pull the rope, the cart moved easily. The king gave him land; at the place called Phulatoṭā Maṭha (now Kuñja Maṭha) he installed **Śrī Baṭa-Kṛṣṇa**, now **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Rasika Rāya**.

## Departure

On the **śukla pratipat of Phālguna, 1574 Śaka (1652 CE)**, he left the village of Sāntā (Sarata) without telling anyone and walked to **Remuṇā**. He spoke of Kṛṣṇa with the devotees there, told them always to serve Kṛṣṇa, had them start saṅkīrtana, entered the temple, touched the feet of **Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha** and entered into Him. His samādhi is there.

## Family, disciples and works

His three sons, **Rādhānanda, Kṛṣṇagovinda and Rādhākṛṣṇa**, are the ancestors of the Mahānta family that still serves Rādhā-Govinda at Gopīvallabhapura. His life is told in the *Rasika-maṅgala* of his disciple **Gopījana-vallabha dāsa**. He wrote the **Śyāmānanda-śataka**, the **Śrīmad-bhāgavatāṣṭaka**, the **Kuñja-keli-dvādaśaka**, and other hymns and songs.

## Dates

**Appearance:** Kārtika śukla pratipat (Dīpāvalī night), 1512 Śaka (1590 CE). **Disappearance:** Phālguna śukla pratipat, 1574 Śaka (1652 CE). The Gauḍīya calendar keeps both.
