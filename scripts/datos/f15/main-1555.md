## In short

**Tribhaṅga dāsa Bābājī** (died 1944), of a Sat-śūdra family of **Kīrtipura** (Murshidabad), was a kīrtanīyā who served the Harivāsara of **Pāñcthupi** and, from 1927, by Nityānanda Prabhu's order, gave the rest of his life to restoring the temples of **Ekacakrā**. He took the Vaiṣṇava dress from **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa Bābājī** at Nīlācala, with the name **Banwārīlāl dāsa**.

## Boyhood and training

His parents were **Chavilāl** and **Garavinī**. As a boy he took to the fields a Deity of **Vṛndā-devī** in a little earthen pot and fed her before he ate. In youth he kept company with two famous Vaiṣṇavas, **Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra Maṇḍala** and **Śrī Banwārīlāl Siṁhajī**, and longed to live like them. Maṇḍala Mahāśaya, seeing the boy's bent, took him from home to **Tithāgrāma**, his guru's seat, and had him initiated in the family of **Mother Jāhnavā**; there he served all the Gosvāmīs, Ṭhākuras and devotees. Later Maṇḍalajī had him learn the **Manoharaśāhī** style of kīrtana from the famous Śrī Dīnu dāsa, and he took further lessons in it from the school of **Advaita dāsa Bābājī** while at Pāñcthupi.

## Pāñcthupi and Nīlācala

Siṁhajī appointed him to serve the Vaiṣṇavas of the Harivāsara at Pāñcthupi. Once, by Siṁhajī's order, he walked to Vṛndāvana and back with only four ānās in his pocket. With Siṁhajī's leave he went to Nīlācala and took the Vaiṣṇava dress (veśa) from **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa Bābājī**, who named him Banwārīlāl dāsa — though everyone went on calling him Tribhaṅga — and after four or five months with the Bābājī at Jhāṅjpīṭā was sent back to serve Siṁhajī. After Siṁhajī's passing his son **Vijayakiśora Siṁha** entrusted the Harivāsara to him.

## Ekacakrā

From **1927**, directed by Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu, he gave himself to reforming and serving the temples of **Ekacakrā**, travelling everywhere to collect funds; from 1929 he arranged continuous saṅkīrtana and prasāda for crowds of devotees at Nityānanda's appearance festival. In **1944**, when the festival had been completed as usual, he said to Nityānanda: "Nitāi, give me my ticket" — closed his eyes and departed.
