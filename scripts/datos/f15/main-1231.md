## In short

**Rāmatanu Mukhopādhyāya «Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa»** (died 1843/44), of Nadia, a master of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and the Gosvāmī-śāstras, preached Gaura-bhajana and the **Gaura-mantra** across Bengal together with **Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābā** and **Jiyaḍa Nṛsiṁha Barāṭ** (#614), founding Gaura communities and defeating the Śāktas in debate. He left this world listening to the Gaura-kīrtanas of Nāgarī-bhāva, attended by his favourite disciple **Nityānanda dāsa**.

## The Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa

He was the second of four brothers. His elder brother **Vedāntavāgīśa**, finding no peace after studying many śāstras, went on pilgrimage, was blessed by a siddha Vaiṣṇava mahānta and took initiation in Śrī Caitanya's Vaiṣṇavism; back home he told Rāmatanu to follow the Vaiṣṇava path. Rāmatanu became expert in the Bhāgavatam and the Gosvāmī books, received the title **Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa**, and gave himself joyfully to the bhajana of Gaurāṅga. At his father-in-law's village of **Ulā** near Rāṇāghāṭ, mostly Śākta, he was persecuted for preaching, and went to live with his sister at **Jirāṭ Balāgaḍ**, where he could preach among devotees.

## With Caitanya dāsa and Nṛsiṁha

He first met **Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābā** on the bank of the Gaṅgā at Navadvīpa; from then on they stayed together, discussing spiritual subjects and going for darśana of Śrī Gaurāṅga, and at his request Caitanya dāsa met **Jiyaḍa Nṛsiṁha Barāṭ** at Jirāṭ Balāgaḍ. By the preaching of these three, the record says, all Bengal was flooded with love of God. Unlike Nṛsiṁha, who also loved bhajana alone, Rāmatanu was eager to give the Gaura-mantra and establish Gaura communities. He was unequalled in reading the Bhāgavatam, and easily defeated the Śāktas with strong words and sharp arguments. Many became his disciples, among them **Gurudayāla Siṁha**, zamindār of Kāṭdahā; he preached in Dhaka, and the Mahārāja of Dinajpur was overwhelmed by his Bhāgavata reading.

## His passing

At last he lived with his favourite disciple, **Nityānanda dāsa**. He fell ill with dysentery and, though Nityānanda dāsa nursed him carefully, grew worse. Sensing his end, he insisted on hearing in public the Gaura-kīrtanas of **Narahari Sarakāra, Vāsu Ghoṣ, Mādhava Ghoṣ** and **Locana dāsa**, full of Nāgarī-bhāva, and while listening he departed, in **1843/44**. Nityānanda dāsa wanted to burn himself with his guru, and the other disciples stopped him only with great effort; after his guru's death he was like a madman.
