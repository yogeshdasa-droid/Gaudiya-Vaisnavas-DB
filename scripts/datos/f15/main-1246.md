## In short

**Rāmacandra Khān of Chatrabhoga** (born 1480), of the fourteenth generation of the family of **Makaranda Ghoṣ**, was a powerful official of Sultan Hussain Shah — for a time the adhikārī of **Chatrabhoga** — who, when Lord Caitanya passed by on His way to Purī after taking sannyāsa, risked his life to escort Him safely through the war-torn frontier to Orissa (Caitanya-bhāgavata Antya 2). He is not the Rāmacandra Khān of Benāpola (#1245), who offended Haridāsa Ṭhākura.

## The official

His family title was Ghoṣ; Hussain Shah gave him the titles «Khān», «Rāya» and «Mahāśaya». He lived at Bhadrakālī, north of Vāligrāma (Howrah), and married the daughter of **Purandara Khān Gopīnātha Vasu**, Hussain Shah's minister. After Hussain Shah's death, and Sher Shah's victory over Humāyūn in 1540, Sher Shah divided Bengal into subās, and Rāmacandra received one stretching from Hijli-Kāṇṭhi (Midnapore) to the far south of Orissa, so that he had to live at **Jaleśvara**. Then misfortune came: he was imprisoned for not paying the annual cess. His relatives collected what little money they could to free him; seeing it was not enough, he used it to free other zamindārs instead — and the Nawab, pleased, set him free with honour and called him «Mahāśaya».

## Meeting the Lord

Chatrabhoga, now under Mathurāpur police station (24 Parganas), was then on the Gaṅgā, and in 1509 the boat route to Purī passed through it. It was a dangerous time: the Subahdar of Bengal and Mahārāja Pratāparudra were fighting over the frontier — in 1510, says the Mādalā Pañji, Hussain Shah's commander Ismail invaded Orissa — and the soldiers of both kings were camped on the land between the Bhāgīrathī and the Subarṇarekhā (Caitanya-bhāgavata Antya 2.97-99). When Rāmacandra saw the Lord's ecstatic love he was overwhelmed and stood humbly with folded hands (Caitanya-bhāgavata Antya 2.82); the Lord asked him to see that He reached Purī safely, and Rāmacandra, staking his own life, escorted Him personally to the border of Orissa.

Every year in Caitra a bathing festival called **Nanda-snāna** is held at Chatrabhoga; Vaiṣṇava historians hold that it celebrates the Lord's arrival there.
