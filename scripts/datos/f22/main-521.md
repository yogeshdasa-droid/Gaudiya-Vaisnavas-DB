## In short

**Jagacchandra Ghoṣ** (1775–1871), of **Pāñcthupi** (Murshidabad), a householder of strict devotion, in 1853 took veśa in Vṛndāvana from a **Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī** and became **Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa**, living there twenty years on mādhukarī; in his old age he was called «Buḍā Bābā» at Madhumaṅgala-kuñja.

## Householder

Born in Agrahāyaṇa (November-December) 1775 at Pāñcthupi, he mastered Bengali and Persian. He kept his daily prayers, japa and austerities and studied the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* and other Vaiṣṇava books; even when poverty forced him to work for a time as a munśī, he never missed his daily rituals. He had great faith in prasāda and would throw none of it away — he would chew even the stone of a hog-plum, or chillies, if given as prasāda. He loved the holy name: once, on his way to his critically ill son, he met a group of kīrtanīyās singing Harināma, forgot his son and joined them. So strict was he that on the night of his daughter's wedding he left home to keep the Harivāsara vow in a neighbouring village, and came back the next morning.

## Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa

In **1853** he went to Vṛndāvana, took veśa from Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī and was named **Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa**. He lived in Vṛndāvana for twenty years on mādhukarī; when old age kept him from going out, he received prasāda at **Madhumaṅgala-kuñja**, where he was called «Buḍā Bābā». He passed away in **1871**.
