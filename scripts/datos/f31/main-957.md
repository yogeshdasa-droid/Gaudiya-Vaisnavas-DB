## In short

**Nābhājī** («Nābhādāsa»), given the name **Nārāyaṇadāsa** at his dīkṣā, was a **Rāmānandī** Vaiṣṇava and the author of the **Bhaktamāla**, the Hindi garland of the lives of the devotees. Born blind in a family of singers near **Rāmabhadrācala** on the Godāvarī, abandoned as a child in a famine, he was given sight by **Kīlhadāsa** and dīkṣā by **Agradāsa**, who installed him as ācārya at **Galtā** near Jaipur.

## Life

Near Rāmabhadrācala lived a Mahārāṣṭrian brāhmaṇa named Rāmadāsa, famed as a partial incarnation (aṁśa) of Hanumān, whose family, still known as «Lākhabhakta», were singers by profession; Nābhājī was born in it. He was blind from birth, but at five, the record says, he was given sight by a miracle. When famine ravaged the land his mother set out for a distant country, and on the way, fearing starvation, left him behind. Then he met **Agradāsa** and **Kīlhadāsa**: Kīlhadāsa gave him sight by sprinkling sanctified water on him, and Agradāsa gave him dīkṣā and the name Nārāyaṇadāsa, took him to Galtā (Gālavāśrama) near Jaipur and installed him there as ācārya.

## The Bhaktamāla

His *Bhaktamāla* has 195 ṣaṭpadīs and 213 poems (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna, vol. 1, p. 370 and vol. 2, p. 1271; Bhaktamāla 1).
