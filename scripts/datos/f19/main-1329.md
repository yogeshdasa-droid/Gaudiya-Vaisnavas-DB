## In short

**Rūpacāṅd Adhikārī** (born early in the eighteenth century, died about 1802/03) — born **Prāṇakṛṣṇa Caṭṭopādhyāya** at **Tālibpur** (Murshidabad) — was a reciter of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and the founder of the school of kīrtana called **Ḍhap-kīrtana**, which he sang in songs of his own.

## Life

He inherited his maternal grandfather's property at **Beldāṅgā**, studied Sanskrit at a local school and became an eloquent reciter of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Gifted with a beautiful voice, he learned music from a sannyāsī of the village of **Simuliyā** near Sālār (Murshidabad), who gave him a powerful drum called the **ḍubkī**; with it he became famous singing the Ḍhap-kīrtanas he composed.

Once, coming home from a performance, he was attacked by a gang of dacoits; he persuaded them to listen to him sing, and at once they were charmed. Descendants of **Jagat Seṭh**, drawn by his singing, gave him a large tract of rent-free land and built him a house. He died about **1802/03**.
