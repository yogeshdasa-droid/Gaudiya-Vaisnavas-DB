## In short

**Caṇḍīdāsa** («Dvija Caṇḍīdāsa»), a brāhmaṇa of **Birbhum**, priest of the goddess **Bāśulī** (Viśālākṣī) in his village, turned at her own command to Kṛṣṇa and wrote the Bengali songs of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa that **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** relished in ecstasy. He was a contemporary of **Vidyāpati**, whom his songs say he met. The Digdarśanī gives his birth as **1383** (1305 Śaka).

## Bāśulī's order

According to the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna he lost his parents young and, with no one to support him, became the priest of Bāśulī-devī in his village. It is said that he first worshipped the goddess and then, by Bāśulī's own order, became devoted to Kṛṣṇa and gave himself to composing padas on Kṛṣṇa-līlā. His bhaṇitās say so: in the famous «ki mohinī jāna bandhu» (Padakalpataru 807), «bāśulī-ādeśe dvija caṇḍīdāse kaya» — «by Bāśulī's order, says Dvija Caṇḍīdāsa» — and likewise in Padakalpataru 206, 213, 853 and 879.

The record tells the story as it is told in a sermon: two brothers, Caṇḍīdāsa, a rich worshipper of Caṇḍī with a great garden, and a poor Vaiṣṇava brother who had a śālagrāma but no flowers. One day the Vaiṣṇava, looking at the garden, mentally offered one of its flowers to his śālagrāma; the next day Caṇḍīdāsa picked that same flower and offered it to Caṇḍī, and she appeared at once. "I have worshipped you every day — why do you come today?" "Because you have offered me a flower already offered to the śālagrāma — it is prasāda." "Then why didn't you tell me to worship the śālagrāma?" "You never asked me who is Supreme. Kṛṣṇa is Supreme; the śālagrāma is the one to worship, not I. Go and worship Him — I am happy." Lamenting his wasted life, Caṇḍīdāsa wrote many songs, and Caitanya Mahāprabhu would listen to them and go into ecstasy.

## Vidyāpati and Rāmī

That he and Vidyāpati lived at the same time two songs show (Padakalpataru 2389, 2391): «Caṇḍīdāsa heard of Vidyāpati's qualities and longed to see him» — and they met, with Rūpanārāyaṇa, embraced, and «floated on waves of love». It is said that when he became Bāśulī's priest, a beautiful young widow, **Rāmī** (Rāmamaṇi), a washerwoman, also found shelter in the temple, and that, as with Vidyāpati and Lachimā, Caṇḍīdāsa loved her — he says so himself (Padakalpataru 641). The Abhidhāna says he saw her as a sakhī, a support of pure love without a trace of lust — «rajakinī-rūpa, kiśorī-svarūpa, kāma-gandha nāhi tāya» — and that many think the story baseless.
