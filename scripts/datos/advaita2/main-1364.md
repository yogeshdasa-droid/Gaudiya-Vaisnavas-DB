## In short

Śaṅkara (Śaṅkara Pāgala) was a disciple of Śrī Advaita Ācārya who was rejected by him for keeping to the path of jñāna rather than the path of bhakti that Advaita taught (*Bhakti-ratnākara*; *Advaita-prakāśa* 20, p. 93; *Prema-vilāsa* 24).

## The dispute

Advaita had for a time taught jñāna; when he later preached bhakti as the highest, Śaṅkara, with Kāmadeva Nāgara and Āgala Pāgala, would not accept it: "We are jñānavādīs; without jñāna no one attains perfection," said Śaṅkara. Advaita said: "Give up jñāna." — "Defeat me in argument." Advaita gave them up, and they were put out of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava community (*Prema-vilāsa* 24; *Abhidhāna*, entry «Kāmadeva Nāgara»).

## An Assamese account

One Assamese book says that this Śaṅkara was born of **Kusumvar Bhuiyan** and **Satyasandhā** at **Bardoā**, Naogaon (Assam), studied Sanskrit under Mahendra Kandalī, went with his teacher on pilgrimage to Bengal, and died in **1489 Śaka (1567 CE)** at the age of 111 (*Gaurāṅga-sevaka*, 1330 B.S. (1923), p. 539). These are the parents and birthplace of **Śrīmanta Śaṅkaradeva**, the great Vaiṣṇava teacher of Assam; that he was Advaita's rejected disciple is the claim of that one book only.
