## In short

**Vaṁśī dāsa Bābājī** (born **Bhairavacandra**), the great avadhūta of **Navadvīpa** in the first half of the twentieth century, lived with his Deities — Gaura-Nitāi, Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa and Lāḍḍu-Gopāla — talking with Them as with his own family, absorbed in vātsalya. Śrīla **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** held him in great respect. Born at **Majitpura**, Kiśoraganj subdivision, Mymensingh (now Bangladesh), he was over ninety in the early 1940s, and returned to Mymensingh to leave this world. His disappearance is on the **Śrāvaṇa śukla caturthī** (Śrīdhara Gaura Caturthī, Gauḍīya calendar).

## Early life

His father was **Sanātana Malobrahma**. Married young as was the custom, he had no taste for family life. He was initiated in the family line of **Narottama Ṭhākura** and took the dress of a renunciant. At first he followed the then widespread **Kālācāṅda** doctrine, but later entered pure Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism. Siddha **Gaurakiśora Bābā**, seeing his sincere service in Navadvīpa, said: "After Gaurakiśora he will be a great successor, but his doctrine will be different."

## With his Deities

In Navadvīpa he lived on alms; when the service of Śrī Gaura fell to him he depended wholly on begging, built a hut near the house of the elder Gosvāmī at **Caumuhanī**, and sometimes carried Gaurahari with him on his rounds. He never shut his door. When a golden chain given for Gaurahari was stolen he asked his Deity, "To whom have You given Your chain?", and went to the thief's house (one text: the thief pushed him off the veranda and he lost the use of a leg; another: the crowd that followed him made the thief return it). Harassed by the municipality, he moved near Jhāutalā; in the rains he covered Gaurahari with a ragged blanket and wiped His wet face; Lalitā-sakhī dāsī could not persuade him to live under a roof. His Deities spoke to him. He went only once to Kendubilva and once to Kheturī. At the end he lived long in a room north of the Bāḍāla ghāṭ, serving Nitāi-Gaura and Gadādhara, Rādhā-Govinda and Gopāla, and knew by heart Narottama's *Prārthanā* and *Prema-bhakti-candrikā*. He never spoke angrily, never said "I" or "my" — only "Vaṁśī dāsa had to go there". A campaka tree gave him two flowers every day; he climbed it like a young man even in old age, until a fall injured him, and then his disciples **Manohara dāsa**, **Govinda dāsa** and **Jagadbandhu dāsa** cared for him.

His worship had no rules: no throne, no changing of dress, no putting to rest — he simply lived with Them, offered flowers at midday and fried chickpeas, recited a few Sanskrit stutis in the morning, and talked with Gaura-Nitāi for hours in his Mymensingh dialect. "I'm hungry, feed Me quickly!" — "Wait, I'm still cooking! Get out!" When a half-mad brahmacārī of the Gauḍīya Maṭha took his Gaura-Nitāi to Māyāpur, thinking them neglected, Sarasvatī Ṭhākura cried, "Everything will be finished if you do not return Them at once!" Once he told Kṛṣṇa, "Get out! You already had date-sugar and sweet rice in Vṛndāvana" — and that day, they learned, the Rādhāramaṇa temple had offered exactly that.

## The avadhūta

He looked almost like a madman, without external consciousness, yet never said anything against the scriptures. He ate only every three or four days, always cooking for himself (a svapākī) and giving his remnants, sent as mahā-mahā-prasāda to sādhus of Navadvīpa and Vṛndāvana. He begged only at a few pious houses, calling "Gaura-Nitāi!" outside, refused most offerings ("They won't eat this"), sometimes picked thrown-out vegetables in the market, and smoked a hookah calling "Bhaktavatsala Hari!" (rumours that he ate fish are false, says Jyotiśekhara Prabhu, who was with him three months). Everything turned to Kṛṣṇa in his ears: "government" — "O Govardhana! Govardhanadhārī!"; a train on a bridge — "Rāsa-maṇḍala!"; the flies near Cuttack — "It is Nanda's house, so much milk and curd, why won't flies come?" On Holi he let the whole town throw colour on him, fasting, till he was a hill of powder. Hearing a boatman sing Narottama's song of the spear in the heart, he said: "You are singing; my heart is breaking." He went by bullock cart from Navadvīpa to Vraja and back in two years, talking all the way with Mahāprabhu and Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa: "I saw Nanda's house, but not Nanda and Yaśodā — only the stones."

## Sarasvatī Ṭhākura and the devotees

He called Bhaktisiddhānta "**Jagannāthera Bimalā**" (from his name Bimalā Prasāda), and when he came would cry, "A mañjarī has come — won't Rādhā come too?" Sarasvatī Ṭhākura visited him with disciples and told them to sit silently and commit no offence. The Gauḍīya Mission sent **Jyotiśekhara Prabhu** and two others to accompany him on foot from Navadvīpa to Purī and Kharagpur; asked for a blessing, he answered through a story told to Gopāla: "The Lord's mercy cannot be had by sādhana alone, any more than a road across the ocean by throwing in sand — first the mercy of the devotees." After Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's departure **Ananta Viśvambhara dāsa** won his way into his small circle by secretly gathering his remnants behind a bush, and read him the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* on the Ganges bank, Rāmānanda's talks making him weep. His intimates were few: Pūrṇa, Ananta Viśvambhara, the merchant Suren Kuṇḍu (who built him a house), Dāmodara Tulasī Bābājī, Pītāmbara dāsa, Dīnabandhu dāsa Bābājī; his two followers from Mymensingh, **Dāmodara** and **Bihārī**, went home with him.

Stories were told of his foresight — the flood he foretold, the girl he pulled back a moment before lightning struck the doorstep, the man cured of colic by a tulasī leaf, the mango brought by a dreaming brāhmaṇa at Balasore on Janmāṣṭamī — and of the night at Jājpur when unknown women came to bow to him where no village was.
