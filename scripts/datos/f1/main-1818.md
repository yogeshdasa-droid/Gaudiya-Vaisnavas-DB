## In short

**Gaura Kiśora Śiromaṇi** (Gauracandra Mukhopādhyāya), "the senior-most paṇḍita of the country", master of the six darśanas, smṛti and purāṇa, became a humble Vaiṣṇava of Katwa and then of **Vṛndāvana**, where he took veśa from **Siddha Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī** of Madana-mohana Ṭhaurā. He was born between **1813 and 1818** at **Guḍapāḍā** (Citāhāṭī), under Kutugrāma police station near Katwa, and passed away in Vṛndāvana in **1890**. "One should meet Śiromaṇi to understand the verse tṛṇād api," said Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī.

## Family and learning

He came of an orthodox brāhmaṇa family: his grandfather was **Guruprasāda Mukhopādhyāya Vidyānidhi**, whose sons were Rāmakṛṣṇa (Tapasvī Ṭhākura), Dhanakṛṣṇa and Prāṇakṛṣṇa; Gauracandra was the eldest of Dhanakṛṣṇa's sons (his brothers were Īśvara, Navadvīpa, Hariścandra Cūḍāmaṇi, Giriśa and Umeśa; his sister Pārijāta Devī). Until twenty he had no schooling and enjoyed village entertainments; rebuked by the villagers for not keeping up his family's learning, he changed at once, studied at Taipura, then at the Sanskrit school of Pañcānana Tarkaratna at Eyopura (Katwa), and later bhakti-śāstra under **Sakhīcaraṇa dāsa Paṇḍita Bābājī** of Gaurāṅgapāḍā, Katwa. He married **Rāmadāsī** of Oragrāma near Guskara, whose mother, a satī, had asked as her last wish that her daughter be given to him; two of their sons, **Rasavallabha Bhaktibhūṣaṇa** and **Vaiṣṇavacaraṇa Bhāgavatabhūṣaṇa**, were well known.

Though born in a Śākta family he took **Kṛṣṇa-dīkṣā** from **Vinodīlāla Ṭhākura** of Faridpur village (Nadia). Vaiṣṇava-sevā became the custom of his house; ignoring the brāhmaṇas' threats he openly ate the Vaiṣṇavas' remnants and drank water they had touched, keeping some to sip daily before his meal. His wife, as devoted as he, wore no jewels but shell bangles and washed the feet of every Vaiṣṇava guest.

## Katwa

Once, bathing at Katwa with his students, he stayed in the nāṭya-mandira of Lord Gaurāṅga and watched **Gopīvallabha Ṭhākura**, **Veṇīmādhava Ṭhākura** and others in kīrtana and iṣṭa-goṣṭhī; his ideas of bhakti changed overnight. He had his students collect the Gauḍīya books, gave up all other śāstras for the Bhāgavatam and its literature, and lived at Katwa: maṅgala-ārati, smaraṇa-kīrtana, bath, japa and meditation till two; then he cleaned the Vaiṣṇavas' latrines and swept the road to the Ganges, bowed to all, picked a grain from the Vaiṣṇavas' leftovers, and ate a little rice and boiled vegetables offered to Giridhārī. When Veṇīmādhava, out of provisions for a crowd of Vrajavāsīs, tore off his doors and window frames to sell, Śiromaṇi fainted, and vowed to make Vaiṣṇava-sevā his first duty. At Veṇīmādhava's request he explained the one verse *janmādy asya* three hours a day for thirty-five days. His goṣṭhī at Katwa was famous: Vṛndāvanacandra Gosvāmī of Advaita's family and his brother Jagadbandhu came there and took veśa from Kiśora dāsa Bābājī.

He would not drink water in a village with no Viṣṇu worship until a śālagrāma was brought ten miles on horseback; when tāntrika brāhmaṇas of Kāgrāma arranged animal sacrifice where he recited the Bhāgavatam, the goddess herself rebuked them, and he made them Vaiṣṇavas. Asked to judge the old dispute between the Gosvāmīs and the Vaiṣṇavas, he said: "Take me as your son: a grown son does not stand where his parents quarrel" — and both sides chanted together.

## "Gaura-vandanā between the lines"

Kuladānanda Brahmacārī (*Sadguru-saṅga*) tells: at a Bhāgavatam reading in his village the reciter began with a vandanā of Gaurāṅga, and Śiromaṇi, angry, asked where *anarpita-carīṁ* was in the Bhāgavatam. The reciter pointed to the white spaces between the lines: "Cleanse your eyes first. Take dīkṣā from a great Vaiṣṇava, practise for a week as I tell you, and on the eighth day, if you cannot see it, I will cut off the tip of my tongue." Śiromaṇi took dīkṣā from **Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī**, practised, and on the eighth day saw Gaura-vandanā in golden letters between every two lines. Weeping, he renounced everything and walked to Vṛndāvana.

## Vṛndāvana

There he taught the Bhāgavatam only in the afternoons — until a listener, to "show" how Śiva danced with Satī's body, sat in meditation chanting "Jaya Gaurāṅga" and was consumed by fire from his own toe; after that Śiromaṇi gave up teaching for stricter bhajana. The great Vaiṣṇavas of Vṛndāvana — Brahmānanda Gosvāmī of Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa, Nṛsiṁhānanda Prabhu, Nīlamaṇi Gosvāmī, the zamindars Gaṅgāprasāda Rāya of Tāḍāś and others — honoured him. Invited by Brahmānanda to eat from silver on a special seat, he insisted on a leaf plate: "You are my prabhu and I your dāsa."

Falling gravely ill, he took **veśa** from **Siddha Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī** of Madana-mohana Ṭhaurā. When, wearing the kaṇṭhī of the bheka, he went to bow to **Siddha Balarāma dāsa Bābājī** of Jhāḍu-maṇḍala, the Siddha rebuked him: "You still cannot do without cooked rice — how do you wear the kaṇṭhī of Sanātana Gosvāmī?" He wept, and lived from then on watery pulses and a little rice. He served his guru by keeping visitors from disturbing his bhajana: "Practise bhajana like the doves in our nahabat-khānā, who never fly away for all the music: all who come are sent by Gaurāṅga."

## Humility

No one could touch his feet or bow to him first; he prostrated himself to everyone, to cats, monkeys, cows, women, men and deities on both sides of the road. He called the woman sweeper "Mā": "She has cleaned my filth since I was born." For fifteen days he only bowed to Harimohana Gosvāmī, a fifteen-year-old descendant of Ṭhākura Jagannātha Ācārya who had come to learn Gaura-tattva, until the boy cried, "Bow as much as you like, but teach me!" — and he embraced him in tears. He sat at the back of gatherings lest his feet touch anyone. For accepting a gift from the Queen of Hetampur the Vaiṣṇavas of Vṛndāvana cut him off; admitting his fault, he recited the Bhāgavatam for a year in every one of the three thousand temples of Vṛndāvana, as they ordered. When devotees refused to eat beside a wrongdoer, he confessed before all every wrong of his own life; when others criticised an ācārya's descendant for taking low-caste disciples, he defended him. He let his wife and sons attend the readings at the Ṭhaurā: "My wife is my godsister; who am I to stop her?" — and Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī, after a day's fast, understood that this was detachment, not attachment.

## Disciples

**Annadāprasāda Rāya**, about to become a Christian, was brought to him by his mother and took initiation from him as **Advaita dāsa** (later Giridhārī dāsa Bābājī); Śiromaṇi told him not to remarry. Through him Annada's brother, **Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya**, came back from the Brāhmo Samāj, came to Vṛndāvana in 1885, and on Śiromaṇi's advice founded a school for the Gosvāmī books and a dispensary for the Vaiṣṇavas. **Mahendranātha Bhaṭṭācārya** of Jamalpur took refuge with him as **Haricaraṇa dāsa** (dīkṣā from Śiromaṇi's son Rasavallabha; he gave himself bheka in the Ganges when no one would). **Paṇḍita Rāmakṛṣṇa dāsa Bābājī**, his godbrother, was told by Siddha Nityānanda dāsa to honour him as guru. The Kaivarta Rajanī dāsa served him.

## His passing

In 1890, seeing off **Rādhikānātha Gosvāmī** at Mathurā station, he wept till he fainted, fell ill with fever in Vṛndāvana and passed away four or five days later. On the night Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī passed away he had seen him in a dream — "Śiromaṇi, here I am!" — and made him a seat in his house; beside it he placed the samādhi of the Baḍa Prabhu of Katwa, and beside that his relatives placed his own, where bhoga is still offered.
