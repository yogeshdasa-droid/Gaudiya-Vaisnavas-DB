## In short

**Haribolānanda Ṭhākura** (born **Navīncandra Bhaṭṭācārya**) spent his life falling at the feet of everyone he met in the streets of Calcutta and holding them until they said "**Haribol**". With a conch in his hand, a hat made of a Jagannātha-prasāda leaf plate and a little bag on his shoulder, he made disciples mostly among the poor and low-caste, named them after Mahāprabhu's associates, and led the street saṅkīrtana of the plague years. He was born on **Janmāṣṭamī** at **Gaṅgānandapur** near Jhikargāchā (Jessore), close to Śrīpāṭa Bodhkhānā, and passed away on **Varāha-dvādaśī**. His disciple **Vipina Bihārī Caṭṭopādhyāya** believed him to be Advaita Ācārya.

## The command at Kālīghāṭ

Until forty he lived as a householder and taught at the village school, a truthful, self-controlled and charming man; his younger brother was Benīmādhava Bhaṭṭācārya. Over a small family property dispute he left home for **Kālīghāṭ** in Calcutta and lay before the goddess until she told him to visit all the holy places of India on foot. He walked for years, penniless, without finding peace, and came back to Kālīghāṭ; it is said the goddess then appeared and told him: "My child, in Kali-yuga the name of Hari is the supreme essence. Chant Haribol without stop and give this name to everyone, begging them to chant it."

## "Haribol"

From then on, living at Bāgbāzār, he walked daily to Kālīghāṭ, clasping the feet of everyone on the way, chanting "Haribol, Haribol", and would not let go until they said it. On Chowringhee he held an Englishman's feet; offered money, he held on; whipped till his back bled, he held on — until a Bengali explained, and the Englishman knelt, apologised and said the name of God in English. He added "Haribol" to everything — rice-Haribol, dāl-Haribol — and spoke to no one until Haribol was said. Sometimes his conch seemed to blend with his chanting into one sound, or to speak like a human voice; so he was called Haribolānanda.

## Disciples and the plague

Many in Calcutta were drawn to him; **Kṣetranātha Mallik** of Rādhānātha Mallik Lane became his disciple. He named his disciples after the pārṣadas — Nityānanda, Rāmānanda, Śrīvāsa, Gadādhara — but never after Gaurāṅga or Advaita, and all chanted Haribol incessantly and fell at every visitor's feet; most were blacksmiths, potters, washermen and sweepers. During the **plague** in Calcutta, with Kṣetra Mallik's money, he had Harināma sung in every lane, day and night, by parties riding the trams; Rāmadāsa Bābājī, who saw it, said Christians took off their hats and knelt, and Muslims invited the singers into their mosques and joined them with their own banner — it seemed Mahāprabhu's mahā-saṅkīrtana again. When Kṣetra himself lay dying of plague, Haribolānanda brought water from Kālīghāṭ; Kṣetra slept, woke hungry and recovered — and Haribolānanda fled the fame to **Kāśī**, to Gaṇeśa-mahallā on the bank of Brahma-kuṇḍa.

He gave Kṛṣṇa-mantra to **Haricaraṇa Dhāḍā** of Uttarpāḍā, naming him **Gadādhara Haribol**, and would swim across the Ganges to his house and lead the children of Bāli, Uttarpāḍā and Konnagar chanting through the streets. His disciples included Kṣetranātha Mallik, **Vipina Candra (Bihārī) Caṭṭopādhyāya** of Mākaḍadaha, Haricaraṇa Dhāḍā, Bhūpāl of Kāmāchāṭī, Rajanīkānta Datta and Cārucandra Pāl of Midnapore, **Vinay Maitra** (Rāmānanda), Gadādhara dāsa, Nivāraṇ, Śarat and Rākhāl.

## Vipina Bābu

When Vipina was munsiff at Rāujān, Haribolānanda came to see him. After kīrtana Vipina begged him to reveal who he was — "My son, I am Haribol" — and at eleven at night held a revolver to his own chest: "Reveal yourself, or bear the sin of a brāhmaṇa's death." "I am Advaitacandra himself, the Lord of Śāntipura." Vipina fell at his feet, and later, before Rāmānanda (Vinay) and others, Haribolānanda granted him his feet. He often stayed with Vipina at Mākaḍadaha and Kālnā, wasting nothing — carrying dry palm branches for the Deities' cooking, sifting ashes to wash clothes.

## His teaching

"Haribol contains guru, Gaura, the gopīs, Rādhā-Śyāma and everyone; chanting Haribol one chants every name. Take me into a forest and cut off my head; if the head does not go on saying Haribol, stop chanting Haribol for ever." A disciple who chanted constantly but kept the rules imperfectly was worth sixty-three paisa, one who kept the rules but cheated in his chanting not one. He overlooked faults in chanters but never an offence to a devotee: from the loud-voiced Gadādhara, who sinned in a devotee's house, he withdrew all the prema he had given, and Gadādhara lost his golden voice for life; Vinay, cast off for disobedience, was forgiven after serving Vipina. He made his disciples bathe three times a day, eat moderately and chant always; he nursed his mother on her deathbed, served his Gopāla with his own hands, and for Vaiṣṇava-sevā cooked a pot of khicuḍi and fed whoever passed.

## After his passing

He loved **Rāmadāsa Bābājī** dearly. Some days after his death he came one morning to the house of the Śīl family blowing his conch and asked **Advaita dāsa Bābājī** for Rāmadāsa; while Advaita went in to call him, he vanished, and Rāmadāsa wept. Only then did they realise he had already left this world.
