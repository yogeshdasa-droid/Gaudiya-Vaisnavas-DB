## In short

Śrī **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**, the **Nāmācārya**, master of the holy name, was born in a Muslim family and chanted **three hundred thousand names** every day. He lived with Advaita Ācārya, bore beating in twenty-two market-places without giving up the name, preached the name with Nityānanda in Bengal, and in Purī left his body before Lord Caitanya, who carried him to the sea and buried him with His own hands (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.43–47; Antya 3, 11). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Brahmā**, with **Prahlāda** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 93–95).

## Birth

He appeared at **Buḍhan** in the old Jessore district (now Satkhira, Khulna). The appendix of the *Advaita-vilāsa* (p. 395) says he was born in **Agrahāyaṇa, 1372 Śaka**, in the house of **Khānaullā Kāzī**, and was orphaned a few months later; brought up by Muslim neighbours, he was known as "Yavana Haridāsa"; some say he was born a brāhmaṇa (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). The *Abhidhāna* gives the story that he was the son of Ṛcīka (Rāma) Muni, who, cursed by his father for offering unwashed tulasī, was born among Yavanas; and that some call him "Brahma-Haridāsa": Brahmā, who stole Kṛṣṇa's calves, came as Haridāsa to atone.

## With Advaita

He studied grammar, literature, philosophy and the *Bhāgavatam* with **Advaita Ācārya** and took the Vaiṣṇava dress (*Advaita-prakāśa*). At **Phuliyā** near Śāntipura he chanted in a cave at the foot of a tree; a great snake that lived there left when he asked it. Advaita offered him the śrāddha plate, which is given only to the best of brāhmaṇas: "feeding you is like feeding ten million brāhmaṇas" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 3.219–221). Rāmacandra Khān's prostitute, sent to tempt him at **Benāpol**, was converted by his chanting and became a great Vaiṣṇavī (Antya 3.98–137).

## The twenty-two markets

The Muslim governor (mulukpati) of Phuliyā had him tried: "give up the name and say the Kalmā." — "Even if my body is cut to pieces, I will not give up the name." He was beaten in twenty-two market-places and felt nothing, praying for his tormentors; taken for dead and thrown into the Gaṅgā, he came out at Phuliyā chanting; the governor begged forgiveness (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 16). In the Mahā-prakāśa the Lord showed him the marks of those blows on His own back (Madhya 10). To a brāhmaṇa of **Harinadī** who objected to loud chanting he answered that chanting aloud benefits even the birds and insects a hundred times more; the brāhmaṇa soon fell ill with leprosy (Ādi 16.267–307). At Cāndpur near Saptagrāma he taught that the name's shadow (nāmābhāsa) gives liberation, and Gopāla Cakravartī, who scoffed, lost his nose (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 3.188–208).

## In Navadvīpa and Purī

He was in the Lord's kīrtanas, went with Nityānanda to preach and to deliver **Jagāi and Mādhāi**, played the constable in the drama, and was at the Lord's sannyāsa. In Purī he lived at **Siddha-bakula**, never entering the temple; every day the Lord brought him prasāda; Rūpa and Sanātana stayed with him (Antya 1, 4). He gave the name to Mahāmāyā (the *Abhidhāna*: to Rāmadāsa at Benāpol, and others).

## Departure

Seeing his end near, he asked the Lord to let him go before Him; the Lord came with the devotees, sang, and Haridāsa, holding the Lord's feet to his heart and looking at His face, left chanting "Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya". The Lord danced with his body, bathed it in the sea, buried it with His own hands and begged prasāda for his festival from the shopkeepers (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 11.16–105). It was **Bhādra śukla caturdaśī**. His samādhi is at Purī.

## His places

Harinadī, Cāndpur near Saptagrāma, Benāpol (Haridāspur nearby; the ruins of Rāmacandra Khān's house at Kāgajpukuriyā), the prison at Gauḍa, Phuliyā, Śāntipura, Navadvīpa and Purī (*Abhidhāna*).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Agrahāyaṇa, 1372 Śaka (*Advaita-vilāsa*, appendix).
**Disappearance:** Bhādra śukla caturdaśī, in Purī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 11; Gauḍīya calendar).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Brahmā**, with Prahlāda; also Brahmā Mahātapā, son of Ṛcīka Muni (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 93–95).

## Not to be confused

Not **Haridāsa Paṇḍita**, the head of Govindadeva's service in Vṛndāvana, disciple of Ananta Ācārya and guru of Rādhākṛṣṇa Cakravartī, author of the *Daśa-ślokī-bhāṣya* — that belonged to his record by mistake; nor **Choṭa Haridāsa**.
