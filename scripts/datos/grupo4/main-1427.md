## In short

Śrī **Śivānanda Sena** of **Kumārahaṭṭa** (Hālisahar), a Vaidya, led the devotees of Bengal to Purī every year to see Lord Caitanya, paying for everyone and arranging their lodging and the tolls on the way (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 16.19–27). He was the father of **Kavi Karṇapūra**. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is the gopīs **Vīrā** and **Dūtī** together (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 176).

## Family

His sons: **Caitanya dāsa** (who wrote a commentary on the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*; Bhaktisiddhānta held he also wrote the Sanskrit *Caitanya-carita*), **Rāmadāsa** — the parrots Dakṣa and Vicakṣaṇa in Kṛṣṇa-līlā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 145) — and **Paramānanda**, whom the Lord named **Purīdāsa** before his birth, later Kavi Karṇapūra. His nephew was **Śrīkānta Sena**. **Śrīnātha Paṇḍita**, Advaita's disciple, was the family priest and Karṇapūra's guru; at **Kāñcanapāḍā**, a mile and a half from Kumārahaṭṭa, are the Gaura-Gopāla deities he installed and the temple of **Kṛṣṇa-rāya**.

## With the Lord

The Lord stayed at his house when He came to Bengal, and Nityānanda often stayed there. Once, on the way to Purī, no lodging was ready; Nityānanda, hungry, cursed: "Let Śivānanda's three sons die!", and then kicked him — and Śivānanda rejoiced at the kick and arranged everything at once. The boy Śrīkānta, hurt for his uncle, went ahead alone to Purī; the Lord, knowing it, said: "Śrīkānta has come unhappy; let him be" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 12.18–38; *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). He took care of a dog that followed the party; when it was lost he fasted; in Purī they found it eating coconut pulp thrown by the Lord, chanting "Kṛṣṇa", and it was liberated (Antya 1.11–33). In his house the Lord came to eat the food offered by Nṛsiṁhānanda Brahmacārī, as He later told in Purī (Antya 2.34–79). Jagadānanda stayed with him on his visits to Bengal (Antya 12).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Disappearance:** Āṣāḍha śukla dvitīyā (Gauḍīya calendar).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vīrā** and **Dūtī**, Rādhā's messengers (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 176, as his son wrote).
