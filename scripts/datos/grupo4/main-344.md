## In short

Śrī **Gopāla Guru Gosvāmī**, an Oriya brāhmaṇa, disciple of **Vakreśvara Paṇḍita**, was the head (mahānta) of the **Rādhākānta Maṭha** at the **Gambhīrā** in Kāśī Miśra's house in Purī, and the guru of **Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī**. His name before was **Makaradhvaja Paṇḍita**; he was the son of **Murāri Paṇḍita** (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). He wrote the **Gaura-govindārcana-paddhati** (*Smaraṇa-paddhati*).

## The name «Guru»

He grew up with Vakreśvara and served the Lord as a boy. The Lord did not like "Makaradhvaja" (Cupid) and called him Gopāla. Once, seeing the Lord hold His tongue between His teeth while going to answer nature's call — so as not to chant then — the boy said: "You are independent; but if a jīva following You died at that moment without chanting, what would become of him?" The Lord said: "You are right, Gopāla: from today you are 'Guru'." When **Abhirāma Ṭhākura**, whose obeisances could break a śālagrāma, came to test him, Gopāla hid in the Lord's lap, and the Lord marked his forehead with His foot, so he could bear the obeisances (*Abhidhāna*; the record).

## Rādhākānta and the Gambhīrā

After the Lord's departure Vakreśvara received the service of the Gambhīrā; after him Gopāla Guru. He renovated the temple of Rādhākānta and installed Rādhā and Lalitā beside Him and dancing Gaura and Nitāi; the oil painting of Advaita was kept. The Lord's quilt, sandals and water-pot are still there. He learnt the ways of rasa under **Svarūpa Dāmodara** and **Raghunātha dāsa**. **Narottama** met him there and they embraced in tears (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 8.382–389). A festival on Māgha śukla dvādaśī marks his becoming mahānta.

## His end

The story is told that when he left his body and Dhyānacandra took it to Svargadvāra, the king's men seized the maṭha because the succession had been made without the king's leave; at Dhyānacandra's tears Gopāla Guru rose from the bier and led a kīrtana to the palace, and the maṭha was given back. He lived on as mahānta through three kings and left again on **Kārtika śukla navamī**. The next year pilgrims saw him doing bhajana at **Vaṁśīvaṭa**; to Dhyānacandra, who ran to bring him back, he said: "I have reached Vṛndāvana; make a deity of me from the nimba tree of Siddha-bakula" — it is in the jagamohana of Rādhākānta. Branches of Rādhākānta: Vaṁśīvaṭa (his samādhi), Nidhuvana, Kāṅgālī Mahāprabhu Maṭha, Arunānanda-kuñja (Rādhā-kuṇḍa), Kiśorī-kuṇḍa (Yāvaṭ), the Gambhīrā Maṭha at Koladvīpa, Mādhavī Devī's Gopīnātha at Benthpur.

## Writings

*Gaura-govindārcana-paddhati* (*Smaraṇa-paddhati*); a *Vakreśvarāṣṭaka*; a commentary on the **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** in two parts (the record has it, in Jagadānanda dāsa's translation).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Disappearance:** Kārtika śukla navamī.
