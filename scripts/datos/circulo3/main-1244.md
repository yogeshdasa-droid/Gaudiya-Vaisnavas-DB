## In short

Rāmacandra Kavirāja was the dearest disciple of **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** and the inseparable friend of **Narottama Ṭhākura**: "Be merciful, Ācārya Prabhu Śrīnivāsa — Narottama dāsa begs for the company of Rāmacandra." A vaidya (physician by caste), a physician and a poet, he received the title **Kavirāja** in Vṛndāvana and is the first of the **eight Kavirājas** of Śrīnivāsa's line. His siddha name is **Karuṇā-mañjarī**.

## Family

His father **Cirañjīva Sena** of Kumāranagara, a dear devotee of Narahari Sarakāra, married **Sunandā**, daughter of the poet **Dāmodara Kavirāja**, and came to live at **Śrīkhaṇḍa** (Bardhamān). With Mukunda, Narahari and Raghunandana he went every year to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā and danced before the cart (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 11.92). Cirañjīva had two sons, **Rāmacandra** and **Govinda** (Govinda dāsa Kavirāja), born at Śrīkhaṇḍa. When their father died young, the boys grew up in the house of their grandfather Dāmodara, a Śākta, and were drawn to Śakti worship. Rāmacandra was handsome, intelligent and persevering, practised medicine and was known as a poet.

## Meeting Śrīnivāsa

After his wedding (his bride was **Ratnamālā**), he was carried in a palanquin past Śrīnivāsa's house at **Jājigrāma**, and saw the Ācārya on the veranda speaking of Kṛṣṇa. Each felt he was seeing a dear friend after a long separation; Śrīnivāsa asked who he was (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 8.530). That night Rāmacandra could not rest at home; he came back to Jājigrāma, spent the night in a brāhmaṇa's house and at dawn fell at the Ācārya's feet. Śrīnivāsa embraced him: "Birth after birth you are my dear friend. As the Lord brought me Narottama in Vṛndāvana, so He has brought you back to me" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 8.530–574). He studied the Gosvāmīs' books with him and took dīkṣā in the Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa mantra.

When the Śāktas of his village asked why he had left his grandfather's religion, he answered: "Śiva and Brahmā are guṇa-avatāras; Kṛṣṇa is the source of all avatāras, and by worshipping Him all others are worshipped. Prahlāda, Dhruva and Vibhīṣaṇa were dear to Śiva because they were Kṛṣṇa's devotees, while Rāvaṇa and Bāṇa, Śiva's worshippers, were destroyed by Him. Brahmā creates by worshipping Viṣṇu, and Śiva is great because he carries the Gaṅgā that washed Viṣṇu's feet." They had no reply. He brought his brother Govinda, gravely ill, to Śrīnivāsa's feet.

## Vṛndāvana

With the leave of Raghunandana he went to Vṛndāvana by Gayā, Kāśī and Prayāga, where Śrīnivāsa was staying, and Śrīnivāsa introduced him to Jīva Gosvāmī. He saw Govinda, Gopīnātha, Madana-mohana and Sanātana's samādhi, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Lokanātha and Bhūgarbha, the forests, and Raghunātha dāsa and Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja at Rādhā-kuṇḍa. Hearing his poetry, Jīva and the Vaiṣṇavas gave him the title **Kavirāja** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 9.214). On the way back he visited Śrīkhaṇḍa, Jājigrāma, Khaḍadaha, Ambikā and Navadvīpa, where the old Īśāna blessed him.

## With Narottama

He was Narottama's life and soul. With **Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī** he defeated the smārta paṇḍitas of **Rājā Narasiṁha** and the digvijayī **Rūpanārāyaṇa** at the market of Kumārapura, he selling clay pots and Gaṅgānārāyaṇa selling pān, both answering in Sanskrit (see Narottama's record). At the Padmā he and Narottama turned the two sons of Śivānanda Ācārya from animal sacrifice; Harirāma took dīkṣā from him. He took a leading part in the **Kheturī** festival, and went with Śrīnivāsa and Narottama to Navadvīpa, where Īśāna showed them the places of the Lord's pastimes. He was a lifelong renunciant at heart and greatly helped the preaching of his guru and Narottama. According to the *Abhidhāna* he was also the śikṣā-guru of King **Vīra Hāmvīra** of Viṣṇupura, and his obedience to Śrīnivāsa was matchless — whatever his guru ordered he did without a second thought (*Karṇānanda* 3).

## Last days

He lived first at Śrīkhaṇḍa, then at **Kumāranagara** on the Ganges, and with Govinda at **Teliyā-Budhuri** (Murshidabad). By the order of Śrīnivāsa and Narottama he went to Vṛndāvana a second time, but the Gosvāmīs had all left this world; overwhelmed by separation he too left his body there, remembering the feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, on the **kṛṣṇa tṛtīyā of Pauṣa** (Māgha kṛṣṇa tṛtīyā in the Gauḍīya calendar, which counts the month from the full moon — the same day). Soon after, Śrīnivāsa left this world, and Narottama, mourning them both, sang "*ye ānila prema-dhana*".

The Deities served by Rāmacandra and Govinda were taken from Teliyā-Budhuri to **Bhagavāngolā** (on the Sealdah–Lālgolā line), where the followers of Rāmadāsa Bābājī serve them.

## Disciples and works

His chief disciple was **Harirāma Ācārya**. He wrote songs in praise of Gaurāṅga ("*dekhā dekhā are bhāi gaurāṅga canda parakāśa*"), and, according to the *Abhidhāna*, the *Smaraṇa-camatkāra*, *Smaraṇa-darpaṇa*, *Siddhānta-candrikā* and a life of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya.

## Other persons of the same name

*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 11.51 names a Rāmacandra Kavirāja in Nityānanda's branch ("Kaṁsāri Sena, Rāma Sena, Rāmacandra Kavirāja; Govinda, Śrīraṅga, Mukunda — three Kavirājas"). The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition takes him to be Cirañjīva's son, but Mṛṇālakānti Ghoṣa rejected this (B. B. Majumdar). Rāmagopāla dāsa's *Śākhā-varṇana* names a Rāmacandra, disciple of Raghunandana.
