## In short

Govinda dāsa Kavirāja (**Govinda dāsa**, **Dāsa Govinda**, Kavīndra) was the younger brother of **Rāmacandra Kavirāja** and a disciple of **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, the foremost poet of **Brajabuli**: about 430 of his songs are in the *Padakalpataru*. He is one of the **eight Kavirājas** remembered every day by the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas. His Śrīpāṭa was at **Teliyā-Budhuri** (Murshidabad).

## Birth and youth

He was a vaidya, the son of **Cirañjīva Sena** and **Sunandā Devī**, born in the house of his maternal grandfather, the Śākta poet **Dāmodara Kavirāja**. His mother's labour was long and painful; Dāmodara, in the middle of his pūjā, signalled to a maidservant to show her the devī-yantra, but the maid bathed the yantra and gave Sunandā the water to drink, and the child was born safely. His father died soon after, and the brothers grew up with their grandfather as Śāktas.

Rāmacandra became a Vaiṣṇava, but Govinda remained a staunch Śākta. Wondering whether worship of Bhagavatī frees one from material life, he seemed to hear the goddess say: "Without worshipping Śrī Kṛṣṇa no one's distress goes away," and then: "Your desire will be fulfilled; be patient." At Rāmacandra's request he moved from Kumāranagara to Teliyā-Budhuri and built a house there. Another account says he fell gravely ill and, waiting for death, asked to touch Śrīnivāsa's feet; Rāmacandra brought the Ācārya, who put his feet on Govinda's forehead, and the next day gave him dīkṣā. Govinda recovered at once, and his first song, written then, showed him a born poet.

## Poet

He wrote the drama **Saṅgīta-mādhava** at the request of King Santoṣa Datta of Kheturī, the **Śrī Rāma-carita-gīta** at the order of King Harinārāyaṇa, fifty-one songs on the eightfold daily pastimes, and a **Gītāvalī**. About 430 songs in Brajabuli signed "Govinda dāsa" are in the *Padakalpataru*, others in the *Padāmṛta-samudra*, seventy-five in the *Gaura-pada-taraṅgiṇī*, seventy-nine in the *Kṣaṇadā-gīta-cintāmaṇi*; in twenty or so his name appears with Vidyāpati, Rāya Vasanta, Santoṣa, Bhūpati or Rūpanārāyaṇa. On his way back from Vṛndāvana he visited Vidyāpati's village **Bisphī** and recovered many of Vidyāpati's lost songs, and completed some unfinished ones. His mastery of the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* shows in his songs of śṛṅgāra-rasa, and he is still the favourite of the rasa-kīrtana singers.

His fame reached Vṛndāvana; Jīva Gosvāmī, Śrīnivāsa, Jāhnavā Mātā and the Vaiṣṇavas gave him the title **Kavirāja** (Kavīndra) and sent him a verse: "*śrī-govinda-kavīndra-candana-gireś cañcad-vasantānilair…*" — the breeze from the sandal-mountain of Govinda Kavīndra, fragrant with Kṛṣṇa, has maddened the bees who shelter at the wish-tree of Śrī Jīva (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 9.176).

He lived on the bank of the river at Paścimapāḍā in Teliyā-Budhuri (now Bubod village), and visited the courts of King Narasiṁha of Pakkapallī and King Pratāpāditya of Jessore; he was close to Vasanta Rāya, Pratāpāditya's uncle.

## Family and last days

His wife was **Mahāmāyā Devī**; their son **Divyasiṁha** was the father of **Ghanaśyāma** (a disciple of Gatigovinda), whose son was Svarūpanātha, and then Haridāsa. He left this world in **1534 Śaka (1612 CE)**. His Deity **Gopāla** and his family line still exist. The Deities he and Rāmacandra served are now at Bhagavāngolā.
