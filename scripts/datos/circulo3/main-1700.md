## In short

Vīra Hāmvīra was the king of **Vana-viṣṇupura** (Mallabhūma, Bankura district), a robber-king who stole the Gosvāmīs' books and was converted by **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, becoming his disciple. Jīva Gosvāmī named him **Caitanya dāsa** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 7.61; 9.5, 265–273). His wife was **Sulakṣaṇā** and his son **Dhāḍī (Dhīra) Hāmvīra**.

## The theft of the books

He was the leader of a band of dacoits. When Śrīnivāsa, Narottama and Śyāmānanda came through Vana-viṣṇupura with the Gosvāmīs' books in a chest on an ox-cart, his astrologer told him it held priceless treasure, and his men stole it one night while the devotees slept. When he opened it and saw Śrī Rūpa's handwriting on the palm leaves, his sins left him; in a dream a golden person told him: "Do not worry; I shall come to you; you are My servant birth after birth."

Śrīnivāsa stayed on at Viṣṇupura; the brāhmaṇa **Kṛṣṇavallabha** took him to the king's daily Bhāgavatam reading. The king fell at the feet of the effulgent stranger and gave him a high seat; Śrīnivāsa's reading melted the whole court, and the king, with a straw between his teeth, begged for mercy and gave back the books.

## Disciple

When Śrīnivāsa came back from Vṛndāvana with Śyāmānanda, the king danced with joy, worshipped him as his guru and held a great festival. Śrīnivāsa gave him dīkṣā (Caitanya dāsa) and his son Dhāḍī Hāmvīra (Gopāla dāsa), and performed with his own hands the installation of the king's Deity **Śrī Kālācāṅd**. From then on Vana-viṣṇupura was called simply Viṣṇupura, and many there took shelter of Śrīnivāsa. He arranged with great pomp Śrīnivāsa's marriage to Gaurāṅgapriyā. According to the *Abhidhāna* Rāmacandra Kavirāja was his śikṣā-guru. Two songs by him are known (*Karṇānanda*, p. 19). He is the "one king" of Śrīnivāsa's line.
