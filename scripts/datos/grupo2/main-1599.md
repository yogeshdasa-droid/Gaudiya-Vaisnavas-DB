## In short

Śrī **Vallabha Bhaṭṭa** (Vallabhācārya), founder of the **Vallabhī sampradāya** (Puṣṭi-mārga), was in Kṛṣṇa-līlā **Śukadeva** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 110). He received Lord Caitanya at his house at **Āḍāila** near Prayāga; later, in Purī, his pride of learning was humbled by the Lord, and with the Lord's permission he took the mantra of **Kiśora-gopāla** from **Gadādhara Paṇḍita**, leaving the worship of Bāla-gopāla (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 7; *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). The *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta* (56) counts "Vallabha Bhaṭṭa, resident of Āyarola (Āḍāila)" in Gadādhara's branch.

## Birth and studies

Son of **Lakṣmaṇa Bhaṭṭa** and **Illammāgāru** (Sallamagaru), Telugu brāhmaṇas of the Bhāradvāja gotra living at Hanumān-ghāṭ in Kāśī. Fleeing a rumoured Muslim attack, they set out for the South, and he was born on the way at **Campāraṇya** (Madhya Pradesh), in **1479** (one text: Vaiśākha kṛṣṇa ekādaśī, 1499). He studied in Kāśī (with Mādhavendra Yati, says one text), was at first a follower of Viṣṇusvāmī of the Rudra sampradāya, and at the court of Vijayanagara, where his maternal uncles lived, he met Vyāsatīrtha, refuted Māyāvāda and was given the title **Ācārya** (*kanakābhiṣeka*).

## Travels and home

He went round India three times; on the second tour he married in Kāśī and, not wanting to live as a householder there, settled at **Āḍāila** near Prayāga. With his merchant disciple **Pūrṇamalla** he built a temple on **Govardhana** and there set up again the **Gopāla** deity found by Mādhavendra Purī; he defeated the Māyāvādī sannyāsīs at Pañcagaṅgā-ghāṭ in Kāśī and built a house at Gokula. His sons: **Gopīnātha** (born at Āḍāila, c. 1510) and **Viṭṭhalanātha** (born at Caraṇādri, 1515). At Āḍāila he finished his commentary on the tenth canto of the *Bhāgavatam* and began the eleventh (*Abhidhāna*).

## Lord Caitanya at Āḍāila

When the Lord came to Prayāga on His way back from Vṛndāvana, Vallabha met Him and took Him by boat across the Yamunā (the Lord jumped into the river in ecstasy at its dark colour) to his house; he washed His feet, sprinkled the water on his family, gave Him new cloth, fed Him and **Rūpa and Anupama**, and pressed His feet. Rūpa and Anupama would not let him touch them; the Lord, to test him, said they were low-born, and he protested. **Raghupati Upādhyāya** of Tirhut sang there of Kṛṣṇa, and Vallabha made his two sons fall at the Lord's feet (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 19).

## In Purī

He came to Purī at the time of Ratha-yātrā and praised the Lord as Kṛṣṇa Himself, the only one who can give kṛṣṇa-prema; the Lord answered that He was a Māyāvādī sannyāsī and introduced him to Advaita, Nityānanda, Sārvabhauma, Rāmānanda and the others. The Lord would not hear his commentary on the *Bhāgavatam* nor his many meanings of the holy name ("I know only Śyāmasundara and Yaśodā-nandana"); the devotees refused too, and only **Gadādhara**, too gentle to say no, listened, with his mind on the Lord. Vallabha asked Advaita why the devotees, who take Kṛṣṇa as their husband, say His name; the Lord answered: "a wife obeys her husband, and He has told us to chant it". He said he did not accept **Śrīdhara Svāmī**; the Lord laughed: "one who does not accept the svāmī (husband) is a prostitute". Seeing the Lord's favour gone, he came humbly and asked forgiveness; the Lord told him to follow Śrīdhara Svāmī and chant the holy name, and dined again at his house. Then, with the Lord's permission, he took **initiation from Gadādhara Paṇḍita** into the Kiśora-gopāla mantra (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 7.1–171). His followers kept to his former way of worship. He placed his son Viṭṭhaleśvara at the Lord's feet (*Abhidhāna*).

## Works and teaching

His sampradāya counts eighty-four works: *Brahma-sūtra-aṇubhāṣya*, the *Subodhinī* on the *Bhāgavatam*, *Tattvārtha-dīpa-nibandha*, *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha*, *Śikṣā-śloka*, *Śruti-gītā*, *Mathurā-māhātmya*, *Madhurāṣṭaka*, *Puruṣottama-nāma-sahasra*, *Parivṛḍhāṣṭaka*, *Nandakumārāṣṭaka*, *Pañca-ślokī*, *Gāyatrī-bhāṣya* and others. He held two paths of bhakti, **maryādā** (vaidhī) and **puṣṭi** (rāgānugā); his doctrine is Śuddhādvaita, and his school has householders, not sannyāsīs.

## Disappearance

At **Hanumān-ghāṭ in Kāśī**, on **Āṣāḍha śukla dvitīyā**, 1531 (*Abhidhāna*; another text: the full moon of Āṣāḍha).

## Others of the name

Upendranārāyaṇa Siṁha did not accept that the Vallabha Bhaṭṭa of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* is the founder of the Vallabhī line, but Kavi Karṇapūra's "Śukadeva" and the *Subodhinī* make it likely. Grierson took him for the Vallabhācārya who was the father of **Lakṣmīpriyā**, the Lord's first wife — a different person (a Vaidika brāhmaṇa of Navadvīpa). In 1331 BS (1924) Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura spoke at the festival of the Puṣṭi-mārgīya Vaiṣṇava Saṅgha in Calcutta.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Śukadeva Gosvāmī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 110).
