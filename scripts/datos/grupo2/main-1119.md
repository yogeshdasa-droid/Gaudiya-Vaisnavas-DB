## In short

Śrī Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi, a Vārendra brāhmaṇa and rich landowner of Chittagong, a disciple of **Mādhavendra Purī**, was in Kṛṣṇa-līlā **King Vṛṣabhānu**, Rādhā's father; so Lord Caitanya called him **"father"** and gave him the name **Premanidhi**, "ocean of love" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 54–57). He lived like a prince, and no one took him for a Vaiṣṇava; yet one verse of the *Bhāgavatam* threw him into ecstasy. He was the guru of **Gadādhara Paṇḍita** and the intimate friend of **Svarūpa Dāmodara**. "Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi, a great branch: the Lord Himself wept taking his name" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.14).

## Birth and family

Father: **Bāṇeśvara Brahmacārī** (some write Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī; according to one opinion a descendant of Śivarāma Gaṅgopādhyāya); mother: **Gaṅgā Devī**; wife: **Ratnāvatī** (in Kṛṣṇa-līlā Kīrtidā). He was zamindar of **Cakraśālā** in Chittagong and also had a house in Navadvīpa: "sometimes he lived in Cāṭigrāma, sometimes he came to stay in Navadvīpa; this great soul was a disciple of Mādhavendra Purī" (*Prema-vilāsa* 22). He was a friend of **Mādhava Miśra**, Gadādhara's father. From childhood he loved Vaiṣṇava dharma and was mad with love of Hari; his learning was famous (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). The family's old home was at **Mekhalā**, near Hāṭ-hazārī, a few miles north of Chittagong; Śrīla Prabhupāda also names Bāghiyā near Dacca, and says the family, Vārendra among Rāḍhīyas, was once ostracised and had in each generation only one son or none (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.14, purport).

## "Father Puṇḍarīka!"

Before they had ever met, the Lord, after dancing in kīrtana in Navadvīpa, sat and wept, "Puṇḍarīka, father! When shall I see you?" The devotees thought He was calling Kṛṣṇa (Puṇḍarīkākṣa). He told them of a learned brāhmaṇa of Cāṭigrāma who looked like a man of pleasure, who would not touch the Gaṅgā with his feet nor bathe in her, but came at night to see her and sipped her water before worshipping the Lord, and asked them to draw him to Navadvīpa (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Madhya 7).

## Gadādhara's doubt and his initiation

He came with many disciples and servants; only **Mukunda Datta** and his brother **Vāsudeva Datta**, also from Chittagong, knew what he was. Mukunda took Gadādhara to him: he sat on a brass-inlaid bed under three canopies, among silk pillows, water-pots, betel and two mirrors, with servants fanning him with peacock feathers. Gadādhara doubted; Mukunda sang the verse on Pūtanā (*Bhāgavatam* 3.2.23), and Puṇḍarīka wept, roared, kicked away all his finery and lay in ecstasy for six hours. Gadādhara repented and asked to be his disciple; Puṇḍarīka said, "only after many births does one get a disciple like this", and initiated him on a dvādaśī. At night he came alone to the Lord, who embraced him: "today I have seen the ocean of love with My own eyes" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Madhya 7.44–112). He saw the deliverance of Jagāi and Mādhāi and was one of the Lord's chief companions in Nadīyā and in Purī.

## In Purī: the starched cloth

He came to Purī for the **Oḍana-ṣaṣṭhī**, stayed by the sea at Yameśvara, and gave Gadādhara the mantra a second time (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 16.76–81; *Caitanya-bhāgavata* Antya 10). At the festival Jagannātha was dressed in new, starched, unwashed cloth; walking home, he joked with Svarūpa Dāmodara about the servants of the temple. That night **Jagannātha and Balarāma** came in a dream and slapped his cheeks: "I have no caste, nor have My servants; I made this rule." In the morning his cheeks were swollen, and he rejoiced at the Lord's mercy; Svarūpa had never heard of the Lord punishing anyone in a dream (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Antya 10.67–187). One year, when the Bengal devotees went home, he stayed on in Purī with Svarūpa (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 16.76–77).

## His home today

The deities of **Lakṣmī-Govinda** he installed are still there; at his birthplace there is an earthen pot with his handwriting, and two Sanskrit plaques on the old temple, damaged by fire. The Chittagong Collectorate keeps the names of Bāṇeśvara Brahmacārī (tauzi 17697) and Vidyānidhi (26837, 17781); the thirteenth generation of his family lives at Mekhalā (*Abhidhāna*). Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī named two descendants, Harakumāra Smṛtitīrtha and Kṛṣṇakiṅkara Vidyālaṅkāra, and Sarojānanda Gosvāmī in Vṛndāvana.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Māgha śukla pañcamī, Vasanta-pañcamī (Gauḍīya calendar).
**Disappearance:** not given in the *Abhidhāna* nor in the Gauḍīya calendar.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vṛṣabhānu**, father of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī; his wife Ratnāvatī was Kīrtidā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 54–57). Because he was a direct disciple of Mādhavendra Purī the Lord honoured him as His own guru.
