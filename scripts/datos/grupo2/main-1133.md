## In short

**Puruṣottama Jānā**, son of **Gajapati Pratāparudra**, the independent king of Orissa, was a disciple of Gadādhara Paṇḍita (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 6.65). He sent **Śrī Rādhā** to Govindadeva in Vṛndāvana.

## The Rādhā of Govinda

Learning that neither Govindadeva nor Madana-mohana in Vṛndāvana had Rādhā at His left, he sent two deities. The priest of Madana-mohana was told in a dream that the bigger was **Lalitā** and the smaller **Rādhā**, and that both were for Madana-mohana, Rādhā at His left and Lalitā at His right; so Govinda was still without Rādhā. Puruṣottama had another made, but that night Govindadeva told him in a dream: "the deity worshipped as **Lakṣmī** in the cakra-beḍa of Jagannātha in Purī is not Lakṣmī but Rādhā: send her to Me." He sent her to Vṛndāvana with great pomp (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 6.63–72). The *Sādhana-dīpikā* (128–129) tells her history: she was first in Vṛndāvana; a devotee took her to Orissa; **Bṛhadbhānu**, a South Indian brāhmaṇa of Rādhānagara, served her at home; after his death a king of Orissa set her in the cakra-beḍa of Jagannātha, where she was worshipped as Lakṣmī.

## The Lord's mercy

For Lord Caitanya's mercy on the crown-prince of Orissa, see *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 12.58–64: seeing the boy, dark and beautiful, the Lord embraced him, and the prince was filled with love of Kṛṣṇa, so that the king saw him as a great devotee.
