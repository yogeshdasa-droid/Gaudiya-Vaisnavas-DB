## In short

**Uddhava dāsa** of Gadādhara Paṇḍita's branch lived in **Vṛndāvana**, in a hut by **Pāvana-sarovara**, very devoted to Sanātana Gosvāmī ("Śrīnātha Cakravartī and Uddhava", *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 12.83). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he had the **āveśa of Candrā** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 112).

## In Vṛndāvana

He was one of the devotees who went with **Rūpa Gosvāmī** for a month to see **Gopāla**, hidden from the Muslims in the house of **Viṭṭhalanātha** at Mathurā (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 18.51; *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta* 20). When **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, Rāghava Gosvāmī and others came to his āśrama on their round of Vraja, he received them warmly (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1333).

## A poet?

He too was a Vaiṣṇava poet (B. B. Majumdar). A poem by an Uddhava dāsa on **Navadvīpa**, telling in detail how Viśvambhara subdued the Kāzī in His street kīrtana, must be by a contemporary (Harekṛṣṇa Mukhopādhyāya, *Bhāratavarṣa* 1341, Kārtika); Vrajamohana dāsa Bābājī quotes it in his *Navadvīpa-darpaṇa* with a different text.

## Not to be confused

The famous padakartā **Uddhava dāsa** (Kṛṣṇakānta Majumdār of Ṭeñā Vaidyapura, Murshidabad), disciple of **Rādhāmohana Ṭhākura**, is another person, with his own record: Jagadbandhu Bhadra already separated the two in his *Gaura-pada-taraṅgiṇī*. **Uddhava dāsa**, guru of Keśivallabha and disciple of Narahari Sarakāra, is another again.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

Āveśa of **Candrā** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 112).
