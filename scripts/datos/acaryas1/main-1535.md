## In short

Śyāmānanda Prabhu, first called **Duḥkhī** and then **Kṛṣṇadāsa** (Duḥkhī Kṛṣṇadāsa), was the disciple of **Hṛdaya Caitanya** of Ambikā-Kālnā (himself a disciple of Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita), studied under **Jīva Gosvāmī** in Vṛndāvana with Śrīnivāsa and Narottama, and spread the faith of Gaura-Nityānanda over **Orissa**. Śrī Rādhā Herself gave him a new tilaka with Her anklet and the name **Śyāmānanda**. In Vaiṣṇava books he is called a manifestation (prakāśa) of **Advaita Ācārya**.

## Birth

His father **Kṛṣṇa Maṇḍala**, a sadgopa (milkman by caste), had come from Gauḍa to **Dhārendā-Bāhādurpur** in the Daṇḍeśvara region of Orissa; his mother was **Durikā Devī**. Their other sons and daughters had died, so the child born to them by the mercy of Lord Jagannātha was named **Duḥkhī** ("sorrowful"). He was born on the **pūrṇimā of Caitra**, in **1456 Śaka (1534 CE)** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.351–401; *Abhidhāna*), very handsome, "like Madana", and quick at grammar, poetry and rhetoric. Hearing of Gaura-Nityānanda from the Vaiṣṇavas of the village, he told his father that his guru was Hṛdaya Caitanya at Kālnā, and went to Bengal with pilgrims going to bathe in the Ganges.

## Hṛdaya Caitanya and Jīva Gosvāmī

At twenty, at **Ambikā-Kālnā**, he was overwhelmed by the Deities of Gaura-Nitāi installed by Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita and fell at the temple door. Hṛdaya Caitanya, who had been expecting someone since dawn, named him **Kṛṣṇadāsa** and soon gave him dīkṣā, then sent him to Vṛndāvana to study the Gosvāmīs' books with Jīva Gosvāmī. On the way he saw Īśāna at Navadvīpa, the Viṣṇu-pāda at Gayā, Tapana Miśra and Candraśekhara at Kāśī, Ādi-keśava at Mathurā. Jīva received him gladly; there he met Śrīnivāsa and Narottama, and the three became lifelong friends.

## Rādhā's anklet

Asking for service, he was given the cleaning of the groves (of the rāsa-maṇḍala). He swept them weeping, sometimes putting the dusty broom on his head. One day he found a wonderful anklet; he touched it to his forehead and tied it in his cloth. It was **Śrī Rādhā's left anklet**, lost in the night's dance. **Viśākhā** came looking for it as a village girl, but he would give it only to its owner; then Rādhā came with Viśākhā. Asked what he wanted, he said: "Only the dust of Her feet." Told to bathe in the kuṇḍa, he became a young gopī and was brought to Rādhā, who touched the anklet to her forehead: "This tilaka will stay on your forehead; from today you will be called **Śyāmānanda**." When he came to himself the bright mark was there. (Another version says he saw the anklet shining in the water of the kuñja as he filled his pot for Jīva.) Since then his followers wear a tilaka with the mark of the anklet. Jīva was amazed and told him to keep it secret; "because he gave joy to Śyāmasundara, Jīva named him Śyāmānanda" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 6.52).

The Vaiṣṇavas began to talk of his new name and tilaka, and the news reached Hṛdaya Caitanya, who came to Vṛndāvana furious and rebuked him again and again; Śyāmānanda bore it smiling. That night Rādhā scolded Hṛdaya Caitanya in a dream — "Pleased with Kṛṣṇadāsa I changed his name and mark; who are you to object?" — and next morning he embraced his disciple in tears.

## The books, and Orissa

In **1504 Śaka (1582 CE)** the three friends left with the Gosvāmīs' books; when they were stolen at Vana-viṣṇupura, Śrīnivāsa stayed and Śyāmānanda went to his guru at Ambikā. As the Lord's associates in Orissa had passed away and the saṅkīrtana there had almost stopped, Hṛdaya Caitanya sent him to preach in Orissa. He preached at his birthplace and at Daṇḍeśvara, where many took shelter of him.

**Rasikānanda.** On the Subarṇarekhā lived the zamindar **Acyutadeva**, whose son **Rasika** cared only for Kṛṣṇa and had heard a voice: "Do not worry, you will soon meet your guru Śyāmānanda." When Śyāmānanda came to their house at **Rohiṇī**, Rasika fell at his feet and was initiated; he became his foremost disciple, **Rasikānanda (Rasika Murāri)**. A famous yogī of Rohiṇī, **Dāmodara**, came to see him and became a devotee who wept as he chanted Gaura-Nitāi's names. With them Śyāmānanda went to **Balarāmapura** and **Nṛsiṁhapura**, where many wicked and rich men became his disciples, and to **Gopīvallabhapura**, where with their help he built a temple, kīrtana hall, lake and gardens and installed **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Govinda** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 15.63–65), giving the temple into Rasikānanda's charge. He also brought many Muslims (yavanas) into Vaiṣṇavism.

He went to the **Kheturī** festival with his disciples, and on the way back saw Gadādhara dāsa at Kaṇṭaka-nagara, Śrīnivāsa at Jājigrāma and Raghunandana at Śrīkhaṇḍa. Back at Gopīvallabhapura he heard that Hṛdaya Caitanya had left this world, and fainted; his guru consoled him in a dream.

His intimate disciples were **Rasika Murāri, Rādhānanda, Puruṣottama, Manohara, Cintāmaṇi, Balabhadra, Jagadīśvara, Uddhava, Akrūra, Madhuvana, Govinda, Jagannātha, Gadādhara, Ānandānanda and Rādhāmohana** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 15.63–65).

## Last days

After a festival at Gopīvallabhapura he went to the house of **Uddaṇḍa Rāya Bhuiyān** at **Nṛsiṁhapura**, held a great festival, and left this world on the **first day of the dark fortnight of Āṣāḍha**, in **1552 Śaka (1630 CE)**. His samādhi is at Gopīvallabhapura, where his worship continues; his **puṣpa-samādhi**, and the place where he found Rādhā's anklet, are in Vṛndāvana, across from the temple of his Deities **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Śyāmasundara**.

## Dates

**Appearance:** Caitra pūrṇimā, 1456 Śaka. **Disappearance:** Āṣāḍha kṛṣṇa pratipat, 1552 Śaka (the Gauḍīya calendar keeps it the day after the Jyeṣṭha pūrṇimā).
