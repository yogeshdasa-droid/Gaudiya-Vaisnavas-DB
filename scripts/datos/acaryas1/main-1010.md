## In short

Śrīla Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura, called **Ṭhākura Mahāśaya**, was the son of a king who became a lifelong brahmacārī, the only disciple of **Lokanātha Gosvāmī**, a student of **Jīva Gosvāmī**, and with **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** and **Śyāmānanda** the great preacher of the Gosvāmīs' teachings. He organised the great festival of **Kheturī**, created the **Garāṇhāṭī** style of kīrtana, and wrote the *Prārthanā* and the *Prema-bhakti-candrikā*, "the very life of the devotees". "A lifelong brahmacārī who visited all the holy places, the best of the great devotees" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.256). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Campaka-mañjarī**, an assistant of Rūpa-mañjarī; he is also widely held to be a manifestation of Nityānanda.

## Birth and family

He was born on the **pūrṇimā of Māgha**, about the middle of the fifteenth century Śaka, at **Kheturī**, the capital of **Rājā Kṛṣṇānanda Datta**, a rich kāyastha zamindar of the Gopālapura pargana (Rajshahi, now Bangladesh), on the Padmā about twelve miles north-west of Rampur Boalia, half a krośa from Prematalī. His mother was **Nārāyaṇī Devī**. Kṛṣṇānanda's brother was **Puruṣottama Datta** — the younger according to some, the elder according to the *Bhakti-ratnākara* (1.466–468) — whose son was **Santoṣa Datta**. "Narottama was born on the Māghī pūrṇimā, left home on the Kārtika pūrṇimā, and was initiated by Lokanātha on the Śrāvaṇa pūrṇimā" (*Prema-vilāsa*; *Bhakti-ratnākara*). He was very intelligent, learnt everything on hearing it once, and from childhood thought only of Caitanya and Nityānanda, who appeared to him in dreams.

## The prema left in the Padmā

Long before, dancing in kīrtana at Kānāir Nāṭaśālā, Mahāprabhu had begun to call out "Narottama! Narottama!" He told Nityānanda: "You do not know your own glories; I have saved the love you wept on the way to Nīlācala, and I will keep it in the Padmā for one named Narottama." At **Kutubpur** (Gaḍer-hāṭ) He bathed and sang, and said: "Padmāvatī, keep this prema; give it to Narottama." — "How shall I know him?" — "When he touches you, you will overflow" (*Prema-vilāsa* 8). The place is called **Prematalī**. When Narottama was twelve, Nityānanda told him in a dream to collect it; at dawn he stepped into the Padmā, she overflowed, and gave him the prema. His complexion changed, and his parents could not bring him back to his former self.

## To Vṛndāvana and Lokanātha

While his father and uncle were away at the capital, he tricked his mother and guards and left home on the Kārtika pūrṇimā (others say he left after his father's death, giving the estate to Santoṣa). He walked barefoot, eating once in two or three days, until a boil on his foot laid him under a tree. A fair brāhmaṇa brought him a pot of milk — "Drink this, Narottama; your foot will heal" — and vanished; in his sleep Rūpa and Sanātana put their hands on his chest: "Drink the milk that Śrī Caitanya brought you."

In Vṛndāvana Jīva Gosvāmī sent him to **Lokanātha Gosvāmī**, who had vowed never to take a disciple. Every night, long before Lokanātha woke, the prince cleaned the place his master used, brought him fine earth and water, and held the broom to his chest weeping: "This is my strength." Lokanātha found out, was moved, broke his vow, and initiated him on the Śrāvaṇa pūrṇimā; he was his one and only disciple (*Prema-vilāsa* 11; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.345–346). He studied the Gosvāmīs' books with Jīva, who gave him the title **Ṭhākura** (Śrīnivāsa he called Ācārya, and Duḥkhī Kṛṣṇadāsa Śyāmānanda). One text says that once, cooking for the Lord in his meditation (mānasa-sevā), he burnt his hands, and Lokanātha told him to take shelter of Jīva.

## Back to Bengal

With Śrīnivāsa and Śyāmānanda he left with the Gosvāmīs' books; when they were stolen at Vana-viṣṇupura, Śrīnivāsa sent him on to Kheturī to do Lokanātha's will (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 7.119). Lokanātha had ordered him to establish the worship of Gaurāṅga and Kṛṣṇa, serve the Vaiṣṇavas and preach the holy name. In the *Prārthanā* he sings of his pain at leaving Vraja: "After so much suffering you dragged me to Vraja with the rope of mercy; fate and illusion have loosened it and thrown me back into the well — catch me by the hair and pull me back."

He went first to see the places of the Lord and His associates. At **Navadvīpa** an old brāhmaṇa told him: "Every day Nimāi Paṇḍita sits with His students at this ghāṭa" and showed him the way to Jagannātha Miśra's house, where Śuklāmbara and old **Īśāna** received him. He went on to Śāntipura, to Ambikā (Hṛdaya Caitanya), Saptagrāma, **Khaḍadaha** (Jāhnavā, Vasudhā, Vīrabhadra, Parameśvarī dāsa), **Khānākula** (Abhirāma Ṭhākura), **Purī** — Gopīnātha Ācārya, Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi, Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha and Māmu Gosvāmī, Gopāla Guru at Kāśī Miśra's house, Guṇḍicā, Narendra-sarovara — where he fainted on hearing of the Lord's disappearance; to Śyāmānanda at **Nṛsiṁhapura**, who would not let him go; to **Śrīkhaṇḍa** (Narahari, Raghunandana), Kaṇṭaka-nagara (Gadādhara dāsa) and **Ekacakrā**.

At Kheturī **Santoṣa Datta**, now king, welcomed him and took dīkṣā from him, and built a great temple.

## The festival of Kheturī

On the **Phālguna pūrṇimā**, the Lord's appearance day, six Deities were installed: **Gaurāṅga, Vallabhīkānta, Vrajamohana, Kṛṣṇa, Rādhākānta and Rādhāramaṇa** ("gaurāṅga vallabhī-kānta śrī-kṛṣṇa vraja-mohana / rādhā-ramaṇa he rādhe rādhā-kānta namo 'stu te"). Almost all the living associates came: Jāhnavā Mātā with Parameśvarī dāsa, Kṛṣṇadāsa Sarakhela, Mādhava Ācārya, Mīnaketana Rāmadāsa, Murāri Caitanya, Jñānadāsa, Kamalākara Pippalāi, Vṛndāvana dāsa; Raghunandana from Śrīkhaṇḍa; Śrīpati and Śrīnidhi from Navadvīpa; Acyutānanda, Gopāla and Kṛṣṇa Miśra from Śāntipura; Hṛdaya Caitanya from Ambikā; Śrīnivāsa, Śyāmānanda and many others. Śrīnivāsa performed the abhiṣeka; by order of Jāhnavā and Acyutānanda, Narottama led the kīrtana, and **Mahāprabhu Himself appeared with His associates** "like lightning among the clouds" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 10.571–607). This was the first public celebration of Gaura-pūrṇimā. After it his fame spread everywhere; one of the six Deities is now in Vṛndāvana, in the Gokulānanda temple near Rādhā-ramaṇa (one text).

## Preaching

His āśrama, **Bhajana-ṭuli**, was about two miles from Kheturī. He preached through kīrtana; the Vaiṣṇava faith of **Manipur** is said to have spread by his power, and his songs are sung in every home there.

- At **Gopālapura**, in the house of the brāhmaṇa **Vipradāsa**, a poisonous snake lived in the grain store; when Narottama opened it the snake was gone, and he came out with Deities of **Gaurāṅga and Viṣṇupriyā** on his lap (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 10.202); they are now at Gambhilā.
- A smārta scholar who called him a śūdra got leprosy; Durgā told him in a dream to beg his forgiveness, and he was cured.
- **Harirāma (Harināma) and Rāmakṛṣṇa**, sons of the śākta zamindar **Śivānanda Ācārya** of Goyāsa, were taking goats and a buffalo for sacrifice when they met Narottama and Rāmacandra Kavirāja at the Padmā; they set the animals free and took dīkṣā (Harirāma from Rāmacandra, Rāmakṛṣṇa from Narottama). They defeated the paṇḍita **Murāri** of Mithilā whom their father brought, and Durgā rebuked Śivānanda in a dream.
- **Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī** of Gambhilā and **Jagannātha Ācārya** (sent by Durgā in a dream) became his disciples. The smārtas complained to **Rājā Narasiṁha** that a śūdra was making brāhmaṇas his disciples; the king set out with the digvijayī **Rūpanārāyaṇa**, but at the market of Kumārapura **Rāmacandra Kavirāja**, selling clay pots, and **Gaṅgānārāyaṇa**, selling pān, answered them in Sanskrit and defeated them all. "If you cannot defeat his ordinary disciples, how will you defeat Narottama?" said the king; after a dream of Durgā he and Rūpanārāyaṇa came to beg forgiveness and took dīkṣā.

## Last days

When **Rāmacandra Kavirāja**, his dearest friend, died in Vṛndāvana, and soon after **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, Narottama, drowning in separation, sang "*ye ānila prema-dhana karuṇā pracura, hena prabhu kothā gelā ācārya ṭhākura*" — "Where has he gone, the Ācārya Ṭhākura who brought the wealth of love? Where are my Svarūpa and Rūpa, where Sanātana, Raghunātha dāsa, the two Bhaṭṭas, Kavirāja?" He went to the temple of Mahāprabhu at **Gambhilā** on the Gaṅgā, had kīrtana sung, entered the river, sat knee-deep and asked Rāmakṛṣṇa Ācārya and Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī to pour water over him; as they sang in two kīrtanas his body **merged into the Gaṅgā**. This was on the **kṛṣṇa pañcamī of Kārtika**. (One text adds that once before, at about eighty, he had left his body and come back to life at Mādhavācārya's prayer, and that he left at 105.)

## Works

**Prārthanā** and **Prema-bhakti-candrikā**, endlessly reprinted, with songs for every devotional mood (among them "*śrī-rūpa-mañjarī-pada, sei mora sampada*"). He put the *Smaraṇa-maṅgala* (eleven verses on the eightfold daily pastimes) into Bengali verse. The *Hāṭa-pattana* attributed to him is probably by one Rāmeśvara dāsa (Haridāsa dāsa); the *Siddha-bhakti-candrikā*, *Sādhya-prema-bhakti*, *Camatkāra-candrikā* and others are doubtful. He is the first exponent of the **Garāṇhāṭī** kīrtana. Viśvanātha Cakravartī wrote the *Narottamāṣṭaka* in his praise.

## Dates

**Appearance:** Māgha pūrṇimā. **Disappearance:** Kārtika kṛṣṇa pañcamī (the Gauḍīya calendar and the texts).
