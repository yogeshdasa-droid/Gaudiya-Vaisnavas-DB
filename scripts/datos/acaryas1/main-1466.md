## In short

Śrīnivāsa Ācārya was the great preacher of the books of the Gosvāmīs. "Through Rūpa and Sanātana the Lord had the bhakti-śāstras written; through Śrīnivāsa He had them preached" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.232–234). He was born by the blessing of Lord Caitanya to a brāhmaṇa couple who had come to Him in Purī; he took dīkṣā from **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī**, studied under **Jīva Gosvāmī**, who gave him the title **Ācārya**, and with **Narottama Ṭhākura** and **Śyāmānanda** brought the Gosvāmīs' books from Vṛndāvana to Bengal. He converted the robber-king **Vīra Hāmvīra** of Viṣṇupura and spread the Gauḍīya faith over western Bengal. He is called a manifestation of Mahāprabhu Himself and, by the Lord's devotees in Purī, Gaura-śakti.

## Birth

His father, **Gaṅgādhara Bhaṭṭācārya**, a rāḍhī brāhmaṇa of **Cākundī** (Cākhandī) on the Bhāgīrathī, north of Agradvīpa (Nadia), was at Kaṇṭaka-nagara when Nimāi took sannyāsa. When the barber Madhu, weeping, cut the Lord's hair, Gaṅgādhara fainted, and from then on, half mad, he repeated only "Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya" — so people called him **Caitanya dāsa**. With his wife **Lakṣmīpriyā**, daughter of the scholar and astrologer **Balarāma Vipra** of Jājigrāma, he went to Purī. The Lord sent them with Govinda to see Jagannātha, and told Govinda: "Their son will soon be born. His name will be Śrīnivāsa. Through Rūpa and Sanātana I shall manifest the bhakti-śāstras; through Śrīnivāsa they will be distributed."

He was born on the **pūrṇimā of Vaiśākha**, under the star Rohiṇī — in **1441 Śaka (1519 CE)** according to the *Abhidhāna* (B. V. Tīrtha gives 1530) — with a body like molten gold, long eyes and arms reaching to his knees. His mother taught him to chant the name of Gaurāṅga. He studied grammar, poetry, rhetoric and śāstra with **Dhanañjaya Vidyāvācaspati** (or Vidyānidhi), and as a boy received the mercy of Govinda Ghoṣa and **Narahari Sarakāra**. When his father died he fasted in grief; he and his mother moved to Jājigrāma, to his grandfather's house.

## Purī, Navadvīpa, Khaḍadaha

Longing to see the Lord, he went to Narahari at Śrīkhaṇḍa, and then with the Bengali devotees to Purī. Gadādhara Paṇḍita embraced him; he met Rāmānanda Rāya, Sārvabhauma, Vakreśvara, Paramānanda Purī, Śikhi Māhiti, Govinda, Śaṅkara and Gopīnātha Ācārya, who saw in him the Lord's power and instructed him. On the way back he heard that Gadādhara had left this world, then Advaita and Nityānanda; each consoled him in a dream.

In Navadvīpa Vaṁśīvadana brought him to **Viṣṇupriyā Devī**, thin as the last crescent of the moon, who put aside a grain of rice for every round of the holy name and ate only that; she blessed him. He met Murāri Gupta, Śrīvāsa, Dāmodara Paṇḍita, Śuklāmbara and Dāsa Gadādhara, and Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī at Śāntipura. At **Khaḍadaha** Jāhnavā Mātā put her feet on his head and ordered him to go to Vṛndāvana. At Khānākula **Abhirāma Ṭhākura** struck him three times with his whip Jaya-maṅgala until Mālinī stopped him, and he was flooded with prema.

## Vṛndāvana

He went by Gayā and Kāśī (Candraśekhara, Tapana Miśra) to Mathurā; on the road he heard that Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī and other great devotees had just left this world, and fainted. He reached **Jīva Gosvāmī** in the afternoon of a Vaiśākha pūrṇimā. The next day at the Rādhā-ramaṇa temple **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī** gave him dīkṣā. He spent three days at Rādhā-kuṇḍa with Raghunātha dāsa and Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja. Jīva taught him the Bhāgavatam and the Gosvāmīs' books, and for his mastery gave him the title **Ācārya**.

There he met **Narottama**, his lifelong friend; Jīva sent the two to wander through the forests of Vraja with **Rāghava Paṇḍita** of Govardhana (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5). Then **Duḥkhī Kṛṣṇadāsa (Śyāmānanda)** came, sent by his guru Hṛdaya Caitanya, and the three studied together under Jīva.

## The books and Vīra Hāmvīra

The Gosvāmīs decided to send the three to Bengal with their books. In the bright fortnight of Agrahāyaṇa, after bowing to Govinda, Gopīnātha and Madana-mohana, they left with the books in a chest on an ox-cart. At **Vana-viṣṇupura** the robber-king **Vīra Hāmvīra**, told that the chest held jewels, had it stolen one night while the devotees slept. They were stunned as if by a thunderbolt. Śrīnivāsa sent Narottama to Kheturī and Śyāmānanda to Ambikā, and stayed to find the books.

When the king opened the chest and saw Śrī Rūpa's handwriting on the palm leaves, his heart was purified, and in a dream a golden person told him: "Do not worry; I shall come to you; you are My servant birth after birth." A brāhmaṇa of Viṣṇupura, **Kṛṣṇavallabha**, received Śrīnivāsa and became his disciple, and took him to the king's daily Bhāgavatam reading. Śrīnivāsa spoke so wonderfully that the whole court melted; the king fell at his feet with a straw between his teeth and gave back the books. Jīva and the Gosvāmīs were delighted to hear of it.

## Marriages and disciples

Back at Jājigrāma he heard that Viṣṇupriyā had left this world. Narahari told him it was his mother's wish that he marry; on Akṣaya-tṛtīyā of Vaiśākha he married **Draupadī**, daughter of Gopāla Cakravartī of Jājigrāma, who was named **Īśvarī**; her brothers Śyāmadāsa and Rāmacandra became his disciples. Dāsa Gadādhara blessed him: "You will taste the Lord's own saṅkīrtana."

At Jājigrāma he met **Rāmacandra Kavirāja**, son of Cirañjīva Sena, passing by with his new bride; "Birth after birth you have been my friend," he said, and initiated him. Rāmacandra became his dearest disciple. The sons of Dvija Haridāsa, **Śrīdāsa** and **Gokulānanda**, took dīkṣā from him on their father's disappearance festival at Kāñcanagaḍiyā.

He went to Vṛndāvana a second time, studied the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* with Jīva and heard the opening of the *Gopāla-campū*. Back at Viṣṇupura he gave dīkṣā to **Vīra Hāmvīra**, named Caitanya dāsa, and to his son **Dhāḍī Hāmvīra** (Gopāla dāsa), and installed **Śrī Kālācāṅd**. King Harinārāyaṇa of Śikhareśvara invited him. He grieved for Narahari (Agrahāyaṇa kṛṣṇa ekādaśī) and Dāsa Gadādhara (Kārtika).

## Kheturī and after

At the great festival of **Kheturī** on the Phālguna pūrṇimā, with Jāhnavā Mātā, he performed the abhiṣeka of the six Deities — Gaurāṅga, Vallabhīkānta, Vrajamohana, Kṛṣṇa, Rādhākānta and Rādhāramaṇa — and in the kīrtana the Lord appeared with His associates. Afterwards he, Narottama and Rāmacandra went to Navadvīpa, where the old **Īśāna**, the Lord's servant, showed them the places of His pastimes. He was with **Raghunandana** at Śrīkhaṇḍa when Raghunandana gave his son Kānāi to his Deities and left this world.

**Gaurāṅgapriyā.** **Rāghava (Raghu) Cakravartī** of Gopālapura dreamt that he gave his daughter **Gaurāṅgapriyā** (Padmāvatī) to Śrīnivāsa; Śrīnivāsa agreed, and Vīra Hāmvīra arranged the wedding with great pomp. Jāhnavā Mātā, coming back from Vṛndāvana, took the bride on her lap.

He spent his days at Jājigrāma teaching the Gosvāmīs' books and singing kīrtana until his voice gave out. Śrīnivāsa, Narottama and Rāmacandra were of one heart: "Be merciful, Ācārya Prabhu Śrīnivāsa — Narottama dāsa begs for Rāmacandra's company."

## Family

From Īśvarī: **Vṛndāvana-vallabha**, **Rādhākṛṣṇa**, **Hemalatā**, **Kṛṣṇapriyā**, **Kāñcanalatikā**; from Gaurāṅgapriyā: **Gatigovinda** (the *Abhidhāna*'s table; the texts name three sons — Vṛndāvana-vallabha, Rādhākṛṣṇa, Gatigovinda — and three daughters — Kṛṣṇapriyā, Hemalatā, Phulapi Ṭhākurāṇī). Gatigovinda's sons were Kṛṣṇaprasāda, Sundarānanda and Śrīhari; Kṛṣṇaprasāda's son Jagadānanda had a son Yādavendra by his first wife and Rādhāmohana, Bhuvanamohana, Gauramohana, Śyāmamohana and Madanamohana by his second; Bhuvanamohana's line lives at Māṇikyahāra (Murshidabad).

## His followers

- **The six Cakravartīs:** Śrīdāsa, Gokulānanda, Śyāmadāsa, Vyāsa, Govinda and Nārāyaṇa Cakravartī.
- **The eight Kavirājas:** Rāmacandra, Govinda, Karṇapūra, Nṛsiṁha, Bhagavān, Vallabhīkānta, Gopīramaṇa and Gokula Kavirāja.
- **The six Ṭhākuras:** Rāmakṛṣṇa Caṭṭarāja, Kumudānanda, Rādhāvallabha Maṇḍala, Jayarāma Cakravartī, Śrīrūpa Ghaṭaka and Ṭhākura dāsa.
- **One king:** Vīra Hāmvīra (and his son Dhāḍī Hāmvīra).

He preached through Mallabhūma-Viṣṇupura, Mānbhūm, Siṁhabhūm, Birbhūm and the other "bhūms" between the Subarṇarekhā and the Kaṁsāvatī.

## Works

Only five songs are known (one in the *Karṇānanda*, three in the *Padakalpataru*); a commentary on the catuḥ-ślokī of the Bhāgavatam; *Śrīman-narahari-ṭhākurāṣṭaka*; *Ṣaḍ-gosvāmy-guṇa-leśa-sūcaka*. The **Manoharaśāhī** style of kīrtana is attributed to him. His life is told in the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, *Prema-vilāsa*, *Karṇānanda*, *Anurāga-vallī* and *Narottama-vilāsa*; a modern study is Jīmūtavāhana Rāya, *Śrīnivāsa Ācārya o ṣoḍaśa śatābdīra Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava samāja* (Śāntiniketana, 1984).

## Dates

**Appearance:** Vaiśākha pūrṇimā (1441 Śaka according to the *Abhidhāna*). **Disappearance:** the Gauḍīya calendar keeps it on the **śukla aṣṭamī of Kārtika**.
