## In short

**Advaita dāsa Bābājī Mahāśaya** (born **Bhīmakiśora Rakṣita**) of Vṛndāvana was a scholar of the *Hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇa* and the last great master of the **Garāṇhāṭī** style of kīrtana introduced by Narottama Ṭhākura: "the Garāṇhāṭī kīrtana remained unimpaired as long as Advaita dāsa Bābājī lived." He studied grammar with **Jagadānanda dāsa Paṇḍita** of Rādhākuṇḍa, and he was the grandfather of the scholar **Bimanbihari Majumdar**.

## Early life

He was born in a poor Kāyastha family of the Vārendra clan at **Cāḍiyā**, Sirajganj, in the Pabna district. His parents died when he was a child, leaving him with a widowed sister; with little schooling he learned the work of an amīn (land surveyor) and was employed by **Padmalocana Nāg Mahāśaya**, dewān of the Sānyāl zamindars of Salap and a great devotee, who had no son and brought him up as his own, planted devotion in his heart and had him initiated by a descendant of **Śrī Advaita**. He married. When a descendant of Nityānanda visited, Bhīmakiśora went away with him to **Murshidabad**, where he began to study the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; unable to read words like *kurvan* or *san*, he joined a tol to learn the *Laghu-hari-nāmāmṛta*, and when the paṇḍita told him that without renunciation there is no real knowledge, at twenty-seven, with only a few bighās of land, he took veśa — to the loud grief of his childless wife and widowed sister.

## Study

In search of a better teacher he went to Navadvīpa and to Purī; hearing that **Jagadānanda Paṇḍita** of Rādhākuṇḍa was the best teacher of the *Hari-nāmāmṛta*, he walked to Vṛndāvana by Sambalpur, bearing the hardship in memory of Mahāprabhu and the Gosvāmīs who had walked that way. Disturbed by his teacher's many visitors, he went to Śāntipura and studied with **Rāma Śiromaṇi** of Advaita's line, and at thirty-six returned to Rādhākuṇḍa and completed the *Hari-nāmāmṛta* with Jagadānanda dāsajī.

## The kīrtanīyā

At Rādhākuṇḍa he took his first lessons in kīrtana from the famous **Gopīdāsa**, accompanying him. At the yearly festival of Jāhnavā Mātā, during the parikramā, Gopīdāsa suddenly stopped singing and Advaita dāsa took up the song; **Siddha Gauracaraṇa dāsa Bābājī** of Kuñjara embraced him and announced that Rādhākuṇḍa had one more musician. Then he learned other styles from **Kṛṣṇadayāla Candra** of Pāñcthupī and many famous musicians, and from Vaiṣṇavacaraṇa and his sister, descendants of the old kīrtanīyā Mahānanda Basāk. **Nīlamaṇi Prabhu** of Advaita's family sent him at his own expense to learn from various masters and then learned everything from him. Later famous kīrtanīyās — Navadvīpa Vrajavāsī, Rai Bahadur Dhagendranāth Mitra, Gadādhara dāsa Bābājī, Bhakticaraṇa dāsa Bābājī — learned from his singing. The Mahārājā of Kāśimbāzār engaged him to teach kīrtana; when the students could not follow, Rādhikāprasāda Gosvāmī of Viṣṇupur tried to write his songs down in notation, but found their very slow tempo beyond the rules of notation. Once he sang at Rādhikāprasāda's house from nine in the morning till eleven at night without a break and without a companion.

## Family and last years

At Katwa his wife and widowed sister came to see him, so poor that the great Vaiṣṇavas there — the elder and younger Prabhus of Advaita's family and Veṇīmādhava Ṭhākura — persuaded him to return to his wife; he lived with his family in Navadvīpa, a daughter was born, married into a Vārendra Kāyastha family, and her son was **Bimanbihari Majumdar, M.A.** He gave his property to his daughter and went to Vṛndāvana with his wife, under the shelter of Rājarṣi Bahādur; after her death he lived at the abode called **"Kāṅgālera Mahāprabhu"**. He honoured Padmalocana Nāg, who spent his last years in Vṛndāvana, as his guru and his wife as gurupatnī, and performed her funeral rites. Nearly blind, he taught the *Hari-nāmāmṛta* and studied Vedānta; at eighty-five he went back to Navadvīpa to study Nyāya, but his daughter died and he returned to Kāṅgālera Mahāprabhu. (The record listed Gopīdāsa, Kṛṣṇadayāla Candra and Nīlamaṇi Prabhu as his "disciples"; the first two were his teachers of kīrtana, Nīlamaṇi his pupil.)
