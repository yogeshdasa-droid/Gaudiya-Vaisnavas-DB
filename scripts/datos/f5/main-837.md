## In short

**Lalitā-sakhī dāsī** (born **Gopāla Kṛṣṇa**, 1873–1946), one of the most intimate followers of **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa Deva**, was so absorbed in the bhāva of **Lalitā** that Rādhāramaṇa named him "Lalitā dāsī"; after his guru's passing he never left **Rādhāramaṇa Bāg** in Navadvīpa, and published his guru's life, the *Carita-sudhā*.

## Early life

He was born in **1873** in the Vaidika family of **Kālīkānta Bhaṭṭācārya** and Gaurasundarī Devī at **Hariseṇa** (Gauranadi, Barisal). Six days after his birth the astrologer Rāmacandra Sarvajña foretold that the child would not stay in the family; a band of fourteen sannyāsīs, served by the family with their 250–300 Deities, said the same. From childhood he loved to serve sādhus and Vaiṣṇavas and to ask them about worship and meditation, and soon wished to wear the saffron cloth. He studied at the village school (1877) and with Kārtikacandra Sarvajña, then grammar at Gaila, poetry with Varada Vidyāratna at Kālīghāṭ, Vedānta with Kṛṣṇadāsa Vedāntavāgīśa and at the Caitanya Catuṣpāṭhī in Navadvīpa. After his father's death in 1894 he founded a school and a charity in his village, begging door to door for them. In 1895, living with his elder brother in Calcutta, he refused to marry — he saw all women as his mother — and secretly left home with some sannyāsīs; he met Gaura-mātā of Baranagar, went to Tārakeśvar and Kāśī, and practised yoga at Hardwar under **Hariharānanda Svāmī**.

## Jaygopāla

In **1898** at Navadvīpa he met **Navadvīpa dāsa** and **Govinda dāsa**, the two disciples of Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa, and became Navadvīpa dāsa's friend. When Bābājī Mahāśaya came to Purī by train before the Ratha-yātrā, Navadvīpa dāsa gave Gopāla garlands and tulasī and told him to stay hidden until called; but as the train came in, Bābājī, who had never seen him, called "**Jaygopāla**" as if he knew him and asked for the garland, embraced him and took him on his lap. From then on Gopāla was wholly devoted to his feet. **Caitanya dāsa**, Bābājī's closest companion, who worshipped Kṛṣṇa as a gopī, was pleased with him and at his death gave him all his spiritual power — the only shelter of Jaygopāla's bhajana. He was given the cooking and the care of Bābājī; with Rādhāvinoda, Karuṇā, Śarat and Raghunātha dāsa he would stay absorbed in bhāva for days. One day, in the bhāva of Lalitā, he shut himself in his room weeping, and when Bābājī knocked a voice told him not to disturb Gopāla, who had reached a bhāva Bābājī himself had not; from that day Bābājī called him **Lalitā dāsī**. Like a shy woman he stayed inside the temple and never came before the public.

## Later

After Rādhāramaṇa's death he lived at **Rādhāramaṇa Bāg** and went nowhere else. By his grace Rādhāramaṇa's *Carita-sudhā* was published; he also published the *Śrī Surata-kathāmṛta* and a Bengali translation of the *Saṅgīta-mādhava*, and wrote many padas on Jhūlana, Rāsa and Holi. He passed away in **1946**.
