## In short

Śrī **Gaṅgāmātā Gosvāminī** — **Śacī**, the only daughter of **King Nareśa Nārāyaṇa of Puṭiyā** (Rajshahi) — gave up her kingdom, received the mantra in Vṛndāvana from **Haridāsa Paṇḍita**, of Gadādhara Paṇḍita's line, did severe bhajana at Rādhā-kuṇḍa, and by her guru's order lived in **Purī** in the house of Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya, reviving the worship of his Dāmodara śālagrāma and speaking the *Bhāgavatam*. When the Gaṅgā carried her into Jagannātha's temple on the Vāruṇī day, she received the name **Gaṅgāmātā**; the place is the **Gaṅgāmātā Maṭha** (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*).

## Renunciation and guru

From childhood she thought only of Madana-gopāla and refused to marry "anyone who must die". When her parents died she ruled for a time, then left the kingdom to relatives and went on pilgrimage in search of a guru; from Purī she was inspired to go to Vraja. There Haridāsa Paṇḍita, to test her, told her a princess could not live as a poor devotee; she gave up her ornaments and fine cloth and begged from door to door "like a bee", sleeping on the bank of the Yamunā and cleaning the temple. Pleased, he gave her the **eighteen-syllable Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa mantra** on **Caitra śukla trayodaśī** (one text). With his disciple **Lakṣmīpriyā**, who chanted three lakhs of names a day, she lived by Rādhā-kuṇḍa and went round Govardhana every day. Then he sent her to Purī, to preach the message of Gaurasundara, and forbade her to leave it.

## In Purī

In Sārvabhauma's ruined house she restored the service of his **Dāmodara śālagrāma** and her *Bhāgavatam* classes became famous. King **Mukunda Deva**, told by Jagannātha in a dream, gave her land by the **Śveta-gaṅgā**. On a Mahā-vāruṇī day, kept by her guru's order from going to the Gaṅgā, she was told by Jagannātha in a dream to bathe in the Śveta-gaṅgā: the Gaṅgā rose, carried her into the temple, and she was found inside at night and jailed as a thief. Jagannātha ordered the king to free her and take initiation from her; he and the paṇḍās (paḍichās) took her mantra, and from then she was called Gaṅgāmātā (*Abhidhāna*; the record). She also initiated the smārta brāhmaṇa **Mahīdhara Śarmā** of Dhanañjayapura, who preached in Bengal; and the service of **Rasika Rāya** came to her maṭha.

## Her guru

The *Abhidhāna* says she took dīkṣā from "**Śrī Haridāsa Paṇḍita, an anuśiṣya** (a later disciple in the line) of … Gosvāmī", of Gadādhara Paṇḍita's line. The texts of the record take him as Haridāsa Paṇḍita, the disciple of Ananta Ācārya praised in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 8), and one of them dates her 1601–1721.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Jyaiṣṭha śukla daśamī (Gauḍīya calendar); 1601 (one text).
**Disappearance:** Āśvina śukla ekādaśī, 1721 (one text).
