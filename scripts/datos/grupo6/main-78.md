## In short

**Siddha Balarāma dāsa Bābājī** of **Jhāḍu-maṇḍala** in Vṛndāvana received dīkṣā at forty from **Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī** of Govardhana, quickly mastered Sanskrit and copied the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* in his own hand for his guru. He lived in strict renunciation and was revered by all; every day he read the *Bhāgavatam* to the Vaiṣṇavas of Vraja on the bank of the Yamunā.

## Strictness

When **Jagadānanda dāsa** of Orissa came to him after taking veśa from Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī still wearing his sacred thread, he said: "You changed your dress and kept your thread to show your caste" — and Jagadānanda threw it into the Yamunā. He never looked at women and sometimes went to the temples blindfolded with a stick; to the young Madhusūdana Gosvāmī of Rādhā-ramaṇa, who laughed at it, he said that as long as one is in a body of flesh the desire remains (the record). Prasāda from Prabhupāda Nīlamaṇi's temple once appeared to him stained with blood; Nīlamaṇi found that the festival had been paid by women of ill fame from Calcutta. Once, reading the *Bhāgavatam*, he was disturbed by a woman's uncovered breast; he stopped, declared himself unfit to be called "Siddha Bābā" and wanted to drown himself in the Yamunā, and his devotees held him back.

## In kīrtana

When the notice of a nagara-saṅkīrtana arrived a day ahead, as was the custom, his body would take a dancing posture.
