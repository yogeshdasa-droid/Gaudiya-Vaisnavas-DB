## In short

**Pisimā Gosvāminī** (**Candraśaśī Devī**, of the Mukhopādhyāya family of Ulāgrām, Nadia) served the **Gaura-Nitāi deities** found under a bush in Birbhum around 1770 and called themselves her children — **"Pisimār Gaura-Nitāi"** of Vṛndāvana. She took Viṣṇu-dīkṣā from **Mahānta Balarāma dāsa Bābājī** of Orissa, who served those deities.

## The deities

A cow gave her milk every day on one spot in a bush between Ghoḍāḍāṅgā-Pārulia and Kālipur-Kāḍyā (Birbhum); the milkman dug and found wooden deities of **Gaura and Nityānanda** on an old throne inscribed "Dāsa Murāri Gupta", a **Rādhā-Gopīnātha** and the śālagrāma **Śrīdhara**. They were worshipped at **Siuri**, where Balarāma dāsa Bābājī, an Oriya saint on pilgrimage, was told in a dream to stay and serve them.

## "Mother, do not go"

Candraśaśī came to Siuri on estate business and stayed in the temple guest-house; Gaura-Nitāi called her "mother" in a dream and asked to eat her cooking. She took Viṣṇu-dīkṣā from the Mahānta and cooked for them. When she prepared to leave, the two boys pulled the edge of her sāri so hard that it tore; in the morning she found her sāri torn, and the Mahānta found the missing piece in the deities' hands. After a scandal, the deities told them to go to **Vṛndāvana**; they settled in a new temple at Luibāzār, Banakhaṇḍī. There **Bhakta**, a woman from Nadia whom everyone called "Pisimā" (aunt), became like her sister, and Candraśaśī too was called Pisimā Gosvāminī. She bathed three times a day in the Yamunā; told by the deities, "act like any mother", she no longer had her monthly impurity (the record, after Haridāsa Gosvāmī's *Nitāi-Gaura Vigraha-līlā Kāhinī*).
