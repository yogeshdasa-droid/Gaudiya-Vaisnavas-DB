## In short

**Siddha Rāmakṛṣṇa dāsa Paṇḍita Bābājī** (born **Rāmapratāpa Miśra**, Paṇḍā) of Jaipur, a great scholar and siddha of Vraja, took dīkṣā and veśa from **Siddha Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī** and knew the whole *Govinda-līlāmṛta* by heart.

## Life

He was born in **Saṁvat 1914** (1857) at Bhuratipa Pañcagalī, Jaipur, in a Gauḍa brāhmaṇa family of teachers patronised by the king; his father, a Rāmānandī, died in Saṁvat 1922; his mother's family was of Vallabha's line, his grandfather a Rāmānuja Vaiṣṇava. As a boy he fetched water daily from a distant spring for Kṛṣṇa's worship and passed a tiger eating a man without fear. At nine he finished Pāṇini's grammar. Through **Nṛsiṁhānanda Bhaṭṭa** of Karnataka, a disciple of Nityānanda dāsa, he was drawn to Vṛndāvana; he ran away three times and at thirteen reached it, studying nyāya with Sudarśana Śāstrī of Raṅgajī, the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* with Nīlamaṇi Gosvāmī, the *Bhāgavatam* with Nṛsiṁha dāsa and the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* with Gopīlāl Gosvāmī.

## Bhajana

At twenty, after his studies, he took dīkṣā and veśa from Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī; his guru sent him to Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī for further instruction and then to Varṣāṇā. There he spent his time learning music from **Gauracaraṇa dāsa Bābājī** until his guru called him back. He never spoke to his guru directly, only through his godbrother Śiromaṇi. At Varṣāṇā, unable to fix his mind, he chanted the eighteen-syllable Gopāla mantra for seventeen days and saw Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā under a kadamba at the Uddhava quarry (the record). Manohara dāsa Bābājī went round Vraja with him.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Saṁvat 1914. **Disappearance:** Saṁvat 1997 (1940).
