## In short

**Śrī Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa Deva** («Rājen Bābu», «Baḍa Bābājī»), a great kīrtanīyā and composer of the 19th century, was born in **1853** at **Mahiṣkholā** (Naḍāil, Jessore), son of **Mohanacandra Ghoṣ** and his second wife **Kanakasundarī**. He received his mantra by the **Sarayū** at Ayodhyā from a guru whose name the record does not give, and in Navadvīpa drew many to the kīrtana of Gaura; his disciples included **Govinda dāsa Bābājī** of Purī.

## Life

An excellent student, from boyhood he loved the songs of the Vaiṣṇava beggars. He married at seventeen; his only son died at six; he married three times. Managing the estate of Ghoḍākhālī, he was first initiated by Yogendranātha Bhaṭṭācārya of Mulghar (Khulna), founded schools, dug wells and fed brāhmaṇas, and was made superintendent of the Sātara pargana by the zamindar of Māmudpur. Ordered to seize the crops of rebellious tenants, he wept at their future suffering and left home that day. At the Kālī temple of Bhavānīpur (Bogra), in a solar eclipse, the goddess told him his guru awaited him by the Sarayū; he walked to Ayodhyā, met him and received the mantra and instructions. In Navadvīpa he stayed with **Jagadānanda dāsa Bābājī**, who misheard his name as "Rājen Bābu"; with **Navadvīpa dāsa**, **Kṛṣṇagovinda** and **Rasamohana** he sang through the town; he called all "brother", and those who came as disciples became brothers. He composed songs on the spot in ecstasy (the record).

## Disciples

Govinda dāsa Bābājī (Haridāsa Ṭhākura Maṭha, Purī), Caitanya dāsa, Rādhāvinoda, Kiśorī-gopāla dāsa.
