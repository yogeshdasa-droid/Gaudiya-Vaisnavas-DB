## In short

When **Jāhnavā-devī** came on pilgrimage with her devotees to **Ekacakrā**, the birthplace of her husband, Nityānanda Prabhu, they met an **old brāhmaṇa** who had stayed on alone in the deserted village, hoping that Nityānanda would one day return. He told them the history of the place and of Nityānanda's birth and childhood (Bhakti-ratnākara 11).

## His story of Ekacakrā

The pilgrims offered him obeisances and asked about Ekacakrā. "The glory of this place has come down through the ages. The Pāṇḍavas, in their exile, came to Ekacakrā and killed the demon Baka. Once this was a charming, prosperous village full of people, beautiful with its many temples along the banks of the wide river — the abode of **Ekacakreśvara**, Śiva and Pārvatī. But now even what I saw in my youth is gone."

He told of Nityānanda Prabhu's ancestry, His birth, His childhood and at last His leaving for sannyāsa — and there his voice broke. Jāhnavā-devī burst into tears, and all the devotees wept. After Nityānanda left, he said, the village slowly lost its charm; a rich Yavana who lived there moved to his own land across the river, and gradually all the people followed — even Nityānanda's childhood friends left. Only he, the poor brāhmaṇa, stayed, hoping the Lord would come back. Saying this he cried "O Nitāi!" and wept aloud.
