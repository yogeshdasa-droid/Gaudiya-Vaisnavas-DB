## In short

**Jaṅgalīpriyā dāsī** («Jaṅgalī») was a disciple and attendant of **Sītā-devī**, the wife of Advaita Prabhu (Prema-vilāsa 24), who did sādhana in the forest at **Jaṅgalī-ṭoṭā** (Maldah). The Prema-vilāsa tells of her as a woman; Lokanātha dāsa's *Sītā-caritra* says Jaṅgalī was a man, **Yajñeśvara Cakravartī** («Rājakumāra») of **Hariharapura** near Śāntipura. Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā 89: **Vijayā-sakhī** in Kṛṣṇa-līlā.

## The Bādśāh

In time Jaṅgalī attained great powers. Once, as she worshipped Kṛṣṇa in a dense forest full of tigers and bears, the Bādśāh of Gauḍa came upon her while hunting and, overwhelmed by her beauty, tried to seduce her — and suddenly saw that Jaṅgalīpriyā was not a woman but a man. "Are you a man or a woman?" "Women see me as a woman and men as a man." To test it he sent a woman attendant, who came back saying she was a woman, and then a man, who came back saying Jaṅgalī was a man. Realizing his folly, the Bādśāh went back to beg her pardon; she forgave him and blessed him, and in respect he built her a house there. The place came to be called **Jaṅgalī-ṭoṭā** (Prema-vilāsa 24).

## Yajñeśvara of Hariharapura

According to the *Sītā-caritra* of Lokanātha dāsa, Jaṅgalī was a man: Yajñeśvara Cakravartī, called Rājakumāra, of Hariharapura near Śāntipura, who after taking dīkṣā from Sītā-devī was known as Jaṅgalīpriyā, because he did his sādhana at Jaṅgalī-ṭoṭā in Maldah. His disciple **Nandarāma**, also called **Haripriyā**, wrote the *Kṛṣṇa-miśra-caritra* (see the record #482). (Buchanan-Hamilton's Purnea report, p. 273; Uttara-rāḍhīya kāyastha-kāṇḍa III, 185-187.)
