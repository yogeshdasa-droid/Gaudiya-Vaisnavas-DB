## In short

A **Vaiṣṇava of Vṛndāvana**, whose name is not known, came to see **Rūpa Gosvāmī** just as Rūpa, in meditation, was laughing at a pastime of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. Thinking Rūpa was laughing at him, he went away hurt — and Rūpa lost the vision. **Sanātana Gosvāmī** explained what had happened, and the Vaiṣṇava begged Rūpa's forgiveness in tears (Bhakti-ratnākara 5.3805-3835).

## The mirror

One day Rūpa Gosvāmī was absorbed in the Lord's pastimes in Vṛndāvana. He saw the sakhīs dressing Rādhikā's hair while She was not yet fully dressed; when they finished they brought a mirror for Her to look at Herself. Kṛṣṇa, hidden behind Her, was gazing at Her lotus face, and when Rādhā looked in the mirror She was shocked to see Kṛṣṇa's face there, and hurried to cover Herself — and the sakhīs burst out laughing. Rūpa laughed with them.

## The misunderstanding

Just then the Vṛndāvana-vāsī arrived, eager to meet Rūpa. Finding him laughing aloud, he thought Rūpa was laughing at him, and with a sad heart went to Sanātana Gosvāmī and told him his sorrow (Bhakti-ratnākara 5.3814-3815). The all-knowing Sanātana understood and explained, and the Vaiṣṇava was deeply grieved at having misunderstood Rūpa. Meanwhile, as soon as he had left, Rūpa could no longer see the līlā; thinking that some Vaiṣṇava must have come and gone away disappointed, he went to Sanātana and heard what had happened. The Vaiṣṇava fell at Rūpa's feet, weeping, and begged his forgiveness.
