## In short

Śrī Jagannātha Miśra, called **Purandara** ("Miśra Purandara"), was the **father of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu**. A brāhmaṇa from Śrīhaṭṭa who settled in Navadvīpa, he married **Śacī Devī**, daughter of Nīlāmbara Cakravartī; of their eight daughters none survived, and their two sons were **Viśvarūpa** (later Śaṅkarāraṇya Purī) and **Nimāi**, Śrī Caitanya. In Vraja-līlā he is **Nanda Mahārāja**, and Kaśyapa, Daśaratha, Sutapā and Vasudeva are said to have entered him too (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 37–39). In him, says the *Abhidhāna*, the fatherhood of *sarva-vāsudeva-tattva* — pure goodness, in which the Lord appears — is present.

## Family

According to the *Prema-vilāsa* (24), **Madhu Miśra** had four sons: **Upendra** (whose wife was **Kalāvatī**), Raṅgada, Kīrtida and Kīrtivāsa. Upendra had seven sons: **Kaṁsāri, Paramānanda, Padmanābha, Sarveśvara, Jagannātha Miśra, Janārdana and Trailokyanātha** — so Jagannātha was the fifth. Eight generations down from Paramānanda came Jagajjīvana Miśra, the author of the *Manaḥ-santoṣiṇī* (*Abhidhāna*).

Jagannātha Miśra had eight daughters and two sons: **Viśvarūpa**, who took sannyāsa as Śaṅkarāraṇya Purī, and **Nimāi**, Śrī Caitanya-deva (*Abhidhāna*).

## Life in the Caitanya-bhāgavata

The *Abhidhāna* gathers his pastimes from the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* and the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*:

- In Miśra Purandara the father-mood of the Lord's pure being is united (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 2.136–138); Śacī helped him in his service to Kṛṣṇa (Ādi 2.139).
- The great festival in his house at the Lord's birth (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 3.6–42; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 13.80–118, 14.9–94); the Lord's first rice-feeding (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 4.54–59).
- When the child brought him his books, the sound of ankle-bells was heard in the house (Ādi 5.3–15).
- The visiting brāhmaṇa pilgrim whose offering the child ate (Ādi 5.16–121); the beginning of the Lord's studies and the other ceremonies (Ādi 6.2–3); the complaints of the neighbours about the child's mischief (*olāhana-līlā*, Ādi 6.56–135).
- When **Viśvarūpa** took sannyāsa, Jagannātha Miśra was overwhelmed by the loss of his devotee son (Ādi 7.74–88); then he was anxious about Viśvambhara's studies and discussed them with Śacī (Ādi 7.120–196).
- The Lord's sacred-thread ceremony (Ādi 8.8–23), and Jagannātha Miśra gave his son to **Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita** to study (Ādi 8.28–30).
- In a dream he saw that his son would take sannyāsa, and grieved (Ādi 8.92–108).
- His **disappearance** (*antardhāna*, *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 8.109; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 15.23). After his death, Candraśekhara Ācārya looked after the family.

Like Advaita Ācārya, Śrīvāsa, Murāri Gupta and Nīlāmbara Cakravartī, he came from **Śrīhaṭṭa** (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 2.35). Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita and his brothers were his close friends, and Mālinī Devī helped Śacī.

## Guru and disciples

- The sources do not name his guru. (The record had "Lord Caitanya" as his disciple — He was his son.)

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Nanda Mahārāja**; Kaśyapa, Daśaratha, Sutapā and Vasudeva are said to have entered him too (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 37–39).
