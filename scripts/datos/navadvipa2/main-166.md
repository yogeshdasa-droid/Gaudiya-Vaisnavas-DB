## In short

Śrī Candraśekhara Ācārya — known as **Ācāryaratna** (also Ācāryaśekhara, Candraśekhara Deva) — was the **uncle of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu**, the husband of His mother's sister. A branch of the Caitanya tree, he came from Śrīhaṭṭa to Navadvīpa and lived near Jagannātha Miśra's house; childless, he and his wife loved Nimāi as their own son. In his house the Lord **danced as the Goddess** in His famous drama; and as the Lord's guardian, Candraśekhara performed the rites of His **sannyāsa** at Kāṭoyā. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was one of the nine nidhis (**Śaṅkhanidhi**, *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 102) and has in him the presence of Candra, the moon (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 112).

## Family

"His mother's sister's husband, Candraśekhara" (*Abhidhāna*, entry Ācāryaratna). Śacī Devī's father **Nīlāmbara Cakravartī** had two daughters, **Śacī and Sarvajayā**, and two sons, Yogeśvara Paṇḍita and Ratnagarbha Ācārya (*Abhidhāna*, entry Lokanātha Paṇḍita). With other people of Śrīhaṭṭa, Candraśekhara came to live in Navadvīpa near Jagannātha Miśra's house, praying for the Lord's appearance.

## With the child Nimāi

Candraśekhara and his wife were present in Jagannātha Miśra's house from the birth of Nimāi and helped to bring Him up; sometimes they took Him to their house, and since they had no children of their own, they considered Him their son. After Jagannātha Miśra's death, Candraśekhara took responsibility for the family, and Śacī Mātā consulted him before every important step.

## The Lord's drama

"Ācāryaratna, whose name is Śrī Candraśekhara, in whose house the Lord danced in the mood of the Goddess" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.13). The Lord held kīrtanas in his house as well as in Śrīvāsa's courtyard (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 8.111). One day He wished to perform a play there. At sunset all the devotees gathered; Advaita Ācārya, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita, Haridāsa Ṭhākura and Candraśekhara took their parts; Śacī Mātā came with Viṣṇupriyā. The Lord entered dressed as **Lakṣmī Devī**, and everyone thought the goddess herself had come down from Vaikuṇṭha. "Is this Lakṣmī herself?" asked Śacī. "Don't you recognise your son Nimāi?" said Candraśekhara's wife. "That is my Nimāi? It cannot be!" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 18.28–198). For several days the Lord enjoyed such plays, and the ecstasy of Navadvīpa increased. Candraśekhara also took part in the great torch-lit saṅkīrtana against the Kāzī (Madhya 23.151).

## The sannyāsa

When the time came for the Lord to take sannyāsa, Candraśekhara fell unconscious and soaked the ground with his tears. Nityānanda consoled him: "If you want to see the Lord's further pastimes, hold yourself together; the Lord will stay by your side, bound by your love." That evening he came to the Lord's house and wept, looking at His face. The Lord embraced him. "Will You plunge Nadīyā into darkness by going away?" — "Ācāryaratna, be patient. I am eternally bound by your love; how lovingly you raised Me as a child — can I ever repay that debt?" They held each other and wept. "My pastimes are for the deliverance of all; even as a sannyāsī I will remain bound in the temple of your heart. You must perform the ceremonies of My sannyāsa."

So Candraśekhara went quietly to the āśrama of **Keśava Bhāratī** in **Kāṭoyā** and, as the Lord's guardian, with his heart breaking, performed the required rites (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 28.12, 28.104–134). Then the Lord sent him back; he told the news in Navadvīpa and Śāntipura (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 3.20, 117), the devotees cried out in grief, and he consoled them and told them of the Lord's future pastimes (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 1.33).

## In Śāntipura and Purī

When Nityānanda brought the Lord to Śāntipura after three days of wandering in Rāḍha, Candraśekhara came there with Śacī Mātā and the devotees of Navadvīpa. Some months later they went to Nīlācala in the rainy season. At Āṭhāranālā the Lord came to meet them and gave garlands of Jagannātha to Advaita Ācārya, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita and Candraśekhara. In Purī the devotees invited the Lord in turn — first Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī, then Mālinī Devī, then Candraśekhara's wife, whom the Lord considered non-different from His mother. She cooked for Him herself and also fed Him what Śacī Mātā had sent. "Māsimā, give My obeisances to My mother and tell her I come to her every day. She thinks she is dreaming, but I really come and eat." He met Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.82), took part in the Lord's pastimes in Purī and in the water-sports in Narendra-sarovara (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 8.125).

His home in Navadvīpa was at **Vraja-pattana**, where Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura later established the main centre of the **Śrī Caitanya Maṭha**.

## Guru and disciples

- A branch of the Caitanya tree (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.13; *Abhidhāna*). (The record had Keśava Bhāratī as his guru: Keśava Bhāratī gave sannyāsa to the Lord, and Candraśekhara performed the rites at his āśrama.)

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Śaṅkhanidhi**, one of the nine nidhis (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 102); the presence of **Candra** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 112).
