## In short

Śrī Śacī Devī — "Śacī Mātā", also called **Āi** — the **mother of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu**, was the daughter of **Nīlāmbara Cakravartī** of Belpukhuriyā in Navadvīpa and the wife of **Jagannātha Miśra**. She lost eight daughters, saw her elder son Viśvarūpa take sannyāsa, lost her husband and her daughter-in-law Lakṣmīpriyā, and finally saw Nimāi take sannyāsa — yet she never wavered from the spiritual path and never stood in the way of her sons. At her order the Lord made Nīlācala His residence. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā she is **Yaśodā**, and Aditi, Kauśalyā, Pṛśni and Devakī are said to have entered her too (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 37–39; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 17.285).

## Family

She was the daughter of **Nīlāmbara Cakravartī**, an astrologer from Śrīhaṭṭa who settled at Belpukhuriyā. The *Abhidhāna* names two sons, Yogeśvara Paṇḍita and Ratnagarbha Ācārya, and two daughters, Śacī and Sarvajayā; the *Prema-vilāsa* (7) says Nīlāmbara had one daughter and two sons. According to the *Vaiṣṇavācāra-darpaṇa* (1.343), Viśveśvara Cakravartī was her brother.

After her marriage she had **eight daughters, who all died**, and then **Viśvarūpa**. In his early youth Viśvarūpa took sannyāsa and left home, and shortly afterwards Jagannātha Miśra passed away; then Gaurāṅga was her only solace. Her two daughters-in-law, **Lakṣmīpriyā** and **Viṣṇupriyā**, were embodiments of Lakṣmī.

## Mother of Nimāi

She was the embodiment of patience and was never disturbed by her son's mischief. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* tells of her conceiving Viśvarūpa (Ādi 2.195–226) and Gaurāṅga (Ādi 4.3–85); the rituals at Nimāi's birth (Ādi 5.5–32); the sound of ankle-bells and the divine footprints in the house (Ādi 5.52, 6.41); the child eating the rice offered by a visiting brāhmaṇa (Ādi 6.72–134); the *olāhana-līlā*, when the neighbours complained to her; Nimāi sent to Advaita's house to call His elder brother (Ādi 7.74–114); her wailing when Viśvarūpa took sannyāsa (Ādi 7.151–192); Nimāi sitting on the discarded cooking-pots on the rubbish heap (Ādi 8.8–24); His sacred thread (Ādi 8.109–119); her grief at Jagannātha Miśra's death (Ādi 8.127–182); Nimāi's annoyance at her delay in gathering items for the Gaṅgā-pūjā (Ādi 10.47–128); the preparations for His marriage (Ādi 12.214–255); the sound of the flute she heard, when the Lord showed her His opulence (Ādi 14.106–188); her grief at the death of Lakṣmīpriyā (Ādi 15.38, 17.406); and Nimāi's marriage with Viṣṇupriyā (Madhya 2.88, 3.103). In the *Caitanya-maṅgala* Nimāi strikes her and gives her a coconut (Ādi 2.227–242), she stops Him playing with a puppy (Ādi 2.283–317), and after Lakṣmīpriyā's death He consoles her by telling of Lakṣmīpriyā's former birth (Ādi 5.143–157); He tells her of His dream of Kṛṣṇa (Madhya 5.5–13). The *Advaita-prakāśa* (10) tells how Advaita offered flowers and sang the glories of her pregnancy, and circumambulated her.

She observed **Ekādaśī** at her son's instruction and gave up eating grains that day (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 15.10, 29–30).

## The Lord's ecstasy and her offence

When the Lord came back from Gayā showing ecstatic love, Śacī thought her Nimāi had a mental disorder and arranged for treatment (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 2, 8.68–122). She worshipped Viṣṇu and the Gaṅgā for His protection and placed Viṣṇupriyā near Him. Gaurāṅga and Nityānanda revealed their divinity to her (Madhya 10.91, 11.67, 18.161–201). Once the Lord used His own mother as an example to show how serious an **offence against a Vaiṣṇava** is, and she was freed from her offence at the feet of Advaita Ācārya (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 22.10–483).

## The sannyāsa and after

Śacī's state of mind when the Lord took sannyāsa is described in *Caitanya-bhāgavata* Madhya 27.18–51 and 28.60–65. She did not stop Him. When He came to **Śāntipura** after His sannyāsa, she went to meet Him there, stayed some time and cooked for Him (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 1; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 3). It was at **her order** that the Lord settled at **Nīlācala**. She met Him again at Advaita's house in Śāntipura when He was going to Rāmakeli (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 16.210). From Nīlācala the Lord regularly sent her Jagannātha prasāda, the Deity's cloth and His humble obeisances, through Jagadānanda and others (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 19.5–15). Nityānanda visited her when He came to Navadvīpa (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.421, 9.170, 219), and the Lord appeared to her (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2.34, 79). Her household servant was **Īśāna Ṭhākura**.

## Her greatness

Haridāsa Dāsa writes in the *Abhidhāna*: "One is amazed to see the humility of Śacī Devī — the highest ideal of a mother and a wife — who bore Viśvarūpa and Viśvambhara, both of whom took sannyāsa for the good of the world, whose husband was the embodiment of pure goodness, and whose two daughters-in-law were Lakṣmī herself. Her whole household was devoted to the service of Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas; her household was truly Kṛṣṇa's. She never hesitated to hear and follow spiritual instruction even from her son — her Ekādaśī vow and her atonement at Advaita's feet are the proof. Through hundreds of obstacles — the death of eight daughters, Viśvarūpa's sannyāsa, her husband's death, the passing of her beloved daughter-in-law Lakṣmīpriyā, Nimāi's sannyāsa, poverty and helplessness, the care of her young daughter-in-law Viṣṇupriyā — she never moved from the spiritual path even a little. She never opposed her son's sannyāsa, never expected anything material from Him, and received from Him spiritual nourishment."

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Yaśodā**; Aditi, Kauśalyā, Pṛśni and Devakī are said to have entered her too (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 37–39).
