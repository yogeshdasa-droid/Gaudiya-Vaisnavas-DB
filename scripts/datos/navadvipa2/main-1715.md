## In short

Śrīmatī Viṣṇupriyā Devī, the **second wife of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** (after Lakṣmīpriyā), was the daughter of **Sanātana Miśra**, the Rāja-paṇḍita of Navadvīpa. She is the manifestation of the Lord's **Bhū-śakti** and was **Satyabhāmā** in Kṛṣṇa-līlā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 47–48). After the Lord's sannyāsa she lived in Navadvīpa with Śacī Mātā in severe austerity: for every round of the holy name she chanted she set aside one grain of rice, and at the end of the day cooked those grains, offered them to the Lord and ate a little as prasāda. She was the first to install and worship the **Deity of Mahāprabhu**, worshipped today in Navadvīpa as **Dhāmeśvara**.

## Dates

- **Appearance:** Māgha Śukla Pañcamī (Vasanta-pañcamī) (B. V. Tīrtha).
- **Disappearance:** not given in the sources.

## Family

Her father, **Sanātana Miśra**, was a pious and charitable brāhmaṇa of Navadvīpa, a devotee of Viṣṇu, famous as a court scholar (**Rāja-paṇḍita**), who maintained many people; in Dvāpara-yuga he was **King Satrājit**. One genealogy runs: **Durgādāsa Miśra** → his sons **Sanātana Miśra** and **Kālidāsa Miśra**; Sanātana's daughter Viṣṇupriyā; Kālidāsa's son Mādhava Miśra, whose son was **Yādava Miśra**. According to another view, Viṣṇupriyā was the daughter of Durgādāsa Miśra, his son was Yādava, and Yādava's son Mādhava. The *Prema-vilāsa* says that **Yādavācārya** took dīkṣā from Viṣṇupriyā and served the Deity of Lord Gaurāṅga; his descendants are called "of the Viṣṇupriyā family".

## Childhood

From early childhood she bathed in the Gaṅgā two or three times a day, followed her elders in worship and vows, served the Deity and Tulasī, and was devoted to her parents. When she met Śacī Mātā at the bathing place she humbly offered her respects, and Śacī blessed her — "*yogya pati hauk*", "may you get a worthy husband" — while thinking: "This girl should marry my son" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 15.46–48).

## The marriage

After the death of Lakṣmīpriyā, Śacī wished her son to marry again, and He did not oppose her. She sent for **Kāśīnātha Paṇḍita**, who went to Sanātana Miśra: "Offer your daughter Viṣṇupriyā to Viśvambhara Paṇḍita — they are like Kṛṣṇa and Rukmiṇī." The Rāja-paṇḍita, his wife and relatives agreed with joy (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 15.49–214).

The Lord's students were jubilant. The rich **Buddhimanta Khān** offered to bear the whole cost — "this will not be a poor brāhmaṇa's wedding; everyone will think a prince is getting married" — and **Mukunda Sañjaya** protested that he wanted to do something too. At the *adhivāsa* the house was decorated with water-pots, lamps, mango leaves and rice-paste designs; the Lord received the guests with garlands, sandal and betel, and told His people to give to everyone three times, so that the greedy who came back again and again were satisfied and ashamed; the flowers, sandal and betel that fell on the ground would have sufficed for five weddings. Sanātana Miśra came with gifts and placed tilaka on the Lord's forehead.

The next day, after bathing, worshipping Viṣṇu and honouring the forefathers, the Lord was dressed as a bridegroom — sandal and perfume, a half-moon tilaka, a crown, garlands, a golden-yellow silk dhoti, kajal, golden earrings. In a palanquin brought by Buddhimanta Khān, with musicians, dancers, jesters, lamps and banners, the procession went first to the Gaṅgā under the nearly full moon, then through Navadvīpa — the brāhmaṇas with unmarried daughters lamented — and at the hour of *go-dhūli* reached Sanātana Miśra's house. The bride's mother worshipped the Lord with seven ghee lamps; Viṣṇupriyā, beautifully dressed, circumambulated Him seven times, bowed with folded hands and placed a garland at His feet, offering Him her life; smiling, He placed it round her neck. The two parties competed in throwing flowers, and unseen, Brahmā and the demigods showered flowers too. Sanātana Miśra gave his daughter with cows, land, furniture, servants and other gifts — fortunate like Nagnajit, Janaka, Bhīṣmaka and Jāmbavān — and the fire sacrifice was performed. The couple spent the night in the bride's house, and the next afternoon went in a palanquin to Śacī Mātā's house, while the people said: "They are Lakṣmī and Hari, Rāma and Sītā, Śiva and Gaurī!" The Lord gave gifts to all and embraced Buddhimanta Khān (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 15). The *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* describes the marriage only briefly (Ādi 15).

## In Navadvīpa with the Lord

After the marriage the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* mentions her only occasionally. When the Lord returned from Gayā completely changed, Śacī thought He was ill, worshipped Viṣṇu and the Gaṅgā for His protection and seated Viṣṇupriyā before Him; He looked at her without seeing her and only cried "Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!" Once, hearing of the oppression of some atheists, He roared "I will destroy them!" and, not recognising Viṣṇupriyā, began to strike her, then came to Himself and was ashamed (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 2.87).

One day she overheard Śacī tell the Lord a dream in which He and Nityānanda were playing with their Deities Rāma and Kṛṣṇa and fighting over the food. "Rāma and Kṛṣṇa really live in our house," said the Lord; "I used to suspect your daughter-in-law of eating Their prasāda, but now I don't" — and Viṣṇupriyā, listening, laughed. Sometimes she offered Him betel, and in her happiness at His happiness she forgot whether it was day or night; whenever she sat by Him, Śacī was content (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 11.65) — the meditation of the worshippers of Śrī Śrī Gaura-Viṣṇupriyā. With Śacī she watched the Lord dance as Rukmiṇī (Lakṣmī) in the drama at Candraśekhara's house (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 18.29).

## The night of the sannyāsa

Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura does not mention her in the account of the sannyāsa, but **Locana dāsa** in the *Caitanya-maṅgala* (2.12.1–40) tells of the Lord's words to her that night: "Everything in this world is false; the only reality is Bhagavān and His devotees. Sons, husbands and friends — in the end no one belongs to anyone. Kṛṣṇa is the husband of all. Your name is Viṣṇupriyā — if you worship Viṣṇu, your name will have its meaning. Do not grieve; fix your mind on Kṛṣṇa's feet." "You are the Supreme Controller — withdraw Your illusion, then Viṣṇupriyā can be happy." Her sorrow left her and she saw His four-armed form; yet she could not stop thinking of Him as her husband: "My only worth is that I am Your servant — why must I become nothing again?" "Hear Me, Devī Viṣṇupriyā: whenever and wherever you think of Me, I will at once be with you." She thought: "You are the independent Lord; do what makes You happy."

In the middle of the night, while she slept, the Lord bowed at His mother's door, showed her His opulence, swam across the Gaṅgā and went towards Kāṭoyā. **Vāsu Ghoṣa** describes the dawn: Viṣṇupriyā put out her hand to touch Him and found only emptiness — "as if a thunderbolt fell on her head". Without binding her hair she ran crying to Śacī's room, rolling on the ground, her breath failing, her mouth dry.

## A life of separation

After the Lord's sannyāsa she never left the house except to bathe in the Gaṅgā with Śacī Devī. The devotees who came to take prasāda saw only her feet, never her face, and never heard her voice; tears constantly streamed down her face. She ate only Śacī's remnants and always chanted the holy name. The Lord heard news of her from **Jagadānanda** (*Advaita-prakāśa* 21).

"In separation from the Lord sleep left her eyes; if ever she slept, it was on the bare floor. Her golden complexion grew pale; she became as thin as the moon on the fourteenth day of the dark fortnight. Counting her rounds of the holy name with grains of rice, she cooked those grains, offered them to the Lord and took them. No one knew how she kept her life" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 4.48–52). The *Prema-vilāsa* (5) describes her *nāma-bhajana*: "She would place two new earthen pots beside her, one empty and one full of rice. For each round — the sixteen names, thirty-two syllables — she put one grain into the empty pot, and so she chanted until the third quarter of the day. Those grains alone she cooked and offered to the Lord with tears. Day and night she chanted the holy name… Through the name she planted the Lord's power." The *Abhidhāna* says that though she was the beloved of Śrī Gaurasundara, she never encouraged the doctrine of enjoyment (*sambhoga-vāda*); every verse of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* lived in her as an ideal.

## The Deity of Mahāprabhu

**Murāri Gupta** writes in his Kaḍacā that Viṣṇupriyā Ṭhākurāṇī was the first to install a Deity of Mahāprabhu and begin His worship: the Lord's own expansion was present before her in that form — "Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present in this form" — and she worshipped Him. This Deity is worshipped today in Navadvīpa as **Dhāmeśvara**. She also served a picture of the Lord with great love.

## Her household

After the Lord's departure **Īśāna Ṭhākura**, the servant of Śacī Mātā's house, looked after Śacī and Viṣṇupriyā; **Vaṁśīvadana Ṭhākura**, very dear to her, also stayed with them. In a dream the Lord told her to show mercy to **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 4.25–36); when Śrīnivāsa came to Māyāpura he met the aged Īśāna and Viṣṇupriyā, and with motherly affection she placed her feet on his head (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 4.44–46).

## Guru and disciples

- **Disciple:** **Yādavācārya** (Yādava Miśra), who took dīkṣā from her and served the Deity of Gaurāṅga (*Prema-vilāsa*).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

The Lord's **Bhū-śakti** (of His three energies Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā); **Satyabhāmā**, daughter of Satrājit (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 47–48).
