## In short

**Rādhāvallabha Gosvāmī**, a descendant of **Śrī Madhu Paṇḍita**, was shamed into seeking Kṛṣṇa by a disciple's wife who asked him to speak about Him. Giving up family life — though he was the only male heir — he went to Vṛndāvana and lived at the **Gopīnātha temple** as its accountant, reading the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* to the people and spending every paisa on the service of poor and sick Vaiṣṇavas.

## The question

He lost his parents early and could not finish his studies. Once his elders sent him to a disciple of the family to ask for money. The disciple was out, but he was invited in for lunch, and afterwards the disciple's wife asked him to speak about Kṛṣṇa. He knew nothing about Kṛṣṇa — only that it was his right to visit disciples' houses and take food and money — and thought that had he known he would be asked to speak of Kṛṣṇa, he would never have come. He left, vowing never to visit his disciples again until he understood the topics of Kṛṣṇa.

## At the Gopīnātha temple

Instead of marrying to carry on the family line, he left home, went to Vṛndāvana and took shelter at the temple of **Gopīnātha**, where he kept the accounts for two meals a day and a rupee a month. Every morning after his ablutions he read the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* or other Vaiṣṇava books in Bengali, and many people delighted to listen. He never bought clothes or anything for himself, using only what devotees gave him; he saved nothing of what he earned from the temple or his reading, spending it at once on Vaiṣṇava service — above all for the very poor and the sick. He never let anyone take the dust of his feet or eat his remnants. His favourite subject was complete detachment from money and everything worldly, even food. When he was about to leave this world and found he still had two rupees ten ānās, he spent them at once on Vaiṣṇava service.
