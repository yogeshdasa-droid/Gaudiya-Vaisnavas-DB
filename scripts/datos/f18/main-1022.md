## In short

**Navakiśora Gosvāmīpāda**, of the family of **Lakṣmīkānta Gosvāmī Prabhu** — a scion of a disciple of Śrī Nityānanda — was the disciple of **Kṛṣṇakiśora**, Lakṣmīkānta's eldest son, and the guru and inseparable friend of **Locanānanda Gosvāmīpāda** (#1846). Posing as low-caste Hāḍis, the two served **Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābā of Govardhana**, who taught them bhajana; they died together at Purī during the Ratha-yātrā, and their samādhi is by **Narendra-sarovara**.

## Guru and disciple, two friends

Lakṣmīkānta Gosvāmī had nine children, all talented; his eldest son Kṛṣṇakiśora was his disciple, Navakiśora was Kṛṣṇakiśora's, and Locanānanda was Navakiśora's. Though guru and disciple, Navakiśora and Locanānanda were the best of friends, «like twins of one body and one mind». Both were great scholars, versed in the six systems of Indian philosophy, deeply renounced and lovers of bhajana.

## With Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābā

As bachelors they left home for Vṛndāvana to do bhajana. Fearing that they would soon be famous if their identity were known, they said they were low-born men of the **Hāḍi** caste, and took shelter of **Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābā of Govardhana**. Charmed by their spirituality, looks, learning, devotion and renunciation, and believing their story — "even low-born men may become great" — the Siddha Bābā began to teach them bhajana. He was very old by then and let them serve him; they did so with great affection, cleaning his refuse, washing his vessels and keeping everything clean.

## Purī

Having received the Siddha Bābā's mercy, they went back to their home in East Bengal, installed Deities and preached. At last they went to Śrī Kṣetra for the Ratha-yātrā. As they watched Śrī Jagannātha's **Pāṇḍu-vijaya**, the elder, Navakiśora, left his body. Locanānanda sat at his feet: "My Lord, from my birth I have been your lifelong companion and disciple — will you leave me behind now?" — and followed him at once. The astonished people of Purī gave them samādhi on the bank of Narendra-sarovara, and their Deity is still worshipped there by the Nātha brāhmaṇas.
