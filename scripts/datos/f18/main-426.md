## In short

**Guṇānanda Guhā** (Majumdār), father of the well-known **King Basanta Rāya** of Jessore, settled in **Vṛndāvana** between 1563 and 1572 and, about 1570, with funds from his son, built the temple of **Madana-mohana** south of the temple of Kṛṣṇadāsa (some say Rāmadāsa) Kapūra. There Madana-gopāla, once two Rādhās sent by Puruṣottama Jānā had been installed beside Him, came to be called Madana-mohana.

## The family

The temple's stone inscription, read by Growse, says that **Rāmacandra**, of the Guhā family, came from East Bengal and served the royal government first at Saptagrāma and then at Gauḍa. His three sons — **Bhavānanda**, **Guṇānanda** and **Śivānanda** — held important posts in the government. Bhavānanda's son **King Vikramāditya** and Guṇānanda's son **King Basanta Rāya** founded the royal estate of Jessore; the famous **Mahārāja Pratāpāditya**, ruler of Bengal, was Vikramāditya's son, so Basanta Rāya was his paternal uncle. Guṇānanda settled in Vṛndāvana during the reign of Suleiman Karrani and lived there until his death.

## The temple of Madana-mohana

The Deity **Madana-gopāla** was worshipped in this temple long before the temple of Kṛṣṇadāsa fell into decay. **Puruṣottama Jānā**, son of Mahārāja Pratāparudra, had two Deities of Rādhā made and sent to Vṛndāvana; as ordered in a dream, the smaller was installed as Rādhā on Madana-gopāla's left and the larger as Lalitā on His right, and from then on the Deity was called **Madana-mohana**. Later, because of Aurangzeb's tyranny, Madana-mohana and other Deities were taken to Jaipur, and from there **Gopāla Siṁha**, King of Karauli and brother-in-law of the King of Jaipur, installed Madana-mohana at Karauli. In Guṇānanda's original temple Lord Caitanya and Nityānanda Prabhu are now worshipped.
