## In short

Śrī **Gadādhara dāsa Ṭhākura** (Dāsa Gadādhara) was an intimate associate of both **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** and **Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu**. His Śrīpāṭa is at **Eṛiyādaha** (Ariadaha), on the bank of the Bhāgīrathī four krośas north of Calcutta (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Candrakānti**, an expansion (vibhūti) of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, joined with **Pūrṇānandā**, the dear friend of Balarāma (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 154-155): as Gadādhara Paṇḍita is Rādhārāṇī Herself, Gadādhara dāsa is the luster (dyuti) of Her body. Though he was with Nityānanda, whose followers were mostly cowherd boys in the mood of friendship, he was always absorbed in the mood of a gopī (mādhurya-rasa).

## With the Lord and with Nityānanda

He lived first at Navadvīpa, then with Mahāprabhu at Purī. When the Lord sent Nityānanda Prabhu to preach the holy name in Bengal, He sent **Rāmadāsa** and **Gadādhara dāsa** with Him (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.13-14); Śrīla Prabhupāda calls him one of Nityānanda's chief assistants in that preaching. On the way back from Purī he forgot himself and called out loudly like a girl of Vraja selling yogurt; once, carrying a pot of Ganges water on his head, he cried, "Who will buy milk? Who will buy yogurt?", and laughed at the joke. When Mahāprabhu stayed in the house of Rāghava Paṇḍita on His way to Vṛndāvana, Gadādhara dāsa went to see Him, and the Lord, very pleased, put His foot on his head.

## The Dāna-līlā at Eṛiyādaha

In his temple at Eṛiyādaha he served a beautiful Deity of **Bāla-Gopāla**. One day Nityānanda Prabhu took Gopāla from the altar, held Him to His chest and danced, roaring, while **Mādhava Ghoṣa** sang the *Dāna-khaṇḍa* (Kṛṣṇa's dāna-līlā); the ordinary people of the village tasted that day the love sought by yogīs and munis. Vṛndāvana dāsa writes: "Nityānanda Prabhu was personally present within the body of Śrī Gadādhara dāsa; such are his glories" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*). His temple, the place of the Dāna-līlā, the "Gadādhara-aṅgana" and his samādhi-vedī are still at Eṛiyādaha (Abhidhāna).

## The Kāzi

Among the Lord's associates no one would go near the Muslim Kāzi, for fear of losing caste. One night Gadādhara dāsa, mad with kṛṣṇa-prema and singing the holy name, came to the Kāzi's house at Eṛiyādaha and called him loudly. The Kāzi stormed out in anger, but on seeing him he was stunned and asked, "Ṭhākura, why have you come?" — "Gaura and Nityānanda have appeared to give the nectar of the holy name to everyone, even the most fallen. Why don't you chant it?" — "I shall chant Hari tomorrow." Hearing the two syllables "Ha-ri" from his mouth, Gadādhara dāsa began to dance: "Why tomorrow? You have just said 'Hari'! All your sins have gone; simply continue." The Kāzi took shelter at his feet and became a devotee of Hari; in this way he delivered many sinners and yavanas.

## Navadvīpa and Katwa

After the Lord's disappearance he stayed at Navadvīpa and looked after **Śacī Mātā** and **Viṣṇupriyā Devī**; when they too had passed away he went to **Katwa** and there, at the place where Mahāprabhu took sannyāsa from Keśava Bhāratī, installed a Deity of **Lord Gaurāṅga** (Abhidhāna; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 9). The Deity was one of three carved from one nimba tree, as the Lord ordered in dreams (to Narahari Sarakāra and the carver, says one text; another says to Daityāri and Kaṁsāri Ghoṣa of Kulāigrāma); the other two went to Śrīkhaṇḍa and Gaṅgānagara. A Deity of Nityānanda (Balarāma), once carried about by Mīnaketana Rāmadāsa, came later, so the Deities are known as **Śrī Śrī Nitāi-Gaurāṅga**. He was present at the **Daṇḍa-mahotsava** of Pāṇihāṭi, and was older than Śrīnivāsa Ācārya.

His disciples were the brāhmaṇa **Yadunandana Cakravartī** of Katwa, whose descendants still serve the Deity there (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 9.352), and the renunciate **Vidyānanda Paṇḍita**, who by his mercy served the great Deity of Gaurāṅga at Kaṇṭaka-nagara (Katwa) (*Narahari-sakhā-nirṇaya*).

## His departure

"What shall I say? On the kṛṣṇa aṣṭamī of Kārttika my master disappeared here" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 9.362, Yadunandana speaking). **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** presided over his festival, attended by mahāntas from many places; the Vaiṣṇavas remember it like the festival of Kheturī. His samādhi is at Mahāprabhu's place in Katwa, near the samādhi of Keśava Bhāratī, together with the samādhi of the Lord's hair cut by the barber Madhu Śīla; the memorial samādhi is at Eṛiyādaha.

Two texts of the record give the **śukla** aṣṭamī of Kārttika; the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, the Abhidhāna and the third text give the **kṛṣṇa** aṣṭamī (the Gauḍīya calendar keeps both days).

## Later history of Eṛiyādaha

The tomb at Eṛiyādaha was kept by the Saṁyogī Vaiṣṇavas and later by Siddha Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī of Kālnā; by his order **Madhusūdana Mullik** of the Nārikeladāṅgā Mullik family of Calcutta founded a pāṭa-bāḍī there in 1256 BS and arranged the worship of **Śrī Rādhākānta**; his son **Balāicānd Mullik** installed Gaura-Nitāi there in 1312 BS and restored the holy places. B. B. Majumdar found the Bāla-Gopāla neglected when he visited. The Kālnā edition of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* calls him a Kāyastha; his descendants at Eṛiyādaha call themselves brāhmaṇas.
