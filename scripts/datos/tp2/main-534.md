## In short

Śrī **Jagadīśa Paṇḍita** of **Yaśoḍā** (near Cākdaha, Nadia) is the fifteenth branch of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu: "Jagadīśa Paṇḍita, the deliverer of the fallen: love of Kṛṣṇa showered from him like torrents of rain" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.30). A Rāḍhīya brāhmaṇa (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna), in Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Candrahāsa**, the famous dancer — "and as Candrahāsa took pleasure in dancing before Kṛṣṇa, Jagadīśa enjoyed dancing in the saṅkīrtana of Śrī Caitanya" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 153).

**Two Jagadīśas.** The Abhidhāna, like the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, gives two persons: this Jagadīśa of Nityānanda's branch, brother of **Maheśa Paṇḍita**, and another **Jagadīśa Paṇḍita of Navadvīpa**, of Mahāprabhu's branch (Ādi 10.70-71), brother of **Hiraṇya Paṇḍita**, in whose house the child Gaura ate the Ekādaśī offering, a yajña-patnī in Kṛṣṇa-līlā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 192) — now in a record of his own. The texts of this record (Bhakti Ballabh Tīrtha Mahārāja, *Śrī Caitanya: His Associates*, and others) take them as one; what they tell of the Ekādaśī is kept below as they tell it.

## Family

He was born in the region of Gauhāṭī (Prāgjyotiṣa); his father was **Śrī Kamalākṣa Bhaṭṭa**, son of **Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa** of Goyghar Bandyaghāṭa, and both his parents were great devotees of Viṣṇu. After they passed away he came with his wife, **Duḥkhinī Devī**, and his younger brother **Maheśa** to live on the bank of the Ganges, near the house of Jagannātha Miśra. His son was **Śrī Rāmabhadra Gosvāmī**; his daughter **Rasamañjarī** was the wife of Gopālavallabha (*Jagadīśa-caritra* 12.16). His descendants live at Dhubariyā, near Jāphargañj in the Dhaka district (Abhidhāna).

## The Jagannātha of Yaśoḍā

Śrī Gaurasundara ordered him to preach the holy name at **Nīlācala**. There he took shelter of Lord Jagannātha, and by his prayer obtained a Deity of the lotus-eyed Lord, which he brought, hanging from a staff, to **Yaśoḍā** on the bank of the Ganges; the staff is still worshipped in the temple (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, *Anubhāṣya* to Ādi 11.30). Mahāprabhu and Nityānanda sometimes came to Yaśoḍā for saṅkīrtana festivals. In the temple, now in the care of the Śrī Gauḍīya Maṭha (a rickshaw ride from Cākdaha station), are **Śrī Jagannātha**, **Śrī Rādhā-Vallabha** and the golden **Śrī Gaura-Gopāla**. The Abhidhāna adds that the Deities are still offered rice of boiled (siddha) paddy, as of old, and that an ancient bakula tree stood there.

**Gaura-Gopāla.** After a saṅkīrtana festival of many days in Jagadīśa's house, the Lord was about to leave for Nīlācala; Duḥkhinī, knowing His mind, was overwhelmed by the coming separation. Mahāprabhu gave her this Deity and said, "I will eternally remain in your house in the form of this Deity" — and Gaura-Gopāla has been worshipped there ever since; it is said that Duḥkhinī installed Him.

## The Ekādaśī (as the record's texts tell it)

On an Ekādaśī the child Gaurahari cried and would not stop: "If you don't want Me to die, go at once to the house of the two brāhmaṇas Jagadīśa and Hiraṇya. They are fasting, but they have prepared offerings for Viṣṇu; if I eat that Viṣṇu-prasāda I'll be well" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 6.20-23). Śacī Mātā struck her head; the neighbours laughed — how could such a child know it was Ekādaśī? But the two brāhmaṇas, close friends of Jagannātha Miśra who knew that Hari had appeared in his house, were delighted and brought everything; as He ate with His friends they saw Him as Bāla-Gopāla, dark as a fresh rain cloud, a peacock feather on His head, and chanted "Hari! Hari!" (*Gaurāṅga-campū*). The Abhidhāna gives this to the other Jagadīśa, of Navadvīpa.

## Later life

He probably came to Yaśoḍā after Mahāprabhu took sannyāsa. Every year he went with the devotees of Bengal to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā, and he was at Nityānanda's **Ciḍā-dadhi (Daṇḍa) mahotsava** at Pāṇihāṭi. **Kamalākānta dāsa** of Durgāpur was his disciple (Jayānanda, *Caitanya-maṅgala*). His life is told in the *Jagadīśa-caritra* of Ānanda dāsa, of the fifth generation of his line (printed as a puthi in 1737 Śaka, says the Abhidhāna).

His appearance is on the śukla dvādaśī of Pauṣa and his disappearance on the śukla tṛtīyā of Pauṣa (B. V. Tīrtha; the Gauḍīya calendar). One text gives the kṛṣṇa trayodaśī of Pauṣa, which is the day of his brother Maheśa Paṇḍita.
