## In short

Śrī **Raghunandana Ṭhākura** of **Śrīkhaṇḍa** was the son of **Mukunda dāsa** (Mukunda Sarakāra), the eldest brother of Śrī Narahari Sarakāra Ṭhākura: "Śrī Khaṇḍavāsī Mukunda and his son Raghunandana" are a branch of Lord Caitanya (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.78). A Vaidya by caste, he was born in **1432 Śaka** (1510 AD), on **Vasanta-pañcamī** (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Pradyumna** — Kandarpa, Kṛṣṇa's son in Dvārakā — and the priya-narma-sakhā Ujjvala (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 70); according to some he was **Kandarpa-mañjarī**.

## Childhood: Gopīnātha eats the lāḍḍu

He grew up in the care of Narahari Ṭhākura (Abhidhāna). In Mukunda's house the family Deity, **Śrī Gopīnātha**, had been served for many generations. One day Mukunda, a physician, had to go out on business and asked his little son — barely five years old (another text says eight) — to feed the Deity: "Just as your mother feeds you and me every day, so also He has to be fed every day." When the boy saw that Gopīnātha had not eaten, he cried and begged Him, "Please eat! Eat!", and Gopīnātha, conquered by that simple love, ate everything without leaving any remnants. When Mukunda came back and asked for the prasāda, the boy said, "I offered everything just as you told me, and Gopīnātha ate everything; so what should I bring you now?"

Another day Mukunda asked him to offer again and watched in hiding. Gopīnātha had eaten half of a lāḍḍu when He saw Mukunda, and ate no more. Mukunda took his son on his lap, his voice choked with love and tears pouring from his eyes. The poet **Uddhava dāsa** sings this pastime in a pada (quoted in the record's texts), and "even today those who are fortunate can still see the half-eaten lāḍḍu in Gopīnātha's lotus hand" at Śrīkhaṇḍa.

At eight he praised Mahāprabhu with a hymn of his own, the *Gaura-bhāvāmṛta* (Abhidhāna; the Abhidhāna also names a *Navadvīpa-candra-stavarāja* by him).

## "Who is the father and who is the son?"

Mukunda was the royal physician of the Bādśāh **Husain Shah**. Once, seeing the king fanned with a peacock-feather fan, he remembered Kṛṣṇa and fainted; he said it was epilepsy, but the king understood his state and sent him home with respect (in Mukunda's own record). Every year the three brothers went to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā. There the Lord asked Mukunda, "Between you and Raghunandana, who is the father and who is the son?" Mukunda answered, "Raghunandana is my father: through him I have found Kṛṣṇa bhakti." The Lord said, "Your judgement is correct: whoever gives us Kṛṣṇa-bhakti is our guru and our father," and ordered Raghunandana to serve the Deity and think of nothing else (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 15).

"Raghunandana is constantly engaged in serving the temple of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Beside the entrance of the temple is a lake, and on its banks is a kadamba tree, which daily delivers two flowers to be used for Kṛṣṇa's service" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 15.128-129) — the kadamba on the bank of the **Madhu-puṣkariṇī**, which flowered for him in every season (Abhidhāna). The *Bhakti-ratnākara* (8) says he served a Deity of Mahāprabhu. Gaurāṅga, the father of saṅkīrtana, made Raghunandana — whom He took as His son — the one entitled to give the garland and sandal-paste at the adhivāsa of the saṅkīrtana-yajña, and to break the pot of yogurt and turmeric at its end (Abhidhāna).

## The anklet of Ākāihāṭ

Once **Abhirāma Gopāla Ṭhākura** came to Śrīkhaṇḍa and bowed down to him; Raghunandana embraced him, and they began kīrtana together at **Baḍaḍāṅgā**. As he danced in ecstasy, wishing to bless his disciple **Kṛṣṇadāsa**, his anklet (nūpura) came off and fell two krośas away, at Kṛṣṇadāsa's house in **Ākāihāṭ**. The pond there is still called **Nūpura-kuṇḍa**; the texts say the anklet is kept by the local devotees (one text: by the mahāntas of Baḍui village, south of Ākāihāṭ).

## Kheturī and his last days

He went to the great festival of **Kheturī** held by Narottama Ṭhākura and Śrīnivāsa Ācārya, and sang kīrtana there. Before leaving the world he told **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya** that a difficult time was coming — "doubts of many kinds will arise in people's minds" — and quoted the *Kṛṣṇa-bhajanāmṛta*: when Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and Nityānanda have withdrawn, the Vaiṣṇavas will be troubled and doubtful. Then he blessed him: "Do not worry; the Lord Gaurarāya will do much through you. … By your power those who are against Kṛṣṇa will turn to Him and take shelter of you" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13.177-179).

After this he placed his son **Kānāi Ṭhākura** at the feet of **Śrī Śrī Gaura-Gopāla**, sang only the holy name for three days, and on the fourth day left for his own abode, repeating "Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya". "Blessed is that day, the śukla caturthī of Śrāvaṇa: who does not sing the glories of Raghunandana?" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13.184). Kānāi Ṭhākura called the devotees of the time and held his festival.

## Disciples and descendants

His disciples were the poets **Kaviśekhara (Rājaśekhara)** — who wrote two padas on him (*Padakalpataru* 2373-2374) — and **Nayanānanda Kavirāja**, and **Mahānanda Caudhurī**; in his branch (sakhā) were Janānanda Caudhurī, Pītāmbara dāsa, Kavirañjana, Śrīman Sen and others of Śrīkhaṇḍa.

Kānāi had two sons, Madana Rāya (a disciple of Narahari Sarakāra) and Vaṁśīvadana; at least four hundred men are counted in this line, all recorded at Śrīkhaṇḍa, and Pañcānana Kavirāja was born in it (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, quoted by Śrīla Prabhupāda). His descendants still live at Śrīkhaṇḍa, four miles west of Katwa.
