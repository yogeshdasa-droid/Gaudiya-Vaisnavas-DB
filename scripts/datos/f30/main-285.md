## In short

**Gaṇeśa** («Rājā Gaṇeśa»), zamindār of **Bhāturiyā Paragaṇā** in north Bengal, was head of revenue and administration under Āzam Shāh, king of Gauḍa, and the real power behind his successors; in **1407**, guided by **Narasiṁha Nāḍiyāl** — Advaita Prabhu's grandfather — he had Sultan Śamsuddīn killed and took the throne of Gauḍa (Bālya-līlā-sūtra; Advaita-prakāśa 1). **Padmanābha**, great-grandfather of Rūpa and Sanātana, was probably given his high post at court through him.

## At the court of Gauḍa

Narasiṁha Nāḍiyāl came from Śrīhaṭṭa to Rāmakeli and, after mastering Sanskrit and Persian, joined Gaṇeśa's council of ministers. After Āzam Shāh's death his son Hamzā Shāh took the throne, and then Āzam's grandson Śamsuddīn; both were mere tools in Gaṇeśa's hands. In 1407, under Narasiṁha's guidance, Gaṇeśa had Śamsuddīn removed and became king of Gauḍa himself. His court included the scholars Padmanābha and Narasiṁha, and the poet **Kṛttivāsa** received an award there (*Baṅga-bhāṣā o Sāhitya*, 4th ed., pp. 130-131).
