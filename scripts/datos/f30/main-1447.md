## In short

**Śrīkṛṣṇa Sārvabhauma**, of the Sānyāl family of the Vārendra clan and in the line of Rāmakṛṣṇa Vidyāvāgīśa, was a court scholar and poet of three kings of **Navadvīpa** — Rāmakṛṣṇa, Rāmajīvana and Raghurāma — early in the eighteenth century. He wrote the **Padāṅka-dūta** and the **Kṛṣṇa-padāmṛta** kāvya (250 ślokas, 1711), and, as a Nyāya scholar, the **Mukunda-padamādhurī** and the **Siddhānta-cintāmaṇi**.

## Life

A deed of 2 Jyaiṣṭha 1110 (1703) records that King Rāmakṛṣṇa Rāya of Navadvīpa gave him land; document 16633 of the Nadia collectorate says that on 10 Kārttika 1123 (1706) Śrīkṛṣṇa gave that land to his disciple **Rāmajīvana Pañcānana**.

## His works

The *Padāṅka-dūta* was written at the command of King Raghurāma Rāya, and the *Kṛṣṇa-padāmṛta* (250 ślokas, Śaka 1633, 1711) at the command of King Rāmajīvana, as their closing verses say. Two more works found later, the *Mukunda-padamādhurī* and the *Siddhānta-cintāmaṇi*, show him also a gifted Nyāya scholar and are of great value to historians: in the first Lord Kṛṣṇa is presented as the Paramātmā, and the introduction of the second praises Him too.
