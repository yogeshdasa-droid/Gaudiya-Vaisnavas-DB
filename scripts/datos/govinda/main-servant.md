## In short

Govinda was the personal servant and door-keeper of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Purī, from the Lord's return from South India to the end of His pastimes. A disciple of **Īśvara Purī**, he came to the Lord by his guru's last order (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.131–148). The *Abhidhāna* calls him a kāyastha and a branch of the Caitanya tree; he himself said to the Lord: "I am a śūdra, the servant of Īśvara Purī."

## Sent by Īśvara Purī

Govinda and **Kāśīśvara Brahmacārī** were both disciples of Īśvara Purī and served him without rest. Before leaving this world, Īśvara Purī ordered them to go and serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya. Govinda came first to Nīlācala and told the Lord his guru's order. The Lord hesitated: "The servant of My guru is worthy of My respect; how can I accept service from him? And yet My guru has ordered it." Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya settled it: the guru's order must be obeyed. So the Lord accepted Govinda, and Kāśīśvara who came after him (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.131–148; *Abhidhāna*).

## His service

Govinda massaged the Lord's feet, gave Him His meals, kept the door, and looked after everything the devotees brought for the Lord — he kept with great care the famous bags of food (**Rāghavera jhāli**) that Rāghava Paṇḍita sent from Pānihāṭi every year (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 10.12–56). He presented the devotees' offerings and told the Lord who had sent each one. After the Lord had eaten, Govinda took the remnants.

**Crossing over the Lord.** One day after lunch the Lord lay down across the door of His room, and Govinda could not get in to massage His feet. "Please turn a little, so that I can come in." — "I have no strength to move My body." Govinda begged again and again; the Lord did not move. Then Govinda spread his own cloth over the Lord's body, stepped over Him, went in and massaged His feet, and the Lord fell asleep. After a while He woke and saw Govinda still sitting there: "Why have you not gone to eat?" — "How could I? You are lying in the doorway." — "Then go out the way you came in." Govinda thought: "For His service I do not count even millions of offences; but for my own sake I fear even a shadow of offence" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 10.83–99). And he sat there without eating.

In the Lord's last years he was with Him in the Gambhīrā, with Svarūpa Dāmodara and Rāmānanda Rāya, in His ecstasies of separation (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 14.56, 15.82–100, 19.56, 20.118). When the Lord left Choṭa Haridāsa, He said to Paramānanda Purī: "I will go to Ālālanātha with Govinda" (Antya 2).

## After the Lord

When Śrīnivāsa Ācārya came to Purī after the Lord's disappearance, he found Gadādhara Paṇḍita and Govinda dried up by separation from Gaurāṅga: "they both lifted Śrīnivāsa and held him in their arms" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 3.189–190).

## Other persons of the same name

The record «Govinda» mixed him with many others; they now have their own records: Govinda (Vaikuṇṭha-pārṣada Puṇḍarīkākṣa), Govinda dāsa Kavirāja the poet, Govinda Kavirāja of Nityānanda's branch, Govinda Ācārya, Govindānanda Purī, the Govindas disciples of Śyāmānanda, Śrīnivāsa and Rasikānanda, Govinda Dāsa of Ghāṭaśilā, Govinda dāsa Bābājī of Purī, and the Govinda of Vṛndāvana («Govinda Bhakata»).
