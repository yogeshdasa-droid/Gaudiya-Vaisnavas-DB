## In short

**Guṇarāja Khān** — **Mālādhara Vasu** — of **Kulīnagrāma** (Burdwan) wrote the **Śrī Kṛṣṇa-vijaya**, the oldest Bengali verse life of Kṛṣṇa, from **1395 to 1402 Śaka** (1473–1480). "Khān" is not a name but a title given him by a king of Gauḍa. His father was **Bhagīratha Vasu** and his mother **Indumatī**; he was of the thirteenth generation from **Daśaratha Vasu**, whom King Ādiśūra brought from Kānyakubja (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna).

## "Kṛṣṇa, the son of Nanda, is my life"

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu praised the book in one line: "Guṇarāja Khān of Kulīnagrāma compiled the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-vijaya*, in which there is a sentence full of love: *nanda-nandana kṛṣṇa mora prāṇa-nātha* — 'Kṛṣṇa, the son of Nanda, is my life and soul.' By this sentence I am sold into the hands of his descendants" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 15.99-100). The Lord's great mercy to the people of Kulīnagrāma is told in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 10.80-83): "Even a dog of Kulīnagrāma is dear to Me." His line includes **Satyarāja Khān** and **Rāmānanda Vasu**, branches of Lord Caitanya (in their own records; the Abhidhāna gives the family table).

## The Śrī Kṛṣṇa-vijaya

It is a sung verse retelling of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, not a literal translation: it tells the stories of the tenth and eleventh cantos and part of the twelfth (Abhidhāna). The record's text is the English translation by **Kuśakratha dāsa**, with the Bengali verses. It opens with obeisances to Nārāyaṇa, "who has no beginning, the creator, maintainer and destroyer of the worlds", and the verse the Lord loved: *eka-bhāve vanda hari joḍa kari hāta, nanda-nandana kṛṣṇa mora prāṇa-nātha* — "With folded hands I offer my prayers to Kṛṣṇa, the son of Nanda, who is my life and soul." Then come chapters (each with its rāga) such as the description of Kṛṣṇa's beauty at twelve years old, the beauty of Vṛndāvana with its trees and flowers, the prayers of the demigods to Kṛṣṇa in the womb, the prayers of Kuvera's two sons, and the rāsa-līlā; each ends with the poet's signature: "With one heart, O people, hear Guṇarāja Khān's description of Govinda's feet, which lifts us out of the world of birth and death."
