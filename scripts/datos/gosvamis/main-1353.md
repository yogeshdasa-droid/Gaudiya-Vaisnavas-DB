## In short

Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī, the eldest of the three brothers Sanātana, Rūpa and Anupama and one of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, was the chief minister of Hussain Shah of Gauḍa, with the title **Sākara Mallika**. After meeting Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli he was imprisoned by the Badshah, escaped, and in Kāśī received from the Lord two months of instruction on sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana, with four tasks: to establish the conclusions of pure bhakti, to recover the lost holy places of Vraja, to manifest the service of Kṛṣṇa, and to give the Vaiṣṇavas their smṛti. In Vṛndāvana he served **Śrī Madana-mohana** and wrote the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, the *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta* and the *Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*. He is the **sambandha-ācārya** of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas, regarded by Rūpa as his spiritual master, and famous for his humility and renunciation. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Rati Mañjarī** (also called Lavaṅga or Rāga Mañjarī), and Sanātana Kumāra also entered him (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 181–182).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** 1410 Śaka (1488 AD) (*Abhidhāna*: "about 1410 Śaka").
- **Disappearance:** 1480 Śaka (1558 AD), on the full-moon day of Āṣāḍha (Āṣāḍha Pūrṇimā).
- He lived twenty-seven years at home and forty-three in Vraja: seventy years in all.
- *Other dates given:* one text notes that the date printed in the *Abhidhāna* (p. 1397) is not clear and may read 1420; the family table in the *Abhidhāna* (entry «Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī») gives 1383–1476 Śaka (about 1461–1554 AD).

## Family

The family line, from Sarvajña Jagadguru, king of Karṇāṭa, down to Kumāradeva, is given by Śrī Jīva at the end of his *Laghu-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī* (see Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī and Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī): Yajurvedic brāhmaṇas of the Bharadvāja gotra. Sanātana was **Kumāradeva's eldest son**; Rūpa was the second and **Vallabha (Anupama)** the youngest of the three; **Jīva** was Vallabha's only son. One text says Kumāradeva moved to Bākla Candradvīpa because he was oppressed by other members of the family. Sanātana's sister was married to **Śrīkānta**, a government officer.

The names Sanātana and Rūpa were given by Mahāprabhu; their former names are not certainly known — some say **Āmara** (Sanātana) and **Santoṣa** (Rūpa); another text gives them the other way round.

## Youth and studies

With his brothers he studied at the house of their maternal uncle at **Sākurmā**, near the capital of Gauḍa. His teachers, named in his commentary on the Tenth Canto, were **Vidyā-vācaspati**, the brother of Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya, who taught him all the scriptures and to whom he was devoted beyond description (Vidyā-vācaspati often stayed at Rāmakeli); **Paramānanda Bhaṭṭācārya**; **Rāmabhadra** ("Ramapada Bhadrapada"); and, in rhetoric, **Vidyābhūṣaṇa-pāda**. From childhood he loved the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam above everything (*Abhidhāna*).

**The Bhāgavatam.** When he was very young, he dreamt that a brāhmaṇa gave him a Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; he woke and was sad to find no one. The same morning, after his bath and worship, a brāhmaṇa came and handed him the Bhāgavatam: "Take this and study it always; all perfection will be yours." From that day he studied only the Bhāgavatam as the essence of all scriptures. In his *Kṛṣṇa-līlā-stava* he says to it: "You are my only companion, my only friend, my guru, my wealth, my deliverer, my fortune and the form of my joy — I bow to you" (*mad-eka-bandho mat-saṅgin mad-guro man-mahā-dhana…*).

## Minister of Gauḍa

According to the *Abhidhāna* and the record's texts, Sanātana's paternal grandfather **Mukunda** entered the royal service at Gauḍa under Sultan **Barbak Shah** (1460–1470). Barbak's son (Yusuf Shah) reigned seven years and was succeeded by **Fateh Shah**. Barbak had brought many slaves and eunuchs from Abyssinia (the "Habshis") to guard the kingdom and the palace; they conspired and killed Fateh Shah, four of them reigned for six or seven years and perished, and the vizier of the last, **Hussain Shah**, took the throne. Mukunda died in Fateh Shah's time and Sanātana was appointed to his post; he kept himself safe during the rule of the Habshis and under Hussain Shah rose to the highest office. He sometimes also took charge of defence.

- **His title:** the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (2.1.184) and the *Abhidhāna* (entry «Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī») call Sanātana **Sākara Mallika** and Rūpa **Dabira Khāsa**. *Other versions:* the *Abhidhāna*'s entry on Sanātana says that Sanātana's high post was called "Dabir Khās" (Private Secretary) — a word for the post, not a name or title; one text of the record calls him Dabir Khas. "Sākara" may come from the Bengali *sughar* (wise, accomplished, virtuous), and "Mallika" was a title given by the Muslims to respected families close to the government.
- **Chief minister:** for his great intelligence Hussain Shah made Sanātana his **chief minister** and Rūpa his deputy (*Abhidhāna*); the Badshah ruled as Sanātana advised, while Rūpa governed the border regions. Anupama is said to have been superintendent of the royal mint. Many Hindu devotees then held office under the Shah: Keśava Vasu Khān, Gopīnātha Vasu and Purandara Khān, the physician Mukunda, and Keśava Chatrī, the royal counsellor.
- The brothers were given the **parganas of Yusufpur and Cenguṭiyā** (Fateyābād) for their own use, and built a great palace there on the bank of the **Bhairava** river, at Premabhāga (*Jaśohar-Khulnār Itihās* 1.349–358); at **Rāmakeli**, the capital founded by Sultan Barbak in 1486 (now in Maldah district, about five miles from Maldah station), they built mansions and many tanks. A house near the Gaṅgā called **Bhaṭṭavāṭī** is said to have been their residence; brāhmaṇas from Karṇāṭa settled near them, and paṇḍitas from Navadvīpa came to serve them.
- Associating constantly with Muslims, Sanātana considered himself fallen from his brāhmaṇa caste, and always presented himself so — a humility celebrated in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*.

Even as ministers the brothers spent their time at home discussing the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (*Abhidhāna*). Near their house they made gardens of tamāla, kadamba, yūthikā and tulasī, with ponds named Rādhā-kuṇḍa and Śyāma-kuṇḍa, and served **Śrī Madana-mohana**. They longed to see Nimāi Paṇḍita; when He took sannyāsa and went to Purī they fainted, and a voice from the sky promised them His darśana at Rāmakeli itself. They wrote to Him, and He answered (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.196–197).

## Meeting Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli

When Mahāprabhu came to Rāmakeli (1514), the two brothers, leaving their royal dress, went to Him at night in humble disguise. They met Nityānanda Prabhu and Haridāsa Ṭhākura first, and fell before the Lord with straw between their teeth, saying they were millions of times lower than Jagāi and Mādhāi — servants of meat-eaters, enemies of cows and brāhmaṇas. The Lord said: "My dear Dabira Khāsa and Sākara Mallika, you are My old servants; from today your names are Rūpa and Sanātana. Give up this humility. I came to Bengal only to see you two." He placed His hands on their heads; they touched the feet of Nityānanda, Haridāsa, Śrīvāsa, Gadādhara, Mukunda, Jagadānanda, Murāri and Vakreśvara. Vallabha also received the Lord's mercy and the name **Anupama**. Before leaving, the brothers warned the Lord that the Badshah could not be trusted for long and that Vṛndāvana, a place of rustic simplicity, should not be visited with thousands of followers; the next morning the Lord went to **Kānāi-nāṭaśālā**, considered Sanātana's advice, and returned to Purī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.174–226; see Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī).

## Prison and escape

To obtain the Lord's feet the brothers had brāhmaṇas perform two puraścaraṇas of the Kṛṣṇa mantra (*Abhidhāna*; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.19.2–4). Rūpa and Anupama sent the family to Candradvīpa and Fateyābād and left with their wealth; Sanātana stayed alone. Pleading illness, he stopped going to court and sat at home studying the Bhāgavatam with fifteen or twenty paṇḍitas. The Badshah's physician found nothing wrong with him; then the Badshah himself came: "Everything depends on you, your brother has gone, my kingdom will fall — what are you doing?" — "Find someone else; you are the ruler of Gauḍa, punish me as you like." The Badshah had him imprisoned. Leaving to make war on Orissa, he asked Sanātana to come with him; Sanātana refused: "You will give pain to the Deities and to the sādhus" (2.19.12–29).

A letter came from Rūpa: he had left money with a grocer (eight hundred gold coins, in one account; ten thousand in another); Sanātana should free himself, for Rūpa and Anupama were going to Vṛndāvana (2.19.31–35). Sanātana told the Muslim jailer, who knew the Koran, that whoever frees a prisoner is himself freed by God, offered him five thousand and then seven thousand gold coins, and told him to say that Sanātana had jumped into the Gaṅgā in his shackles and drowned, and that he would go as a mendicant to Mecca. That night the jailer cut his shackles and took him across the Gaṅgā (2.20.3–32).

Avoiding the main road, walking day and night, he came with his servant **Īśāna** to the hilly tract of **Pātaḍā** (the Hazaribagh hills). A landlord — the leader of the local robbers — received him kindly, and Sanātana, once a minister, wondered why; Īśāna admitted he carried seven gold coins (in fact eight). Sanātana gave the landlord the seven. "I knew you had eight; tonight I would have killed you for them. I am satisfied with you; I will take you across simply as a pious act." — "If you don't take them, someone else will kill me for them." The landlord took them and gave him four watchmen to cross the hills by night. Then Sanātana sent Īśāna home with the last coin and went on alone, with a waterpot and a torn quilt, free of all anxiety. At **Hājipura** his brother-in-law **Śrīkānta**, who was buying horses for the emperor with 300,000 gold coins, begged him to rest and dress as a gentleman; Sanātana would not stay a moment, and accepted only help to cross the Gaṅgā and a fine woollen blanket (bhoṭa-kambala).

## Two months in Kāśī

In **Vārāṇasī** he heard that Mahāprabhu was there and sat at the door of **Candraśekhara**'s house. The Lord said, "There is a devotee at the door"; Candraśekhara saw only "a Muslim mendicant" — "Bring him in." The Lord ran to him and embraced him; Sanātana, faltering, begged, "Do not touch me"; the two wept shoulder to shoulder. The Lord cleaned Sanātana's body with His own hand: "I touch you to purify Myself; saints like you are places of pilgrimage." He introduced him to Candraśekhara, Tapana Miśra and the others, told him He had met Rūpa and Anupama at Prayāga, had him shaved and bathed in the Gaṅgā, and Sanātana refused new clothes, accepting an old cloth from Tapana Miśra; he wished to live by mādhukarī (2.20.33–77). Seeing the Lord glance again and again at his costly blanket, he exchanged it on the bank of the Gaṅgā for the torn quilt of a Bengali mendicant: "Kṛṣṇa has removed my last attachment" (2.20.77–89).

Then, clasping the Lord's feet, he asked: "Who am I? Why do the threefold miseries trouble me? What is my duty?" For **two months** Mahāprabhu taught him (2.20.92–2.25): the soul as Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant; Kṛṣṇa's energies — svarūpa-, māyā- and taṭasthā-śakti; karma, jñāna and bhakti (the parable of Sarvajña the astrologer); Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Person, His forms, expansions and avatāras, Goloka and Vaikuṇṭha, His opulence and His sweetness, the bewilderment of Brahmā; the six kinds of surrender; Kṛṣṇa as dīkṣā-, śikṣā- and caitya-guru; faith, the three classes of devotees and the twenty-six qualities of a Vaiṣṇava, kindness above all (Haridāsa Ṭhākura and Vāsudeva Datta); the need of sādhu-saṅga; vaidhī- and rāgānugā-sādhana, the sixty-four limbs of devotion and the five most important; bhāva and prema; Kṛṣṇa's sixty-four qualities; true and false renunciation, with the story of Nārada and the hunter; and **sixty-one explanations of the ātmārāma verse** (2.24). He blessed him that whenever Sanātana took up the pen Kṛṣṇa would make the writing flow (2.24.236–257). According to the *Abhidhāna*, the Lord taught him sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana, infused him with power, established him as ācārya and gave him four tasks: (1) to establish in the world the conclusions of pure bhakti, (2) to recover the lost holy places of Vraja, (3) to manifest the service of Kṛṣṇa (the Deities), and (4) to give the Vaiṣṇavas their smṛti — for which the Lord Himself gave him the outline (sūtra) of the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* (2.24.326–345). Sanātana took part in the kīrtana on the day Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī was converted at the Bindu-mādhava temple (2.25.54). Then the Lord sent him to Vṛndāvana and returned to Nīlācala; the devotees of Kāśī wept.

## Vṛndāvana and Purī

In Mathurā Sanātana met **Subuddhi Rāya** and heard of Rūpa and Anupama — Rūpa had left by another road, so they missed each other. He wandered through the forests in renunciation, collected the *Mathurā-māhātmya* literature and began locating the lost holy places (2.25.162–215).

He then went alone to **Jagannātha Purī** through the Jhārikhaṇḍa forest; from bad water and little food he developed an itching skin disease with running sores, and resolved to throw himself under the wheel of the Ratha-yātrā cart in the Lord's presence. In Purī he stayed with **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**; the Lord embraced him despite his sores and told him of Anupama's death and Anupama's faith in Lord Rāmacandra (3.4.5–51). Knowing his intention, the Lord forbade it: "Suicide is in the mode of ignorance; you have given Me your body — it is not yours. Through it I must do much: preach devotion and excavate the lost places of Vṛndāvana" (3.4.53–66); Haridāsa too explained the error (3.4.68–99).

One day the Lord called him to **Yameśvara-ṭoṭā**; Sanātana came along the burning sand of the beach, not by the Siṁha-dvāra road used by Jagannātha's servants, out of respect for the temple — and did not notice the blisters on his feet; the Lord was delighted (3.4.110–129). Ashamed that his sores touched the Lord, he asked **Jagadānanda Paṇḍita**, who advised him to return to Vṛndāvana after the Ratha-yātrā; the Lord rebuked Jagadānanda — Sanātana was his senior and more learned — and told Sanātana that, like a father, He felt no disgust for His children; He embraced him again and the disease vanished (3.4.130–192). Sanātana praised Jagadānanda's good fortune in being rebuked, and blamed himself for being praised. He saw the Ratha-yātrā, met the devotees of Bengal and Purī, played Holi, and after a year, on the Lord's order, returned to Vṛndāvana by the road the Lord had taken, as described to him by Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya (3.4.100–204).

## In Vṛndāvana

**Life.** Rūpa and Sanātana had no fixed home: they slept under a different tree each night, begged a little dry bread and fried chick-peas, carried only waterpots and wore torn quilts, chanted and discussed Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, danced, slept an hour and a half or not at all, wrote, and heard about Mahāprabhu (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.19.128–132). Sanātana lived at **Mahāvana**, near Kṛṣṇa's birthplace, in a grass hut; later the Vrajavāsīs built him a cottage. He also lived at **Cakaleśvara** near Govardhana and by **Pāvana-sarovara**. Through Jagadānanda, the Lord asked him to repair a Vaiṣṇava monastery at **Dvādaśāditya-ṭīlā**, and he lived in a hut in front of it (3.13.64). When Jagadānanda came to Vṛndāvana, Sanātana resolved his difficulties and sent gifts to the Lord through him; Jagadānanda was angry to see him wearing a red cloth given by another sannyāsī, and Sanātana explained that red was the colour of the Māyāvādī sannyāsīs and unfit for Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas — which is why Gauḍīya sannyāsīs wear saffron (3.13.43–67). He cared for **Raghunātha dāsa** like a brother and heard from him about Mahāprabhu (1.10.94–95).

**Śrī Madana-mohana.** Begging along the Yamunā, he met Madana-gopāla playing with the cowherd boys; the boy ran to him — "Baba, I'll come with you!" — "What will you eat? I eat only dry chapātīs and chick-peas." — "Then I'll eat that." Sanātana sent Him home. That night the boy appeared in a dream, laughing: "I am Madana-gopāla; tomorrow I come to you." In the morning a radiant Deity of Gopāla stood at his door. Weeping, Sanātana bathed and worshipped Him in his hut, and Rūpa, overwhelmed, sent the news to Mahāprabhu (*Bhakti-ratnākara*). *Another account (Prema-vilāsa):* the Deity, once worshipped by Advaita Ācārya, was in the house of **Dāmodara Caube** of Mathurā, whose wife Ballabhā and son (also called Madana-mohana) served Him like a child; Sanātana, begging there, taught her the rules of worship, but seeing the Deity and the boy eating together he told her to serve Him as her heart dictated; then both had the same dream and the Deity came to Sanātana, who built a leaf hut for Him on a hillock near **Sūrya-ghāṭa**.

Busy writing, Sanātana could often offer only dry chapātīs, without oil or salt, and grieved; once Madana-gopāla refused to eat without salt — "Today salt, tomorrow ghee! I have no time to chase rich men." Then **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kapūra**, a rich kṣatriya merchant of Multan, whose boat was stuck on a sandbank of the Yamunā, came for Sanātana's blessing and found him writing, emaciated, in a kaupīna. "I am a beggar — tell it to Madana-gopāla." Kapūra promised the Deity all his profit; that afternoon a storm raised the river and freed the boat. With his profit he built a temple and kitchen and arranged royal service; Sanātana was overjoyed and later **initiated him**. The Madana-mohana temple still stands in Vṛndāvana. The Deity is now worshipped at **Karauli**, Rajasthan: when a princess of Jaipur married the king of Karauli she asked for Madana-mohana as her dowry; her father agreed only if she could pick Him blindfolded among many Deities, and Madana-mohana told her she would know Him by the softness of His arm.

**Other pastimes** (*Bhakti-ratnākara*):
- *Madana-gopāla at play.* At Mahāvana he saw a cowherd boy who looked like Madana-gopāla playing by the Yamunā; another day he followed Him at dusk and saw Him enter the temple.
- *The milk.* Fasting and meditating by **Pāvana-sarovara**, he was brought a pot of milk at dusk by a cowherd boy — "I'll come for the pot tomorrow; I must milk the cows" — who vanished: Kṛṣṇa Himself. From then on he lived by mādhukarī, a little from many homes (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1303).
- *Rādhārāṇī's braid.* At Rādhā-kuṇḍa with Rūpa and Raghunātha dāsa, he doubted a verse of Rūpa's *Catuḥ-puṣpāñjali* comparing Rādhārāṇī's braid to a jewelled black serpent (*vyālāṅganā-phaṇām*). Bathing in the kuṇḍa he saw girls playing under a tree and what looked like a black snake on one girl's back; he ran to warn her — it was Śrī Rādhā, who laughed with Her friends and vanished — and he understood Rūpa's comparison.
- *The sweet rice.* When Rādhārāṇī, disguised as a milkmaid, brought Rūpa milk, rice and sugar to make sweet rice for Sanātana, Sanātana wept: "It was Śrī Rādhā Herself — accepting service from Her, whom we long to serve, will ruin us. Don't desire like this again" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1322).
- *The Govardhana-śilā.* He walked around Govardhana Hill every day (fourteen miles). When he grew old, Kṛṣṇa came as a cowherd boy and, since Sanātana would not break his vow, gave him a stone from Govardhana bearing His footprint (with the marks of a calf's hoof, a stick and a flute): "Go around this and your vow is kept." From then on he circumambulated the śilā.
- *The mosquitoes.* At Cakaleśvara the mosquitoes made it impossible to chant or write; Lord Śiva appeared in a dream and told him to go on serving Mahāprabhu there, and from then on there were no mosquitoes.
- Following Mahāprabhu, Rūpa and Sanātana never climbed Govardhana, considering it non-different from Kṛṣṇa; when Rūpa was too old to go to Govardhana, Gopāla came to Mathurā and stayed a month at the temple of Viṭhṭhaleśvara so that Rūpa could see Him.

**With the Vrajavāsīs.** The people of Vraja loved Rūpa and Sanātana and fed them milk and yogurt; the brothers knew all their families, cows, harvests and illnesses, and became their life (see Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī). Under their influence the great temples of Vṛndāvana were built — the Govinda temple bears an inscription of saṁvat 1647 (1590 AD) "under the direction of Rūpa and Sanātana" — and Akbar came to visit them. Until the end of the sixteenth century the land around Mathurā and Vṛndāvana was mostly forest; it was the Gosvāmīs who gave every lake and grove of Vraja its name.

## His position

- **Sambandha-ācārya.** Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja bows first to **Śrī Rādhā-Madana-mohana** (*jayatāṁ suratau paṅgor…*, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.1.15), then to Govinda and Gopīnātha (1.1.19). Madana-mohana, Sanātana's Deity, establishes our relationship with Kṛṣṇa (sambandha); Govinda, Rūpa's Deity, accepts our service (abhidheya); Gopīnātha, Raghunātha dāsa's Deity, is the goal (prayojana) — the three names of the eighteen-syllable mantra, *Kṛṣṇāya, Govindāya, Gopījana-vallabhāya* (Śrī Jīva on the *Brahma-saṁhitā*; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura; Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura; Śrīla Bhaktivedānta Svāmī Prabhupāda). "Sanātana Gosvāmī is the ideal spiritual master, for he gives the shelter of the feet of Madana-mohana."
- **His mercy.** Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī writes: "I was unwilling to drink the nectar of devotion mixed with renunciation, but Sanātana, an ocean of mercy who suffers at others' suffering, made me drink it; I take shelter of him" (*vairāgya-yug-bhakti-rasaṁ prayatnair…*, *Vilāpa-kusumāñjali* 6).
- With Rūpa and Jīva and their disciple Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja, he gave Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism its present form; Mahāprabhu's two "generals" were Rūpa and Sanātana. His literary contribution is matched only by Rūpa's and Raghunātha dāsa's.

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who taught him in Kāśī and Purī. One text says he took **dīkṣā from Madhusūdana Vācaspati** (others name Vidyā-vācaspati as his teacher).
- **Disciples:** Śrī **Rūpa Gosvāmī** (one text says Rūpa took dīkṣā from Sanātana, and Rūpa regarded him as his guru); **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kapūra** (dīkṣā). Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī received his mercy (*Vilāpa-kusumāñjali* 6).

## Works

- *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, with his own commentary *Dig-darśinī* — the Vaiṣṇava smṛti, in twenty chapters (vilāsas), on guru and disciple, initiation, cleanliness, tilaka and beads, worship of the Deity and the śālagrāma, the holy name and the offences, Ekādaśī and the yearly vows (Kārttika, Janmāṣṭamī and others), the forms of Viṣṇu, the installation of Deities and the building of temples. It follows the outline the Lord gave Sanātana in Kāśī and is said to be based on notes collected by **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī**, under whose name it is often known. Since the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (1463 Śaka) quotes it (1.2.72, 201), it was written before 1463 Śaka (*Abhidhāna*).
- *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*, with its commentary — Parīkṣit's conversation with his mother Uttarā: Nārada's search for the dearest devotee of Kṛṣṇa (Brahmā, Śiva, Prahlāda, Hanumān, the Pāṇḍavas, Uddhava, the gopīs), and the journey of Gopa-kumāra through all the worlds to Goloka; fourteen chapters in two parts.
- *Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī* (*Daśama-ṭippanī*), commentary on the Tenth Canto, completed in 1476 Śaka (*Bhakti-ratnākara*), which Śrī Jīva edited as the *Laghu-toṣaṇī*.
- *Kṛṣṇa-līlā-stava* (*Daśama-carita*), on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes up to Mathurā.
- *Also attributed:* a commentary *Tātparya-dīpikā* on Kālidāsa's *Meghadūta* (India Office Catalogue VII, 1422–1423); a *Gopāla-pūjā* (Madras Oriental Manuscripts Library); and a short *Laghu-hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇa*, which the Dhaka University Library has under Rūpa's name.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Rati Mañjarī**, the dearest friend of Rūpa Mañjarī, also called **Lavaṅga Mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 181), or Rāga Mañjarī; Sanātana Kumāra, one of the four Kumāras, also entered him (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 182; *Abhidhāna*). He was considered a personal extension of the body of Śrī Caitanya. With Rūpa and Anupama he is one of the three branches of the Caitanya tree "counted in the west" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.10.84).
