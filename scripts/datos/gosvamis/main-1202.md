## In short

Śrī Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī — "Bhaṭṭa Raghunātha" — one of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, was the son of **Tapana Miśra** of Vārāṇasī. As a boy he served Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in his father's house in Kāśī; grown up, he spent eight months with the Lord in Purī, returned to serve his parents without marrying, and after their death came back to Purī, from where the Lord sent him to Vṛndāvana to live with Rūpa and Sanātana. There he read the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam to Rūpa Gosvāmī in a voice sweeter than the cuckoo's, overwhelmed by love, and had a temple built for Śrī Govinda-deva. He never heard or spoke ill of anyone. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Rāga Mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 185).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** 1427 Śaka (1505 AD), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of Āśvina (Āśvina Śukla Dvādaśī).
- **Disappearance:** 1501 Śaka (1579 AD), on the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Jyaiṣṭha (Jyaiṣṭha Śukla Daśamī). He lived 74 years.
- He lived twenty-eight years at home (*Abhidhāna*).

## Family: Tapana Miśra

His father, **Tapana Miśra**, was a learned brāhmaṇa of East Bengal. Though he had discussed the scriptures at length, he could not reach a conclusion about the goal of life (*sādhya*) and the means (*sādhana*). When Nimāi Paṇḍita, still a householder, came to East Bengal, to the bank of the **Padmā**, as a travelling teacher of grammar, a divine person told Tapana Miśra in a dream: "Don't worry, Miśra. Nimāi Paṇḍita has come near here; He will teach you. He is not a man but Nara-Nārāyaṇa, the cause of the universe, who has come in human form" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 14.123). In the morning Tapana Miśra found Him seated among His students like the moon among the stars — lotus eyes, curly black hair, a white sacred thread, yellow cloth — fell at His feet and asked about sādhya and sādhana. The Lord taught him that in each age the Lord gives the method fit for the strength and lifespan of the people — meditation in Satya-yuga, sacrifice in Tretā, Deity worship in Dvāpara, and **nāma-saṅkīrtana** in Kali — and that in Kali the holy name is everything: *Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare*; "the name is both the means and the goal, and the name and the Lord are not different" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 14.137). Tapana Miśra wished to follow Him to Navadvīpa, but the Lord told him to go to **Kāśī**, where they would meet again; Tapana Miśra and his wife settled there.

In Kāśī, says the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, the Lord had three devotees: Candraśekhara the vaidya, Tapana Miśra, and Raghunātha, the Miśra's son (1.10.152–153).

## Childhood in Kāśī

Years later Mahāprabhu, now a sannyāsī, passed through Kāśī on His way to Vṛndāvana through the Jhārikhaṇḍa forest (Choṭa Nāgpur). At **Maṇikarṇikā-ghāṭa** He chanted "Hari! Hari!" so loudly that Tapana Miśra, bathing there, was amazed to hear the holy name in the city of the Māyāvādīs — "like water in the desert". Recognising Nimāi Paṇḍita, he fell at His feet; the Lord embraced him. Tapana Miśra took Him home, washed His feet, and drank that water with his family; he placed his little son **Raghunātha** at the Lord's feet, and the Lord took the child on His lap. **Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya** cooked; after the meal the boy massaged the Lord's feet while He rested. Candraśekhara, the Mahārāṣṭrian brāhmaṇa and the other devotees came; the Lord visited Viśveśvara, Bindu-mādhava and Daśāśvamedha-ghāṭa, stayed at Candraśekhara's house and took His meals at Tapana Miśra's. When the Mahārāṣṭrian brāhmaṇa told Him that Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī could say "Caitanya" but not "Kṛṣṇa", the Lord said that the name of Kṛṣṇa does not come from the lips of offenders, and went on to Vṛndāvana.

On His return He delivered Prakāśānanda and the sannyāsīs of Kāśī, and the city was flooded with the holy name. The texts of the record say He then stayed ten days; according to the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* and the *Abhidhāna* He stayed **two months**, living at Candraśekhara's house and taking His meals at Tapana Miśra's — and during that time the boy Raghunātha served Him, cleaning His plate and massaging His feet (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.10.154). When the Lord left, Raghunātha clung to His feet weeping; the Lord took him on His lap, dusted him off and said: "Stay and serve your father and mother; later you may come to see Me in Purī." Raghunātha soon mastered Sanskrit grammar, rhetoric, poetry and the scriptures, and served his aging parents.

## In Purī with Mahāprabhu

When he came of age his father sent him to Purī. His mother packed many Bengali delicacies for the Lord. On the way he met **Rāmadāsa**, a learned kāyastha in government service, a devotee of Rāma and a teacher of the *Kāvya-prakāśa*, who insisted on carrying the bag on his head: "I am a low-born śūdra; serving a brāhmaṇa will do me good." Raghunātha begged him to give it back to the servant; they discussed the scriptures all the way (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.13).

Walking, Raghunātha feared the Lord would not remember a small boy among so many dear devotees. But as he fell before Him, the Lord called out "Raghunātha!" and embraced him (*daṇḍa-praṇāma kari' bhaṭṭa paḍilā caraṇe, prabhu 'raghunātha' jāni' kailā āliṅgane*, 3.13.101). "How do You still remember me?" — "In this life and life after life I shall not forget the love of your parents, who fed Me every day." The Lord introduced him to the devotees, heard the news of Tapana Miśra, Candraśekhara and the devotees of Kāśī, and told Govinda to put away the mother's sweets carefully. He sensed that Rāmadāsa desired liberation and showed him little affection. Raghunātha bathed in the sea, saw Jagannātha, ate the Lord's remnants from Govinda, and stayed **eight months**, sometimes cooking for the Lord and seeing Him dance before the Ratha-yātrā car.

Then the Lord sent him home: "Serve your aged parents — they are Vaiṣṇavas; **do not marry**; read the Bhāgavatam with Vaiṣṇavas; come again to see Jagannātha." He gave him His own tulasī neck-beads and Jagannātha's prasāda for the devotees of Kāśī.

In Kāśī Raghunātha served his parents and studied the Bhāgavatam. When they passed away he took renunciation and, unmarried, returned to Purī; the Lord glorified his parents' devotion. After another eight months the Lord told him: "Go to Vṛndāvana. There is much to do there; My mother's order keeps Me in Purī, so you must do My work. Stay with Rūpa and Sanātana and always discuss the Bhāgavatam." At parting He gave him a garland of Jagannātha's tulasī **fourteen cubits (seven yards) long** and betel mahā-prasāda, and embraced him for the last time (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.13.126). Raghunātha went to Vṛndāvana by the road the Lord had walked, hearing everywhere of His pastimes.

## In Vṛndāvana

Rūpa and Sanātana received him as a brother, and all the Gosvāmīs as a dear godbrother; his humility and gentleness pleased everyone. He stayed with **Rūpa Gosvāmī**, who heard the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam from him (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.10.154–158). Reading, he was overwhelmed by love — tears, trembling, a choked voice — and often could not go on. His voice surpassed the cuckoo's, and he sang each verse in three or four different rāgas; when he read of Kṛṣṇa's beauty and sweetness, he lost all outer awareness (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.13.126–134). The *Bhakti-ratnākara* (6.453–457) says that his learning in the meaning of the scriptures delighted even Bṛhaspati, that Vyāsa and the sages longed to hear his reading of the Bhāgavatam, and that the demigods marvelled at his devotion; his glory was made known through Śrīnivāsa Ācārya.

He surrendered himself to the feet of **Śrī Govinda**, which were his life and soul. He had **his disciples build a temple for Govinda-deva** (with the help of a rich devotee, says another text), and had ornaments made for Him — a flute and makara-shaped earrings. He neither heard nor spoke of worldly things; he would not listen to blame of any Vaiṣṇava — "everyone is serving Kṛṣṇa, that is all he knew"; day and night passed in worship and talk of Kṛṣṇa. When he meditated on the Lord, he tied the tulasī garland given him by Mahāprabhu together with the Jagannātha prasāda and wore them around his neck.

Unlike the other Gosvāmīs, it is not known whether he worshipped a particular Deity. His worshipful **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** is still worshipped at the **Gadādhara Bhaṭṭa Pīṭha**, in another Madana-mohana temple near the Rādhā-vallabha temple in Vṛndāvana, where there is also a beautiful painting of Śrī Śrī Gaura-Gadādhara.

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** **Gadādhara Paṇḍita** (one text of the record); the Lord Himself taught and sent him.
- **Disciples:** those who built the Govinda temple at his request (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*); **Gadādhara Bhaṭṭa** is named in the record. Mahārāja Mān Siṁha of Amber, who gave the red sandstone for the Govinda temple, is said by some to have been his disciple (others say Rūpa Gosvāmī's).

## Works

No written works of his are named in the sources.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Rāga Mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 185).
