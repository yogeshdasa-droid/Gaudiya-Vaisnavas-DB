## In short

Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī, one of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, was the only son of Śrī Vallabha (Anupama) and the nephew and disciple of Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana. As a child he saw Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli; he received the mercy of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu in Navadvīpa, studied Vedānta in Kāśī under Madhusūdana Vācaspati, and lived the rest of his life in Vṛndāvana. Initiated by Śrī Rūpa, he became the foremost scholar and philosopher of the Gauḍīya sampradāya — author of the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* and of at least twenty-five works — and, after the disappearance of Rūpa and Sanātana, the ācārya who guided the Vaiṣṇavas of Bengal, Orissa and everywhere else. He trained Śrīnivāsa Ācārya, Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura and Śyāmānanda Prabhu in the books of the Gosvāmīs and sent them to preach in Bengal. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is Vilāsa Mañjarī.

## Dates

- **Appearance:** 1433 Śaka (1511 AD) (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*, family table), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of Bhādra (Bhādra Śukla Dvādaśī).
- **Disappearance:** 1518 Śaka (1596 AD) (*Abhidhāna*), on the third day of the bright fortnight of Pauṣa (Pauṣa Śukla Tṛtīyā), in Vṛndāvana.
- He lived eighty-five years: twenty at home and sixty-five in Vṛndāvana (*Vaiṣṇava-digdarśanī*).
- This date agrees with the account that Jīva saw Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli as a small child (Mahāprabhu was at Rāmakeli in 1514).
- *Other dates given:* 1455 Śaka (1533 AD) for his appearance and 1540 Śaka (1618 AD) for his disappearance, at the age of 85 (one of the record's texts); the *Vaiṣṇava-digdarśanī* gives his birth as 1445 or 1435 Śaka (1523 or 1513 AD), and names the Śukla Tṛtīyā of Pauṣa and the Śukla Tṛtīyā of Āśvina as his days of appearance and disappearance (the wording of the source is not clear about which is which).

## Family

Jīva's father was **Śrī Vallabha**, better known as **Anupama**, the name given to him by Lord Caitanya; Vallabha's two elder brothers were **Śrī Sanātana** and **Śrī Rūpa**, ministers at the court of the Badshah **Hussain Shah** of Gauḍa. Of the three brothers, Jīva was the only descendant. Richly rewarded for their service, the family lived in great opulence.

The family lived in three places: **Bākla Candradvīpa**, **Fateyābād** and **Rāmakeli**; Jīva spent most time at Rāmakeli with Rūpa and Sanātana.

**The family line** (from the genealogical tables of the texts and of the *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*):

- **Sarvajña Jagadguru**, king of Karṇāṭa (1303 Śaka, 1381 AD), of the Bharadvāja gotra; though a brāhmaṇa he was so learned and virtuous that students came to him from many lands.
- His son **Aniruddha**, a scholar of the Yajur Veda, became king in 1338 Śaka (1416 AD). He had two sons: **Rūpeśvara**, learned in the śāstras, and **Harihara**, skilled in arms. After their father divided the kingdom between them, Harihara seized Rūpeśvara's share; Rūpeśvara went east with his wife and settled under the friendship of King Śikhareśvara (*Abhidhāna*).
- Rūpeśvara's son **Padmanābha** (born 1308 Śaka, 1386 AD), famous for his beauty, learning and wealth, settled at **Navahaṭṭa (Naihāṭī)** on the Bhāgīrathī. He had eighteen daughters and five sons: Puruṣottama, Jagannātha, Nārāyaṇa, Murāri and **Mukunda**.
- Mukunda's son **Kumāradeva**, very strict in religious practice, left Naihāṭī during a religious upheaval and settled at **Bākla Candradvīpa**, with another residence at **Fateyābād** (Jessore) (*Abhidhāna*).
- Of Kumāradeva's many sons three became famous: **Sanātana, Rūpa and Anupama (Vallabha)**. After their father's death they studied at **Sākurmā**, a small village near the capital of Gauḍa, under the shelter of their maternal uncle; at the age of twenty-four or twenty-five Sanātana and Rūpa became ministers of Hussain Shah, with the titles **Sākara Mallika** and **Dabira Khāsa** (*Abhidhāna*). The *Abhidhāna*'s table gives Vallabha's years as 1395–1437 Śaka.
- Anupama's son: **Śrī Jīva**.

More about the family is given under Anupama.

## Childhood

Jīva had a fair, golden complexion, lotus-petal eyes and extremely handsome features. When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu came to **Rāmakeli**, Jīva, still a small child, had His darśana — secretly, according to one account. Mahāprabhu placed the dust of His feet on the child's head and indicated that he would become a great scholar and the future preceptor of the Gauḍīya sampradāya. That darśana changed him: he gave up childish play, developed attachment to Śrī Caitanya, and from then on meditated on the Lord's form while eating, resting, dreaming and awake. No book mentions any later meeting between Jīva and Mahāprabhu (*Abhidhāna*).

When his father and uncles renounced home to be with Mahāprabhu, Jīva, the only child, remained with his mother in the family palace at Fateyābād. Remembering his father and uncles, or Śrī Caitanya, he would lose consciousness and fall to the ground. As he grew, he worshipped Deities of **Śrī Śrī Rāma-Kṛṣṇa** (Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma), decorating Them and offering bhoga and ārati with full attention; he made images of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma himself, and even his games were Kṛṣṇa's pastimes (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, quoted in the *Abhidhāna*). Studying under local paṇḍitas, he became proficient in grammar, poetry and rhetoric; his teachers said such brilliance was rarely seen in a child and that he would surely become a great saint.

Once he dreamt that Rāma and Kṛṣṇa took the forms of **Nitāi and Gaurāṅga** and danced, gave him the dust of Their feet and disappeared (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.732).

After Rūpa, Sanātana and Vallabha went to Vṛndāvana, Rūpa and Vallabha set out for Nīlācala (Purī), and **Vallabha passed away on the way, on the bank of the Gaṅgā**. (According to the *Abhidhāna*, Jīva lost his father in early childhood; its table gives Vallabha's death as 1437 Śaka, when Jīva was about four.) Deeply grieved, Jīva lost all taste for family life; even what remained of the family wealth, after Rūpa and Sanātana had given theirs away, seemed poison to him: "Jewels, fine clothes, splendid beds and rich food — he left them all, and could not bear to hear talk of kingdoms and worldly affairs" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.687–88).

## Navadvīpa and Nityānanda Prabhu

One night Lord Caitanya (and, in one account, Nityānanda Prabhu) appeared to him in a dream. Someone suggested that he go to Navadvīpa to be refreshed at the feet of Nityānanda Prabhu. Telling his family he was going to Navadvīpa to study, he left Bākla Candradvīpa with a group of pilgrims (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.741); near **Fateyābād** he dismissed his escort, keeping only one servant.

Knowing that Jīva was coming, Nityānanda Prabhu also came to Navadvīpa from **Khaḍadaha**. Jīva bowed down to Mother Gaṅgā and found Nityānanda at **Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita's house (Śrīvāsa-aṅgana)**. Nityānanda Prabhu, overwhelmed with affection, placed His feet on Jīva's head (*Bhakti-ratnākara*), embraced him and asked, "Are you the nephew of Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana?" — and said, "Jīva, it is for you that I have come from Khaḍadaha to Navadvīpa. Stay some days in Navadvīpa, then go to Vṛndāvana" (*Abhidhāna*). The devotees of Navadvīpa, Śrīvāsa among them, were delighted to meet the nephew of Rūpa and Sanātana and took great care of him.

The next day Nityānanda took him to the house of **Śacī Mātā**, by then very old and thin, dressed in white, absorbed in remembrance of Gaurasundara. She called her servant Īśāna to wash Nityānanda's feet, blessed Jīva with her hand on his head, and fed them both: "Take prasāda here at your mother's house today, my child." Nityānanda Prabhu then took Jīva on parikramā of the **nine islands of Navadvīpa**, to all the places of Mahāprabhu's pastimes.

Jīva asked either to go to Nīlācala or to remain all his life with Nityānanda Prabhu; Nityānanda refused both and ordered him to go first to Kāśī to study, and then to Vṛndāvana.

## Kāśī

In Kāśī (Vārāṇasī) Jīva stayed at the house of **Tapana Miśra** and studied Vedānta, Nyāya and other subjects under **Madhusūdana Vācaspati**, the favourite disciple of **Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya**. After his defeat by Lord Caitanya in Purī, Sārvabhauma had taught Madhusūdana the Lord's conclusions on Vedānta as found in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, and Madhusūdana had opened a school (ṭol) in Kāśī; from him Jīva mastered those conclusions. In this sense Madhusūdana was Jīva's Vedānta guru (*Bhakti-ratnākara*).

## Vṛndāvana, with Rūpa and Sanātana

From Kāśī Jīva went to **Vṛndāvana**, where his uncles Rūpa and Sanātana received him with joy and heard all the news from him. He stayed with Śrī Rūpa, who taught him the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, **initiated him (dīkṣā)** and engaged him in the service of **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Dāmodara**. According to the *Sādhana-dīpikā*, Rūpa Gosvāmī made this Deity of Dāmodara with his own hands for his dear disciple; Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Dāmodara are now worshipped in **Jaipur**, Rajasthan.

Jīva served his uncles personally: he brought water for their baths, massaged their heads with oil, cleaned their āśrama, worshipped the Deity, cooked, and corrected their manuscripts. Seeing how quickly he grasped the conclusions of the Bhāgavatam, Rūpa engaged him in proof-reading the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, on which Jīva wrote the commentary *Durgama-saṅgamanī*. In 1476 Śaka Sanātana Gosvāmī completed the *Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*, his commentary on the Tenth Canto, and gave it to Jīva to proof-read; on Sanātana's order Jīva wrote on it the *Laghu-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī* (1500 Śaka). Their writings, with those of Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa, Raghunātha dāsa, Kṛṣṇadāsa (Kavirāja), Kāśīśvara Paṇḍita and Madhu Paṇḍita, captivated the learned men of the time: it was the beginning of a golden age in Vraja.

**Vallabha Bhaṭṭa.** One day on the bank of the Yamunā, while Rūpa Gosvāmī was writing the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* and Jīva was fanning him, the famous scholar **Vallabha Bhaṭṭa**, founder of the Vallabha sampradāya, arrived, looked through the pages, and said he would correct the book; then he went to bathe in the Yamunā. Jīva could not bear this pride towards his guru, but would not argue in Rūpa's presence; on the pretext of fetching water he followed Vallabha Bhaṭṭa and asked him where he had found mistakes. A long debate on the śāstras followed, and Vallabha Bhaṭṭa, charmed by the boy's learning, could not refute him (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1635). Returning to Rūpa, he asked who the boy was; Rūpa answered, "His name is Jīva — my disciple, my brother's son" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1638). Vallabha Bhaṭṭa praised Jīva and left. Rūpa, however, did not approve: "Out of kindness the Bhaṭṭa came to me and offered to correct my book, and you could not tolerate such a small remark. Go at once to the east" — he still had to learn to control his mind (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1641–43).

Jīva obeyed immediately. He went east and lay at **Nanda-ghāṭa** (*Abhidhāna*) — on the outskirts of Vṛndāvana, intending to give up his life — refusing to bathe or eat; some days he fasted, on others the Vrajavāsīs pressed him to eat a few fruits and roots, and he became very thin and weak. One day **Sanātana Gosvāmī**, wandering in the forest, heard of him; moved by his state, he begged Rūpa to forgive him and brought him back. Rūpa forgave Jīva and nursed him until he recovered. "All were happy at Jīva's recovery; Rūpa and Sanātana gave him charge of everything. By the grace of Rūpa and Sanātana, the power of Jīva's learning spread over the world" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1664).

**Other debates.** In Vṛndāvana Jīva defeated two renowned scholars: one incident is told in the *Bhaktamālā*, and the *Prema-vilāsa* tells of his encounter with **Rūpanārāyaṇa**. In similar circumstances he defeated a **digvijayī paṇḍita** in a debate on the śāstras that lasted seven days. In his time there was no paṇḍita in India like him; from childhood he was favoured by the goddess Sarasvatī (*Abhidhāna*).

## Ācārya of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas

After the disappearance of Rūpa and Sanātana, Jīva continued the tradition they had begun and became the ācārya of all the Vaiṣṇavas of Bengal, Orissa and the rest of the world, guiding them in devotional service.

- **Agra.** He went to Agra to debate with the Rajputs on the glories of the Yamunā and the Gaṅgā, and established that the Yamunā is the more glorious, since the Gaṅgā flows from Kṛṣṇa's feet while the Yamunā is His consort. The Mughal emperor, pleased, wished to give him something; Jīva asked only for blank paper — very rare then, when most manuscripts were written on leaves — and received some stained paper.
- **The farman.** It is said that when a Mughal emperor (possibly Akbar) wished to honour the Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, they asked for a farman that no living being be killed in Vraja; after that no king came there to hunt.

## Disciples and those he trained

- **Dīkṣā guru:** Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī. **Vedānta teacher:** Madhusūdana Vācaspati (Kāśī).
- He greatly favoured three young devotees, who studied all the literature of the Gosvāmīs under him and whom he later sent to preach it in Bengal:
  - **Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura**, disciple (dīkṣā) of Lokanātha Gosvāmī;
  - **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, disciple (dīkṣā) of Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī;
  - **Śyāmānanda Prabhu**, disciple (dīkṣā) of Hṛdaya Caitanya Prabhu.

## Works

As far as is known he composed and edited **at least twenty-five books** in Sanskrit. At the end of each he noted the Śaka year in which it was completed. The lists in the sources, put together:

- **Philosophy:** *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* — *Tattva-, Bhagavat-, Paramātma-, Kṛṣṇa-, Bhakti-* and *Prīti-sandarbha* (the *Bhāgavata-sandarbha*, written at the command of Rūpa and Sanātana); its own commentary *Sarva-saṁvādinī*; *Krama-sandarbha* (commentary on the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; with it, "seven sandarbhas").
- **Commentaries:** *Laghu-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī* (Tenth Canto); *Durgama-saṅgamanī* (on *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*); *Locana-rocanī* (on *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*); on the *Brahma-saṁhitā*; *Sukha-bodhinī* (on the *Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad*); on the *Yoga-sāra-stava* (*Padma Purāṇa*); *Gāyatrī-vyākhyā-vivṛti* / *Agni-purāṇīya-gāyatrī-bhāṣya* (on the Gāyatrī as described in the *Agni Purāṇa*, chapters 216–217).
- **Grammar:** *Hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇa* — based on the lessons Lord Caitanya gave after His return from Gayā, useful both for grammar and for bhakti-śāstra; *Sūtra-mālikā* (*Dhātu-sūtra-mālikā*); *Dhātu-saṅgraha*.
- **Poetry and drama:** *Gopāla-campū* (in two parts, Pūrva and Uttara); *Gopāla-virudāvalī* ("calling out to Lord Gopāla"; one list calls it *Govinda-virudāvalī*); *Śrī Mādhava-mahotsava*; *Saṅkalpa-kalpavṛkṣa*; *Bhāvārtha-sūcaka-campū*; *Rasāmṛta-śeṣa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-śeṣa*).
- **Worship:** *Rādhā-kṛṣṇārcana-candrikā* / *-dīpikā* (*Kṛṣṇārcā-dīpikā*); *Rādhā-kṛṣṇa-pāda-cihna-vinirṇaya* (*Kara-pada-cihna-samāhṛti*: the marks on the hands and feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa; one list gives separately a description, from the *Agni Purāṇa*, of the Lord's feet, and one of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī's feet).
- **Also named:** *Mukta-caritra*; *Sāra-saṅgraha*.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

According to the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī was formerly the gopī **Vilāsa Mañjarī**.
