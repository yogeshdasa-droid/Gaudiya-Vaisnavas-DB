## In short

Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, the leader of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, was the brother of Śrī Sanātana and of Śrī Anupama (Vallabha) and the uncle and dīkṣā guru of Śrī Jīva. A minister of Badshah Hussain Shah of Gauḍa with the title **Dabira Khāsa**, he met Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli, gave up everything, and was taught by the Lord for ten days at Prayāga and again in Purī. Mahāprabhu charged him with two tasks: to recover the lost holy places of Vṛndāvana and to write and preach the science of bhakti. He uncovered Śrī Govinda-deva, established the worship of the Lord in Vraja, and wrote the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, the dramas *Vidagdha-mādhava* and *Lalitā-mādhava* and many other works. Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura praises him as the one who established in this world the innermost desire of Śrī Caitanya (*śrī-caitanya-mano-'bhīṣṭaṁ sthāpitaṁ yena bhū-tale*). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 180).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** 1392 Śaka (about 1470 AD) (family table of the *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*, entry «Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī»).
- **Disappearance:** 1476 Śaka (about 1554 AD) (*Abhidhāna*, same table), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of Śrāvaṇa (Śrāvaṇa Śukla Dvādaśī).
- He lived twenty-two years at home and fifty-one in Vraja (one text gives his age as 73, another as 75).
- *Other dates given:* 1415 Śaka (1493 AD) for his birth (Śrī Rādhā-ramaṇa Bera and other texts of the record) or 1411 Śaka (1489 AD); 1486 Śaka (1564 AD) for his disappearance, or 1490 Śaka (1568 AD) ("according to another view"). *Note:* the same table makes Sanātana (1383 Śaka) older than Rūpa, but the *Abhidhāna*'s own entry on Sanātana gives "about 1410 Śaka" for Sanātana's birth.

## Family

Rūpa belonged to a Yajurvedic brāhmaṇa family of the **Bharadvāja gotra**, whose forefathers lived in **Karṇāṭa** (*Abhidhāna*). Śrī Jīva gives the line at the end of his *Laghu-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*: **Sarvajña Jagadguru**, a king and a scholar of all the śāstras, seventh in the ascending line from Rūpa; his son **Aniruddha**; Aniruddha's sons **Rūpeśvara** (learned in the śāstras) and **Harihara** (skilled in arms), who seized his brother's share of the kingdom; Rūpeśvara, who went east with his wife and, in one telling, eight horses, and became the friend of King Śikhareśvara (Śekhareśvara); his son **Padmanābha**, who settled at **Naihāṭī (Navahaṭṭa)** on the Gaṅgā — one text adds that there he became friendly with King Danujamardana (Rāja Gaṇeśa?) — and had eighteen (or eight) daughters and five sons, Puruṣottama, Jagannātha, Nārāyaṇa, Murāri and **Mukunda**; Mukunda, who settled at **Bākla Candradvīpa** (or, in another account, his son did), with houses also at Jessore and Fateyābād, where he often visited his yajamānas; and Mukunda's son **Kumāradeva**, Rūpa's father. The family tree of all of them is given under Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī.

- **Father:** Kumāradeva, a very religious man, who moved from Naihāṭī to Bākla Candradvīpa during a religious upheaval and stayed for some time at Fateyābād, between the two.
- **Mother:** Revatī devī (by tradition, Kumāradeva and Revatī had one daughter and five sons).
- **Brothers:** **Sanātana** (the elder) and **Anupama**, whose name was Vallabha (the younger); of Kumāradeva's many sons these three were the great devotees.
- **Nephew:** Śrī **Jīva**, Anupama's only son, Rūpa's disciple.
- According to some scholars, before Mahāprabhu gave them their names Rūpa was called **Āmara** and Sanātana **Santoṣa**.

## Youth and studies

After their father's death the three brothers went to their maternal uncle's house at **Sākurmā**, a small village near the capital of Gauḍa, to complete their studies. Their teacher of philosophy was the famous **Vidyā-vācaspati** (Sarvānanda), brother of Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya; according to the *Daśama-ṭippanī*, their other teachers were **Paramānanda Bhaṭṭācārya** and **Rāmabhadra** ("Ramapada Vadrapada" in the text). They are said to have learned Arabic and Persian with the help of **Syed Fakhruddin**, a scholar and landowner of Saptagrāma. Even very young they were masters of all the scriptures — called *rāja-śiṣṭa*, "kings of learning" — and Rūpa had already written books, among them the *Haṁsadūta*.

## Ministers at Rāmakeli

There are several accounts of how they became ministers of **Hussain Shah**, Badshah of Gauḍa:
- the Badshah's maulavi, who was said to see the future, told him that if he made two very learned brāhmaṇa boys his ministers, his kingdom would prosper;
- the Badshah heard of their glories from many people and appointed them; they accepted unwillingly, out of fear of the Yavana king;
- according to the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, the Nawab forced them, threatening to drive them out and to harm the brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas if they refused; their fear was for the devotees, not for themselves.

Rūpa received the title **Dabira Khāsa** (private or principal secretary) and Sanātana **Sākara Mallika** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.165; *Abhidhāna*, entry «Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī»). *Other versions:* the *Abhidhāna*'s entry on Sanātana says that "Dabir Khās" was the name of the high post held by Sanātana, not a title; it adds that Hussain Shah made Sanātana his chief minister and Rūpa his deputy minister. One text calls Rūpa Revenue Officer and Secretary of the Treasury; another gives the titles the other way round. The Badshah gave them great wealth. They lived at **Rāmakeli**, the capital, in a court that rivalled Indra's, where brāhmaṇas and paṇḍitas from Navadvīpa, Karṇāṭa and all over India, poets, singers, musicians and dancers were received and served with great care. They studied the śāstras constantly. Near their house they made a hidden ("gupta") Vṛndāvana: gardens of tamāla, kadamba, yūthikā and tulasī, with two ponds they named **Rādhā-kuṇḍa** and **Śyāma-kuṇḍa**, where they meditated on the pastimes of Rādhā-Śyāma with tears, and they served **Śrī Madana-mohana** every day (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.585).

Hearing of Śrī Caitanya, they longed to meet Him; a voice from the sky told them to be patient, for the deliverer would soon come to their village. When they heard that He had taken sannyāsa and gone to Purī, they fainted with grief, and again a voice consoled them. They wrote to Him several times. In His reply the Lord told them: "As a woman attached to another man makes a show of devotion to her husband, so, while inwardly attached to the feet of Kṛṣṇa, outwardly carry on with your worldly duties; Kṛṣṇa will soon bestow His mercy on you" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.196–197).

## Meeting Mahāprabhu at Rāmakeli

In **1514**, after five years in Purī, Mahāprabhu went to Bengal to see His mother and the Gaṅgā, stayed some days with Advaita Ācārya at Śāntipura and came to **Rāmakeli**, surrounded by enormous crowds. When the Badshah heard of Him he said, "Without giving anything in charity He has so many followers — He must be a great prophet," and ordered that no one obstruct Him. His officer **Keśava Chatrī** tried to belittle the Lord ("a beggar sannyāsī with a few followers"); the Badshah then asked Dabira Khāsa privately, and Rūpa answered: "Why ask me? Ask your own mind. It is this Gosvāmī who has given you your kingdom; it is your good fortune that He has appeared in your country. Go and see Him yourself" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.165–170).

In the dead of night the two brothers went to Him in disguise. They met first **Nityānanda Prabhu** and **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**, who announced them. With straw between their teeth and cloths around their necks they fell before the Lord, weeping, saying they were worse than Jagāi and Mādhāi. The Lord said: "You two brothers are My old servants. From today your names are **Rūpa** and **Sanātana**. Give up this humility — My heart breaks to see it. I have understood your hearts from your letters. I had no business coming to Bengal; I came only to see you two." He placed His hands on their heads; they placed His feet on their heads. At His request they touched the feet of the devotees present — Nityānanda, Haridāsa, Śrīvāsa, Gadādhara, Mukunda, Jagadānanda, Murāri, Vakreśvara — who all rejoiced. Anupama came too, with his family; the Lord placed His hand on the head of little Jīva. Leaving, He blessed them: "Very soon Kṛṣṇa will free you." The brothers begged Him not to go to Vṛndāvana with such a crowd, warning that the Badshah might harm Him; the Lord turned back to Purī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.1.173–215).

## Leaving everything

Back home, the brothers looked for a way out of government service. They engaged brāhmaṇas to perform puraścaraṇa and chant the holy name. Rūpa deposited **ten thousand gold coins with a grocer** in Gauḍa and took the rest of his wealth in **two boats to Bākla Candradvīpa**, where he divided it among the brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas, his relatives, and a reserve for emergencies (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 2.19.2–8). Hearing that Mahāprabhu was about to leave Purī for Vṛndāvana through the forests of Jhārikhaṇḍa, he sent two men to Purī to learn the date (2.19.10–11). When the Lord left, Rūpa left home with his younger brother Anupama and wrote to Sanātana, who had meanwhile been imprisoned by the Badshah, telling him to buy his freedom with the money left with the grocer (2.19.30–35).

## Ten days at Prayāga

Rūpa and Anupama found Mahāprabhu at **Prayāga**, in the house of a Deccan brāhmaṇa, and fell flat before Him. "Stand up, Rūpa! Kṛṣṇa's mercy cannot be described: He has lifted you both out of the well of material enjoyment." To Rūpa's news that Sanātana was in prison, the Lord answered that he was already free and would soon meet Him (2.19.36–56). Here the brothers offered the famous prayer *namo mahā-vadānyāya kṛṣṇa-prema-pradāya te…*: "O most munificent incarnation, You are Kṛṣṇa Himself appearing as Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, with the golden colour of Rādhārāṇī, freely giving pure love of Kṛṣṇa."

**Vallabha Bhaṭṭa** lived at **Āḍāila** (Araila), across the Triveṇī, and invited the Lord; Rūpa and Anupama went with Him. When Vallabha approached them they ran away: "I am untouchable and most sinful; do not touch me." Mahāprabhu, pleased, said, "Don't touch him — he is of very low caste, and you are a Vedic sacrificer of high birth"; hearing the holy name always on their lips, Vallabha understood: "They chant Kṛṣṇa's name constantly — how can they be untouchable? They are most exalted" (2.19.61–82).

To avoid the crowds the Lord went to **Daśāśvamedha-ghāṭa**, where for **ten days** He taught Rūpa the truths of Kṛṣṇa, of bhakti and of rasa, and empowered him by entering his heart (2.19.104–137). "The ocean of the mellows of devotion has no shore; to let you taste it I describe one drop." Among millions of wandering souls, one fortunate soul receives, by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa and the guru, the **seed of the creeper of devotion** (*bhakti-latā-bīja*); watered by hearing and chanting, it grows through the universe to the wish-fulfilling tree of Kṛṣṇa's feet in Goloka and gives the fruit of prema. Offences to Vaiṣṇavas are a mad elephant that uproots the creeper; material desires are weeds. Like sugarcane juice refined into molasses, sugar and rock-candy, love of Kṛṣṇa matures through its stages; it is tasted in five relationships — śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya and mādhurya — and in Goloka is free from awe and reverence. Rūpa later systematised this in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* and the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, and was called **Rasācārya**. The Lord then sent him to Vṛndāvana and went on to Vārāṇasī (one account says the brothers went with Him as far as Benares), telling him he could later come to Purī through Bengal (2.19.240–241).

## First stay in Vṛndāvana

Rūpa reached **Mathurā** and met **Subuddhi Rāya** at **Dhruva-ghāṭa**; with him he visited the twelve forests of Vṛndāvana (2.25.186–207). He had darśana of **Śrī Gopāla** (2.18.47–54). After a month he went back along the Gaṅgā to Prayāga, and with Anupama to **Kāśī**, where he stayed ten days with the devotees, and then left for Gauḍa (2.25.209–221). In Vṛndāvana he had begun a drama, writing its opening verses, and on the way he planned its action.

## Anupama's death; Satyabhāmā's order

On reaching Bengal (Gauḍa), **Anupama passed away**; Rūpa performed the funeral rites and went on towards Nīlācala (3.1.34–38). He had planned one drama joining Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Vraja and in Dvārakā, to soften the pain of Vraja's separation. Resting one night at **Satyabhāmā-pura** in Orissa, he dreamt of a celestially beautiful woman who told him: "Write a separate drama about me; by my mercy it will be extraordinarily beautiful." He understood it was **Satyabhāmā**, Kṛṣṇa's queen (3.1.40–44).

## With Mahāprabhu in Purī

In **Jagannātha Purī** he went to the hut of **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**, who said the Lord had told him Rūpa would come. Mahāprabhu came, embraced him, asked about Sanātana, and heard of Anupama's death. He introduced Rūpa to all the devotees, asked them to bless him, visited him every day and brought him and Haridāsa prasāda from the temple; Rūpa joined the Lord's daily iṣṭa-goṣṭhī and the cleaning of the Guṇḍicā temple (3.1.45–64). One day the Lord said: "Do not take Kṛṣṇa out of Vṛndāvana; He never goes anywhere else" (*kṛṣṇo 'nyo yadu-sambhūto… vṛndāvanaṁ parityajya sa kvacin naiva gacchati*, 3.1.67) — confirming Satyabhāmā's order. So Rūpa wrote two dramas: the *Vidagdha-mādhava* (Vraja) and the *Lalitā-mādhava* (Dvārakā).

At the Ratha-yātrā the Lord sang a verse from mundane poetry, *yaḥ kaumāra-haraḥ…*, whose meaning only Svarūpa Dāmodara knew. Rūpa understood it and wrote a verse of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī at Kurukṣetra, *priyaḥ so 'yaṁ kṛṣṇaḥ…* ("I have met My dear Kṛṣṇa again, yet My mind longs for the forest on the bank of the Yamunā, where His flute sings the fifth note"), on a palm leaf, and tucked it in his thatched roof while he went to bathe in the sea. The Lord found it, was overwhelmed with ecstasy, slapped him lovingly on his return — "How did you know My mind?" — and Svarūpa Dāmodara said only the Lord's mercy could explain it (3.1.72–88; 2.1.76). The Lord asked Svarūpa to teach Rūpa (3.1.89). He relished Rūpa's verse *tuṇḍe tāṇḍavinī…* on the holy name (3.1.97–99). Finding Rūpa writing, He said his handwriting was "like rows of pearls"; Rūpa, very shy, read only when pressed, and **Rāmānanda Rāya** and **Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya** said no one could know the Lord's mind without His mercy — "your drama is a mine of conclusions" (3.1.100–121). Haridāsa praised his fortune (3.1.210). Rūpa played Holi with the Lord (3.1.215–216).

He stayed in Purī four months, until the Dola-yātrā (another account says ten months). Then the Lord empowered him, considered him able to describe the mellows of Vraja, asked the devotees to bless him (3.1.199–206), and sent him away: "Go to Vṛndāvana and stay there; write transcendental books, excavate the lost holy places, establish the service of Kṛṣṇa and preach the mellows of devotion. You may send Sanātana here. I too will go to Vṛndāvana once more." Rūpa placed the Lord's feet on his head and left for Vṛndāvana through Gauḍa (3.1.217–221). In Bengal he was delayed a full year, dividing his remaining wealth among his family, the brāhmaṇas and the temples.

## In Vṛndāvana

Lokanātha Gosvāmī and Bhūgarbha had been sent to Vṛndāvana earlier, but had not won much support.

**Śrī Govinda-deva.** Rūpa knew that Kṛṣṇa's great-grandson **Vajranābha** had installed Deities in Vraja — Harideva, Madana-mohana, Gopīnātha and Govinda-deva — but could not find them. Sitting on the bank of the Yamunā, he lamented that he had failed the Lord's order. A beautiful cowherd boy (a Vrajavāsī) asked why he was sad, led him to a hillock — **Gomā-ṭīlā** (the Yoga-pīṭha; "Gauratila" in one text) — and said: "Govinda-deva lies inside; every day a cow comes and pours her milk on this hill" — then vanished. (One account adds that villagers had buried the Deity there during a Muslim invasion.) The next day Rūpa saw a beautiful surabhi cow pour her milk on the hill. The Vrajavāsīs dug with spades and axes and uncovered **Śrī Govinda-deva**, more beautiful than millions of Cupids. Rūpa fell weeping at His feet; the gopas brought milk, curd, rice and vegetables; the brāhmaṇas performed the abhiṣeka and cooked the offerings; all the Gosvāmīs came; crowds poured in, and it is said the demigods came too in human form. Rūpa sent the news to Purī, and Mahāprabhu sent **Kāśīśvara Paṇḍita** to help him in the service. The great temple of Govinda-deva, seven storeys high, was built with **Emperor Akbar**'s patronage and the red sandstone of **Mahārāja Mān Siṁha** of Amber (said to be Rūpa's disciple, though some say Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa's); an inscription dates it to saṁvat 1647 (1590 AD), "under the direction of Rūpa and Sanātana" (some say 1593). Akbar himself came to visit them. In the time of Aurangzeb the upper storeys were destroyed and the Deity was taken to **Jaipur**, where He is worshipped today; the great hall remains, praised as one of the finest buildings of Hindu India. After Govinda, Rūpa also installed **Śrī Madana-gopāla**.

**Śrī Vṛndā-devī.** After the reappearance of Govinda, Madana-mohana and the Yoga-pīṭha, Rūpa meditated on Vṛndā-devī. She told him in a dream, "I am on the bank of **Brahma-kuṇḍa**." There he found a golden, effulgent goddess and offered prayers: "O Vṛndā-devī, nurse of Govinda and fulfiller of His desires…". It is said he established her Deity (this is disputed): Growse places it first at present Sevā-kuñja, tradition at the site of the Govinda temple; today she is worshipped in the **Madana-mohana temple at Kāmyavana**.

**Śrī Jīva.** His nephew Jīva came from Bengal, received dīkṣā from him and served him. When Jīva defeated **Vallabha Bhaṭṭa**, who had offered to correct the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Rūpa sent him away to learn to control his mind; Sanātana obtained his pardon, and Rūpa nursed him back to health and entrusted him with proof-reading his books — strict and loving at once, an ideal guru (see Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.1643).

**Lalitā-mādhava and Dāna-keli-kaumudī.** Rūpa gave the finished *Lalitā-mādhava*, full of Kṛṣṇa's separation from Vraja, to **Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī**, who wept day and night, ready to give up his life, and would not return the manuscript (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.768). Rūpa wrote the *Dāna-keli-kaumudī*, on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Vraja, exchanged it for the other, and Raghunātha was filled with joy.

**His breath.** Once, as the Gosvāmīs spoke of the separation of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, Rūpa sighed so hotly that a boil rose where his breath touched a devotee's body (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.326).

**Rādhārāṇī's gift.** At **Ṭera-kadamba**, near Nandagrāma, Rūpa wished to cook sweet rice for Govinda and offer it to his elder brother and guru Sanātana, but had nothing. A beautiful young milkmaid came with milk, rice and sugar — "I have brought this to serve you, a saintly person" — refused to sit, and vanished. Tasting the prasāda, Sanātana was overwhelmed and understood that it had been Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī Herself; since they were Her servants, he told Rūpa never again to accept such a gift (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, fifth wave).

**The Vrajavāsīs.** Rūpa and Sanātana loved the people of Vraja as Kṛṣṇa's dear associates. "They knew everything about the villagers — how many sons and daughters they had and their ages, whose marriages were arranged, how many cows and bullocks they owned, how the crops were growing, how their health was" (*Bhakti-ratnākara*). They grieved at their sorrows and laughed at their jokes; villages would not let them go, and if they did not come for many days the Vrajavāsīs went looking for them. Rūpa and Sanātana were the life of the Vrajavāsīs, and the Vrajavāsīs were theirs. With Sanātana he treated **Raghunātha dāsa** like a brother (1.10.93–104); both preached, built temples and installed Deities (1.10.84–90). Until then the land around Mathurā and Vṛndāvana was mostly forest; it was under the Gosvāmīs that every lake and grove of Vraja received its name.

## His teaching

Mahāprabhu's two "generals", Rūpa and Sanātana, made Vraja known and set down His conclusions. Rūpa revealed the inner meaning of Mahāprabhu's mood (the verse *priyaḥ so 'yaṁ kṛṣṇaḥ*), described both meeting and separation (*vipralambha* and *sambhoga*) — "without separation, meeting is not nourished" (*Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*) — and established the purity and supremacy of the gopīs' paramour love (*parakīya-rasa*) for Kṛṣṇa, the topmost mood of mādhurya-prema that Mahāprabhu came to give (*anarpita-carīṁ cirāt*, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.1.4). The *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* leads from vaidhī-bhakti to rāgānugā-bhakti, following the residents of Vraja; the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* describes the stages of love — prema, sneha, māna, praṇaya, rāga, anurāga, bhāva and mahābhāva — and the ingredients of rasa (sthāyi-bhāva, vibhāva, anubhāva, sāttvika- and vyabhicāri-bhāva). His followers, the Rūpānugas, see themselves as maidservants of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī under Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī (*Vraja-vilāsa-stava* 38). Some say that the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam itself, in its rāsa chapters, may be said to follow Rūpa's direction, since Rūpa Mañjarī is the root of all understanding (Śrī Śrīmad Bhaktivedānta Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja).

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who taught and empowered him at Prayāga and in Purī. One text says that Sanātana took dīkṣā from Madhusūdana Vācaspati and **Rūpa took dīkṣā from Sanātana**; Rūpa called Sanātana his guru and elder brother.
- **Disciples:** Śrī **Jīva Gosvāmī** (dīkṣā). Mahārāja **Mān Siṁha** of Amber is said to have become his disciple (others say Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa's).

## Works

He compiled many large works — sixteen are considered the most important, at least 100,000 verses in all. The lists of the sources, put together:

- **Bhakti-rasa:** *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*; *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*.
- **Dramas:** *Vidagdha-mādhava* (Vraja pastimes, seven acts; completed 1532); *Lalitā-mādhava* (Dvārakā pastimes, ten acts; completed in 1459 Śaka, given as 1546 AD); *Dāna-keli-kaumudī* (a bhāṇikā).
- **Poems and prayers:** *Haṁsadūta*; *Uddhava-sandeśa*; *Stava-mālā* (with *Yamunāṣṭaka*, *Kārpaṇya-pañjikā-stotra*, *Utkalikā-vallarī*, *Prathama Kuñja-vihāry-aṣṭaka*, *Lalitāṣṭaka* and others); *Nikuñja-rahasya-stava*; *Govinda-virudāvalī*; *Padyāvalī* (anthology).
- **Other works:** *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta* (*Saṅkṣepa-bhāgavatāmṛta*); *Rādhā-kṛṣṇa-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (Bṛhat and Laghu); *Mathurā-māhātmya*; *Kṛṣṇa-janma-tithi-vidhi*; *Upadeśāmṛta*; *Nāṭaka-candrikā*; *Prayuktākhyāta-mañjarī* (or *-candrikā*); *Sāmānya-virudāvalī-lakṣaṇa*.

From Mahāprabhu's own dramatic performances at the house of Candraśekhara Ācārya came the inner guidelines of his dramas.
