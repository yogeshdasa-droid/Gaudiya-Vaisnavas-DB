## In short

Śrīla Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī — "Dāsa Gosvāmī" — one of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana and the only one not born a brāhmaṇa (he was a kāyastha), was the only son of the immensely rich Majumdār family of Saptagrāma. From boyhood he wanted only Śrī Caitanya; he left a beautiful wife and a kingdom-like fortune, received the mercy of Nityānanda Prabhu at Pānihāṭi, and was placed by Mahāprabhu in the care of **Svarūpa Dāmodara**, whose assistant he was for sixteen years, a witness of the Lord's last pastimes in the Gambhīrā. After the disappearance of Mahāprabhu and Svarūpa he went to Vṛndāvana to give up his life, but Rūpa and Sanātana kept him as their third brother; he lived at **Rādhā-kuṇḍa**, which he restored, in extreme renunciation, telling every day the pastimes of Mahāprabhu. He is the **prayojana-ācārya**, whose Deity, Gopīnātha, represents the goal; his works are the *Stavāvalī*, the *Dāna-keli-cintāmaṇi* and the *Muktā-carita*. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Rasa Mañjarī** (others say Rati Mañjarī or Bhānumatī) (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 186).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** 1428 Śaka, according to one text of the record (1428 Śaka is about 1506 AD).
- **Disappearance:** 1504 Śaka (about 1582 AD), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of Āśvina (Āśvina Śukla Dvādaśī), at Rādhā-kuṇḍa.
- *Other dates given:* another text gives 1495–1571 AD (which would make him about fifteen at Mahāprabhu's sannyāsa in 1510, as it says); the *Abhidhāna* gives an approximate year that cannot be read clearly in the copy (perhaps 1496).

## Family

He was born in the village of **Śrī Kṛṣṇapura** (Chāndpur), two miles from **Saptagrāma** in the Hooghly district. His father was **Govardhana Majumdār** and his uncle **Hiraṇya Majumdār**, Govardhana's elder brother: kāyastha landholders ("Majumdār" was a title given by the king), lessees of the Saptagrāma district under Hussain Shah. They paid the Nawab 1,200,000 rupees a year — some say as much as their own income, to be left free to worship Kṛṣṇa — and with taxes on trade came to earn 2,000,000; the Nawab left the internal government of the district to them. They were famous for their piety and charity; the *Abhidhāna* says they were "not pure Vaiṣṇavas, but almost Vaiṣṇavas". Mahāprabhu called them His "uncles", perhaps because of their friendship with His maternal grandfather Nīlāmbara Cakravartī. Raghunātha was the only son of the house, brought up like a prince.

## Childhood

He studied at the house of the family priest **Balarāma Ācārya** (Balarāma dāsa), one of the most advanced devotees of Saptagrāma, who was dear to **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**; when Haridāsa visited, the boy heard his discourses and received his devotion. Even very young he felt that material life was temporary, and lost his attachment to wealth, parents and relatives; hearing of Gaurāṅga and Nityānanda, he longed to see Them. "Raghunātha dāsa was totally uninterested in material enjoyment even from his childhood" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*).

## Śāntipura: meeting the Lord

When news came (1510) that Śrī Caitanya had taken sannyāsa and that Nityānanda had brought Him to Śāntipura, the fifteen-year-old Raghunātha begged his father to let him go, then ran to **Advaita Ācārya's house at Śāntipura**. He fell at the Lord's feet; the Lord recognised His eternal associate, embraced him and touched him with His foot. "I will go with You!" — "Not now. When I return from Vṛndāvana, come to Nīlācala on some pretext." After ten days the Lord went to Purī and Raghunātha went home.

Four years later, when the Lord came again to Śāntipura (after His tour of South India and His meeting with Rūpa and Sanātana at Rāmakeli), Govardhana let his son go with a princely escort. The Lord smiled on him like an old friend but sent him back after about six days: "Don't make a show of renunciation. Kṛṣṇa will free you in due time; for now live like a worldly man, inwardly fixed." Raghunātha obeyed, managed his father's estate, and married a wife of heavenly beauty (*Abhidhāna*: "like an Apsarā").

## Arrest by the Nawab

After about two years, the former Turkish governor of Saptagrāma, whose possessions the brothers had taken over, told the Nawab that they kept back far more than they paid and were becoming dangerous. Soldiers were sent; Govardhana was away and Hiraṇya fled; only Raghunātha remained, unconcerned, and was taken to the capital (the Nawab's minister at Saptagrāma, in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*). Questioned and threatened, he told the truth — he did not know where they were — and spoke so beautifully of kindness and of brotherhood under God that the official was moved to tears, released him, called him his son, and the matter of the money was settled (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.6).

## Pānihāṭi: the Daṇḍa-mahotsava

Hearing that **Nityānanda Prabhu** was at **Pānihāṭi**, on the Gaṅgā near Saptagrāma, preaching with hundreds of devotees, Raghunātha — knowing that Mahāprabhu's mercy comes through Nityānanda — got his father's leave and went. He found Nityānanda on a large stone under a banyan tree, effulgent among the devotees, and, ashamed to come as a worldly man, kept at a distance. "You are a thief, Raghunātha! Come here and I'll punish you" — and Nityānanda caught him and placed His feet on his head, while all laughed. The "punishment": "Make a festival and feed all the devotees chipped rice with yogurt." Raghunātha, delighted at last to use his wealth for the Lord, had chipped rice, yogurt, milk, sweets, bananas and more brought from all the villages, with four hundred great earthen pots; brāhmaṇas, villagers and pilgrims came in crowds. The Lord's associates sat on raised platforms, the others around them; each received two pots — chipped rice with condensed milk and chipped rice with yogurt. By Nityānanda's will **Śrī Caitanya Himself appeared**, seen only by the advanced devotees; Nityānanda took a morsel from each plate and put it in Mahāprabhu's mouth, and Mahāprabhu did the same to Him. Afterwards Raghunātha gave gold and jewels to the devotees. This is the **Daṇḍa-mahotsava**, the "festival of punishment", still celebrated every year at Pānihāṭi on the thirteenth day of the bright fortnight of Jyaiṣṭha.

In private Raghunātha said: "I am the lowest of men; like a dwarf trying to catch the moon I have tried again and again to reach Mahāprabhu's feet, and my parents have always held me back. Without Your mercy no one attains Him — place Your feet on my head and bless me to reach Him without difficulty." Nityānanda, pleased, told the devotees: "His happiness equals Indra's, yet by Mahāprabhu's mercy he does not like it at all — bless him to reach the Lord's feet soon." He told Raghunātha that Mahāprabhu had come to the festival only for him and had eaten his chipped rice, and foretold: "The Lord will give you to Svarūpa Dāmodara and make you His confidential servant. Go home; very soon you will reach Him" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.6).

## Flight to Purī

Back home he could no longer pretend. Again and again he ran away and was brought back by the guards his father hired. When his mother suggested tying him to a pillar, Govardhana answered: "If great wealth and a wife as beautiful as an Apsarā cannot bind him, can a rope?" At last the family guru and priest, **Yadunandana Ācārya**, took him along to a ritual, and the guards slept; Raghunātha persuaded the Ācārya to let him finish it alone, and fled that night. Avoiding the road, through the jungle, he walked thirty miles the first day and slept in a cowshed. Govardhana guessed he had gone to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā and sent ten men with a letter to **Śivānanda Sena**, leader of the Bengal pilgrims — but Raghunātha was not with him. In **twelve days** he reached Purī, having eaten only three times on the way.

## Sixteen years with Mahāprabhu and Svarūpa Dāmodara

He found Mahāprabhu sitting with Svarūpa Dāmodara and the devotees and clasped His feet; the Lord embraced him and said: "Svarūpa, I give this Raghunātha to you — take him as your son or servant. From today he is **the Raghu of Svarūpa**." So he became the assistant of the Lord's secretary. For five days he ate the Lord's remnants; then, feeling them too rich, he stood begging at the **Siṁha-dvāra** of the Jagannātha temple. The Lord praised his renunciation. For a time, feeling himself too fallen, he spoke to the Lord only through Govinda or Svarūpa. When at last he asked for instruction, the Lord said: "Svarūpa can teach you better than I. But hear this in short: do not talk like worldly people or hear what they say; do not eat palatable food or dress finely; expect no honour but honour others; always chant the name of Kṛṣṇa, and in your mind serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana. Svarūpa will tell you the details" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.6).

Through Śivānanda Sena he sent word to console his parents, who then sent four hundred coins, two servants and a brāhmaṇa cook; at first he would not touch them, then used them only to invite the Lord twice a month; after two years he stopped. The Lord and Svarūpa understood his mind: food from worldly people dulls the mind, and the Lord had accepted it only out of mercy. Pleased, Mahāprabhu gave him His own **Govardhana-śilā** and **guñjā-mālā** from Vṛndāvana, which He had kept with Him for three years — a sign to all that Raghunātha was no ordinary soul (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.6.266–325). When he gave up begging at the temple too, he lived on rice thrown away by the shopkeepers, washed it, and ate it with salt; the Lord came, took some by force and praised its taste.

"He spent seven and a half praharas (more than twenty-two hours) a day in chanting and remembrance, and slept four daṇḍas (an hour and a half) — and some days not even that" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*). His austerity made him famous; people came from far to see him, and he bowed down to them all. He stayed in Purī **sixteen years** in the Lord's intimate service, with Svarūpa Dāmodara witnessing His last pastimes.

## Vṛndāvana and Rādhā-kuṇḍa

When Mahāprabhu disappeared (1534), the world went dark for him; he stayed in Purī until the disappearance of Svarūpa Dāmodara, then — almost forty years old — went to Vṛndāvana, as the Lord had asked, with one wish: to see the feet of Rūpa and Sanātana and then **throw himself from Govardhana Hill**. "The two brothers would not let him die, and kept him with them as their third brother. They heard from him constantly all the outer and inner pastimes of Mahāprabhu" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.10.91–102). There were already in Vraja Sanātana, Rūpa, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa, Lokanātha, Kāśīśvara and Bhūgarbha; Vallabhācārya also came there at that time.

**His life at Rādhā-kuṇḍa** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 1.10.91–102): he gave up grain and water and took only two or three palas of buttermilk a day; offered a thousand daṇḍavats to the Lord and chanted a hundred thousand names daily; bowed down to two thousand Vaiṣṇavas; served Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in his mind day and night; for one prahara (three hours) told the pastimes of Mahāprabhu; bathed three times a day in Rādhā-kuṇḍa; embraced the Vaiṣṇavas of Vraja; spent seven and a half praharas in devotion and slept four daṇḍas, some days not at all. Sages, scholars and seekers came from all over India to hear him. He had no fixed home, staying by Rādhā-kuṇḍa or by **Mānasa-gaṅgā**, then a jungle full of tigers.

- **The tigers and the hut.** Once, as he sat absorbed by the kuṇḍa, a tiger (a tiger and a tigress, in another telling) came to drink at Pāvana-ghāṭa (Śyāma-kuṇḍa) nearby; Sanātana, coming to bathe — he took his midday meal at Gopāla Bhaṭṭa's hut by Mānasa-gaṅgā — saw it (and, in one account, saw Kṛṣṇa Himself standing guard) and begged him to do his bhajana in a hut. Another account says Sanātana once saw Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī standing behind him in the noon sun, shading him with Her veil and drenched in perspiration, and rebuked him for troubling Her; from then on Raghunātha lived in a small hut — the beginning, it is said, of the kuṭīra tradition of Vraja.
- **Restoring the kuṇḍas.** Mahāprabhu had revealed Rādhā-kuṇḍa when, at **Āriṭ-grāma**, He bathed in a paddy field and recited the hymn to the kuṇḍa, and the villagers understood it was Rādhā-kuṇḍa (*Abhidhāna*). Raghunātha thought, "If money came, the kuṇḍas could be restored" — and at once rebuked his mind for such thoughts. Meanwhile a rich merchant who wished to offer great wealth at **Badarīnārāyaṇa** was told by the Lord in a dream to take it to Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī at Āriṭ-grāma near Mathurā, and, if he refused, to say that Nārāyaṇa had sent him and remind him of Rādhā-kuṇḍa. With that money Raghunātha had **Rādhā-kuṇḍa and Śyāma-kuṇḍa** cleared of mud and restored, and the groves of the eight sakhīs planted around them. When some proposed cutting five trees on the bank, the **five Pāṇḍavas**, who were those trees, appeared to him at night and forbade it; the trees still stand.
- **The tongue of Govardhana.** Wishing to spare the kuṇḍa from the washing of clothes and pots, he had a well dug; the diggers struck something that bled. Fearing an offence he stopped the work, and that night learned they had struck **the tongue of Govardhana**; he built a shrine there, still worshipped.
- **Only Rādhā's.** Considering himself a maidservant in Śrī Rādhā's group, he never went to Candrāvalī's groves or spoke with her friends. **Śrīdāsa Vrajavāsī** brought him a leaf-cup of buttermilk every day; one day the cup was made of large palāśa leaves from Candrāvalī's grove — Raghunātha threw it down: a follower of Rādhā takes nothing from there.
- **The sweet rice.** In meditation he cooked sweet rice for Rādhā, Kṛṣṇa and the gopīs and, honouring Their remnants with intense love, ate a little more than usual. The next day his door stayed shut until afternoon; he said he was unwell. Word was sent to Sanātana, then at the house of Vallabhācārya in Mathurā, and Vallabhācārya's son **Viṭṭhalanātha** sent two physicians, who declared the illness came from eating too much sweet rice — and the devotees understood.
- **Lalitā-mādhava.** When Rūpa gave him the *Lalitā-mādhava*, full of separation, he was plunged into an ocean of viraha, mad and fainting — even a moment's separation, though he lived by the kuṇḍa in Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī's constant presence, was unbearable to him. Rūpa wrote the joyful *Dāna-keli-kaumudī* and took back the other "to correct it"; and Raghunātha, turning to that mood, wrote his own *Dāna-keli-cintāmaṇi* and *Muktā-carita* (*Abhidhāna*).
- **Jāhnavā Mātā.** When Śrī Jāhnavā devī came to Vraja, Raghunātha said to her: "Born in a worldly house, I am full of shame and fear — by what quality will Caitanya's feet give me shelter? Not one day did I serve those feet, yet I beg for them — such a wretch am I." Hearing this, Mother Jāhnavā held his hand and wept (*Prema-vilāsa* 16, p. 127).

His **samādhi** is at Rādhā-kuṇḍa. After his disappearance the **Govardhana-śilā** given him by Mahāprabhu was worshipped at the **Gokulānanda temple** in Vṛndāvana; on the amāvasyā of 15 Vaiśākha 1356 (Bengali era, 1949) its sevāyat, Śrī Vinodī Lāla Gosvāmī, entrusted the service to Śrī Kṛpāsindhu dāsa Bābājī at the Bhāgavata-nivāsa in Vana-vihāra, and the service continues at Gokulānanda (*Abhidhāna*).

## His position

Raghunātha dāsa is the **prayojana-ācārya**, the teacher of the goal, whose Deity Gopīnātha (Gopījana-vallabha) represents the final stage; he takes the feet of Śrī Rūpa as his highest aspiration (see Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī). He is remembered above all as the very emblem of renunciation and of service to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī. Of Sanātana's mercy he wrote: "I was unwilling to drink the nectar of devotion with renunciation, but Sanātana, an ocean of mercy, made me drink it" (*Vilāpa-kusumāñjali* 6).

## Guru and disciples

- **Dīkṣā guru:** **Yadunandana Ācārya**, the family priest (*Abhidhāna*). (The record formerly named Rūpa Gosvāmī as his guru.)
- **Śikṣā:** Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who gave him to **Svarūpa Dāmodara** as his guide; then **Rūpa** and **Sanātana**, who kept him as their brother (Rūpa's feet were his aspiration). His childhood teacher was Balarāma Ācārya, and Haridāsa Ṭhākura gave him his first mercy; Nityānanda Prabhu blessed him at Pānihāṭi.
- **Disciple:** **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī**, who heard from him and wrote the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*.

## Works

- *Stavāvalī* (*Stava-mālā*), his collected prayers, including the *Vilāpa-kusumāñjali*, *Rādhā-kuṇḍāṣṭaka*, *Nāmāṣṭaka*, *Manaḥ-śikṣā* and *Śrī Govardhana-vāsa-prārthanā-daśaka*.
- *Dāna-keli-cintāmaṇi* (*Dāna-carita*), on the toll pastime.
- *Muktā-carita*, "the story of the pearls" (a translation is among this record's texts).
- Many songs (padas).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Rasa Mañjarī**; others say Rati Mañjarī or Bhānumatī (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 186).
