## In short

**Acyutānanda dāsa** of Orissa, a poet of the sixteenth century, is one of the **Pañcasakhās**, the five Oriyā companions of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu at Purī, and wrote a vast number of books on Vaiṣṇava philosophy and religion (*Bhakti Movement in Orissa*, pp. 73-75). In his *Śūnya-saṁhitā* he is said to have been **Sudāmā** in Kṛṣṇa-līlā.

## Birth

He was born in **1482 AD** (*Udaya-kāhānī*) at **Tilkaṇā**, a village a mile from the famous Nembālā (Lembālo), on the bank of the Citropalā, a branch of the Mahānadī, in the Cuttack district. His father was **Dīnabandhu Khuṇṭiyā** and his mother **Padmāvatī**. From boyhood he cared little for his studies, and grew more and more indifferent to the world.

## His meeting with the Lord

Once Dīnabandhu went with his wife and son to Purī to see Lord Jagannātha; Lord Caitanya was there, and when Acyuta touched His feet he felt a surge of spiritual energy. The books tell of his initiation differently:
- the *Śūnya-saṁhitā*: the Lord told Sanātana Gosvāmī of Doligrāma (not the Gosvāmī of Vṛndāvana) to initiate him in the name of Hari;
- the *Guru-bhakti-gītā*: the Lord, disguised, came to rest under a banyan tree where Acyuta was playing, asked to see his parents, told Dīnabandhu the boy should receive Harināma, and Himself initiated him on the ekādaśī of the bright fortnight of Vaiśākha; then, at Dīnabandhu's request, He showed His four-armed form and told Acyuta they would meet again at Purī;
- the Oriyā *Caitanya-bhāgavata* of Īśvara dāsa: Kṛṣṇa told Acyuta to take dīkṣā from Lord Caitanya — "accept Him as preceptor and spread the name of Hari" — and he went to Purī and served Him.

Whoever his guru was, the Lord was the turning point of his life. The Lord gave him cymbals and a khola and taught him tuning, timing and rhythm (*Śūnya-saṁhitā*). By His order Acyuta went on a long pilgrimage, formed a saṅkīrtana (rahasa) party and preached Kṛṣṇa's name in many provinces (the unpublished *Varaṇa-carita*).

## The Pañcasakhās

In the *Śūnya-saṁhitā* (ch. 10) Kṛṣṇa tells Sudāmā: "We shall meet again in Kali-yuga. I shall appear as Buddha, and you will be Sundarānanda; later I shall come in Navadvīpa as Śrī Caitanya and preach the name of Hari from door to door, and you will be with Me as Acyuta." To Sudāmā, Subala and the others Kṛṣṇa said they would be born as the five sakhās at Nīlācala, and King Pratāparudra would serve them. Once, when the King wished to hold a festival at Cuttack, the Lord asked him to bring the Pañcasakhās and embraced them all; in the Mukti-maṇḍapa, with the King and Sārvabhauma, He declared that the five were Kṛṣṇa's devotees in every age, and they answered that they were but five branches of one tree. To show their powers the Lord gave a jackfruit seed to Jagannātha dāsa, and Acyutānanda ripened the fruit (*Caurāsi Ājñā*).

## Works

When the Lord asked him about his writings he said he had written many songs and bhajanas, and the Lord told him to write books on devotional service (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* of Īśvara dāsa; *Guru-bhakti-gītā*). In the *Anākāra-saṁhitā* he says he wrote a lakh of padas and padāvalīs; Dr A. B. Mahanty says he has seen 3,000 manuscripts. His best-known works are the *Śūnya-saṁhitā*, *Śabda-brahmāṇḍa-saṁhitā*, *Anākāra-saṁhitā*, *Mahā-nitya-rahasa*, *Nitya-rahasa*, *Śūnya-rahasa*, *Vṛnda-rasa* and *Rādhā-rasa*; he is still remembered for his *Bhaviṣya-mālikās* (prophecies).
