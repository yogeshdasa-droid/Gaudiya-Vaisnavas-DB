## In short

**Rūpacandra Sarasvatī**, a famous scholar of Assam, conquered the learned men of India in debate, was defeated by Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī in Vṛndāvana, and at last became a disciple of **Narottama Ṭhākura** at Kheturī, who named him **Rūpanārāyaṇa** (*Prema-vilāsa*).

## The scholar

He was born about **1423-1427 Śaka** in the family of **Lakṣmīnātha** and **Kamalā Lāhiḍī** of **Viṭādiyā** (Bhiṭādiyā), near the market town of **Egārasindura** in Kāmarūpa (Assam). As a child he was restless and would not study; one day his father gave him ashes to eat instead of rice, and he left home. He went to Paṇḍitavāḍī and studied in a teacher's house; soon he was a scholar and his teacher gave him the title **Cakravartī**. At Navadvīpa he received the title **Ācārya**, and studying at all the great centres of learning he earned the titles **Sarasvatī** and **Mahā-śrutidhara** ("who remembers everything"). Then he set out to conquer the provinces of India with his learning.

## Purī and Vṛndāvana

At Purī he met Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, but, mad with pride in his learning, he could not recognise the Lord. He went to Vṛndāvana to defeat Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana; the two brothers, showing the best Vaiṣṇava humility, accepted defeat without debate. Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī, angry that his uncles had been "defeated", challenged him, and after seven days of debate defeated him — for which Śrī Rūpa severely chastised Jīva. Later Rūpacandra understood the nobility of the Gosvāmīs and submitted to them.

## At Kheturī

He became the court paṇḍita of King **Narasiṁha** of Pakkapallī. When the King heard that Narottama Ṭhākura, a śūdra by birth, was initiating brāhmaṇas, he was furious and sent Rūpacandra and other paṇḍitas to Kheturī to defeat him. Rūpacandra had already heard of Narottama's glories but did not say so; he pretended to be angry too and urged the paṇḍitas on. They were defeated by Narottama's disciples before they even met him. Then Rūpacandra advised the paṇḍitas to take initiation from Narottama; he himself was initiated, and so were the King and the Queen. Narottama named him **Rūpanārāyaṇa**. It is said that he brought Deities of **Śrī Rādhā-Vrajamohana** from Vraja and installed them at Egārasindura.
