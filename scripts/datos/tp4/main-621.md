## In short

Śrī **Jñānadāsa**, one of the most famous Vaiṣṇava padakartās (poets), was a disciple of **Jāhnavā Mātā**, the wife of Nityānanda Prabhu, and is counted in Nityānanda's branch: "Śaṅkara, Mukunda, Jñānadāsa, Manohara…" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.52). He was also a renowned musician, and was called **Maṅgala Ṭhākura** (and, for his beauty, **Madana-maṅgala**).

## Birth

He was born about **1453 Śaka** (1531 AD) (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna; the Digdarśanī: 1452 Śaka, 1530 AD), in a Rāḍhīya brāhmaṇa family of **Kāndaḍā** (Kāndrā), near Katwa in the Bardhamān district. The *Bhakti-ratnākara* puts his village Kāndaḍā about four miles from Ekacakrā in Birbhum; the *Viśvakoṣa* (vol. VII, p. 249) says his birth year is not known, but as a contemporary of Vṛndāvana dāsa he lived about four hundred years ago.

## Life

After his dīkṣā from Jāhnavā Devī he was wholly absorbed in kṛṣṇa-prema, as his padas show. Once he went back to his village and spread there the **bhuvana-maṅgala harināma**, the holy name that blesses the world; so he became known as Maṅgala Ṭhākura. Inclined to renunciation from his youth, he never married; **Bābā Āula Manohara dāsa** was his lifelong companion (Abhidhāna). Even so, some families claim to descend from Maṅgala Ṭhākura, and some descendants of his cousin at **Kutulpur** in the Bankura district (a few Gosvāmī houses) call themselves of his family.

His **maṭha** is at Kāndaḍā, and every year on the **Pauṣa pūrṇimā** his festival is held there, with a fair of three days.

## Songs

He wrote padas in Bengali and in Vrajabuli, after the style and prosody of Vidyāpati and Caṇḍīdāsa, lucid and full of grace — ornaments of the padāvalī literature: on pūrva-rāga, sakhī-śikṣā, milana, naukā-khaṇḍa, muralī-śikṣā, goṣṭha-vihāra, māna, māthura and praśna-dūtikā.
