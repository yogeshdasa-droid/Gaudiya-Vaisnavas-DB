## In short

**Jagāi and Mādhāi**, the two drunken brāhmaṇa brothers of Navadvīpa, are the famous proof of the mercy of Śrī Gaura and Nityānanda: "The greatest objects of the Lord's mercy were Jagāi and Mādhāi, the two brothers who are witnesses to His name Patita-pāvana, deliverer of the fallen" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.120). Jagāi's real name was **Jagannātha** (some texts give Jagadānanda and Mādhavānanda); in their previous life they were **Jaya and Vijaya**, the door-keepers of Vaikuṇṭha (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 145).

## Family

They were kulīna brāhmaṇas. Their grandfather **Śubhānanda Rāya** was a zamindar of Navadvīpa; Jagāi was the son of his elder son **Raghunātha Rāya**, Mādhāi of the younger, **Janārdana Rāya** — so the two were cousins (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna). They had a house in Navadvīpa and were its kotwāls (police officers) under the Muslim ruler. Gaṅgādāsa told the Lord: "They are the sons of a virtuous brāhmaṇa; they were once upright, but now there is no sin they will not commit": drinking wine, eating meat, stealing and robbing, and taking other men's wives.

## "Then they have already been saved"

Nityānanda and Haridāsa, going from house to house to ask everyone to chant, met them lying drunk by the road and asked them to say "Hari"; the two chased them, and they escaped only because the drunkards fell after a few steps. That evening Nityānanda told the Lord: "As long as they are in Navadvīpa I will not go elsewhere. It is no great thing to make good people chant Kṛṣṇa's name; if these two say it, then I shall know Your glory." — "When they had Your darśana, were they not delivered?" said the Lord. Haridāsa complained to Advaita about his wild companion, and Advaita joked: "That avadhūta is a drunkard too; in a few days he will bring those two drunkards here and dance with them" — and the devotees rejoiced to hear Nityānanda praised in this way.

## The broken pot

One night the brothers lay near the Lord's house and danced to the kīrtana inside. Another evening Nityānanda went up to them. "Who is that?" — "I am the Avadhūta." At that word Mādhāi flew into a rage and struck Him on the head with a piece of a broken clay pot; the blood flowed, but Nityānanda only repeated "Haribol!". When Mādhāi raised his arm again, Jagāi held it back: "What is the use of beating a wandering sannyāsī?" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 13.178).

The Lord ran there with His associates and, trembling with anger, called "Sudarśana!", and the cakra appeared. But Nityānanda folded His hands: "In this incarnation the sinners are not to be killed with weapons; I pray for these two." The Lord told Jagāi, "Because you stopped him, Kṛṣṇa has shown you His mercy; may you love Him," showed him His four-armed form, and Jagāi took His feet on his chest. To Mādhāi He said, "I have no mercy for you: you shed the blood of Nityānanda, who is dearer to Me than My own body. Only if He forgives you can you have My mercy." Mādhāi fell at Nityānanda's feet, and Nityānanda raised him: "All your sins have gone far away; I have taken full responsibility for you. Do not sin any more." The two Lords embraced them: "From today you are Our devotees; whoever feeds you will be feeding Us, and We will sometimes appear in your bodies" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 13.228).

## Devotees

From that day the brothers were counted among the devotees of Gaura and Nityānanda. They sat at a ghāṭa on the Ganges, constantly chanting, and begged forgiveness at the feet of every Vaiṣṇava who came there; Mādhāi was so strict in his practice that the devotees called him **Mādhāi Brahmacārī**. Mādhāi's samādhi-mandira is at Mādhāi-tolā, **Ākāihāṭ**, a rickshaw ride from Gaurāṅga-bāḍī in Katwa, looked after by the followers of Rāmadāsa Bābājī.
