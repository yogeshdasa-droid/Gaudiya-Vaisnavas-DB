## In short

**Balarāma dāsa** of Orissa, called **Matta Balarāma**, was the eldest of the **Pañcasakhās**, the five Oriyā companions of Śrī Caitanya, a great devotee of Lord Jagannātha and Lord Balarāma, and the author of the Oriyā **Jagamohana Rāmāyaṇa**. He lived at Purī. In his *Jagamohana Rāmāyaṇa* he names his father, **Somanātha Mahāpātra**, and his mother, **Mānamāyā**. The Vaiṣṇava-vandanā names him with great reverence.

## The Lord gives him a mantra

The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* of Īśvara dāsa tells his first meeting with the Lord at his own village, **Candrapur** near Jājpur, where the Lord stayed on His way to Purī: Balarāma fell at His feet, the Lord raised him, embraced him, called him **Subala**, and gave the **Rāma-tāraka-brahma** mantra into his ear. Dibākara dāsa, author of the *Jagannātha-caritāmṛta*, calls him a disciple of **Hṛdayānanda**, of the line of Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita. At the Lord's request Balarāma initiated **Jagannātha dāsa**, and then left to travel on pilgrimage all over India.

## "Matta"

He always went with the saṅkīrtana party (*Śūnya-saṁhitā*; Īśvara dāsa). Once an elephant of Gajapati Pratāparudra went mad and killed its keeper; Balarāma caught hold of it and chanted the holy name in its ear, and the elephant became quiet and bowed at the Lord's feet. Astonished, the Lord embraced him and called him **Matta**, "intoxicated"; from then on he was known as Matta Balarāma (also in Dibākara dāsa's *Jagannātha-caritāmṛta*).

## Paramaguru

The brāhmaṇas of Purī once insulted him as an ignorant, low-born man. Balarāma brought before the assembly a deaf and dumb man named Haridāsa and touched his head, and Haridāsa began to explain the Vedānta. All were amazed, King Pratāparudra too, and from that day the King honoured him as **Paramaguru**, as being in some ways even greater than Lord Caitanya, who was Balarāma's own guru. With the Lord and the King he was at the Mukti-maṇḍapa when the jackfruit was ripened (*Caurāsi Ājñā*); there he called himself Subala of Dvāpara-yuga and a śūdra in Kali-yuga, and said that Kṛṣṇa had told him in a dream that He would appear in Navadvīpa as Caitanya and ordered him to serve Him and His followers.

## Works

When the Lord asked the Pañcasakhās about their writings, Balarāma said he had written the Rāmāyaṇa in seven kāṇḍas, a lakh of padas, the Bhagavad-gītā in eighteen chapters, and the *Vedānta-sāra-gupta-gītā* "in the guise of Brahma-vidyā" (Īśvara dāsa). He also wrote a *Brahma-purāṇa*, *Kānta Koili*, *Gupta-gītā*, *Bāṭa Abakāśa*, *Bhāva-samudra*, *Amarakoṣa-gītā*, *Brahmāṇḍa-bhūgola*, *Saptāṅga-yoga-sāra-gītā*, a *Bhāgavata-ṭīkā*, *Beda-parikramā*, *Arjuna-gītā* and others. The *Vedānta-sāra-gupta-gītā* says he had divine powers.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

The *Guru-bhakti-gītā* of Acyutānanda dāsa calls him Rādhā's companion **Samādirā**; an Oriyā *Caitanya-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (manuscript in the Orissa State Museum, L 470 B) calls him the sakhī **Sumandirā**; Īśvara dāsa and the *Caurāsi Ājñā*, Subala.
