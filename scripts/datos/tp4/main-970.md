## In short

Śrī **Nandana Ācārya** of **Navadvīpa** is a branch of Lord Caitanya, famous because the two Lords once hid in his house: "The branch Nandana Ācārya is known all over the world: the two Lords stayed hidden in his house" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.39). When Śrī Nityānanda first came to Navadvīpa He stayed in Nandana's house, and there Mahāprabhu found Him. Nandana was **lame** (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Hariṇī** (Abhidhāna; *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*).

## Family

He was born in a family of astrologer-brāhmaṇas (graha-vipra). His father, **Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa Sarvajña**, an unrivalled astrologer, was present at the Lord's birth and cast His horoscope (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*; *Śrī Viṣṇupriyā-Gaurāṅga* 3.10); his brother was **Bhagavān Adhikārī Sārvabhauma**. The family lived for some time at Bahirkhaṇḍa near Tārakeśvar and then at Śrīhaṭiyā or Dakṣiṇapāḍā in Navadvīpa (Abhidhāna).

## Nityānanda comes to Nadīyā

Some days before, Viśvambhara had said, "O My brothers, within two or three days a great personality will come here." One morning He told the devotees a dream: a chariot with a palm-tree flag stopped at His door, and on it sat a powerful avadhūta with a plough on His shoulder, a staff with a waterpot in His hand and blue cloth on His head, who asked ten or twenty times, "Is this the house of Nimāi Paṇḍita?" — "I am Your brother; we will meet tomorrow." Telling it, the Lord was absorbed in the mood of Haladhara and roared, "Bring Me wine!", until Śrīvāsa calmed Him. Then He sent **Haridāsa** and **Śrīvāsa** to search for the newcomer; they looked through all Navadvīpa for nine hours and found no one. The Lord laughed: "Come with Me, and I will show you where He is," and with the devotees chanting "Jaya Kṛṣṇa!" He went straight to the house of **Nandana Ācārya**. There sat Nityānanda, shining like ten million suns, absorbed in meditation and smiling; knowing that His Lord had come, He bowed down, and Viśvambhara stood before Him, recognising the Lord of His life (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 3). Nandana Ācārya, delighted to have such a guest, kept Him in his house and served Him as best he could. When Śrīnivāsa Ācārya visited Navadvīpa he was shown the house: "Here the Lord came with His devotees and saw Nityānanda seated in meditation" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 12.2422-2423).

## The house where the Lords hid

On the day of the Mahāprakāśa-līlā, when the Lord revealed Himself for twenty-one hours, **Advaita Ācārya** was hiding in Nandana's house; Mahāprabhu too once hid there for a night (Abhidhāna). After the Lord took sannyāsa Nandana followed Him to Purī, and when the Lord came back from South India, lame though he was, he ran ahead of everyone to meet Him: "Nandana Ācārya came with deep love — lame indeed, yet he came before all the others" (verse quoted in the Abhidhāna).

## Other names

In the Oriyā *Guru-bhakti-gītā* of Acyutānanda dāsa the name is Nandinī Ācārya, identified as the gopī Haranayanī; other Oriyā works call Nandana Ācārya Hariṇī in his previous birth, so the names may be of one person (record's text). The Abhidhāna keeps apart **Nandana**, one of three brothers — Viṣṇudāsa, Nandana and Gaṅgādāsa — of Nityānanda's branch, "in whose house Nityānanda had stayed before" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.43), who has his own record.
