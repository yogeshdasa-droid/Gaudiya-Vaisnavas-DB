## In short

Śrī **Kāśīśvara Paṇḍita** (also called **Kāśīnātha Paṇḍita**), a devotee of Lord Caitanya from **Cātarā** near Śrīrāmpur, went to **Vṛndāvana** by the Lord's order and installed there **Śrī Gaura-Govinda**, the deity the Lord gave him as His own self, at the side of Śrī Govindadeva. "Who can tell the glory of Kāśīśvara, who brought Śrī Gaura-Govinda to Vṛndāvana? Kāśīśvara, dear to the Lord, is known in the world; Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana are enchanted by his qualities" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 6.444, 479). In Vraja he was **Kelimañjarī** (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*; *Sādhana-dīpikā* 2.41). As Kāśīnātha he is counted in the sub-branch of **Śaṅkarāraṇya Ācārya**: "Mukunda, Kāśīnātha, Rudra are written as sub-branches" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.106), and among the Lord's associates he is an upa-mahānta (Abhidhāna, «Kāśīnātha Paṇḍita»).

## Birth and family

He was born in **1420 Śaka** (1498 AD) at **Brāhmaṇaḍāṅgā** in the Jessore district. His father, **Vāsudeva Bhaṭṭācārya**, of the Kāñjilāl (Kanu) family and the Vātsya gotra, was wealthy and a great devotee of Hari; his mother was **Jāhnavī Devī**. Vāsudeva had two sons and a daughter: the other son was **Mahādeva Bhaṭṭācārya** (one text calls Mahādeva the elder, another Kāśīśvara), and the daughter's sons were **Ramākānta**, **Rudra** and **Lakṣmaṇa**. Through his association his nephew **Rudra Paṇḍita** became a great devotee; Rudra and Lakṣmaṇa installed Śrī Nandadulāla at Vallabhapur and Saibonā (Abhidhāna; the record's texts).

## To the Lord at Purī

He was drawn to Gaurāṅga from childhood and studied the scriptures along with his schooling. In **1437 Śaka** (1515 AD) he went to Purī without telling anyone and took shelter of the Lord. After some years his mother came and with great trouble brought him home, but she could not make him marry; he never married.

## Gaura-Govinda

When Rūpa Gosvāmī found Śrī Govinda at Gomā-ṭīlā and sent word to Purī, the Lord asked Kāśīśvara to go to Vṛndāvana to help in His service. "To leave You will split my heart in two." Knowing his heart, the Lord gave him, carefully made, a deity of His own form, and sat to eat with it; seeing the deity eat, Kāśīśvara was in great bliss. The Lord named it **Śrī Gaura-Govinda**. Kāśīśvara took Him to Vṛndāvana, seated Him at the right of Śrī Govinda and served Them with great love (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 2.440–444). Gaura-Govinda is still with Govindadeva at **Jaipur**. **Govinda Gosāñi**, the pūjārī of Śrī Govinda, was "the disciple of Kāśīśvara Gosāñi" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 8.66).

## Cātarā

In **1454 Śaka** (1532 AD; one text: 1445) he went to **Cātarā** and installed Deities of **Gaura-Nitāi**; his mother, brother and relatives soon came to live there. He initiated **Murāri**, the son of his brother Mahādeva, and gave him the service. The Muslim ruler granted him much land for a rent of 108 rupees; where the Deity of Gaurāṅga was installed he named the place **Gaurāṅgapur**, and the rest **Vāsudevapur** after his father. The stone temple, built like a Buddhist monastery, still looks new; above its door is a Gaṇeśa without a nose, said to have been broken by the Muslims; of its two dola-mañcas one is left; inside are a tank and a tunnel that no one dares to enter for fear of snakes, and the Gaṅgā, which once flowed near it, has moved east. The descendants of his brother's family still live at Cātarā with the title **Caudhurī**; in Bhaktisiddhānta's time the Vallabhapur temple was in the hands of Śivacandra Caudhurī (Śrīla Prabhupāda, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.138, purport).

He went to the festival of Dāsa Gadādhara at Katwa (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 10.416).

## His departure

When his mother passed away in **1466 Śaka** (1548 AD) he went to Vṛndāvana, and there he left his body in **1486 Śaka** (1564 AD), on the day of the **Caitrī Vāruṇī**; the day is celebrated at Cātarā every year (Abhidhāna). The Gauḍīya calendar keeps his disappearance on the **Kārtika śukla caturdaśī**; one text gives the full moon of Āśvina, the day of Govinda's mahā-rāsa.

## One Kāśīśvara or two?

Kavi Karṇapūra and the *Abhidhāna* have **two** Kāśīśvaras: this Kāśīśvara Paṇḍita of Cātarā and Vṛndāvana (Kelimañjarī), and **Kāśīśvara Brahmacārī (Gosvāmī)**, the disciple of Īśvara Purī, who with Govinda served the Lord in Purī and cleared the crowds before Him in the temple (Bhṛṅgāra; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.138, Madhya 10.131–148), who has his own record. Śrīla Prabhupāda's purport and other books take them as **one person**, the disciple of Īśvara Purī who later went to Vṛndāvana; the texts of this record tell it that way. He is also not Kāśīnātha Paṇḍita of Navadvīpa, who arranged the Lord's marriage with Viṣṇupriyā (his own record).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Kelimañjarī** (*Abhidhāna*); the record also gave Bhṛṅgāra, which belongs to Kāśīśvara Brahmacārī.
