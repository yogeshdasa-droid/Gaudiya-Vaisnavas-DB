## In short

Sāraṅga Ṭhākura (Sāraṅgadāsa Ṭhākura, Sāraṅgadeva; also Śārṅga Ṭhākura, Śārṅgapāṇi, Śārṅgadhara, and "Sāraṅga Murāri" with his disciple) was a branch of the tree of Śrī Caitanya: "Bhāgavatācārya, Ṭhākura Sāraṅgadāsa…" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.113). In Vraja he was **Nāndīmukhī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 172); some say he was Prahlāda Mahārāja, but Kavi Karṇapūra says his father Śivānanda Sena did not accept this. He is remembered as the guru who gave dīkṣā to a dead boy on the Lord's order, the boy who lived again and became **Ṭhākura Murāri**; their service under an old bakula tree at **Māmgāchi** (Modadruma-dvīpa, Navadvīpa) still goes on.

The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna has two entries, Sāraṅgadāsa Ṭhākura and Sāraṅgadeva, and says: "Sāraṅgadeva and he are probably the same person." Bimanbihari Majumdar thinks so too. Here they are one record.

## Life

He lived in Navadvīpa under a bakula tree and worshipped the Lord in a secluded place on the bank of the Gaṅgā. By day he gathered what he needed for the worship of his Deities, **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha**, and in the evening he crossed the Gaṅgā to join the kīrtana of Śrī Caitanya.

**The bakula tree.** One day Mahāprabhu came and saw that the bakula tree in the courtyard was dying. "What will you do?" — "Prabhu, apart from Your mercy I see no hope for it." The Lord embraced the tree, and it has stayed healthy to this day and is now very large.

## Ṭhākura Murāri

He had vowed never to accept disciples, but the Lord inspired him again and again. One day Śrī Gaurāṅga, coming home with Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita after rebuking Devānanda Paṇḍita, met Sāraṅgadeva and asked: "Why do you not make disciples?" "I do not find anyone fit," he answered. The Lord said: "Whoever you take as a disciple will be fit." "Since that is your order," said Sāraṅgadeva, "I will make my disciple the first person I meet tomorrow."

The next morning, while bathing in the Gaṅgā, he felt the body of a young boy floating on a raft touch his feet. He took hold of it — "Who are you? Get up!" — and Mahāprabhu, watching from behind, called out: "Sāraṅga! Say the mantra in his ear!" When the mantra entered his ear the boy came back to life: "My name is Murāri. I am your servant; be merciful to me." He had died of a snake bite on the day of his sacred-thread ceremony, and, as was the custom for a boy, his family had put him in the river on a raft of banana trees instead of burning him. When his parents heard that he was alive they came to take him home, but he would not go: "I will stay in the service of the one who gave me life again." Sāraṅga trained him in the worship of Rādhā-Gopīnātha, and he became famous as **Ṭhākura Murāri** (Sāraṅga Murāri); his name is always joined to Sāraṅga's.

One text takes this Murāri to be the **Murāri Caitanya dāsa** whom Vṛndāvana dāsa describes riding on tigers and playing with pythons by Nityānanda's mercy (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Antya 5.426-434); B. B. Majumdar keeps the two apart, as the Vaiṣṇava-vandanās and the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* do (see Murāri Caitanya dāsa's record). Some books call the disciple Murāri Gupta, but Murāri Gupta the physician of Śrīhaṭṭa is another person.

## Places

- **Māmgāchi** (Jannagar; Modadruma-dvīpa, just north of Navadvīpa town): his śrīpāṭa, with Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha and the old bakula tree; a new temple was built in front of the tree and is managed by the Gauḍīya Maṭha (Śrī Śrī Gaurasundara, p. 113). The Deities of Vāsudeva Datta, Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Madanagopāla, are there too. It is reached by rickshaw from Navadvīpa, or by train towards Katwa to the second stop, Bhāṇḍāra-ṭikuri.
- He was born at Budhan; according to Abhirāma he was from Kuliyā.
- His samādhi is at Garbetā (Midnapore).
- Ṭhākura Murāri's family line still lives at Sār village in Bardhamān.

## Remembered

- His appearance: **Āṣāḍha kṛṣṇa caturdaśī**; his departure: **Agrahāyaṇa kṛṣṇa trayodaśī** (the record's texts; the Gauḍīya calendar), the day after Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa's.
- Vaiṣṇava-vandanā of Jīva (213), of Devakīnandana (101) and of Vṛndāvana dāsa (91); Viṣṇupriyā Gaurāṅga Patrikā vol. 7, no. 10, p. 386.
