## In short

Śrī **Nṛsiṁhānanda Brahmacārī** was called **Pradyumna Brahmacārī** before the Lord renamed him: seeing his extraordinary devotion to his Deity of **Nṛsiṁha**, Lord Caitanya gave him the name Nṛsiṁhānanda (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.55-56). His Śrīpāṭa was at **Piyārīgañj** near Kālnā. He is famous for the road of jewels he built in his mind for the Lord, and for bringing the Lord in meditation to Śivānanda Sena's house to eat his cooking.

## The road in his mind

When the Lord left Orissa for Vṛndāvana by way of Gauḍa and came to **Kuliyā**, Nṛsiṁhānanda began to build in his mind a road for Him: lined with jewels and flowers, with ponds of bright lotuses along it, birds singing and cool breezes. He could build it as far as **Kānāi Nāṭaśālā**, and no further; so he said that this time the Lord would not reach Vṛndāvana but would turn back at Kānāi Nāṭaśālā — and so it happened (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 1.155-162).

## The Lord comes to eat

The Lord told Śrīkānta Sena, Śivānanda's nephew: "Forbid the devotees of Bengal to come to Purī this year; this Pauṣa I shall go to Bengal and come to Śivānanda's house; Jagadānanda is there to give Me offerings of food." In Pauṣa Jagadānanda and Śivānanda prepared everything and waited every day until evening, but the month passed and the Lord did not come. Then Nṛsiṁhānanda arrived: "Do not be sad; I shall bring Him here within three days." He sat in meditation and on the third day said: "I have brought the Lord as far as Pānihāṭi (another text: to Rāghava Paṇḍita's house); tomorrow at noon He will come. Bring all kinds of ingredients; I shall cook for Him." He cooked soups, piṭhā, sweet rice and many vegetables and sweets, and offered them in meditation to Śrī Caitanya, to Jagannātha and to his own Nṛsiṁhadeva — and saw the Lord come quickly, sit down and eat everything, even the plate of Nṛsiṁha. "You and Jagannātha are one, but why did You take Nṛsiṁha's food? Now my Lord must go hungry," he cried, overwhelmed with love (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2.51-78).

Śivānanda could not believe it. The next year at Purī, before all the devotees, the Lord Himself spoke of it: "Last Pauṣa, when Nṛsiṁhānanda gave Me so many sweets and vegetables, they were so good that I had never eaten anything like them." All were astonished, and Śivānanda was convinced.

## Not Nakula Brahmacārī

The record's long text (from R. G. Nāth's edition of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, appendix, written under **Nakula Brahmacārī**) also tells that at **Ambikā** "Pradyumna" was possessed by the Lord's presence and that Śivānanda tested him by asking him to tell his own iṣṭa-mantra. In the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Antya 2.16-18) this is **Nakula Brahmacārī** of Āmbuyā-muluka, who has his own record; the story is told there.
