## In short

Śrī **Yadunandana dāsa** (Yadunandana Ṭhākura), a Vaidya, was the favourite disciple of **Hemalatā Ṭhākurāṇī**, the daughter of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya, and the author of the **Karṇānanda**, a life of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya. He is also famous for his Bengali verse translations of the *Vidagdha-mādhava*, the *Govinda-līlāmṛta* and the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*. He was "one of the five followers of Śrī Caitanya named Yadunandana" (*Gaura-pārṣada-caritāvalī*).

## Birth

"In 1459 Śaka the great composer Śrī Yadunandana dāsa was born in the village of **Khālihāṭī** (Mālihāṭī, Meleti), on the western bank of the Bhāgīrathī, north of Kaṇṭaka-nagara (Katwa)" (*Gaura-pārṣada-caritāvalī*). His Śrīpāṭa was there, to the north of Katwa.

## His guru

He was very devoted to Hemalatā and often stayed at her house at **Budhaipāḍā** on the western bank of the Bhāgīrathī, serving her with great care. Hemalatā, a genius among the Vaiṣṇavas of Gauḍa, preached Lord Caitanya's teaching like her father; by her influence even the most wicked turned to devotion. At the end of every chapter of the *Karṇānanda* he names her: "Hemalatā, the daughter of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya Prabhu, is like a creeper of divine love, a beautiful creation of the Lord. I, Yadunandana dāsa, take pleasure in writing the *Karṇānanda*, keeping her lotus feet in my heart." In his *Govinda-līlāmṛta* he writes: "Worship the feet of the guru, the only abode of peace and mine of all good qualities. The daughter of Ācārya Prabhu is Śrī Hemalatā, whose name fulfils all desires. Finding me in the darkness of ignorance, she gave me her kind refuge, and by her mercy my eyes have been opened."

## Works

When Hemalatā heard his life of Śrīnivāsa she was deeply pleased and named it **Karṇānanda**, "joy of the ears". He also translated into Bengali verse the *Vidagdha-mādhava*, the *Govinda-līlāmṛta* and the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*, wrote a book of poems, the *Kuñja-rasa-stava*, and many padas on Gaura-līlā and Kṛṣṇa-līlā, some of them in the *Padāmṛta-samudra*. Nothing is recorded of a marriage or children. (See the book *Vaiṣṇava Sāhitya o Yadunandana*.)
