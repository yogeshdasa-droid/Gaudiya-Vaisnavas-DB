## In short

**Rāmacandra Purī** was the **rejected disciple** of Mādhavendra Purī: "a dry knower of Brahman, without any relation with Kṛṣṇa; he blames everyone and is bent on blaming" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 8.25). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Vibhīṣaṇa**, but Lord Caitanya said that **Jaṭilā**, Rādhā's mother-in-law, had entered him (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 92–93).

## Rejected by his guru

At the end of his life Mādhavendra Purī, in separation from Kṛṣṇa, was crying *ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha*. Rāmacandra Purī, his disciple, fearlessly instructed his guru: "You are full Brahman: remember the bliss of Brahman. Why do you weep?" Mādhavendra was pained: "Get away, sinner! I am dying because I have not got Kṛṣṇa's mercy nor Mathurā, and you come to burn me more. Do not show me your face." Meanwhile **Īśvara Purī** served him and was blessed with prema; so one became an ocean of love and the other a dry critic of everyone (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 8.17–31; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*).

## In Purī

When he came to Purī, the Lord, as the keeper of propriety, bowed down to him as His guru's godbrother, and so did Paramānanda Purī. He pressed **Jagadānanda** to eat more and more, and then criticised him for eating too much; and he criticised the Lord for His eating, so that the Lord cut His meals to half. The devotees were very sad; when he left Purī, they felt as if a great stone had fallen from their heads (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 8). Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī notes that people still called him "Gosvāmī" only because he wore the dress of a sannyāsī.

## In the Vaiṣṇava-vandanās

He is named in the *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā* of Jīva Gosvāmī (125), of Devakīnandana (45) and of Vṛndāvana dāsa (43). He had a maṭha in Vārāṇasī (B. V. Tīrtha).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vibhīṣaṇa**; Jaṭilā entered him (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 92–93).
