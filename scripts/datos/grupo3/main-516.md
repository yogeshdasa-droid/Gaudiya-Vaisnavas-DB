## In short

Śrī Īśvara Purī, the dearest disciple of **Mādhavendra Purī**, was the **dīkṣā-guru of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu**, who received the mantra from him at **Gayā**. "The seed of Kṛṣṇa-prema next fructified in Śrī Īśvara Purī, and then the gardener Himself, Caitanya Mahāprabhu, became the trunk of the tree of bhakti" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 9.11). He wrote the **Śrī Kṛṣṇa-līlāmṛta**.

## Birth

He was born at **Kumārahaṭṭa** (Hālisahar, on the Gaṅgā north of Calcutta) (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 17), son of **Śyāmasundara Ācārya**, a Rāḍhī brāhmaṇa (*Prema-vilāsa* 23; *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). Returning from Gayā the Lord stopped at Kumārahaṭṭa, wept, and took dust from his birthplace: "this earth is My life".

## Service to his guru

He was with Mādhavendra when he met Nityānanda in the West and wept to see their love (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 9.161). In Mādhavendra's last days, when he could not move, Īśvara Purī served him with his own hands, cleaning his stool and urine, always chanting and telling him Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; Mādhavendra blessed him: "May you have love of Kṛṣṇa", and he became an ocean of prema (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 8.28–31; *Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 11.125).

## In Navadvīpa

He came to Navadvīpa unknown and sat humbly in **Advaita's** house, calling himself "a śūdra of the lowest kind"; when **Mukunda Datta** sang of Kṛṣṇa he fainted in ecstasy, and they knew he was Mādhavendra's disciple. He stayed some months at **Gopīnātha Ācārya's** house; he met Nimāi Paṇḍita on the road, dined at His house, and taught **Gadādhara** his *Kṛṣṇa-līlāmṛta*. He asked Nimāi to find its faults; the Lord said, "the words of a devotee are never at fault — Kṛṣṇa takes only the love in them", and, pressed, showed him one point of grammar on a verbal root, which Purī answered the next day (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 11.70–126).

## Gayā

At Gayā, after offering piṇḍa, the Lord was overwhelmed at the footprint of Viṣṇu; Īśvara Purī came by chance. "My pilgrimage to Gayā is fruitful because I have seen your feet." A few days later the Lord asked him for the mantra, and he gave Him **dīkṣā** with the ten-syllable Gopāla mantra; then the Lord cooked for him with His own hands, fed him, and smeared him with sandal (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 17.46–112). The Lord came back to Navadvīpa a changed person.

## Govinda and Kāśīśvara

Before he left this world, he ordered his two servants and disciples, **Govinda** and **Kāśīśvara Brahmacārī**, to go to Purī and serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya; the Lord, after hesitating to take service from His guru's servants, accepted them by Sārvabhauma's advice: "the guru's order must not be questioned" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 10.131–148).

## His book

The **Kṛṣṇa-līlāmṛta** has not been found. Rūpa Gosvāmī quotes two verses from it (perhaps from its Rukmiṇī-svayaṁvara) in the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, in the chapter on the sāttvika bhāvas; there are verses of his in the *Padyāvalī* (*Abhidhāna*, «Kṛṣṇa-līlāmṛta»).

## Others

The *Prema-vilāsa* (24) makes him also the guru of **Nityānanda**; the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* says Nityānanda took Mādhavendra as His guru.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Jyaiṣṭha pūrṇimā, at Kumārahaṭṭa (B. V. Tīrtha).
**Disappearance:** Māgha kṛṣṇa dvādaśī; the Gauḍīya calendar, counting months from full moon to full moon, calls it Phālguna (Govinda) kṛṣṇa dvādaśī.
