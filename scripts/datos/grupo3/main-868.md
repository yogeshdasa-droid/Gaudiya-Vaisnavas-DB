## In short

Śrī Mādhavendra Purī, a sannyāsī of the Purī order, is the **root of the tree of prema-bhakti**: "the storehouse of all love of Kṛṣṇa, the desire tree of bhakti, in whom the seed first sprouted" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 9.10). He was the guru of **Īśvara Purī** and of **Advaita Ācārya**, and so the **parama-guru** of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; Nityānanda Prabhu revered him as His guru. He revealed **Śrī Gopāla** on Govardhana, and **Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha** of Remuṇā stole a pot of sweet rice for him. Lord Caitanya said again and again that there is no one like him in relishing the mellows of love; the *Abhidhāna* calls him "the first stage-manager of bhakti-rasa".

## His line

The Gauḍīya guru-praṇālī (Gopāla Guru, Baladeva) runs from Madhva through Jayadharma, Puruṣottama, Brahmaṇya and **Vyāsatīrtha** to **Lakṣmīpati**, his guru, and from him to Īśvara Purī and Advaita, and from Īśvara Purī to Śrī Gaurāṅga; by this line the Gauḍīyas are counted in the Mādhva sampradāya (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*, «Madhvācārya»). Historians discuss the link. Outwardly he was a sannyāsī of Śaṅkara's daśanāmī order; neither Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja nor Vṛndāvana dāsa says anything of his caste or birthplace.

## Gopāla on Govardhana

Mad with love, he went round Govardhana, bathed in Govinda-kuṇḍa and sat under a tree, fasting. A cowherd boy brought him a pot of milk — "in My village no one fasts" — and vanished. In a dream the boy led him to a thicket: "I am **Gopāla**, the lifter of Govardhana, installed by Vajra; when the Muslims attacked, My priest hid Me here and fled. Take Me out and set Me on the hill." With the villagers he cleared the jungle, found the deity and installed Him on the hill; nine hundred pots of water from Govinda-kuṇḍa bathed Him and the **Annakūṭa** was held, Gopāla eating everything while all remained as before. He gave the service to two brāhmaṇas from Gauḍa, and for two years worship went on in great style (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 4).

## Sandalwood, and Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha

Gopāla told him in a dream: "My body still burns: bring sandalwood from Nīlācala." On the way east he came to **Advaita Ācārya** at Śāntipura and initiated Him, and stayed with **Jagannātha Miśra and Śacī** in Navadvīpa, blessing them. At **Remuṇā** he wished to taste Gopīnātha's sweet rice to make it for Gopāla, then felt ashamed and went to sit in an empty market; Gopīnātha told the priest in a dream that He had hidden a pot of sweet rice behind His cloth for the sannyāsī Mādhavendra Purī — so He is called **Kṣīra-corā**, "the thief of sweet rice". Mādhavendra broke the pot and ate a piece every day; fearing fame, he left before dawn. In Purī the king's men gave him a maund of sandalwood and twenty tolas of camphor; back at Remuṇā Gopāla told him: "My body and Gopīnātha's are one; smear it on Him." So all summer Gopīnātha was anointed (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 4). The story was told by Lord Caitanya to Nityānanda at Remuṇā.

## Nityānanda

Nityānanda Prabhu met him "near the western tīrthas", with Īśvara Purī, Brahmānanda Purī and his other disciples; both fainted, rolled on the ground and embraced in tears. "Wherever one has the company of Nityānanda, that place is more than Vaikuṇṭha." He treated Nityānanda as a friend, and Nityānanda treated him as His guru (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 9.155–192; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.2330–2332). At the sight of a dark cloud he would fall unconscious (Ādi 9.175).

## His disciples

"Śrī Īśvara Purī, Raṅga Purī and all the disciples of Mādhavendra are mad with bhakti-rasa; in Gauḍa, Utkala and other lands his followers are devoted to love of Kṛṣṇa" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 5.2272–2274). The texts of the record name Advaita Ācārya, Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi, Īśvara Purī, Paramānanda Purī, Raṅga Purī, Brahmānanda Purī, Brahmānanda Bhāratī, Keśava Bhāratī, Kṛṣṇānanda Purī, Rāmacandra Purī, Nṛsiṁha Tīrtha, Raghupati Upādhyāya, Sukhānanda Purī and, by some, Nityānanda; the brāhmaṇa of the **Sanoḍiyā** community of Mathurā, whose food he accepted; **Mādhava Miśra**, Gadādhara's father. **Rāmacandra Purī**, who told him at the end to "remember Brahman" instead of weeping, he drove away: "Go, sinner!"

## The end

**Īśvara Purī** served him to the last, even cleaning his stool and urine, chanting and telling him Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; pleased, he blessed him: "May you have love of Kṛṣṇa" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Antya 8.28–31; *Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 11.125). At the end he repeated one verse and attained perfection: *ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha he mathurā-nātha kadāvalokyase / hṛdayaṁ tvad-aloka-kātaraṁ dayita bhrāmyati kiṁ karomy aham* — "O my Lord, merciful to the poor, O master of Mathurā, when shall I see You? My heart, unable to see You, wanders; O beloved, what shall I do?" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 4.197). The Gauḍīyas take it as the essence of the mood of separation; in 1702 Śaka Kiśorī dāsa wrote a commentary on it, *Bindu-prakāśa* (*Abhidhāna*). Advaita kept the festival of his tithi.

## Writings

Five verses in Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Padyāvalī* (79, 96, 164, 286, 330). His Gopāla is now at **Nāthdvārā** (Rajasthan), worshipped by the followers of Vallabhācārya.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Vaiśākha pūrṇimā (Gauḍīya calendar).
**Disappearance:** Phālguna śukla dvādaśī (Gauḍīya calendar), at Remuṇā according to tradition.
