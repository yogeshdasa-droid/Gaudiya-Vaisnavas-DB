## In short

Śrī **Raṅga Purī**, a disciple of Mādhavendra Purī: "Śrī Raṅga Purī, the disciple of Śrī Mādhava Purī" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 9.285). Lord Caitanya met him at **Paṇḍharpur**, and from him learnt that His elder brother **Viśvarūpa** had left this world there.

## At Paṇḍharpur

After His tour of the South the Lord came from Uḍupī to Paṇḍharpur on the Bhīmā, saw Viṭṭhala and danced. A brāhmaṇa told Him that Raṅga Purī was staying in the village; the Lord went at once and fell at his feet, with tears and trembling. "Get up, Śrīpāda: You must be related to Mādhavendra Purī — without him there is no such love." The Lord told him He was Īśvara Purī's disciple, and for five or seven days they talked of Kṛṣṇa (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 9.282–303).

## Śacī's kitchen and Viśvarūpa

Hearing that the Lord came from Navadvīpa, Raṅga Purī remembered going there with Mādhavendra Purī and eating at **Jagannātha Miśra's** house — a curry of banana flowers he had never tasted, and Śacī, chaste and devoted, who fed sannyāsīs as her own sons. He told the Lord that one of her sons had taken sannyāsa young, as **Śaṅkarāraṇya**, and had attained perfection at Paṇḍharpur. The Lord said: "In My former āśrama he was My brother; Jagannātha Miśra was My father." Then Raṅga Purī went on to Dvārakā; later he too came to Nīlācala and stayed there to the end; the Lord honoured him as His guru (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 9; the record's text).
