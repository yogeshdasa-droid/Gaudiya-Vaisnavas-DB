## In short

Śrīmad **Viṣṇu Purī**, author of the **Bhakti-ratnāvalī**, is one of the nine sannyāsī "roots" of the tree of bhakti (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 9.13–14: "Viṣṇu Purī, Keśava Purī, Purī Kṛṣṇānanda, Śrī Nṛsiṁha Tīrtha and Purī Sukhānanda"). The *Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna* calls him a disciple of **Jayadharma**, an ācārya of the Mādhva sampradāya; some say a disciple of Mādhavendra Purī.

## Life

His name before sannyāsa was **Viṣṇuśarmā**. He was born in the Karmaha family at **Tiraudhi** in Tirhut (Mithilā), knew the Vedas and kept to the rites. Ill-treated by his wife, he left home and meditated on Mahādeva in the village temple of Śiva; troubled there too by the villagers, he came to **Kāśī** and lived near **Bindumādhava**. Lord Jagannātha then told the king and the pūjārīs in a dream that He wished to wear "the garland of jewels" that Viṣṇu Purī had: they wrote to him from Purī and he sent the **Bhakti-ratnāvalī**. It is said that each verse was put in a small ball, and the pūjārīs put the garland of balls on Jagannātha (*Abhidhāna*).

## The book and its date

The *Bhakti-ratnāvalī* is a collection of verses from the *Bhāgavatam* on bhakti; Jīva Gosvāmī counts it among the nibandhas (*Bhakti-sandarbha* 23). Haraprasāda Śāstrī reads that he finished it in 1555 Śaka (1633), but manuscripts were copied in 1595 (Eggeling), and S. K. De places him long before the Lord. Assamese books (Daityāri's *Śaṅkara-carita*, the *Guru-caritra*) say he took it from Śaṅkaradeva Kaṇṭhabhūṣaṇa. For his being a contemporary: the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; Priyādāsa's commentary on the Hindi *Bhaktamāla* (he compiled it on receiving a letter from Lord Caitanya); Buchanan Hamilton, who heard at Purnea in 1809 of a learned sannyāsī Viṣṇu Purī "about three hundred years ago" who later married; Jayānanda and Locana, who count him among the Lord's devotees. B. B. Majumdar: perhaps Jayadharma's disciple who, living long, received the mercy of Mādhavendra Purī and of Lord Caitanya.
