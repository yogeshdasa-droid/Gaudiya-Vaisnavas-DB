## In short

**Anupanārāyaṇa** Tarkālaṅkāra «Vidyābahādur», son of **Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa**, a scholar of Kāśī at the end of the eighteenth century, wrote the **Āmoda-kāvya** on Kṛṣṇa-līlā and other works. He revered Lord Caitanya and His associates, but in siddhānta he did not follow Him: his loyalty was to Sītā-Rāma and the Rāmānandī sādhus.

## His works

The **Āmoda-kāvya**, in fifteen chapters, deals with Kṛṣṇa-līlā; at its end he says he was the son of Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa and that **Śrī Campakalatā** poured the nectar of Kṛṣṇa-kathā into his ears. He also wrote a vṛtti called **Sāmañjasa**, whose conclusion names Lord Caitanya, Rūpa Gosvāmī and Svarūpa (Calcutta Sanskrit Sāhitya Pariṣat ms. Sa 855); the **Vidvad-vinodinī Sūcikā** on the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; and the **Sītā-śataka-kāvya** (Sanskrit collections, Benares 1897-1901, p. 9).

## At Kāśī

The last verse of the Sītā-śataka says he joined the assembly headed by **Kāśīnātha** and received the titles «Tarkālaṅkāra» and «Vidyābahādur». According to Gopīnātha Kavirāja, the «Varṣāntara-nāyaka» addressed in that verse is **Mr. Duncan**, Political Resident in Lord Cornwallis's time (1786-1793), on whose initiative the Sanskrit College of Kāśī was founded; Kāśīnātha was its first principal (1791-1801). So Anupanārāyaṇa was Kāśīnātha's contemporary.

## His siddhānta

He had great reverence for Lord Caitanya and His associates, and faith in the sādhus of the Rāmānandī sect; but in the Sītā-śataka he declares his loyalty to Sītā and Rāma, and his *Sannyāsa-vṛtti* is dualist (dvaita-para), not acintya-bhedābheda.
