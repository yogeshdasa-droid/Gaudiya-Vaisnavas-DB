## In short

At **Śrī Raṅga-kṣetra**, while Lord Caitanya spent the Cāturmāsya in **Veṅkaṭa Bhaṭṭa**'s house, an unlettered brāhmaṇa Vaiṣṇava recited all eighteen chapters of the **Bhagavad-gītā** every day in the temple of Raṅganātha, mispronouncing the words but weeping in ecstasy. The Lord told him he had understood the Gītā's real purport, embraced him and revealed Himself to him, and the brāhmaṇa did not leave the Lord's company for the four months (Caitanya-caritāmṛta Madhya 9.93-103).

## «I see Kṛṣṇa»

People sometimes criticized him and laughed at him, but he did not care, full of ecstasy as he read. Seeing the transcendental changes in his body while he recited, Lord Caitanya one day asked: "Which part of the Bhagavad-gītā gives you such transcendental pleasure?"

"I am illiterate," he answered, "and do not understand the meaning of the words; I pronounce them sometimes rightly, sometimes wrongly. But I read the Bhagavad-gītā on the order of my spiritual master, and while I read I simply see my Lord Kṛṣṇa standing before me in His beautiful form as Arjuna's charioteer. That is why nothing can distract my mind from this reading."

"You are the right person to read the Bhagavad-gītā," the Lord said, "for you have understood its real purport." He embraced him, and the brāhmaṇa fell at His feet and wept. The Lord revealed Himself to him and asked him not to tell anyone. The brāhmaṇa became a great devotee of Lord Caitanya and did not leave Him for the four months of Cāturmāsya.
