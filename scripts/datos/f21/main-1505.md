## In short

**Sūryānanda**, head abbot of the **Galtā** seat of the Śrī Vaiṣṇavas at Jaipur (Rajasthan), a strong-minded and devout man, cursed his disciple **Raghunātha dāsa** with leprosy for refusing to take charge of the Deity's service, and promised to cure him in his next life. Drawn by **Rasikānanda**'s affection at Gopīvallabhapura, he was reborn, with Śyāmānanda's and Rasikānanda's blessing, as **Nayanānanda** (#2006), eldest son of Rādhānanda and grandson of Rasikānanda, and there Raghunātha was healed.

## The curse

Wishing to go on pilgrimage, Sūryānanda wanted to leave the service of the Deity to his disciple Raghunātha dāsa. Raghunātha was reluctant, and Sūryānanda cursed him to suffer from leprosy. When Raghunātha begged forgiveness, Sūryānanda told him that he himself would soon leave his body and be born again; that one day, on his way to Purī, Raghunātha would meet him and be cleansed by drinking the water that had touched his feet; and that the mark of a sword on his back would be there in his next life too, so that Raghunātha could know his guru.

## Gopīvallabhapura

On his travels Sūryānanda came to the śrīpāṭa of **Gopīvallabhapura**. Attracted by Rasikānanda Prabhu's affection, he told **Śyāmānanda Prabhu** that he wished to be born as Rasikānanda's son; Śyāmānanda and Rasikānanda approved, and he was told to take birth as the son of **Rādhānanda** (Rasikānanda's son). He left the **Lakṣmī-Nṛsiṁha śālagrāma** he served at Gopīvallabhapura and died at Purī, and was reborn as Rādhānanda's eldest son, **Nayanānanda Deva**.

Meanwhile Raghunātha, as his guru had told him, went on pilgrimage and at last came to Gopīvallabhapura. Finding the mark of a sword on Nayanānanda's back, he drank the water that had touched his feet and was freed from his sin; then he went back to Galtā and became its mahānta.
