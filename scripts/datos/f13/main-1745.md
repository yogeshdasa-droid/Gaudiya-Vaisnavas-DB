## In short

**Vṛndāvana dāsa Bābājī** lived at **Varṣāṇā**, and every day walked to Vṛndāvana and back with his japa-mālā, visiting the seven great temples; he never took prasāda anywhere. When, out of courtesy, he once did, the thumb of his chanting hand froze — and **Manohara dāsa Bābājī of Govinda-kuṇḍa** told him how to atone.

## His day

He rose early, finished his morning duties and walked to Vṛndāvana with his mālā; there he visited the seven famous temples and took caraṇāmṛta, Tulasī and the dust of the temples, came back to Varṣāṇā in the evening and went for mādhukarī.

## The thumb

Once many Vaiṣṇavas were invited for prasāda at the temple of **Śrī Govinda** in Vṛndāvana, and all of them asked him to join them; thinking it improper to refuse, he ate and went back to Varṣāṇā. At dawn, as he set out with his mālā, the thumb of his right hand would not move, as if paralysed, though there was no pain. He ran to **Śrī Manohara dāsa Bābājī** of Śrī Govinda-kuṇḍa, threw himself at his feet and cried: "Bābā, I am undone! My right thumb will not move while I chant the holy name." The Mahārāja asked where he had taken prasāda the day before and told him to find out where the money for it had come from: it had been given by a prostitute. Manohara dāsa told him to bathe in Śrī Kuṇḍa and walk round Girirāja in wet clothes for three days, eating only what came to him on the way. He did so, and on the fourth day his thumb was well again.
