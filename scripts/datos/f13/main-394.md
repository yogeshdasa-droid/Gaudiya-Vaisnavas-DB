## In short

**Gorācāṅd dāsa Bābājī** did bhajana at **Rādhā-kuṇḍa** at the beginning of the twentieth century, in a mud kuṭīra on the north side of the kuṇḍa, its little door hung with jute. Deeply devoted to Lord Caitanya, he knew the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* by heart. His story was told by **Navadvīpa dāsa Bābājī Mahānta** to **Haridāsa dāsa**.

## «Jñāna-vastu»

One afternoon, while he was chanting alone in his kuṭīra, Navadvīpa dāsa Bābājī called "Jaya Rādhe" from outside and went in. He found Gorācāṅd lying in a mosquito net; when he got down from his cot to receive him, Navadvīpa dāsa saw he was very frail, with open wounds on his chest and back.

Navadvīpa dāsa asked why Kṛṣṇa is called *jñāna-vastu* in the verse of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, «advaya-jñāna-tattva-vastu vrajendra-nandana». Until then Gorācāṅd had sat with half-closed eyes; at the question he began reciting verses from memory, and his whole appearance changed — with a lion's energy and deep fervour he sang Kṛṣṇa's glories for about half an hour, until his attending disciple came and he fell completely unconscious. The disciple disapproved of Navadvīpa dāsa's provoking him, and Navadvīpa dāsa left, embarrassed and sorry. The story shows, the record says, how the descent of *bhagavad-āveśa* lifts one beyond identification with the body.
