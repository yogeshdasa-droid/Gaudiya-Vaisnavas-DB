## In short

**Veṅkaṭācārya** — **Vedānta Deśika** (1268–1371), as the ṭīkā on Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 15.68 calls him — was a great scholar of the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava** community, born in a village near **Kāñcī**. He brought glory to the Śrī-sampradāya by his spotless character and his refutation of Advaita-vāda, and when the Muslims sacked **Śrīraṅgam** he helped save the Deity of **Śrī Raṅganātha** and bring Him back. Jīva Gosvāmī cites his **Śata-dūṣaṇī** in the Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī (10.87.2).

## Śrīraṅgam

He travelled to holy places all over India. In 1310 Malik Kāfūr, commander of Sultan Alāuddīn, campaigned in the South, and in 1326 the Muslims entered Śrīraṅgam and plundered the town and temple. Vedānta Deśika, with Lokācārya's help, had Śrī Raṅganātha taken by a forest route to Tirupati, while he himself, with the two sons of Sudarśana Sūri, went to Yādavādri carrying Sudarśanācārya's ṭīkā, the *Śruta-prakāśikā*. Later, with the help of a very influential brāhmaṇa ruler, **Goppaṇārya**, he succeeded against the Muslims and brought Raṅganātha back to His seat at Śrīraṅgam in **1371**; he died the same year.

## His works

He wrote a **Tattva-ṭīkā** on the Śrī-bhāṣya, and many books for the Śrī-sampradāya, among them the **Śata-dūṣaṇī**, which shows a hundred defects of Śaṅkara's Māyāvāda.
