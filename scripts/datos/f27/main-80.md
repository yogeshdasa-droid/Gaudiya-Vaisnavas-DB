## In short

**Balarāma dāsa «Mādhavī»** (born 1478), son of the landowner **Śrīdāma Taraphdār** of Kāmyasātpura near Rāṇāghāṭ and of **Kṛpāmayī**, a contemporary of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, rose to high office under **Hussain Shah**; meeting **Nityānanda Prabhu** on the road, he was initiated by Him on the spot and put in the care of **Kānu Ṭhākura**. He wrote the **Śrī Patita-pāvana-avatāra**.

## In Hussain Shah's service

As a boy he lived at Phuliyā and learned Persian from Munśī Qutab Khān. Impressed by his Persian, **Ālī Khān**, the Kāzī of Śāntipura, sent him with a letter of recommendation to Hussain Shah, king of Gauḍa, and he soon rose to be chief writer of the army. When the Magh pirates attacked Chittagong, he fought as fourth commander with remarkable skill, pleasing **Parāgal Khān**; Hussain Shah gave him the title «Khān» and the village of Cuṭipura, some twenty-four miles from Rāṇāghāṭ.

## Nityānanda's mercy

Then one day, walking on the road, he had the good fortune to meet Nityānanda Prabhu, who showered His mercy on him, initiated him at once and placed him in the care of Kānu Ṭhākura. Later he wrote the *Śrī Patita-pāvana-avatāra*.
