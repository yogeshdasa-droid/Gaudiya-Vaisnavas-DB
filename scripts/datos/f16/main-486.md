## In short

**Harirāma Vyāsa** («Vyāsajī», born Saṁvat 1567, 1510 CE), a brāhmaṇa of **Oṛchā** (Bundelkhand), was a disciple of **Mādhava**, of the line of **Mādhavendra Purī**, Lord Caitanya's guru. Cast out by his family for honouring Vaiṣṇavas above caste, he settled with his wife in **Vṛndāvana**, where she founded the temple of **Yugala-kiśora** («Naval-kiśora»). Their samādhis are in his garden, **Kiśora-vana** («Vyāsajī kā gherā»). He is not Harirāma Ācārya, the disciple of Rāmacandra Kavirāja.

## Cast out

Once, at a wedding in his house, Harirāma himself served the fine food to the brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas, which led to a quarrel with his brothers. Another time some men of the low Hāḍi caste passed his house carrying prasāda from a festival, and Harirāma, indifferent to caste, took some from them and ate it. For these reasons his brothers and cousins drove him out, and he and his wife went to Vṛndāvana and settled there (Bhaktamāla 20.8).

## In Vṛndāvana

At a performance of the **rāsa-līlā** the string of the ankle-bells of the boy playing Rādhikā broke; Harirāma at once tore off his sacred thread and tied the bells with it. He divided his property among his three sons and tried to send his wife back to Oṛchā with them, but she would not leave him.

Once, serving at a Vaiṣṇava feast, his wife by mistake put the best cream on Harirāma's plate instead of a Vaiṣṇava's; furious, he drove her out of the house. Obedient, she sold her ornaments for ten thousand rupees and with them founded a temple and installed **Yugala-kiśora**, and so they were reconciled. This Deity is also called **Naval-kiśora**; some say Harirāma found Him in a well at Kiśora-vana. Harirāma would stand in constant attendance at Yugala-kiśora's court, holding a spittoon. It is said that Emperor **Akbar** gave him many estates in appreciation of his honesty.

## His works

Several *vāṇīs* or padāvalīs by Harirāma and his wife survive; the best known is the **Svadharma-paddhati**. In the **Navaratna** he discusses the nine prameyas accepted by Madhvācārya.
