## In short

**Īśāna Ṭhākura** was the household servant of **Śrī Jagannātha Miśra** in Navadvīpa. After Miśra's passing he looked after the whole household, loving **Śacī-mātā** as his own mother, and after Mahāprabhu's sannyāsa he cared for Śacī-mātā and **Viṣṇupriyā**. In his old age he took **Śrīnivāsa, Narottama** and **Rāmacandra** round the nine islands of Navadvīpa, and left this world soon after (Bhakti-ratnākara 12-13).

## Servant of Śacī's house

He did every household duty — bringing water from the Gaṅgā, growing vegetables, collecting the year's paddy, going to market, receiving guests and washing their feet, cleaning the room after meals; when Nityānanda Prabhu came to Śacī-mātā's house, Īśāna washed His feet (Caitanya-bhāgavata Madhya 1.59). When Nimāi was a small, restless boy, it was Īśāna who had to satisfy His whims (Bhakti-ratnākara 12.97), and Nimāi would go nowhere without him. "Īśāna was always engaged in Śacī-mātā's service; he is the most fortunate in the fourteen worlds, and Śacī-devī was affectionate to him" (Caitanya-bhāgavata). Devakīnandana's *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*: "I offer my obeisances to Śrī Īśāna Ṭhākura, to whom Śacī-devī was very affectionate."

After Mahāprabhu's sannyāsa the whole care of Śacī-mātā and Viṣṇupriyā fell on him; after Śacī-mātā's passing, **Vaṁśīvadanānanda** looked after Viṣṇupriyā and Īśāna.

## With Śrīnivāsa, Narottama and Rāmacandra

When Śrīnivāsa Ācārya first came to Māyāpura from Purī he had darśana of Viṣṇupriyā; but when the three companions came later, only Īśāna was left: "They saw Īśāna sitting alone, his body shining like the sun; though he tried to restrain himself, tears flowed from his closed eyes" (Bhakti-ratnākara 12.113). Understanding that they were Mahāprabhu's favourites, he embraced them, and they took the old man with them round the nine islands to the places of Gaurasundara's pastimes, and on parting offered prayers at his feet (Bhakti-ratnākara 13.9). They felt the separation keenly; soon after they reached Raghunandana's house at Śrīkhaṇḍa, the news came from Māyāpura that Īśāna Ṭhākura had ended his pastimes (Bhakti-ratnākara 13.21).
