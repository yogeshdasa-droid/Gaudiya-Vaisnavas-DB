## In short

**Puruṣottama Deva**, king of Orissa, son of **Kapilendra Deva** and Pārvatī-devī (Sarasvatī-vilāsa) and father of **Mahārāja Pratāparudra**, inherited the throne by Lord Jagannātha's order. Insulted as a «sweeper» by the King of Kāñcī for sweeping the road before Jagannātha's chariot, he conquered Kāñcī with the help of **Jagannātha and Balarāma** Themselves and married the princess **Padmāvatī**. He brought **Sākṣī-gopāla** to Cuttack and was a Sanskrit scholar and poet.

## The throne

According to the *Gaṅgā-vaṁśānucarita*, though not the eldest son, he received his father's kingdom by the divine order of Śrī Jagannātha. His outraged brothers challenged the order; Puruṣottama stood before them unarmed while they threw weapons at him, and when he remained unhurt they accepted that the throne was his.

## Kāñcī

The Oṛiyā *Kāñci-kāverī* tells that the King of Kāñcī came to Purī at Ratha-yātrā to arrange the marriage of his daughter Padmāvatī; seeing Puruṣottama sweep the road before Lord Jagannātha with a golden-handled broom, he refused to give his daughter to a «sweeper». Deeply offended, Puruṣottama set out for Kāñcī with Jagannātha's leave and promise of help. On the way a milkmaid, **Māṇikā**, gave him a ring: two soldiers who had passed before had given it for curd and milk, telling her their king was coming behind and would pay. Seeing the ring, the king understood that the soldiers were Jagannātha and Balarāma. He paid for the milk and curd, defeated the King of Kāñcī, and offered the jewelled throne of Kāñcī to Jagannātha. He brought away the Gaṇeśa the King of Kāñcī had worshipped, which had helped him in battle, and named him **Bhaṇḍa Gaṇeśa** («the false»), still famous by that name. By Jagannātha's wish he married Padmāvatī.

From Pratāparudra's Anantavarman inscription it appears that his father also defeated Nṛsiṁha, king of Karnāṭa, and brought the Deity of **Sākṣī-gopāla** to Cuttack.

## His works

A great scholar and poet, he wrote the Sanskrit drama **Abhinava-veṇī-saṁharaṇa** in the style of the *Veṇī-saṁhāra*, the **Abhinava-gīta-govinda** (H. P. Śāstrī's report, 1895-1900) and the **Mukti-cintāmaṇi**.
