## In short

Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Mahāprabhu is Śrī Kṛṣṇa Himself, who came as His own devotee to give pure love of Godhead and the holy name to everyone, without considering who was qualified: the **Kali-pāvana avatāra**, "the source of all avatāras" (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). He was born in **Māyāpura, Navadvīpa**, on the full moon of Phālguna, 1407 Śaka (1486), lived twenty-four years in Navadvīpa as Nimāi Paṇḍita, took sannyāsa from **Keśava Bhāratī** at Kāṭoyā, and spent the rest of His life travelling and then in **Purī**, where He disappeared in His forty-eighth year. His dīkṣā guru was **Īśvara Purī**, the disciple of Mādhavendra Purī. He wrote only the eight verses of the **Śikṣāṣṭaka**; the theology of His followers was set down by the Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana.

## Names

**Viśvambhara** (given by His maternal grandfather Nīlāmbara Cakravartī), **Nimāi** (from the nim tree near which He was born), **Gaura, Gaurāṅga, Gaurahari, Gorā** (for His golden colour), **Śacīnandana**, **Nimāi Paṇḍita**; after sannyāsa, **Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya**. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Madhya 28.179–181) explains the name: "You awoke the consciousness (caitanya) of everyone and made the whole world chant the name of Kṛṣṇa, therefore You are Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya." It is His foremost name: the great biographies are all called after it (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, *Caitanya-maṅgala*), even that of Locana dāsa, the preacher of Gaura's supremacy.

## Birth and family

He appeared just after sunset, during an eclipse of the moon, on the **full moon of Phālguna** — Saṁvat 1542, **Śaka 1407**, 23 Phālguna of the Bengali year 892; **18 February 1486** by the Julian calendar (27 February by the Gregorian), a Saturday — while the people of Nadia were bathing in the Gaṅgā with loud cries of "Haribol" (*Abhidhāna*, from Phaṇibhūṣaṇa Datta's *Śrī Caitanya-jātaka*, which also gives His horoscope; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, *The Life of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu*).

His father was **Jagannātha Miśra** and His mother **Śacī Devī**, brāhmaṇas whose families came from **Śrīhaṭṭa** (Sylhet). Eight daughters born to them before Him died; their elder son **Viśvarūpa** took sannyāsa young. He married first **Lakṣmīpriyā**, daughter of Vallabha Ācārya, who died of a snakebite while He was in East Bengal; then, at His mother's wish, **Viṣṇupriyā**, daughter of the rāja-paṇḍita **Sanātana Miśra**.

## Childhood and studies

As a baby He cried until the women around Him chanted "Hari". His early pastimes are told by all His biographers: He ate clay instead of sweets and argued that both were clay; He ate the offering of a travelling brāhmaṇa three times and at last showed him His form as Kṛṣṇa; two thieves who carried Him off for His ornaments were led back to His own door; He asked for and received the Ekādaśī offerings of Hiraṇya and Jagadīśa; He sat on thrown-away cooking pots and told His mother there was nothing impure in them.

He entered the ṭol of **Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita** in His eighth year, mastered grammar and rhetoric in two years, and studied smṛti and nyāya on His own. After Viśvarūpa's sannyāsa His father died. As **Nimāi Paṇḍita** He opened a school of grammar and became the foremost paṇḍita of Nadia; He defeated the "world-conqueror" **Keśava Kāśmīrī** at Bārakoṇā-ghāṭa. He travelled to East Bengal (on the Padmā, and Śrīhaṭṭa), where He taught **Tapana Miśra** and sent him to Vārāṇasī.

## Gayā and the saṅkīrtana in Navadvīpa

At sixteen or seventeen (Bhaktivinoda; other texts say a year after His tour of East Bengal) He went to **Gayā** and took dīkṣā from **Īśvara Purī**. He came back a different person: at the name of Kṛṣṇa He wept and fainted, could teach nothing but Kṛṣṇa, and closed His school, singing "haraye namaḥ kṛṣṇa yādavāya namaḥ" with His students — the beginning of the saṅkīrtana. **Nityānanda**, **Advaita**, **Gadādhara**, **Śrīvāsa** and many others gathered round Him; the nightly kīrtana was held in **Śrīvāsa's courtyard**, where He showed His divine forms (the eyewitness Murāri Gupta). He sent Nityānanda and Haridāsa from door to door to ask everyone to chant; they met **Jagāi and Mādhāi**, who were delivered. When the **Chānd Kāzī** broke a mṛdaṅga and forbade the kīrtana, He led the whole town in fourteen kīrtana parties with torches to the Kāzī's house, and the Kāzī was won over.

## Sannyāsa

At **twenty-four** He took sannyāsa from **Keśava Bhāratī** at **Kāṭoyā** and received the name Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya. He wanted to go to Vṛndāvana, but at Advaita's house in **Śāntipura** His mother asked Him to live in **Purī**, so that she would have news of Him. He went along the Gaṅgā to **Chatrabhoga**, by boat to Prayāga-ghāṭa in Midnapore, and on foot by Balasore, Cuttack and Bhuvaneśvara to Purī.

## Purī, the South, Vṛndāvana and Vārāṇasī

In Purī He stayed with **Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya**, the greatest naiyāyika and Śaṅkara-vedāntist of his day, heard his Vedānta for seven days in silence, and then explained the sūtras without Śaṅkara's commentary; Sārvabhauma became His follower. Then He toured **South India** with the brāhmaṇa **Kṛṣṇadāsa**: He cured the leper **Vāsudeva** at Kūrmakṣetra, met **Rāmānanda Rāya** on the Godāvarī, spent the four months of the rains at **Śrī Raṅgam** in the house of **Veṅkaṭa Bhaṭṭa** (whose son **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa** later became one of the six Gosvāmīs), went as far as Cape Comorin and came back by **Paṇḍharapura**; in two years He was in Purī again. **King Pratāparudra** became His devotee, and every year the devotees of Bengal came to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā.

In His twenty-eighth year He went to Bengal as far as **Rāmakeli** near Gauḍa, where the ministers of Hussain Shāh, **Sākara Mallika and Dabira Khāsa** — **Sanātana** and **Rūpa** — came to Him. He went back to Purī by Śāntipura, where He saw His mother again, and then went to **Vṛndāvana** with only **Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya**, through the forests, where tigers and elephants danced at the name of Kṛṣṇa; in Vraja He found **Rādhā-kuṇḍa and Śyāma-kuṇḍa**. At **Prayāga** He taught Rūpa (and Anupama) for ten days and met **Vallabhācārya**; at **Vārāṇasī**, staying with **Candraśekhara** and eating at **Tapana Miśra's**, He taught Sanātana for two months and won over **Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī** and the Māyāvādī sannyāsīs. He sent Rūpa and Sanātana to Vṛndāvana to write the books of bhakti and to find the lost places of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.

## The last eighteen years

From His thirty-first year He lived in Purī in the house of **Kāśī Miśra**, in deep communion with the Lord, among a few intimate companions: **Svarūpa Dāmodara** (who examined every work before it was read to Him) and **Rāmānanda Rāya**, who sang to Him, and **Paramānanda Purī**. He put **Nityānanda** in charge of preaching in Bengal and sent the Gosvāmīs to Vṛndāvana; He punished Choṭa Haridāsa, taught Raghunātha dāsa, and honoured Haridāsa Ṭhākura above caste.

He disappeared in His **forty-eighth year, in 1455 Śaka** (1533; the *Abhidhāna* gives 1534 CE) — according to Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, during saṅkīrtana in the temple of **Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha** in Purī.

## Teachings and works

His own words are the eight verses of the **Śikṣāṣṭaka**; the commentator Viṭṭhaleśvara says that the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-premāmṛta-stotra* was also spoken by Him. His teachings were written down by Rūpa, Sanātana and Jīva Gosvāmī (*Ṣaṭ-sandarbha*, *Krama-sandarbha*, *Sarva-saṁvādinī*).

His life is told in Murāri Gupta's *Kaḍacā*, Kavi Karṇapūra's *Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka* and *Caitanya-carita-mahākāvya*, the *Gaura-kṛṣṇodaya*, Prabodhānanda's *Caitanya-candrāmṛta*, the *Gaurāṅga-campū*, and in Bengali the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Vṛndāvana dāsa), *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja) and *Caitanya-maṅgala* (Locana dāsa).

The **Gaura-mantra** is in the *Ūrdhvāmnāya-tantra* (3.14–16), the *Īśāna-saṁhitā*, Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī's *Paddhati* (54–55), the *Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka* (9), the *Advaita-prakāśa* (10, 12) and the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Antya 2.31). Prayers to Him: aṣṭakas by Sārvabhauma, Narahari Sarakāra (*Śacīsutāṣṭaka*), Rūpa Gosvāmī (three *Caitanyāṣṭakas*), Prabodhānanda and Raghunātha dāsa (*Śacīsūnvaṣṭaka*); hundred-and-eight names and the *Nāma-viṁśati* by Sārvabhauma; thousand names by Narahari Sarakāra, Kavi Karṇapūra and Rūpa Gosvāmī; the *Gaurāṅga-stava-kalpataru* of Raghunātha dāsa; the *Caitanya-śataka* of Sārvabhauma; and his meditation in Dhyānacandra's *Paddhati* (72–77) and Viśvanātha Cakravartī's *Smaraṇa-maṅgala*.

## His time

When He was on earth Bahlol and Sikandar Lodī, Ibrāhīm Lodī, Bābar and Humāyūn ruled in Delhi; in Bengal Husain Shāh (1493–1519) and Nusrat Shāh; in Orissa Puruṣottama Deva and **Pratāparudra** (1497–1540); Vasco da Gama came to India (1502) and the Portuguese settled on its coasts. In Navadvīpa the great school of nyāya flourished: **Vāsudeva Sārvabhauma**, Raghunātha Śiromaṇi, Raghunandana the smārta, Kṛṣṇānanda Āgamavāgīśa (author of the *Tantra-sāra*, a contemporary of the Lord) and others (the *Abhidhāna* lists thirty-four).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** **Gaura Pūrṇimā**, the full moon of Phālguna (Gauḍīya calendar); 1407 Śaka, 18 February 1486.
**Sannyāsa:** kept in the Gauḍīya calendar as «Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's sannyāsa day».
**Disappearance:** 1455 Śaka, in His forty-eighth year, in Purī.
