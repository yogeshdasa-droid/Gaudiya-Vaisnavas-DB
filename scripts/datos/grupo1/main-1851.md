## In short

Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu is **Balarāma** (Halāyudha) Himself, the first expansion (prakāśa) of the Lord (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 1.7–11), the object of worship of all Gauḍīyas (Ādi 1.18–19) and, with Advaita, "the trunk of the tree of bhakti" (Ādi 9.21, 10.115). He was an **avadhūta** who wandered over all India until His twentieth year, joined Śrī Caitanya in Navadvīpa, delivered Jagāi and Mādhāi, and was sent by the Lord to give the holy name to Bengal; He later married and lived at **Khaḍadaha**. "By His mercy alone the glories of Śrī Caitanya are understood" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*).

## Birth and family

He appeared on the **thirteenth day of the bright fortnight of Māgha, 1395 Śaka** (1473/1474 CE), at **Ekacakrā** in Birbhum (Rāḍha-deśa). His father was **Hāḍāi Paṇḍita** (Hāḍo Ojhā; also called Mukunda), his mother **Padmāvatī**; his grandfather was **Sundarāmalla Nakaḍi Bāḍurī**, a Rāḍhī-śreṇī brāhmaṇa of the Śāṇḍilya gotra. His former name was **Kuvera** (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). Buchanan Hamilton recorded a short history of the family in his Purnea Report. At His birth all famine and misery left Rāḍha; and on the day Śrī Gaurāṅga was born in Navadvīpa He roared with joy, and the sound filled the universe (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 9).

## Childhood

For twelve years He played with the boys of Ekacakrā at nothing but the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, Rāma and the other avatāras: the petition of the earth to Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, Kṛṣṇa's birth in Kaṁsa's prison, Pūtanā, the cart, the butter, the killing of Dhenuka, Baka and Agha, the lifting of Govardhana, Akrūra taking Kṛṣṇa away (when He wept like the gopīs), Kaṁsa's death; Vāmana and Bali; Rāma's bridge, and Lakṣmaṇa struck down by Rāvaṇa — playing Lakṣmaṇa He lay lifeless until the boy playing Hanumān brought the medicine from Gandhamādana, and His parents fainted. Asked where He had learnt all this, He laughed: "These are all My pastimes" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 9.12–99). A later narrative in His record tells how a sannyāsī asked His parents for Him and took Him away at twelve, and of the grief of Hāḍāi and Padmāvatī, who lived on calling His name.

## Twenty years of pilgrimage

Until His twentieth year He went round the holy places of all India: Vakreśvara, Vaidyanātha, Gayā, Kāśī, Prayāga in Māgha, Mathurā and the twelve forests of Vṛndāvana, Hastināpura, Dvārakā, Siddhapura, Kurukṣetra, Naimiṣāraṇya, Ayodhyā and the land of Guhaka (where He lay three days in ecstasy), Haridvāra, Śrī-śaila (where Śiva and Pārvatī, as a brāhmaṇa couple, cooked for Him), Veṅkaṭa, Kāñcī, Śrī Raṅgam, Setubandha, Badarikāśrama (where Vyāsa received Him as Balarāma), Kanyākumārī, Gokarṇa, Kerala and many more (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 9.100–236).

On the way He met **Mādhavendra Purī** with his disciples **Īśvara Purī** and **Brahmānanda Purī**; both fainted in love at the sight of each other and travelled together for days. Mādhavendra said: "Wherever there is the company of Nityānanda, that place is more than Vaikuṇṭha." **Mādhavendra treated Him as a friend, and He revered Mādhavendra as His guru** (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*). He went on by Setubandha, Vijayanagara and Kūrmakṣetra to **Jagannātha Purī**, then to the meeting of the Gaṅgā and the sea, and back to **Vṛndāvana**, where He waited, living on a little milk, for Śrī Gauracandra to reveal Himself.

## With Śrī Caitanya in Navadvīpa

He came to Navadvīpa to the house of **Nandana Ācārya** and met the Lord (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 3.120–4.76). The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* tells of His **Vyāsa-pūjā** of the Lord and the Lord's six-armed form (Madhya 5); His meeting with Advaita; His stay as a child in **Śrīvāsa's** house in the care of Mālinī, and His meals at Śacī's; the Lord's abhiṣeka and the giving of His foot-water; the deliverance of **Jagāi and Mādhāi**; the drama; the umbrella He held at the Lord's Mahā-prakāśa and the city kīrtana; the vision of the universal form; and the Lord's sannyāsa, when He brought Śacī to Śāntipura and broke the Lord's daṇḍa (Madhya 3–28; Antya 1–2). The *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* adds that He heard from the Lord the story of Mādhavendra and told the story of Sākṣi-gopāla (Madhya 4–5).

## Preaching in Bengal

In Purī the Lord spoke with Him alone and sent Him to **Gauḍa** to give the holy name to everyone. At **Pāṇihāṭi** He danced in ecstasy, was bathed (abhiṣeka) and wore a garland of kadamba flowers and rich ornaments; He played the dāna-līlā, wandered through **Saptagrāma**, Śāntipura and Navadvīpa, where He saw Śacī; He delivered thieves and dacoits who came to rob His ornaments and removed the doubts of a brāhmaṇa (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5–6). He came each year to Nīlācala, took his meal at **Gadādhara's** temple and played in the water at Narendra-sarovara (Antya 7–8). The *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* tells of the Lord appearing in His dance (Antya 2.34, 80), of **Rāmacandra Khān's** offence (Antya 3.147–155), of **Raghunātha dāsa's** curd-and-flat-rice festival at Pāṇihāṭi (Antya 6.42–154) and of His mercy-kick to **Śivānanda Sena** on the way to Purī (Antya 12.19–78).

## Khaḍadaha, wives and children

According to the *Prema-vilāsa* He married and settled at **Khaḍadaha** with **Vasudhā** and **Jāhnavā**, daughters of **Sūryadāsa Sarakhela**. Seven sons were born and died as soon as **Abhirāma** offered them his obeisances; then **Vīracandra (Vīrabhadra)** and **Gaṅgā** were born, and lived. Gaṅgā married **Mādhava Ācārya**. Abhirāma's hymn to Gaṅgā Devī is part of this account.

## Guru

The sources do not agree: the *Abhidhāna* calls Him a disciple of **Mādhavendra Purī** (the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* shows Him revering Mādhavendra as His guru); the *Bhakti-ratnākara* says He took dīkṣā from **Lakṣmīpati Tīrtha** (the line followed by the Gauḍīya Maṭha); the *Prema-vilāsa* (24) names **Īśvara Purī**; Jīva Gosvāmī, **Saṅkarṣaṇa Purī**, a disciple of Mādhavendra.

## Nityānanda-tattva

**Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa**, **Śeṣa** and the other expansions of Balarāma (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 63–64); as Ananta Śeṣa He becomes the Lord's couch, garments and ornaments. He is the **sandhinī-śakti**; **Anaṅga-mañjarī** entered Him (*Anaṅga-mañjarī-sampuṭikā*); outwardly He is prakṛti, openly puruṣa (the Dharaṇī–Śeṣa dialogue of the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*; Vṛndāvana dāsa's *Aiśvaryāmṛta-kāvya*; the *Rasa-kalpa-sāra-tattva*).

## Mantra, prayers and books

His **mantra** is in the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* and Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī's *Paddhati* (56–57), with His meditation and **gāyatrī** (*Paddhati* 50, 72). **Aṣṭakas** by Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya and Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura; a *Nāma-dvādaśaka* by Sārvabhauma; **a hundred and eight names** in the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* and by Sārvabhauma. Works about Him: the Sanskrit *Nityānanda-prabhor aiśvaryāmṛta-kāvyam* (128 ślokas, ascribed to Vṛndāvana dāsa; Barānagar Pāṭhbāḍī manuscript copied in 1853) and the *Rasa-kalpa-sāra-tattva*; the *Nityānanda-vaṁśa-vistāra* (Vīracandra as avatāra, his family, Jāhnavā's pilgrimage to Vṛndāvana). **Rāmarāya**, His disciple, wrote the *Nityānanda-bhāṣya* on the Śikṣāṣṭaka.

## His associates

The **twelve gopālas** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 126–132): Abhirāma Ṭhākura (Śrīdāmā), Sundarānanda (Sudāmā), Dhanañjaya (Vasudāmā), Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita (Subala), Kamalākara Pippalāi (Mahābala), Uddhāraṇa Datta (Subāhu), Maheśa Paṇḍita (Mahābāhu), Puruṣottama dāsa (Dāma), Parameśvara dāsa (Arjuna), Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa (Lavaṅga), Kholāvecā Śrīdhara (Kusumāsava) and others; and Gadādhara dāsa, Purandara Paṇḍita, Raghunātha Vaidya and the branch of Nityānanda in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 11).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** **Nityānanda Trayodaśī**, Māgha śukla trayodaśī (Gauḍīya calendar); 1395 Śaka, at Ekacakrā.
**Disappearance:** not given in the *Abhidhāna* nor in the Gauḍīya calendar.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Balarāma** (Halāyudha); as Saṅkarṣaṇa and Ananta Śeṣa (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 59–64).
