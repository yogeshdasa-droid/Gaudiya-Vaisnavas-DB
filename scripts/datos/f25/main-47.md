## In short

**Ananta Purī**, named in Abhirāma dāsa's *Pāṭa-paryaṭana*, had his śrīpāṭa at **Baḍabeluna** (Burdwan), where he began the service of the Deity **Gopīnātha** before Lord Gaurāṅga's birth. The Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā (96-97) counts him among the Purīs, as the perfection **Aṇimā-siddhi**. He passed away on the **śukla aṣṭamī of Agrahāyaṇa**.

## Gopīnātha of Baḍabeluna

The standards he set in the Deity's service, the care of guests and the celebration of festivals went on long after his death. Later, at Mahārāja Mānasiṁha's recommendation, the Emperor of Delhi granted 409 bighās of land to the Gopīnātha temple; Kṛṣṇarāma Rāya, Rājā of Burdwan, gave 200 bighās rent-free, and his successor Rājā Tejaścandra 163 rupees a year.

Ananta Purī commanded **Rādhāvallabha Rāya**, of the village of Bāṅkuḍā south-east of Baḍabeluna, in a dream (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna), to install a Deity of Rādhā on Gopīnātha's left. It is said that **Rājā Rāmacandra Rāya**, leader of the dacoits of Bhāṭākula (Burdwan), came to steal the temple's jewels; but on seeing the Deities he was captivated by Their beauty, lost all wish to rob Them, and fled only after making over 100 bighās of rent-free land between Bhāṭākula and Baḍabeluna.
