## In short

**Kṛṣṇadeva Rāya**, king of **Vijayanagara**, invaded Mahārāja **Pratāparudra**'s kingdom three or four times and occupied parts of it, until Pratāparudra made an alliance with him, giving him his daughter **Jaganmohinī-devī** (Tukkā) in marriage with the land south of the river Kṛṣṇā as dowry. A patron of the arts, he wrote in Telugu and Sanskrit; his most famous work is the **Āmukta-mālyadā**.

## The Āmukta-mālyadā

Also called *Viṣṇucittīya*, it is based on a Tamil Vaiṣṇava tradition: a girl who fell in love with the Deity used to offer Him the garlands she had first worn herself, and the priest **Viṣṇucitta** was told by the Deity in a dream to bring her to **Śrīraṅgam**, where she was to marry Him. Though essentially a religious poem, it throws light on the social and economic life of the time (*An Encyclopedia of South Indian Culture*, Calcutta 1983, pp. 237-238).
