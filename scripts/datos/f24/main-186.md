## In short

**Danujamardana**, a kāyastha and high official of King **Gaṇeśa** of Gauḍa, declared himself independent after Gaṇeśa's son embraced Islam, and became king of **Pāṇḍunagara** (Pāṇḍuā), where Gaṇeśa's Hindu ministers took refuge; **Padmanābha**, the ancestor of Rūpa and Sanātana (#1997), came to live in his kingdom at **Navahaṭṭa** (Naihāṭī). Driven out by the Pathans, he founded the kingdom of **Candradvīpa**; the old royal family of Bāklā-Candradvīpa (Barisal) descends from him.

## Gaṇeśa's kingdom

The record says that the zamindār **Gaṇeśa** of Bhāturiyā Paragaṇā, on the advice of his chief counsellor Narasiṁha Nāḍiyāl, had Sultan Śamsuddin killed and took the throne of Gauḍa; his court was adorned by Padmanābha, Narasiṁha Nāḍiyāl, the poet Kṛttivāsa and others. After his death his son **Yadu** embraced Islam as **Jalāluddīn** and took the throne, ending Gaṇeśa's dream of a Hindu kingdom.

## Pāṇḍuā and Candradvīpa

Then Danujamardana declared his independence and became king of Pāṇḍunagara, and all Gaṇeśa's Hindu ministers took shelter with him; the new kingdom was in turmoil for years. Padmanābha moved his family to safety and came alone to Navahaṭṭa, in Danujamardana's land, to spend the rest of his life by the Gaṅgā. After three years Danujamardana was defeated by the Pathans and driven from Pāṇḍuā; he went east with his army and set up his kingdom at Candradvīpa. Coins bearing his name, dated 1339-40 Śaka (1417-18), are kept in the Baṅgīya Sāhitya Pariṣat.

## Sources

The record's text. (It dates Gaṇeśa's coup to 1485 and Padmanābha's move to 1495, but Danujamardana's coins are of 1417-18; the events must be earlier.)
