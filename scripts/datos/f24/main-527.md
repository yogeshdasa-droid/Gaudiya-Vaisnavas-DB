## In short

**Jagadeva Pramāra**, an exalted Vaiṣṇava of the **Bhaktamāla**, famed for his honesty, refused to marry the princess of his land; so overwhelmed was he by a singer's Harināma that he cut off his own head and offered it to the artist. The princess, vowing to marry no one else, placed the head back on his body, and he came to life and at last married her; later he renounced home.

## The princess

Charmed by what she heard of his character, the princess of the state where he lived wanted to marry him. The king agreed, invited Jagadeva to the palace and treated him kindly, but Jagadeva, averse to worldly pleasures, refused. The princess vowed to marry no one but him, and the king, to win him over, had a supremely gifted professional artist sing Harināma. Hearing the Lord's glories sung so beautifully, Jagadeva was so overwhelmed that he cut off his head and presented it to the singer in token of appreciation.

The grief-stricken princess put his head on a golden platter and gazed at it; it is said (Viśvakoṣa) that the face kept turning upside down, as if to keep his vow against marriage, and however she tried she could not keep it facing her. At last she put the head back in its place on his body, and he came to life again. Seeing the princess's Vaiṣṇava feelings and the sincerity of her prayers, Jagadeva consented to marry her. He lived in family life for some time, and later renounced home.
