## In short

**Kulaśekhara**, king of **Kerala** and «crest-jewel of Vaiṣṇava kings», wrote the **Mukunda-mālā-stotra**, 53 verses of bhakti-rasa quoted in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya 13.78) and the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (2.6.29). Under his patronage Vaiṣṇavism spread in the South before Śaṅkara's time. He seems to be the same as the Ālvār **Kulaśekhara**, whose Tamil hymns are in the Nālāyira Prabandham, though some scholars keep the two apart.

## The king

He invited Vaiṣṇava scholars from other provinces to check the advance of Buddhism and other non-devotional schools, and built the Kulaśekhara temple in a suburb of Kraṅganore, another at Kulaśekharapuram, and a Vaiṣṇava maṭha at Kumbakonam. Prabhākara is said to have been employed by him to fight Buddhism; Prabhākara came before Padmapāda, Śaṅkara's disciple, and in Śaṅkara's lifetime the kings were Rājarāja and Rājaśekhara, not Kulaśekhara. Since Kulaśekhara says in the Mukunda-mālā that he was a king, he seems to have lived before Śaṅkara.

## The Mukunda-mālā

The **Mukunda-mālā-stotra** has 53 verses on bhakti-rasa; Veṅkaṭeśa and Ānandarāghava wrote ṭīkās on it, and verses of it are quoted in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya 13.78) and the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (2.6.29).
