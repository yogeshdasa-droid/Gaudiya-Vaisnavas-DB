## In short

**Prabhu Jagadbandhu Sundara** (Jagadbandhu Prabhu, "Bandhu"), born on **Sītā-navamī, 17 Vaiśākha 1871**, at **Dāhāpāḍā** on the Ganges (Murshidabad), led a great movement of nāma-saṅkīrtana in Bengal, lifting the outcast and the low-caste — the Ḍoms of Calcutta, the Bāgdis of Faridpur, the tribal people of Jessore and Khulna — into the chanting of Harināma. He founded the śrī-aṅganas of Brahmankāṇḍā, Bākcara and **Goālcāmaṭ** (Faridpur), lived long in silence (mauna), and passed away on the **first day of Āśvina, 1328 BS (1921)**.

## Childhood

His father was **Dīnanātha Nyāyaratna** and his mother **Vāmāsundarī**. He lost his mother at one and his father at seven, and was brought up by his father's elder brother **Bhairava Cakravartī** at Faridpur; he studied at the Baṅga Vidyālaya and the Faridpur district school, then at Ranchi and at the Pabna district school up to the entrance examination. Very handsome and gifted with every auspicious quality, he loved Harināma from childhood; he kept the three daily baths, the āhnika, self-control and brahmacarya all his life, spoke softly, avoided enjoyment and preferred solitude. Watching open-air plays of Prahlāda and Dhruva he forgot everything around him; he spent long hours in detachment at Kālīkadambatalā and the temple of Jayakālī, and the sāttvika-vikāras of ecstasy appeared in him in kīrtana. Some attacked and tortured him for his preaching; many respected people followed him.

## The saṅkīrtana

From Pabna he went to Vṛndāvana and came back by Calcutta to **Brahmankāṇḍā** (Faridpur), where he founded a kīrtana-sampradāya and held every year a nagara-saṅkīrtana of seven sampradāyas with fourteen mādalas, and kīrtana processions day and night; with his devotees he went to Navadvīpa and sang his own padas. He lived for a time at the śrī-aṅgana of **Bākcara** (built 1889), and from 1899 at **Goālcāmaṭ**, Faridpur, where his power and his Harināma drew people of every community. He named a low-caste sardār, **Rajanī Bāgdi**, "Haridāsa Pāśa (mahānta)", and his group became the **Mahānta-sampradāya**, through which even wild tribes of Jessore and Khulna became devotees. In Calcutta he raised the Ḍom people of Cāsādhopāpāḍā and Rāmbāgān and gave them the right to sing Harināma. Wearing sometimes a rosary on a gold wire, sometimes tulasī beads, rubber sandals and his body fully covered, he travelled in India and abroad in complete silence, and lived sixteen years at Goālcāmaṭ (1902–1918), long in a place without sunlight, in mauna.

## His devotees

The foremost were Pratāpa Bhaumika, Rameśa Cakravartī, **Rādhikā Gupta** (later Rāmadāsa Bābājī), **Atula Campati**, Jayanitāi (Devena Cakravartī), Tārakā Gāṅgulī of Koāghāṭ, Dr Uṣā Majumdar and Navadvīpa dāsa. His books: *Candrapāṭa*, *Hari-kathā*, *Śrīmatī-saṅkīrtana*, *Śrī Śrī Nāma-saṅkīrtana*, *Vividha-saṅgīta*, *Padāvalī* and *Trikāla-grantha*.

## His passing

His own texts give his passing as the first of Āśvina **1931**; the record of Atula Campati, written by Haridāsa dāsa, who knew them, gives **1 Āśvina 1328 BS (1921)**, which agrees with the rest of his life — the 1931 is surely a misprint.

## Sources

The record's texts; the records of Atula Candra Campati and Rāmadāsa Bābājī (Haridāsa dāsa; *Śrī Bandhu-līlā-taraṅgiṇī*). He is not the Jagadbandhu Prabhu of Advaita's family, brother of Vṛndāvanacandra Gosvāmī, who took veśa at Katwa (record of Gaura Kiśora Śiromaṇi).
