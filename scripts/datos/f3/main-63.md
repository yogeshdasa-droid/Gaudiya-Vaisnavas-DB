## In short

**Atula Candra Campati** of Calcutta, a brilliant B.A. (honours in English, Sanskrit and mathematics) and a headmaster, became the most ecstatic devotee of **Prabhu Jagadbandhu Sundara**, his wife's maternal uncle. He gave up his career, wandered the streets of Calcutta chanting Harināma, begged among outcasts and prostitutes "as mothers of Vraja", and passed away in **1925**. Haridāsa dāsa, the author of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna, knew him and wrote his life.

## The sign on the rails

He lived at 1 Madan Mitra Lane, North Calcutta, a favourite student of the mathematician Gaurīśaṅkara Bābu. He married **Kṣīrodā**, only daughter of Digambarī Devī, Jagadbandhu's sister, and so met Bandhu at his wedding. When his daughter Sarajū died while he was headmaster of the Arrah High School, Bandhu came unannounced to console him and told him privately: "The only substance in this unreal world is Kṛṣṇa-bhajana. Do not be caught in māyā; you are to help me in my mission." Later, during a quarrel with his assistant teacher, a letter came from Bandhu at Dāhāpāḍā telling him to stop quarrelling and be absorbed in love. To be sure whether Bandhu was God, one dark night he lay on the rails in a tunnel: if the Bombay Mail stopped five feet from him, it would be Bandhu's doing — and the train stopped a few feet away, through some mechanical trouble. He leapt up shouting "Jaya Prabhu Jagadbandhu!", and his career as a teacher was over (told by Atula himself to Haridāsa dāsa).

## Bandhu's orders

In Calcutta, in saffron cloth, he searched the streets for Bandhu chanting, heedless of food or rest, until on Strand Road he heard a voice from a closed carriage — "Hare Kṛṣṇa, come to me" — jumped on its roof and rode chanting to 67 Cāsādhopāpāḍā Lane. Bandhu gave him two rupees to buy a set of loincloths and cymbals, and told him: bathe at Jagannātha-ghāṭa at dawn, put off the saffron, go through the streets with the cymbals chanting "Kṛṣṇa Govinda Gopāla Śyāma", bathe at Kālīghāṭ and again at Jagannātha-ghāṭa, day and night as long as you can; Govinda will feed you; then you will see a great soul. He did it, forgetting family, birth, learning and status, and afterwards, at a festival of Bandhu's appearance among the Ḍoms of Rāmbāgān, embraced everyone. Bandhu took him to Pabna and left him with the "mad" mahāpuruṣa **Haraṇa Kṣepā**, who made him eat the leftovers of one Vāṅku Maṇḍala, embraced him, revealed Bandhu's true identity and blessed him with great power (*Śrī Bandhu-līlā-taraṅgiṇī*). Once he picked up Bandhu's spittle from a drain and swallowed it like nectar; Bandhu gave him a "Magna Carta": from then on he might do as he pleased, and Bandhu would answer for it.

## Among the outcasts

Dressed as an outcast he went from door to door by the wine shops and the prostitutes' quarters, chanting at the top of his voice, "to reclaim the outcasts". He supervised the service at Bandhu's śrī-aṅgana at **Goālcāmaṭ** (Faridpur), where from 1910 to 1912 his wife cooked the bhoga in silence, her nose covered with a cloth. After Bandhu's passing (1 Āśvina 1328, 1921) he lived mostly in Madan Mitra Lane, wandering the streets in kīrtana, oblivious of clothes, food and rest; his kīrtana was loved by the whole city.

**Vipina Bihārī Caṭṭopādhyāya**, the munsiff of Mākaḍadaha, was his schoolfellow and friend: they would quarrel in jest, then sing and dance together. Once at Kāśī, in a quarrel, Vipina had a barber shave half his head, moustache and beard; Atula went out singing in the streets as if nothing had happened, and Vipina told his junior Giridhārī dāsa: "This is called nirabhimāna; without it no one reaches God." Vipina died in Atula's lap.

## "Bhakti is attained through bhikṣā"

He respected all religions. Seeing Haridāsa dāsa pale on an Ekādaśī, he begged a passer-by, who brought him rābḍi; then he took him begging — eight annas "for a brāhmaṇa boy with nothing to eat" (he got two), then into the Sonāgāchi quarter, falling at the doorsteps chanting "Haribol! Jaya Prabhu Jagadbandhu Sundara! Jaya sundara bāijī!" while the women abused him. When the boy thought him mad he turned: "You fool, bhakti is attained through bhikṣā." At Rāmbāgān, a cloth round his neck, he begged of each poor Ḍom hut as of "mothers of Vraja" and they gladly gave half a paisa each. Asked for a blessing, he wanted sixteen annas; told the boy had nothing but his pride, he said: "Born from urine and dead when the breath stops — what is the value of pride?"
