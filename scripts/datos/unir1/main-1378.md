## In short

Sāraṅga Ṭhākura (Sāraṅgadāsa Ṭhākura, Sāraṅgadeva) was a branch of the tree of Śrī Caitanya: "Bhāgavatācārya, Ṭhākura Sāraṅgadāsa…" (Caitanya-caritāmṛta Ādi 10.113). In Vraja he was **Nāndīmukhī** (Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā 172; some say Prahlāda). He is remembered as the guru who gave dīkṣā to a dead boy on the Lord's order, the boy who lived again and became **Ṭhākura Murāri**; their service under an old bakula tree at **Māmgāchi** (Modadruma-dvīpa, Navadvīpa) still goes on.

The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna has two entries, Sāraṅgadāsa Ṭhākura and Sāraṅgadeva, and says: "Sāraṅgadeva and he are probably the same person." Bimanbihari Majumdar thinks so too. Here they are one record.

## Life

He lived in Navadvīpa under a bakula tree. By day he gathered what he needed for the worship of his Deities, and in the evening he crossed the Gaṅgā to join the kīrtana of Śrī Caitanya.

One day Śrī Gaurāṅga, coming home with Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita after rebuking Devānanda Paṇḍita, met Sāraṅgadeva and asked him: "Why do you not make disciples?" "I do not find anyone fit," he answered. The Lord said: "Whoever you take as a disciple will be fit." "Since that is your order," said Sāraṅgadeva, "I will make my disciple the first person I meet tomorrow," and he bowed down and went away.

The next morning, while bathing in the Gaṅgā, he saw the body of a young boy floating by on a raft. Obeying the Lord, he gave him dīkṣā; when the mantra entered his ear the boy came back to life. He had died of a snake bite on the day of his sacred-thread ceremony, and, as was the custom then, his family had put him in the river instead of burning him. His name was **Murāri**. When his parents heard that he was alive they came to take him home, but he would not go: he stayed to serve Sāraṅga for the rest of his life, and later became famous as **Ṭhākura Murāri** (Sāraṅga Murāri).

Some books call this disciple Murāri Gupta, but Murāri Gupta the physician of Śrīhaṭṭa is another person.

## Places

- **Māmgāchi** (Jannagar or Māmgāchi; Modadruma-dvīpa, Navadvīpa): his śrīpāṭa; the service begun by Murāri is still kept beneath a very old bakula tree (Śrī Śrī Gaurasundara, p. 113).
- He was born at Budhan; according to Abhirāma he was from Kuliyā.
- His samādhi is at Garbetā (Midnapore).
- Ṭhākura Murāri's family line still lives at Śar (Sār) village in Bardhamān.

## Remembered

- His departure: **Agrahāyaṇa Kṛṣṇa Trayodaśī** (Gauḍīya calendar), the day after Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa's.
