## In short

Śrī **Govinda Ghoṣa** (Ghoṣa Ṭhākura) of **Agradvīpa** was one of three brothers, all great kīrtana singers and branches of Lord Caitanya: "The three brothers Govinda, Mādhava and Vāsudeva were the eighty-second, eighty-third and eighty-fourth branches of the tree. Lord Caitanya and Nityānanda used to dance in their kīrtana" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.115). He installed the Deity of **Śrī Gopīnātha** at Agradvīpa, who still performs his śrāddha every year. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā the three brothers were **Kalāvatī**, **Rasollāsā** and **Guṇatuṅgā**, who sang the songs composed by Viśākhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 188); Govinda was Kalāvatī.

## The three brothers

They were uttara-rāḍhīya Kāyasthas. Their mother's family, some say, was at Buḍhan (Burangi) in Śrīhaṭṭa; their father later lived at **Kumārahaṭṭa**, and after his passing the brothers came to **Navadvīpa**. Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura says they are expansions of Śrīmatī Rādhikā's body, in mādhurya-rasa, with Rādhikā as their shelter. All three wrote sweet songs on Gaura's childhood, His saṅkīrtana and sannyāsa, and the beauty of Nityānanda; "Nityānanda Prabhu Himself would dance as they sang" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.259).

When the Lord sent Nityānanda to preach in Bengal, Mādhava and Vāsudeva went with Him, "but Govinda Ghoṣa remained with the Lord and was very satisfied" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.117-118). At the Ratha-yātrā in Purī the Lord made Govinda Ghoṣa the lead singer of one of the kīrtana parties, with Choṭa Haridāsa, Viṣṇudāsa, Rāghava Paṇḍita and his two brothers singing the response, and Vakreśvara Paṇḍita as the dancer (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 13.42-43). Every year the brothers came to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā; later Govinda lived at Agradvīpa, Mādhava at Dāiṅhāṭ and Vāsudeva at Tamluk. The *Govindānanda* of the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya 8.16) is probably he (Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna).

## The harītakī and Śrī Gopīnātha

When Mahāprabhu set out from Nīlācala for Vṛndāvana, Govinda went with Him. One day after lunch the Lord held out His hand for some **harītakī**, and Govinda ran to the village to fetch it. The next day he gave it at once: he had kept some from the day before. "Govinda, you have not given up the habit of saving things: stay here, become a householder and manifest the service of Gopīnātha" (Acyutacaraṇa Caudhurī, *Śrīhaṭṭera Itivṛtta*). Govinda stayed at Agradvīpa, unhappy to be left behind, but the Lord comforted him.

A few days later, while he was bathing in the Ganges, something heavy, like wood, touched his back. That night he dreamt, "Keep this carefully and give it to the Lord when He comes"; going to fetch it, he saw it was a **kṛṣṇa-śilā**. The next morning the Lord came to his house: "Govinda, you have nothing more to worry about. Tomorrow a sculptor will come and carve a Deity from this stone, and you will install Him." So Śrī Gopīnātha was installed.

## Gopīnātha's promise

By the Lord's order Govinda married and served Gopīnātha with his wife; a son was born, but first his wife and then his son passed away. In his grief he stopped the service, lay down and cooked nothing. That night Gopīnātha came — one text says He massaged his feet — and said, "When a man loses one son, should he starve the other to death?" — "I had only one son." — "Am I not also your son?" — "Now no one will offer the śrāddha and the piṇḍa for me and my forefathers." — "I promise: every year I will perform your śrāddha on the day of your death. Now give Me something to eat." Govinda got up happily and cooked for Him. (One text says simply that he had no children.)

When he died — he had asked not to be cremated but buried beside the **dola-prāṅgaṇa** — Gopīnātha took kuśa grass in His fingers and performed his śrāddha; to this day, on his disappearance day, the Deity is dressed in plain white cloth and offers the piṇḍa with a ring of kuśa on His finger. Gopīnātha's temple is a rickshaw ride from Agradvīpa station on the Navadvīpa-Katwa line.
