## In short

Śrī **Devānanda Paṇḍita** of **Kuliyā** (Koladvīpa, Navadvīpa) was a famous teacher of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam whom Mahāprabhu first rebuked and later blessed, using him to show the world the glory of the Bhāgavatam and of the devotee: "Devānanda, the Bhāgavata reciter, by the mercy of Vakreśvara received from the Lord the devotional meaning of the Bhāgavatam" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.77). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Bhāguri Muni**, the sabhā-paṇḍita who recited the Vedic literatures in the house of Nanda Mahārāja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 104). He lived in the same village as Maheśvara Viśārada, the father of Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 21).

## The offence to Śrīvāsa

Before the Lord's appearance, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita once came to hear Devānanda's recitation; hundreds of students sat around him, some following in their own manuscripts. When the nectar of the Bhāgavatam entered Śrīvāsa's ears he wept and rolled on the ground, and the students said, "This fellow is mad; he is disturbing us," and carried him out. Devānanda saw it and did not stop them: "as the guru was ignorant, so his students were sinful." Śrīvāsa went home sad and told no one. Years later, when Mahāprabhu revealed Himself in Śrīvāsa's house, He asked him, "Do you remember the day you went to hear the Bhāgavatam at Devānanda's house?", and Śrīvāsa fell at His feet in wonder.

## "What does this fellow know?"

One day, walking through Nadīyā, the Lord passed Devānanda's house as he was explaining the Bhāgavatam and became angry: "What does this fellow know of the Bhāgavatam? In none of his births has he understood even one verse. The Bhāgavatam is Kṛṣṇa Himself in the form of a book; its only subject is bhakti. The four Vedas are milk, the Bhāgavatam is the butter; Śukadeva churned it and Parīkṣit ate it. Whoever sees a difference between Me, My devotees and the Bhāgavatam is destroyed" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 21.13). He turned to go, and to the devotees who followed He said: "To understand the book Bhāgavata, one has to serve the devotee Bhāgavata." Devānanda heard it all from a distance, but paid no attention.

## Vakreśvara's mercy

After the Lord took sannyāsa and went to Nīlācala, Devānanda began to feel remorse: "Such a great soul, full of love of God, came near my house, and I never once went to Him." One evening **Vakreśvara Paṇḍita** came to Kuliyā and sang and danced at a devotee's house through the night. Devānanda was stunned by his effulgence; as the crowd grew he took a cane and held it back so that Vakreśvara's dancing would not be disturbed, and when Vakreśvara fell in ecstasy he took his head on his lap, wiped the dust from his body with his own cloth and smeared that dust on himself. That day his service to the devotees began.

## Forgiveness at Kuliyā

Five years after His sannyāsa the Lord came back to Navadvīpa, and at Kuliyā — the place of the **aparādha-bhañjana** — He forgave all who had offended Him. Devānanda bowed down from a distance and stood aside. The Lord said, "Because you have served My dear Vakreśvara, I am pleased with you; Kṛṣṇa's full potency is in him, and whoever serves him gets Kṛṣṇa's mercy." Devānanda begged, in a faltering voice, to be taught the real meaning of the Bhāgavatam, and the Lord answered: "In its beginning, middle and end the Bhāgavatam teaches only devotion to Viṣṇu. Like Kṛṣṇa's avatāras, it appears and disappears by its own will; it came from Vyāsa's mouth by Kṛṣṇa's mercy. Whoever thinks himself learned does not understand it; whoever remembers it, knowing himself ignorant, does. Now go and beg forgiveness at Śrīvāsa's feet: the book Bhāgavata and the devotee Bhāgavata are not different" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 3.505-516). Devānanda fell at Śrīvāsa's feet; Śrīvāsa embraced him and his offence went far away, and from that day he was counted a great devotee among the Lord's followers.

His disappearance is on the **kṛṣṇa ekādaśī of Pauṣa**.
