## In short

**Sundarānanda Vidyāvinoda** (**Subodha Bābu**), a gṛhastha scholar, was one of the two disciples Śrīla **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** called his hands: "Sundarānanda is my right hand, and my other hand is Ananta Vāsudeva." He was the editor of the **Gauḍīya** magazine, later the secretary of the **Gauḍīya Mission**, and was to complete the **Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā**. Long after his guru's departure he took "re-initiation" from Kanupriya Gosvāmī of Navadvīpa.

## The two hands of Sarasvatī Ṭhākura

Ananta Vāsudeva and Sundarānanda were liked by almost everyone for their pleasing ways; scholars and writers absorbed in spiritual subjects, they helped Sarasvatī Ṭhākura in his writing. In Darjeeling he said: "Whatever I have told and whatever I have not told will be explained by Ananta Vāsudeva and Sundarānanda Vidyāvinoda." When, while speaking, he could not remember a śloka, he would ask one of them, and they would supply it at once. Under his instruction the two tried to print the **Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā** ("treasure-chest"), an encyclopedia of Vaiṣṇavism, at the Kṛṣṇanagara press, and helped him with books such as the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* with the *Anubhāṣya*.

## "He is Vyāsa"

Subodha Bābu met Sarasvatī Ṭhākura and was initiated around **1929**, at the time of the parikramā. Chosen as a very sincere gṛhastha devotee to write many books, he gave his life to his guru's work, who engaged him, a paṇḍita, in research. The *Gauḍīya* magazine began with him as its editor. He was also a good speaker: at public meetings in the great halls of Calcutta, such as the Albert Hall, Sarasvatī Ṭhākura would preside and have Sundarānanda speak as well as himself. Some sannyāsīs resented that a gṛhastha was encouraged, and Sarasvatī Ṭhākura answered: "He is **Vyāsa**!"; from then on many books outside the Gauḍīya Maṭha were reviewed by him in the *Gauḍīya*. When the sannyāsīs objected again to his lecture at the Albert Hall, he said: "Sundarānanda may be a gṛhī, but he is accepted by me. Kṛṣṇa will be pleased. Your lecture will be boasting, a competition to show you are better than he and than each other. But Kṛṣṇa will be pleased with him — yasmin tuṣṭe jagat tuṣṭam." He also said: "One who stands against Sundarānanda is a-sundara." Sarasvatī Ṭhākura left him the task of completing the Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā, but it was never finished.

## Later

Long after Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's departure, Sundarānanda came to disagree with some of his teachings, especially that the sampradāya is in the Madhva line. He put himself under **Kanupriya Gosvāmī** of Navadvīpa and took "re-initiation" in Vṛndāvana, outside the line of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura — though he himself, it seems, denied rejecting his guru. His father had been a disciple of Kanupriya Gosvāmī's father, a scholar and devotee whom Sarasvatī Ṭhākura respected; Kanupriya too was a scholar and wrote many books.
