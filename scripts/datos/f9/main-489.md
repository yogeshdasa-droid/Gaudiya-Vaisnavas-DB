## In short

**Harisundara Bhaumik** (Bhuiṅyā) of **Kāyāda**, near Sirājganj (Pabna), was a householder who, just as he began to earn his living, was carried away by bhakti and reached bhāva-bhakti. **Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya Bāhādur** took him as his **śikṣā-guru** and kept him with his family, first at Banwārīnagara and from 1894 in Vṛndāvana, where Harisundara passed away in **1895**, at sixty-five, on the kṛṣṇa-ekādaśī of Kārttika.

## Into bhakti

His father, Kṛṣṇamohana Bhaumik, was by family tradition of **Narottama Ṭhākura's line**. After a short family life the company of devotees so moved him that from the start he was lost to the world when he heard kīrtana; the sudden change made him seriously ill, but he gave up worldly life, went on with bhajana all through the illness and soon attained bhāva-bhakti.

## With Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya

In **1890** he went to Vṛndāvana with his family just when **Rājarṣi Bāhādur** arrived there; the two were delighted to know each other, and the Rājarṣi offered him the place of śikṣā-guru and begged him to stay. (The Rājarṣi had already visited Kāyāda with his Deity **Rādhā-vinoda** and met him.) They came back together through Navadvīpa, and the Rājarṣi brought Harisundara and his family to his capital, **Banwārīnagara**; Harisundara went back and forth between it and his village. In **1894** the Rājarṣi returned to Vṛndāvana with his family and Rādhā-vinoda, and brought Harisundara's family to live with him there.

## "Nothing without Rādhārāṇī"

Most of the time he was in a transcendental state, his mind seldom away from Rādhārāṇī's feet; he would not even do his daily routine unless She so directed. He taught: "Never do any work without Rādhārāṇī's approval, and never do anything out of tune with Her mood." For days he would go without food; often he fell critically ill, yet his mind stayed fixed on his goal.

## His passing

In June–July 1895, when **Kāminī Ghoṣ** and the Rājarṣi's eldest son came to take leave for Bengal, he said: "You are set on going — but better come back soon. I shall not live long; the hour of meeting has been fixed." When Kāminī returned in early October and paid obeisances, he said, "Good, you have come back." Four or five days later he had a slight fever and passed away in Vṛndāvana, aged sixty-five, on the **kṛṣṇa-ekādaśī of Kārttika**.
