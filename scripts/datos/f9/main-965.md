## In short

A completely renounced Vaiṣṇava, whose name is not known, did bhajana long ago in a cave on the bank of **Yaśodā-kuṇḍa** near Nandīśvara (**Nandagrāma**). Through a ghost in the form of a dog he learned how to meet Kṛṣṇa, and he saw the Lord in His three-fold bending form and then the Divine Couple with Their sakhīs; he left his body shortly afterwards. Told by **Rāmadāsa Bābājī** to **Haridāsa dāsa**.

## The dog

He left his cave once a day for his ablutions and in the evening went to the village for mādhukarī; in old age he went nowhere but Nandagrāma. Once a bābājī from Govardhana begged him so many times to come to a nāma-yajña at Cakleśvara that he went, and after two days came back to his routine. As he was entering his cave after mādhukarī he heard a pitiful voice: "Revered Bābājī, I have not eaten for two days. I am the dog to whom you give a share of your mādhukarī every day. In my past birth I was the priest of the Nandīśvara temple; out of greed I ate a big laḍḍu before it was offered to the Deity, and for that sin I was born a ghost. I eat from the mādhukarī of a totally renounced Vaiṣṇava, hoping to be freed by your mercy."

"Since you belong to the other world," the Bābā asked, "you surely see Yugala-kiśora and Their līlā?" "I do, but in this form I cannot relish it as one with a human body can." The Bābā could not be shown it, but begged to be told how he might see it. "Tomorrow, wait at the edge of Yaśodā-kuṇḍa when the cows come home. The cowherd boy who comes last will be none other than Kṛṣṇa." And the voice vanished.

## At Yaśodā-kuṇḍa

The night seemed endless; he cried, laughed and danced restlessly. At dawn he hid under a bush by the kuṇḍa, now rolling in the dust in tears — "Am I fit to see Kṛṣṇa? It is absurd!" — now singing — "Kṛṣṇa is an ocean of mercy; surely He will bless this wretch." At twilight the cows and buffaloes came, with one or two boys, and at last a dark boy with a limp, staff in hand. The Bābā rushed out and clasped his feet. "Bābā, I am a trader's son; it is an offence to let you touch my feet, and my mother will beat me. Come to my house — I'll give you curd, sugar candy, mādhukarī, whatever you like — but let go of my feet!" The Bābā only begged: "My beloved Lord, cool my burning heart; give me shelter at Your feet without any more pretence."

From evening until midnight they stayed there, until the Lord said: "Well, Bābā, come, look at My real form," and showed Himself in His three-fold bending form with the flute. "My Lord, I have never meditated on You alone; I worship the Yugala. Give me darśana once of You with Your associates." Then **Yugala-kiśora**, surrounded by the sakhīs, appeared before him, and he drank the nectar of Their beauty. His treasured desire fulfilled, he left his body shortly afterwards.
