## In short

**Balarāma Ṭhākura** (born 1655/56), son of Tārācāṅd Bhāgyavanta of **Baladākhān** (Dhaka), was of **Nityānanda Prabhu's parivāra**, though some of his forefathers were of Advaita Prabhu's circle. He wandered on pilgrimage from boyhood, mad with Gaura-prema, always carrying the Deity **Śrī Keśavarāya**, and at last established his śrīpāṭa at **Bhuiṅkhālī** (Pabna), with the title Gosvāmī.

## Śrī Keśavarāya

It is said that in a former birth he was **Śukadeva Gosvāmī**, born in Advaita Prabhu's house. Śukadeva gave the Deity Keśavarāya (a Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa mūrti) to a dear brāhmaṇa disciple: "Serve Him with the utmost care until I come back, and when I return give me back my treasure. You will not die until I come again." Śukadeva went away and soon passed on; the disciple served the Deity for many years, until one day he saw Balarāma, recognized him as his guru, danced with joy, put Keśavarāya in his arms and left his body in full consciousness. From then on Balarāma never parted with the Deity, not even for a moment.

## The Nawab and the Mahārāja

His serene, sweet form and his miraculous powers won the reverence of Hindus and Muslims alike. The Nawab of Murshidabad, believing that no calamity could come to a land where such a saint lived, pressed him to accept a zamindārī called **Bore**; Balarāma refused, and agreed only to settle at **Biyājitpur** near Daulatpur (Nadia) — and even there he took not the whole village but twenty bighās of land, where he installed Keśavarāya. Long afterwards the **Mahārāja of Nāṭor** came for his darśana and, charmed, persuaded him to settle at Bhuiṅkhālī in his own estates (post office Sāithiyā, Pabna).

## His family and Deities

Late in life, by the Lord's inspiration, he married, and had two sons, **Nandakiśora** and **Saccidānanda**. Besides Keśavarāya there is a **Nīla-mūrti** he worshipped, and his wooden staff is worshipped beside the Deities. Keśavarāya's **Rāsa-yātrā** is still celebrated with great pomp.
