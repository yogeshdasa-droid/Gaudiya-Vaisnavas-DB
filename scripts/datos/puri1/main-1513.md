## In short

Śrī Svarūpa Dāmodara Gosvāmī — before his sannyāsa **Puruṣottama Ācārya** of Navadvīpa — was the most intimate associate of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Purī, "like a second form of the Lord". "The heads of all, the two who knew the Lord's heart: Paramānanda Purī and Svarūpa Dāmodara" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.125). He is counted among the "three and a half" most intimate devotees of the Lord. In Vraja he is **Lalitā Sakhī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 160; *Abhidhāna*).

## Family and youth

His father was **Padmagarbha Ācārya** and his maternal grandfather **Jayarāma Cakravartī**; the family came from **Bhiṭādiyā**. Jayarāma, who lived in Navadvīpa, gave his daughter in marriage to Padmagarbha, and the couple settled in Navadvīpa, where Puruṣottama was born. Padmagarbha left his family in the care of his father-in-law and went to Mithilā, Kāśī and other places to study the Veda and Vedānta; in Vārāṇasī he met **Lakṣmīpati**, the guru of Mādhavendra Purī, and took dīkṣā from him. Back in Bhiṭādiyā he married again, and his second wife, Kamalā Devī, gave birth to **Lakṣmīnātha Lāhiḍī**, Svarūpa's half-brother. Puruṣottama grew up in Navadvīpa, in the house of his mother's family (*Abhidhāna*; the record's text).

"Puruṣottama lived in Navadvīpa with his maternal grandfather; he became a dear devotee of Caitanya, a heap of good qualities" (*Prema-vilāsa* 24). In Navadvīpa he was always with the Lord, sang in His kīrtanas, and was a friend of Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi from before (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 8.124 and 10.36–37).

## Sannyāsa in Vārāṇasī

When he saw the Lord take sannyāsa he became like a madman, went straight to Vārāṇasī and took sannyāsa from a sannyāsī named **Caitanyānanda** (Bhāratī), who told him: "Read the Vedānta and teach it to everyone." He gave up his śikhā and sacred thread but did not take the saffron cloth nor a sannyāsa title: he remained a naiṣṭhika-brahmacārī with the brahmacārī name **Svarūpa** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.102–108; *Prema-vilāsa* 24). The *Abhidhāna* adds that he studied Vedānta with Caitanyānanda for some time, but the svāmī was very fond of the Vedānta and Svarūpa did not want to hear Māyāvāda; so he could not stay with him and went to Purī. "Because he gave up the śikhā and thread, the learned call him Svarūpa" (*Sakhā-nirṇayāmṛta* 37).

## Meeting the Lord again

With the permission of his sannyāsa-guru he came to Nīlācala. He fell at the Lord's feet reciting a verse: "O Śrī Caitanya, ocean of mercy, may Your mercy arise in me — which easily drives away all distress, which is all-purifying, which reveals the highest bliss, which ends the disputes of the śāstras, which rains rasa on the heart and maddens it with prema, the limit of sweetness…" (*Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka*; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.119). The Lord lifted him up and embraced him: "Today I saw in a dream that you had come. As a blind man is happy to get his eyes back, so am I to get you." Svarūpa answered: "Forgive me. I left Your feet and went to a far country; I gave You up, but You did not give me up: with the rope of Your mercy round my neck You have dragged me back to Your feet." The Lord embraced him again: "Kṛṣṇa is very kind; He has brought us together again" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.109–129). The Lord added **Dāmodara** to his name.

## With the Lord in Purī

"Svarūpa was the limit of learning, but he spoke with no one; he stayed alone, and few knew him. His body was the very form of prema for Kṛṣṇa — a second form of the Lord. Whoever brought a book, a verse or a song to the Lord, Svarūpa examined it first, and only if it was pure did he let the Lord hear it: things against the conclusions of bhakti, or with rasābhāsa, gave the Lord no joy. Vidyāpati, Caṇḍīdāsa and the *Gīta-govinda*: with these songs he gave the Lord joy. In music he was like a Gandharva, in the śāstra like Bṛhaspati; there was no one as great as Dāmodara. He was most dear to Advaita and Nityānanda and the life of Śrīvāsa and the other devotees" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.110–117).

Whatever mood the Lord was in, Svarūpa sang kīrtana to feed it. With **Rāmānanda Rāya**, who arrived from Vidyānagara at the same time, he was the Lord's companion at night, when He tasted the rasa of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa: as Lalitā and Viśākhā are to Śrī Rādhā, so were Svarūpa and Rāmānanda to the Lord. "In the pain of separation, only Rāmānanda's talks of Kṛṣṇa and Svarūpa's songs kept the Lord alive" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 6.6).

Episodes (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; list of the record and the *Abhidhāna*):

- He wrote the pastimes of the Lord in his **Kaḍacā** (Ādi 13.16, 42), and was "the treasurer of the jewels of the Lord's pastimes" (Madhya 2.84, 94; 8.312).
- Meeting with Rāmānanda Rāya and with the devotees from Bengal (Madhya 10.118–129, 11.208); he served the prasāda to the devotees.
- At the cleaning of the Guṇḍicā temple (Madhya 12.109) he disciplined the Gauḍīya devotee who washed the Lord's feet (12.125–128).
- He led the singing before the Ratha (Madhya 13.74, 112–114), knew the Lord's heart in His ecstasy (13.122–167), joined the water sports (14.80, 101) and tasted the Vṛndāvana-līlā of Jagannātha (14.116–202).
- He was a close friend of Bhagavān Ācārya and gave his view about Gopāla Ācārya (Antya 2.85, 100); he consoled Choṭa Haridāsa (2.138–141, 153); he met Sanātana (4.109).
- He examined and rejected the drama of a Bengali poet (Antya 5.95–189), and relished the verses of Rūpa Gosvāmī (Antya 1.76–124).
- The Lord put **Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī** in his care: "Svarūpa's Raghu", his right hand (Antya 6.192–323).
- He sang kīrtana at the passing of Haridāsa Ṭhākura (Antya 11.49–78) and met Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa (13.104).
- He made a bed for the Lord out of banana leaves (Antya 13.10–88), and looked after Him in the Gambhīrā in His last years: when the Lord left the Gambhīrā and fell at the Siṁha-dvāra (14.57–82), when He ran to Caṭaka-parvata (14.89–104), when He was found among the Telaṅgī cows (17.13–37), when He fell into the sea (18), and when the riddle of Advaita arrived (19.24–67).

The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* tells how he enjoyed the episode of Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi, slapped on the cheeks by Jagannātha and Balarāma for finding fault with the starched cloth of the Deity (Antya 10.86–175).

He lived in Purī at the place later called the **Sātāsana Maṭha**, near the sea at Svarga-dvāra, close to Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Bhakti-kuṭī.

## Disappearance

He disappeared on the second day of the bright fortnight of Āṣāḍha (**Āṣāḍha Śukla Dvitīyā**), the day of the Ratha-yātrā; so also Whisper's Gauḍīya calendar. One of the record's texts says "the second day after the full moon of Āṣāḍha".

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Lalitā Sakhī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 160; *Abhidhāna*). The record's English text gave Viśākhā, and Lalitā according to Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī. The *Sakhā-nirṇayāmṛta* (37) counts him in the branch of **Gadādhara Paṇḍita**.

## Works

His **Kaḍacā** (notebook) of the Lord's pastimes, used by Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 13.16). One of the record's texts attributes to him a book of music, *Saṅgīta-dāmodara*.
