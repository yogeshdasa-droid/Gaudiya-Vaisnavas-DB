## In short

Gopīnātha Ācārya of Navadvīpa was the husband of the sister of **Vāsudeva Sārvabhauma** and a branch of the Caitanya tree: "A great branch was Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya; his sister's husband was Śrī Gopīnātha Ācārya" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.130). In Purī he was the first to proclaim that Śrī Caitanya was the Lord Himself. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Brahmā**, and some also see in him the sakhī **Ratnāvalī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 75, 178).

## In Navadvīpa

He lived in Nadīyā, the son-in-law of **Maheśvara Viśārada** of Vidyānagara and so brother-in-law of Sārvabhauma and Vidyā-vācaspati: "Then came Gopīnātha Ācārya, a resident of Nadīyā, the son-in-law of Viśārada" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6.18). When **Īśvara Purī** came to Navadvīpa in the Lord's childhood, he stayed some months in Gopīnātha's house (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 11). He was one of the Lord's companions in kīrtana and in the nagara-saṅkīrtana, in the water sports, and in the drama at Candraśekhara's house, where he was one of the actors (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 8.115, 13.337, 18.22). "Gopīnātha saw the Lord's pastimes in Nadīyā; by the Lord's wish he went ahead to Nīlācala" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 12).

## In Purī

He lived near Sārvabhauma. When the Lord fell unconscious in the temple and was carried to Sārvabhauma's house, Gopīnātha met **Mukunda Datta** and the devotees and brought them there. He declared, with the śāstra, that the young sannyāsī was Kṛṣṇa Himself, and Sārvabhauma and his students laughed at him; the Lord said that Sārvabhauma was His guru and spoke for everyone's good (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6). When Sārvabhauma was converted, Gopīnātha was full of joy.

The Lord gave him the care of the devotees who came every year from Bengal: he found them their lodgings and arranged their prasāda (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 11.173–204). He danced before the Ratha (Madhya 13.40, 14.83).

When **Narottama Ṭhākura** came to Purī after the Lord's disappearance, he found "Gopīnātha Ācārya and the other great Vaiṣṇavas grown very old and weak" (*Narottama-vilāsa* 4).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Brahmā** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 75); some also see in him the sakhī **Ratnāvalī** (178).

## Another view

The note of the record quotes «BBM»: Gopīnātha was a brāhmaṇa of Navadvīpa who did not live in Nīlācala but came to Purī as a visitor from Gauḍa; it relates the Vaiṣṇava-vandanā (21) to a «Gopīnātha Ṭhākura».
