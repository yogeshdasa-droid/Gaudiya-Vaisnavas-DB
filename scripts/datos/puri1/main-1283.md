## In short

Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya (Rāya Rāmānanda) was the governor of the region of the Godāvarī under Mahārāja **Pratāparudra** of Orissa, a great poet, and one of the two most intimate companions of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Purī. The Lord said: "Between Rāmānanda and Me there is only a difference of body" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.134). Their talks on the banks of the Godāvarī are the *Rāmānanda-saṁvāda* (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 8). In Vraja he is **Viśākhā Sakhī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 120–124; *Abhidhāna*).

## Family

He was the son of **Bhavānanda Rāya** (formerly Mahārāja Pāṇḍu; his wife was formerly Kuntī), who gave all his five sons to the service of the Lord: **Rāmānanda**, **Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka**, **Kalānidhi**, **Sudhānidhi** and **Vāṇīnātha** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.133–134). He was born in a kāraṇa (scribe) family, which he himself called śūdra.

The *Abhidhāna* says he was probably born near Cuttack in the last part of the fourteenth century of the Śaka era (the 1300s Śaka). The *Dinamaṇi-candrodaya*, written by his descendant **Manohara Rāya**, gives the family: Vāṇīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka, Rāmānanda's brother; his sons Gokulānanda and Harihara Rāya; Harihara's son Govindānanda, who set up a household in Cuttack; and his sons Nityānanda and Manohara. The family home was the village of Rāmāi-Ānandakola near Jājpur, and they still had a house in Vidyānagara. When Govindānanda died the king took all but seven villages; Nityānanda went to Bardhamān and later brought Manohara there.

## Governor of the Godāvarī

He ruled both banks of the Godāvarī for King Pratāparudra, with his seat at **Vidyānagara**. When the Lord set out for the South, Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya begged Him: "Do not neglect him thinking he is a śūdra engaged in worldly work; meet him without fail. No other devotee can compare with him in knowledge of the mellows" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 7.63–64).

## Meeting at the Godāvarī

On the bank of the Godāvarī, where the river reminded the Lord of the Yamunā, Rāmānanda came to bathe in a palanquin, with music and many Vedic brāhmaṇas. Seeing the wonderful sannyāsī, "as bright as a hundred suns", he fell at His feet. The Lord asked: "Are you Rāmānanda Rāya?" — "I am Your lowest servant, of the śūdra caste." The Lord embraced him and both fell to the ground in ecstasy, trembling, weeping and stammering "Kṛṣṇa", while the brāhmaṇas wondered how a sannyāsī could weep embracing a śūdra. Each praised the other, and the Lord said Sārvabhauma had sent Him to soften His hard heart (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 8.10–47).

In the evenings that followed, the Lord asked and Rāmānanda answered: varṇāśrama, offering the fruits of work to Kṛṣṇa, renunciation, devotion mixed with knowledge, pure devotion — "This is external; go further" — then the love of servants, friends, parents and, highest of all, the conjugal love of the gopīs, and among them the love of **Śrī Rādhā**. When he sang his own song of *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*, the Lord covered his mouth with His hand. Then came the questions and answers: the best learning is devotion to Kṛṣṇa; the greatest wealth, love for Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa; the greatest sorrow, separation from Kṛṣṇa's devotees… At the end the Lord showed him His form of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa together, and Rāmānanda fainted; the Lord asked him to keep it secret and to come to Purī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 8.53–311).

The *Caitanya-maṅgala* of Locana dāsa puts the meeting of the Lord and Rāmānanda at Kāñcī, and the *Caitanya-maṅgala* of Jayānanda in Purī; but according to the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Madhya 1.104) they met in Vidyānagara, on the Godāvarī (*Abhidhāna*).

## In Purī

When the Lord came back from the South, Rāmānanda took leave of the king and came to live in Purī (Madhya 11.15–). He spoke with the Lord about Pratāparudra (Madhya 12.55–57). When the Lord went towards Bengal, he went with Him as far as Bhadraka (Madhya 16). He relished the dramas of **Rūpa Gosvāmī** with the Lord (Antya 1.106–205), and by the Lord's order spoke about Kṛṣṇa to **Pradyumna Miśra** (Antya 5.11–85).

He wrote dramas and had them acted for Lord Jagannātha by young devadāsīs, whom he himself bathed and dressed: the Lord said that his mind did not waver at all, where a yogī's mind would be shaken by a wooden doll (Antya 5.16–26). With **Svarūpa Dāmodara** he was the Lord's constant companion in His last years, tasting the rasa of separation in the Gambhīrā (Antya 15.11–94, 16.116–150, 17, 19.33–110): "only Rāmānanda's talks of Kṛṣṇa and Svarūpa's songs kept the Lord alive" (Antya 6.6). When the Lord disappeared, Pratāparudra fell to the ground unconscious and Rāmānanda Rāya could barely keep alive (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 3.218).

His house still stands at the Jagannātha-vallabha garden on the Grand Road in Purī.

## Guru

The *Bhajana-nirṇaya* says he was a disciple of **Rāghavendra Purī**, a disciple of Mādhavendra Purī (*Abhidhāna*).

## Works

The Sanskrit drama **Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭaka**, where he writes about King Pratāparudra and his own father Bhavānanda Rāya. The *Padakalpataru* (576) has a song of his in Brajabuli (*Abhidhāna*).

## Disappearance

According to the *Abhidhāna*, probably in 1455 or 1456 Śaka, soon after the Lord's disappearance, on the Kṛṣṇa Pañcamī of (gauṇa) Vaiśākha. The record gives Jyaiṣṭha Kṛṣṇa Pañcamī (others: Vaiśākha Kṛṣṇa Pañcamī), from B. V. Tīrtha; Whisper's Gauḍīya calendar has his tirobhāva on 7 May 2026.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Viśākhā Sakhī**; also the Pāṇḍava **Arjuna** and Kṛṣṇa's priya-narma-sakhā Arjuna (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 120–124). According to the *Padma Purāṇa* Arjuna got the body of a gopī named **Arjunīyā**, so the sakhā Arjuna, the Pāṇḍava Arjuna and the gopī Arjunīyā all entered him. Some say Lalitā Sakhī is also in him, but this is not accepted by all (*Abhidhāna*).
