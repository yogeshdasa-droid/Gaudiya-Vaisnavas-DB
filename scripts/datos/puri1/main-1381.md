## In short

Vāsudeva Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya was the greatest logician of his time, the court paṇḍita of Mahārāja **Pratāparudra** in Purī and a teacher of Śaṅkara's Vedānta, who was defeated by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, saw His real form and became His servant. "A great branch was Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya; his sister's husband was Śrī Gopīnātha Ācārya" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.130). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Bṛhaspati**, the guru of the demigods (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 119).

## Family

A Rāḍhīya brāhmaṇa, born in Navadvīpa in the first part of the fourteenth century of the Śaka era (*Abhidhāna*). His father was **Maheśvara Viśārada** — called **Narahari Viśārada** in other sources: Lakṣmīdhara's commentary on the *Advaita-makaranda*, the kulaji texts quoted by N. N. Basu (*Baṅger Jātīya Itihāsa*, Brāhmaṇa-kāṇḍa I), and Sārvabhauma's own ṭīkā on the *Advaita-makaranda*, where he calls his father "full of the knowledge of Vedānta" (vedānta-vidyāmayāt). His brother was **Vidyā-vācaspati** (Ratnākara, according to the kulaji). His sister's husband was **Gopīnātha Ācārya**. His son was **Jaleśvara Vāhinīpati**, author of the *Śabdāloka-uddyota*, who married the daughter of Sudhākara; his daughter was **Ṣāṭhī**, whose husband **Amogha** offended the Lord (*Abhidhāna*; the record's text).

His birthplace is placed at Vidyānagara, a few miles from Navadvīpa near Cāṁpāhāṭi, in the old Kuliyā; others say the village of Piralyā (now Parulia), near Navadvīpa.

## The logician of Navadvīpa

After his first studies in Navadvīpa he went to Mithilā to study nyāya with **Pakṣadhara Miśra**. The paṇḍitas of Mithilā let no student copy their books, so that nyāya would stay their glory; Vāsudeva, with his wonderful memory, learned the books by heart and wrote them down again at home, and from then on there was a school of nyāya in Navadvīpa, which soon became the centre of logic in India. Dīneśa Candra Bhaṭṭācārya (*Baṅge Navya-nyāya-carcā*, p. 40) does not accept this: he shows that Sārvabhauma learned navya-nyāya from his own father and never went to Mithilā (*Abhidhāna*). His son Jaleśvara says he mastered the six darśanas — nyāya-vaiśeṣika, Vedānta, Mīmāṁsā and the rest. In Navadvīpa he wrote a commentary on the *Tattva-cintāmaṇi*. The famous logician **Raghunātha Śiromaṇi**, author of the *Dīdhiti*, was his student; so was **Madhusūdana Vācaspati**, who later taught in Kāśī the explanations of Vedānta he had heard from the Lord, and from whom Śrī Jīva studied.

## To Purī

According to Jayānanda, when the fear of the king came over Navadvīpa at the time of the Lord's birth, Sārvabhauma left for Purī (*Abhidhāna*); Mṛṇālakānti Ghoṣa answers that his brother and family never left Navadvīpa. Hearing of his learning, Mahārāja Pratāparudra brought him to Nīlācala with great honour and wealth and made him his court paṇḍita; D. C. Sen writes that the king gave him a golden seat next to his own. There he taught Śaṅkara's Vedānta to many sannyāsīs.

## Meeting the Lord

When the Lord first entered the temple of Jagannātha He fell unconscious; Sārvabhauma had Him carried to his house. His brother-in-law **Gopīnātha Ācārya** met Mukunda Datta and the devotees and told him who the young sannyāsī was; the devotees came, the Lord woke, and Sārvabhauma received them all and gave them mahā-prasāda, and lodged the Lord at his aunt's house. Gopīnātha proved from the śāstra that the Lord was Kṛṣṇa Himself; Sārvabhauma and his students laughed. The Lord said: "Sārvabhauma is My guru; whatever he says out of affection is for our good" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6).

For seven days the Lord listened in silence to his explanation of the *Vedānta-sūtra*. Asked whether He understood, He answered: "I understand the sūtras clearly, but not your explanations", and He showed that the Absolute Truth is a person with eternal form and unlimited energies, one and different from His creation. Sārvabhauma was defeated. At his request the Lord explained the **ātmārāma verse** of the *Bhāgavatam* in eighteen ways, and then showed him His real form — first with four arms, then as Kṛṣṇa with His flute (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6.202); the tradition of Purī speaks of the six-armed form (ṣaḍ-bhuja). Sārvabhauma recited a hundred verses in His praise and became His servant. One morning he ate the mahā-prasāda the Lord brought him without even washing his mouth; another day he changed *mukti-pade* into *bhakti-pade* in a verse of the *Bhāgavatam*, and the Lord was very pleased. The other paṇḍitas of Purī followed him (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6).

## In the Lord's service

He asked the Lord to meet **Rāmānanda Rāya** on His way south (Madhya 7.63–64), and arranged, against the Lord's refusal, the meeting of the Lord and King Pratāparudra at the Ratha-yātrā. When the Lord ate at his house, his son-in-law Amogha criticised Him; the next morning Amogha fell ill with cholera, and the Lord saved him and made him chant (Madhya 15).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Bṛhaspati** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 119).

## Works

The *Abhidhāna* names the **Sārvabhauma-nirukta**, a commentary on the *Tattva-cintāmaṇi*, the ṭīkā on the *Advaita-makaranda*, the **Śrī Caitanya-śataka**, and hymns of 108 names of Caitanya, Nityānanda, Advaita and Gadādhara Paṇḍita; and the two verses of surrender *vairāgya-vidyā-nija-bhakti-yoga…* and *kālān naṣṭaṁ bhakti-yogaṁ nijaṁ yaḥ…* (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6.254–255). The record names his nyāya books *Samasavāda* and *Sāravatī* (a commentary on the *Tattva-cintāmaṇi*). One of the record's texts (a modern author) doubts the hymns of 108 names circulated in his name, saying they are full of errors; it accepts only the two verses, one of which Sanātana Gosvāmī placed at the beginning of the *Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*. The record keeps the text of the *Śrī Gaurāṅga-aṣṭottara-śata-nāma-stotra*.
