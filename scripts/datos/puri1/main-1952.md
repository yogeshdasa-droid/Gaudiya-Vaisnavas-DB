## In short

Kāśī Miśra of Orissa was the chief servant of Lord Jagannātha in Purī and the guru of Mahārāja **Pratāparudra**. In his house, in the secluded room called the **Gambhīrā**, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu lived for the last eighteen years of His life. "Kāśī Miśra, Pradyumna Miśra, Rāya Bhavānanda" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.139). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Kubjā**, the Sairindhrī of Mathurā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 193).

## The Lord's house

When the Lord came back from the South, the king and Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya chose the house of Kāśī Miśra, quiet and near the temple, for Him to live in. When Kāśī Miśra surrendered at His feet, the Lord showed him His four-armed form and made him His own (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.32–33). Paramānanda Purī was also given a room and a servant there.

After the cleaning of the Guṇḍicā temple, he and **Tulasī Paḍichā** brought prasāda for five hundred people, and the Lord and His devotees accepted it (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 12.154). As the Lord danced before the Ratha he saw His wonderful pastime (Madhya 13.57). See also Madhya 11.45, 14.106–115, 15.20, and Antya 9.59–104 and 11.80–86.

Whenever Pratāparudra was in Purī, he came every day to the house of his guru Kāśī Miśra, massaged his feet after his midday meal, and asked whether the offerings of Jagannātha were going well: "The king had this rule: while he stayed in Puruṣottama he came every day to massage Miśra's feet and to hear about the service of Jagannātha" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya; *Abhidhāna*, entry «Pratāparudra»).

## After the Lord

After the Lord's disappearance **Vakreśvara Paṇḍita** lived in his house, and Vakreśvara's disciple **Gopāla Guru Gosvāmī** installed there Śrī Śrī Rādhākānta: it is the **Rādhākānta Maṭha**, founded by Kāśī Miśra.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Kubjā** (Sairindhrī) of Mathurā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 193).

## Another Kāśī Miśra

The *Vaiṣṇava Digdarśanī* also names a Kāśī Miśra as the matchmaker of the Lord's marriage with Viṣṇupriyā in Navadvīpa: perhaps another person.
