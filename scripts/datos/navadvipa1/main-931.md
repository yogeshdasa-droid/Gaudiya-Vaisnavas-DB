## In short

Śrī Mukunda Datta Ṭhākura, Mahāprabhu's classmate and His **singer**, came from a respected Ambaṣṭha (vaidya) family of **Cakraśālā** in Chittagong; with his elder brother **Vāsudeva Datta** he settled in Navadvīpa. His kīrtana made the Lord dance in Navadvīpa, at His sannyāsa in Kāṭoyā and in Purī. He brought Gadādhara Paṇḍita to Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Madhukaṇṭha**, a singer of Vraja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 140).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** not known.
- **Disappearance:** the full-moon day of Jyaiṣṭha (**Jyaiṣṭha Pūrṇimā**, May–June).

## Family

"In the land of Chittagong is the village of Cakraśālā, where the respected Datta family of Ambaṣṭhas lives. In that family two devotees were born, Śrī Mukunda Datta and Vāsudeva Datta; **Vāsudeva was the elder and Mukunda the younger**. Both came to live in Navadvīpa" (*Prema-vilāsa* 22, quoted in the *Abhidhāna*). His śrīpāṭa was at Cakraśālā (*Abhidhāna*); one text of the record names his village Cakrasada, under Paṭiyā thānā. (One text of the record wrongly calls him Vāsudeva's son.) Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi came from the same region of Chittagong.

Kavi-karṇapūra writes that Kṛṣṇa's singers **Madhukaṇṭha and Madhuvrata** were born in Kali-yuga in the Datta family and became the singers of Śrī Gaurāṅga.

## With Nimāi at school

They were the same age and studied together in the school of **Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita**; Nimāi and Mukunda argued all the time about the śāstras (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 11.28–30; 12.6–19). From childhood Mukunda was a devotee of Kṛṣṇa and wanted to speak and sing of nothing else. Whenever Nimāi saw him He would not let him go without answering His questions on logic; Mukunda answered with questions on rhetoric, and began to avoid Him. Once, forced to discuss rhetoric, he found that Nimāi answered all his difficult questions and that he could not refute Him; he thought: "Such genius cannot belong to an ordinary person — and since He is a devotee of Kṛṣṇa, I should never leave His company."

Every day Mukunda came to Advaita's house; Advaita, Śrīvāsa and all the Vaiṣṇavas loved his kīrtana, rolled on the ground in ecstasy, and Advaita would weep and take him on his lap. When **Īśvara Purī** came to Navadvīpa, he too was overwhelmed by Mukunda's songs, and everyone recognised him as a disciple of Mādhavendra Purī.

When the Lord came back from Gayā, completely changed, He began kīrtana in His house with Mukunda leading; hearing Mukunda, He fell unconscious, and when He woke He put His arm round Mukunda's neck: "Mukunda, you are the most fortunate! I wasted My time on useless learning, and My life passed without Kṛṣṇa." Mukunda was the main singer in the kīrtana of the Hari-vāsara and sang at the Lord's abhiṣeka (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 8.141, 9.32).

## Gadādhara and Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi

Mukunda knew that **Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi** understood all spiritual truth, and asked the renounced **Gadādhara Paṇḍita** if he would like to meet a real Vaiṣṇava. Gadādhara found Puṇḍarīka sitting like a prince on a white bed, chewing betel and fanned by servants, and doubted. Understanding him, Mukunda sang a verse of the Bhāgavatam; at once Puṇḍarīka cried "Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!", all the eight ecstatic symptoms appeared, and he rolled on the ground. Ashamed, Gadādhara asked Mukunda to arrange for him to take shelter at Puṇḍarīka's feet (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 7.39–121).

## "After millions of births"

On the day of the Mahā-prakāśa at Śrīvāsa's house, the Lord called every devotee and told them their past lives — but not Mukunda, who waited outside. When Śrīvāsa pleaded for him, the Lord said in feigned anger: "Do not let him come near Me. He goes from group to group — sometimes knowledge is greatest, sometimes devotion. Those who speak against Me sometimes and worship Me at other times are hypocrites." Shocked, Mukunda decided to give up his life; Śrīvāsa pleaded again, and the Lord said: "He will have My mercy after millions of births." Hearing this, Mukunda began to dance with joy: "I will have Him after millions of births!" — and could not even hear Śrīvāsa calling him. When the Lord Himself called him, he fell at His feet: "I am sinful; Duryodhana saw Your universal form and still perished, because he had no devotion." The Lord embraced him: "By your firm faith you have crossed those millions of births in a moment. You are My dear devotee; I did this only to show your glory. Wherever I appear, you will always be My singer" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 10).

## Sannyāsa and Purī

At the Lord's **sannyāsa** in Kāṭoyā He asked Mukunda to sing, and danced (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 26.160–166, 28.85–149). Mukunda went with Him to Nīlācala (Antya 2.35, 122, 133), stayed with Him there, and the Lord heard his kīrtana regularly; he played in the water-sports in Narendra-sarovara (Antya 8.123). At the Ratha-yātrā he sang in the kīrtana groups with Vāsudeva Datta, Gopīnātha, Murāri and others.

## Guru and disciples

- A branch of the Caitanya tree: "Mukunda Datta, the Lord's classmate, at whose kīrtana Caitanya Gosāñi dances" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.40). (The record had his elder brother Vāsudeva Datta as his guru.)

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Madhukaṇṭha**, a singer of Vraja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 140).
