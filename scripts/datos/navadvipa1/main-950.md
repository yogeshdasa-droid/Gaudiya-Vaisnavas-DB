## In short

Śrī Murāri Gupta Ṭhākura — "Murāri Gupta, a hidden storehouse of love" — was a physician from **Śrīhaṭṭa** who lived in Navadvīpa near the house of Jagannātha Miśra. A childhood friend and classmate of Nimāi Paṇḍita in the school of Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita, he became one of His most intimate devotees. He worshipped **Sītā-Rāma**, and the Lord showed Himself to him as Rāmacandra and reminded him that he was **Hanumān**. He wrote down in Sanskrit what he had seen of the Lord's early life — the ***Śrī Kṛṣṇa-caitanya-caritāmṛta***, known as **Murāri Gupta's Kaḍacā** — the main source for the Ādi-līlā of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*.

## Dates

- **Appearance:** not known. The *Abhidhāna* calls him the Lord's **same age**, a childhood friend; the texts of the record say he was a few years older.
- **Disappearance:** Āśvina Pūrṇimā (Śarad Pūrṇimā, the day of the autumn rāsa festival) (B. V. Tīrtha).

## Family and home

"An expert physician named Murāri Gupta, who cures both the diseases of the body and the disease of material existence" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.51), appeared in **Śrīhaṭṭa** (Sylhet), like Advaita Ācārya, Śivānanda Sena, Jagannātha Miśra and Nīlāmbara Cakravartī (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 2.35); Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita and Candraśekhara Ācārya also came from there. From Śrīhaṭṭa he came to Navadvīpa and lived near the Lord's house (*Abhidhāna*). He belonged to a family of physicians (vaidya); one text of the record says "vaiśya". His parents' names are not known; his devoted wife appears in several pastimes.

## Student with Nimāi

Murāri, Kamalākānta, Kṛṣṇānanda and others studied with Nimāi in the school of **Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita** (*Abhidhāna*). Nimāi loved to argue about logic and grammar; their quarrels on the bank of the Gaṅgā ended in mud-slinging, so that the women could not fill their water-pots and the brāhmaṇas could not bathe (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 8). Nimāi soon became first in the school and all accepted defeat, but Murāri would not, and Nimāi teased him: "You are a doctor — what use is grammar to you? Go and collect leaves and creepers to treat the sick; you will find nothing here about mucus, bile and indigestion!" Murāri was hurt but did not show it, and simply looked at Him gently. He could never defeat Him even once, and began to think: "Such genius cannot belong to an ordinary man." After their arguments they went to bathe in the Gaṅgā as friends. The Lord often tested him with tricky questions (*phāṅki*) and scolded him in play (*Abhidhāna*). In His childhood the Lord once climbed on Murāri's shoulders and, in a four-armed form, went round the courtyard (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 11.33).

## Hanumān

When the Lord returned from Gayā and began to show ecstatic love, Murāri saw Him weeping in the house of **Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī** and became His devoted follower.

One day the Lord came to Murāri's house in the mood of **Varāha**, roared, and lifted a water-pot from the floor with His teeth. Murāri fell flat before Him. "Murāri, sing My praises!" When Murāri recited verses, the Lord said: "I tell you the truth: I am the essence of all the Vedas. I have come to perform saṅkīrtana and make others chant. I cannot tolerate malice towards My devotees — even if it is My own son, I will tear off his head; Narakāsura is the proof." He also hinted His displeasure with Prakāśānanda (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 3.24–52).

During the twenty-one hours of the Mahā-prakāśa at Śrīvāsa's house the Lord called him: "Murāri, all this time you have not known who I am. See My form!" Murāri saw his worshipful Lord **Raghunātha**, green as dūrvā grass, seated in vīrāsana with His bow, Jānakī and Lakṣmaṇa at His sides and the monkey chiefs singing hymns all around — and seeing himself among the monkeys, he fainted. "Get up, Murāri! Have you forgotten who burned Rāvaṇa's Laṅkā? It was you, Hanumān! Look at Lakṣmaṇa, your very life, and bow to Sītā, for whose sorrow you wept so much." Murāri recited his **Śrī Rāmāṣṭaka** for the Lord (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 10.7–20).

Another day, when the Lord showed His four-armed Viṣṇu form at Śrīvāsa's house, Murāri took the mood of **Garuḍa**, spread his arms like wings and carried the Lord on his shoulders round the courtyard (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 20.81–102). So he is called Hanumān — or, some say, Garuḍa.

## Nityānanda first

One evening at Śrīvāsa's house Murāri bowed first to Mahāprabhu and then to Nityānanda. "You have made a mistake," said the Lord. "Go home; you will understand." That night he dreamed of Nityānanda going in front as Balarāma — cloth tied like a wrestler, the many-headed serpent above Him, plough and club in His hands — and behind Him Viśvambhara with a peacock feather. "Do you understand now? If you break the etiquette, how will it look?" Murāri cried out "Nityānanda! Nityānanda!" in his sleep until his wife woke him. The next day he bowed first to Nityānanda and then to the Lord: "As You have taught me" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 20.17–21). Pleased, the Lord gave him His chewed betel; Murāri ate it and wiped his hand on his head: "Today my whole body is purified."

## The Lord's indigestion

Still intoxicated with that prasāda, Murāri went home; his wife served him rice, and he kept making balls of rice with ghee and putting them on the ground, saying "Eat! Eat!" At dawn the Lord came to his door: "Murāri, you forgot? Last night you kept saying 'Eat! Eat!' and fed Me so much rice with ghee that I have indigestion. The medicine is your water." And He drank from Murāri's water-pot, though Murāri protested that the water of a man of low birth was not fit for Him (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 20.53–71). Murāri and his family wept with joy.

## The hidden knife

Once Murāri thought: "Who knows what the Lord intends? Let me leave my body first, in His presence." He made a large knife and hid it. The Lord at once came to his house, found the knife and said: "Murāri, I have so many pastimes to finish with you — if you go, how will I go on? I know everything." Murāri caught His feet and wept (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 20.114–126; *Abhidhāna*).

## In Purī

After the Lord's sannyāsa, Murāri came every year with his wife and the devotees of Bengal for the Ratha-yātrā, bringing many preparations, which Govinda served to the Lord: "This was brought by Vāsudeva Datta, this by Murāri Gupta, this by Buddhimanta Khān!" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya-līlā).

## His Kaḍacā

What he had seen with his own eyes of the Lord's early life, Murāri wrote in Sanskrit under the name ***Śrī Kṛṣṇa-caitanya-caritāmṛta*** (*Abhidhāna*), known as **Murāri Gupta's Kaḍacā** (diary). It covers the first twenty-four years of the Lord's life up to His sannyāsa; the rest was recorded by Svarūpa Dāmodara. Murāri's diary is the basis of the Ādi-līlā of the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, and Svarūpa Dāmodara's of the Madhya and Antya-līlā. Murāri also composed **padas** (*Abhidhāna*).

The Gauḍīya Maṭha has built a temple in his memory near **Ballāla-dīghi** in Śrī Dhāma Māyāpura.

## Guru and disciples

- A branch of the Caitanya tree: "Śrī Murāri Gupta, a hidden storehouse of love; hearing his humility, the Lord's heart melted" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.49).
