## In short

Śrīla Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita — "Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura", also called **Śrīnivāsa** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 4.227) — is one of the **Pañca-tattva** and the incarnation of **Nārada Muni** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 90). Born in Śrīhaṭṭa, he lived with his brothers in Navadvīpa on the bank of the Gaṅgā. Mahāprabhu was born in the house of Jagannātha Miśra, but His saṅkīrtana pastimes — the "Vṛndāvana pastimes" of Navadvīpa — took place in **Śrīvāsa's courtyard (Śrīvāsāṅgana)**: there the Lord first revealed His four-armed form, there He showed His Mahā-prakāśa for twenty-one hours, there Nityānanda lived as the son of Śrīvāsa's wife **Mālinī**, and there the Lord brought Śrīvāsa's dead son back to speak. After the Lord's sannyāsa Śrīvāsa lived at **Kumārahaṭṭa** (Hālisahar).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** Caitra Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī, in Śrīhaṭṭa (B. V. Tīrtha; the year is not given).
- **Disappearance:** Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa Daśamī (B. V. Tīrtha).

## Family

According to the *Prema-vilāsa* (23), **Jaladhara Paṇḍita**, a Vaidika brāhmaṇa of Śrīhaṭṭa, came with his wife to live in Navadvīpa. He had five sons: **Nalina, Śrīvāsa, Śrīrāma, Śrīpati and Śrīnidhi** (also called Śrīkānta). The family had houses both at Kumārahaṭṭa and in Navadvīpa (*Abhidhāna*). Nalina's daughter was **Nārāyaṇī**, the mother of Śrīla **Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura**, the author of the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (*Abhidhāna*, entry Jaladhara Paṇḍita).

The texts usually speak of four brothers — Śrīvāsa, Śrīrāma, Śrīpati and Śrīnidhi — with Śrīvāsa as the leader. "Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita and Śrīrāma Paṇḍita, two brothers, two branches known to the world; Śrīpati and Śrīnidhi their two brothers; the four brothers with their servants and maidservants, the household of Śrī Caitanya, in whose house the Lord always performed saṅkīrtana. The four brothers with their whole family serve Caitanya and know no other god" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.8–11). **Śrīrāma Paṇḍita** (Rāmāi) was Parvata Muni, Nārada's dear friend (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 90); Śrīnidhi was Padmanidhi.

His wife, **Mālinī Devī**, was Ambikā, Kṛṣṇa's nurse in Vraja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 42), and a close friend of Śacī Devī. The story of his mother-in-law hiding in a basket during kīrtana is in her own record.

## In Navadvīpa before the Lord appeared

Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura sings: "In Navadvīpa lived the holy Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita, and in his house Caitanya enjoyed His kīrtana. The four brothers always sang the names of Kṛṣṇa, worshipped Kṛṣṇa and bathed in the Gaṅgā three times a day" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 2.96). The brothers went every day to the house of **Advaita Ācārya** to hear the Bhāgavatam and chant the holy name, and they became close friends of **Jagannātha Miśra**; Śrīvāsa was so often in his house that he was like his shadow. Distressed by the condition of the people of Kali-yuga, the devotees prayed for the Lord to come.

On the full-moon night of Phālguna, 1407 Śaka (1486), the Lord appeared in the house of Jagannātha Miśra. As Advaita understood it in Śāntipura, Śrīvāsa and the devotees of Navadvīpa understood it too; Mālinī went to help Śacī Devī, and Śrīvāsa dropped hints about the divine child. Bewildered by Yogamāyā, the devotees saw the Lord's childhood wonders only with parental love; Śrīvāsa and Mālinī advised Jagannātha Miśra and Śacī how to raise Him, and the Lord looked on them as a second father and mother.

When the young Nimāi Paṇḍita seemed proud of His learning, Śrīvāsa told Him: "Why do people study? To understand devotion to Kṛṣṇa. If learning does not give devotion to Kṛṣṇa, what is the use of it? If you have really learned something, begin worshipping Kṛṣṇa now." Nimāi laughed: "By your mercy, that will certainly come to pass."

## The Lord reveals Himself

After His initiation by Īśvara Purī in Gayā, the Lord began to flood Navadvīpa with kīrtana. The non-devotees were angry: "Behind all this mischief is that brāhmaṇa Śrīvāsa — he and his three brothers scream 'Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!' like madmen just to fill their bellies… The whole country will be ruined. I heard in the king's court that two boats with government men are coming to arrest anyone who sings kīrtana in Nadīyā." (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 11.56: the kīrtana of the four brothers burned the skeptics.) Soft-hearted Śrīvāsa grew anxious for the Vaiṣṇavas.

Then the Lord, walking on the bank of the Gaṅgā, saw the cows grazing, became overwhelmed, roared "I am He! I am He!" and ran to Śrīvāsa's house, where Śrīvāsa was worshipping Lord Nṛsiṁha behind closed doors. Kicking open the door, He cried: "Whom are you worshipping? On whom are you meditating? The one you worship is here!" He sat on the Viṣṇu altar and showed His **four-armed form** with conch, disc, club and lotus. "Called by your loud saṅkīrtana and Advaita's roaring, I have left Vaikuṇṭha and come with My associates. I have come to protect the saintly and punish the wicked. Do not fear — go on chanting My glories" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 2.258).

Śrīvāsa fell down and prayed: "I bow at the feet of Viśvambhara, dark as a new rain-cloud, dressed in cloth like lightning; at the feet of Śacī's son, adorned with peacock feather and guñjā beads; at the feet of the pupil of Gaṅgādāsa, whose face defeats ten million moons; at Your feet, who carry the buffalo horn, stick and flute, whom the four Vedas call the son of Nanda" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 2.272). "Today my birth and my work are fulfilled; my ancestors are delivered; my house is blessed; I see Him whose feet are served by Lakṣmī."

The Lord told him to bring his wife, brothers, relatives and servants; all worshipped His feet, and He placed His feet on their heads. About the king's boats He said: "I am in the king's heart. If he orders your arrest, I will be the first to step into the boat; I will make the king and his men, and even his elephants, cry and chant Kṛṣṇa's name." To show it, He told Śrīvāsa's little niece **Nārāyaṇī**, four years old: "Nārāyaṇī, chant Kṛṣṇa and cry!" — and the girl cried out "Kṛṣṇa!" with such tears that the ground was soaked. Śrīvāsa raised his arms: "Even when You come as all-devouring time, I will chant Your name without fear. Now that You are in my house, what fear can I have?"

A maidservant of the house, **Duḥkhī** ("unhappy"), brought water every day for the Lord's bath. "From today her name is **Sukhī** ('happy')," said the Lord: those who serve the Lord and His devotees cannot be unhappy.

## Śrīvāsāṅgana

Lord Kṛṣṇa appeared in Vasudeva's prison but played in the house of Nanda; Lord Caitanya appeared in Jagannātha Miśra's house but His Vṛndāvana pastimes were in Śrīvāsa's house. Every night the Lord danced there (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 15.5); there He remained in the mood of the Supreme Lord for twenty-one hours — the **Mahā-prakāśa** — and all the devotees saw His wonders (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 17.11). When **Nityānanda Prabhu** came to Navadvīpa, He lived in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, and **Mālinī** served Him as her own son. There too took place the episode of **Gopāla Cāpala** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 17.38–59).

## The son who spoke after death

One night, while the Lord danced in kīrtana, Śrīvāsa's only son died of an illness in the inner rooms. Hearing the women wail, Śrīvāsa went in and, grave and wise, told them: "You know the glories of Kṛṣṇa — do not cry. Even a great sinner who hears the holy name at death goes to Kṛṣṇa, and Kṛṣṇa Himself is dancing in our courtyard; this boy's fortune is complete. If you cannot control yourselves, cry later; but if your crying disturbs the Lord's kīrtana, I will throw myself into the Gaṅgā. Not a blade of grass moves without Kṛṣṇa's will; Kṛṣṇa gives and Kṛṣṇa takes away." Then he went back to the kīrtana.

Late at night the Lord said: "Today My heart feels some sorrow; has something happened in Śrīvāsa's house?" — "How can there be sorrow where Your smiling face shines?" Then the devotees told Him. The Lord wept: "Why did you not tell Me?" — "I could not break Your kīrtana. If all of us died while seeing You, it would be happiness; if You had stopped dancing, I would have died." The Lord wept: "How can I leave the company of such devotees?" — words that made the devotees uneasy, for they did not yet know He would take sannyāsa.

He went to the body and asked: "Boy, why are you leaving Śrīvāsa?" Life returned, and the boy said: "Lord, all happens by Your will. I stayed as long as I was destined; now I go to another body. Many times I have been born and died, but this time I die happily, having seen Your face. Who is father, who is son? Let me never forget You." Then he fell silent (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 25; also told in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*). The family fell at the Lord's feet, and He said: "**Nityānanda and I are your two sons**; do not grieve." With kīrtana they carried the boy to the Gaṅgā.

## Kumārahaṭṭa

When the Lord took sannyāsa, Śrīvāsa no longer stayed in Navadvīpa and went to live at **Kumārahaṭṭa** (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.8–10). Every year he and his brothers went to Purī to see the Lord, and he often visited Śacī Mātā in Māyāpura, staying a few days in his old house. At the Ratha-yātrā he once slapped **Haricandana** in front of the chariot (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 13.92–95).

When the Lord came back to Bengal five years after His sannyāsa to see His mother and the Gaṅgā, He stayed some days with Advaita and then came to Śrīvāsa's house at Kumārahaṭṭa. There He gave him a boon: "There will never be poverty in your house; even if you never step outside, whatever you need for worship will come to your door" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*); and He told Śrīrāma Paṇḍita to serve his elder brother (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.66).

Across the Gaṅgā from Hālisahar, in **Cuṅcuḍā (Chinsura)**, there are beautiful Deities of Śrī Śrī Nitāi-Gaurāṅga, now served by the Gauḍīya Maṭha; when the Maṭha took over, the temple was called "Śrīvāsa-Mahāprabhu Mandir", so they may be Śrīvāsa's own Deities. The meditation-mantra and gāyatrī of Śrīvāsa are given in Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī's *Paddhati* (53, 72).

## Guru and disciples

- He is a branch of the Caitanya tree and one of the Pañca-tattva (*Abhidhāna*). In the Paramparā Tree of this app he is placed under Śrī Mādhavendra Purī (dīkṣā); the sources read here do not name his dīkṣā-guru.
