## In short

**Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī** (Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmīpāda, "Gosāiji"), a descendant of **Śrī Advaita Prabhu** in the eighth generation, went from Śaṅkara's Advaita to the Brāhmo Samāj, of which he became a leading preacher, and back to the devotion of Mahāprabhu; initiated by **Brahmānanda Paramahaṁsa** of Gayā, he founded the āśrama of **Geṇḍāriā** in Dhaka and had many disciples. He was born on **19 Śrāvaṇa 1841, Jhūlana-pūrṇimā**, at **Śikārpur** (Nadia), and passed away on **22 Jyaiṣṭha 1306 BS** (1899) at Purī, after eating sweets poisoned by the priests of a maṭha.

## Family and youth

His father, **Ānandacandra Gosvāmī**, seventh in descent from Advaita, a great scholar of the Bhāgavatam, cooked all the bhoga for Śrī Śyāmasundara himself, washing even the firewood in the Ganges, and always wore the śālagrāma round his neck; his mother **Svarṇamayī Devī** was very devoted and kind. Restless and naughty as a child, he survived many accidents; at the Sanskrit school of Śāntipura he quickly mastered the *Mugdhabodha* grammar. He married while at the Sanskrit College, and, reading Śaṅkara's commentary, became an Advaitavādī. Hearing a disciple of his at Āmalāgāchi (Rangpur) speak of his devotion to him, he gave up being a guru; a voice told him, "Think of the world beyond death," fever seized him, and his faith in Vedānta collapsed.

## The Brāhmo Samāj

In 1860 **Maharṣi Debendranāth Ṭhākura** converted him to the Brāhmo religion and he threw away his sacred thread; he preached at Bāghāñcar, Sāntrāgāchi, Śāntipura and elsewhere and was made a leader (ācārya) of the Ādi Brāhmo Samāj. At Śāntipura, at the request of the devotee Harimohana Pramāṇik, he began to read the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* and found peace; he met **Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī** and **Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī** and loved their company, and after hearing a devotional song from his elder brother Vrajagopāla Prabhu he tried to bring kīrtana into the Brāhmo Samāj. From 1865, living at Dhaka, he preached and practised medicine in Barisal, Tripura, Noakhali and Sylhet, then gave up medicine for preaching, encouraged by **Keśava Sen**; overwork brought on heart trouble.

## Initiation

At Kāśī **Trailaṅga Svāmī** took him to a lonely place by the Varuṇā and initiated him. Searching for a guru, in **Āṣāḍha 1883** he received initiation and the method of worship from **Brahmānanda Paramahaṁsa** of Mānasa-sarovara on the Ākāśagaṅgā hill at Gayā, who later appeared to him in meditation and told him to take sannyāsa from **Hariharānanda Sarasvatī** at Kāśī — and afterwards, by the same order, to live again with his wife. In **Bhādra 1888**, on Janmāṣṭamī, he founded the āśrama of **Geṇḍāriā** in Dhaka and lived there with his family. By his guru's order he stayed a year in Vṛndāvana in the Deuji temple of Gopīnātha Bāgh (the text says 1927, impossible since he died in 1306 BS), meeting often with **Gaura Kiśora Śiromaṇi**; after the death of **Yogamāyā Devī** (his wife) he went to the Kumbha Melā at Hardwar.

## Disciples

Among those who took shelter of him were **Harimohana Caudhurī**, later **Svāmī Saccidānanda Vrajabālā**, who took sannyāsa from him at Geṇḍāriā in 1888, and **Śrīdhara**, who tells how Gaura Kiśora Śiromaṇi's feet turned away from his obeisances (*Sadguru-saṅga*). He said one should meet Śiromaṇi to understand the verse *tṛṇād api*. His books: *Vaktṛtā*, *Upadeśa* and *Āśāvatīra Upākhyāna*.
