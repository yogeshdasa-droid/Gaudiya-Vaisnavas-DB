## In short

**Svāmī Saccidānanda Bālakṛṣṇa Vrajabālā** (born **Harimohana Caudhurī**, 1853–1928) of **Dhāmrāi** (Dhaka), a disciple of **Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī**, became famous in Vṛndāvana as **Vrajabālā**, a maidservant of Śrī Rādhā, after learning to worship as a gopī from **Rādhikānātha Gosvāmī**. He founded the kuñja **Śrī Yogamāyā-Vijayakṛṣṇa** near the Gopīnātha temple and brought the Deity of **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vrajanāgara** to his village.

## Teacher and householder

He was born in **1853** at Dhāmrāi, by the grace of Śrī Baladeva of Purī, so it was said. He passed his B.A. from Dhaka College and studied for some time for the M.A. As a boy he would sit alone in the forest in meditation; at twelve a voice told him to stay indifferent to family life. He married, without attraction, and taught school without interest; he left his post to meditate in the forest until his relatives forced him back to teaching in Dhaka. He had a son, **Aśvinī Kumāra**.

## Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī

When **Vijayakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī** came to the Geṇḍāriā āśrama in Dhaka, Harimohana went to see him and found the great soul he had seen many times in dreams; he and his wife took shelter at his feet. After initiation his detachment grew; he ran wherever saṅkīrtana was sung, the family fell into poverty — his wife sometimes had nothing to cook and sent their son to her father's — and he gave up teaching altogether. In **1888**, after the Dhulaṭa festival at Geṇḍāriā, he took **sannyāsa** from Gosāiji as **Svāmī Saccidānanda** and went on pilgrimage; later he took the full renunciation of a daṇḍī from **Digambara Svāmī Viśuddhānanda Sarasvatī** at Nasik. His full life is told by Haridāsa Basu in the *Sadguru-līlā*.

## Vrajabālā

In Vṛndāvana he met **Rādhikānātha Gosvāmī** and took lessons from him in worship as a gopī, giving up the ascetic's dress for a gopī's clothes and ornaments. One night Kṛṣṇa Himself led him to Rādhikānātha's kuñja; on the way he found a fruit and fed Kṛṣṇa. At the door, Rādhikānātha had just dreamt that Saccidānanda was feeding Kṛṣṇa; seeing the peel in his hand, both wept in ecstasy. From then on he was known as **Vrajabālā**, Rādhā's maidservant in Vraja. In a drought the Vrajavāsīs held a nāma-yajña; he composed a song and sang it round Govardhana, came back to Rādhākuṇḍa shaking with fever, jumped into the kuṇḍa and was cured — and soon heavy rain fell over Vraja.

## His kuñja and Deity

In **1897–98** he founded the kuñja **Śrī Yogamāyā-Vijayakṛṣṇa** near the Gopīnātha temple and, with Dewān Saṁsāracandra Sen, brought the Deity of **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vrajanāgara** from Jaipur; he fell into debt and would not ask his many disciples for money. To help a Caube brāhmaṇa he married his daughter **Jānakībālā (Kokilā Devī)**, and left the kuñja to his disciple **Bālānanda** and Kokilā Mātā; after Bālānanda's death he brought Kokilā Mātā and the Deity to Bengal and installed Vrajanāgara at Dhāmrāi, served by his first wife **Vindhyavāsinī** and his son Aśvinī. At a saṅkīrtana in Navadvīpa in 420 Gaurābda, his legs aching from dancing, the devotees said, "If you can dance today we shall believe you are a bhaktāvatāra"; he prayed to Mahāprabhu, danced madly, and the pain was gone. He called the children Gopāla and Gopālī, spoke only of spiritual things, held no prejudice of caste or creed, and danced as soon as he heard the khol.

His books: *Citramocana-kāvya*, *Prīti-kusuma*, *Dhruva-caritra*, *Śrī Rādhā-kṛṣṇa Gaurāṅga in the Best Indian Pelf*, *The Divine Manual*, *Prasūnāñjali*, *Līlāmbudhi* and *Gīti-vaijayantī*. He passed away in **1928**.
