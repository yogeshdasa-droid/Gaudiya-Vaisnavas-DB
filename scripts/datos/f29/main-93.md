## In short

**Banwārīlāl Siṁha** («Siṁhajī», 1838–1906), of a respected Uttara-rāḍhīya kāyastha family of **Pāñcthupi** (Kāndi, Murshidabad), founded **Śrī Harivāsara** in his home, where many renounced Vaiṣṇavas of Vraja, Śrī Kṣetra and Gauḍa-maṇḍala lived and did bhajana under his shelter. His house at Pāñcthupi became a place of pilgrimage. **Tribhaṅga dāsa Bābājī** (#1555) grew up in his service.

## Śrī Harivāsara

As a boy, the spirit of renunciation woke in him through a villager, **Kṛṣṇadayāla**, a great devotee, scholar and kīrtanīyā of the Manoharaśāhī school. As his renunciation and devotion grew, he established Śrī Harivāsara at his home, and with some devotees of his village organized a movement to spread the message of love that, the record says, caused a sensation across the land of Rāḍha. He always served the Vaiṣṇavas and guests generously.

He passed away on the third day of the bright fortnight of **Phālguna, 1313 BS (1906)**. It is said that nine years after his death the Mahānta Mahāśaya experienced Siṁhajī's *aprakaṭa-līlā* at Śrī Harivāsara.
