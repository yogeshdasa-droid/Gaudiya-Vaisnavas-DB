## In short

Jagadānanda Paṇḍita of Navadvīpa was a boyhood friend of Nimāi and one of the most intimate associates of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu: "Paṇḍita Jagadānanda, the very life of the Lord, known in the world as His second self" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.21). He knew nothing but the Lord. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Satyabhāmā**, Kṛṣṇa's proud queen of Dvārakā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 51): his love showed itself as anger and sulking, and the Lord, like Kṛṣṇa with Satyabhāmā, always gave way to it.

## In Navadvīpa

He was Nimāi's friend and classmate, and with Him in the pastimes of Navadvīpa — the subduing of the Kāzī, the deliverance of Jagāi and Mādhāi — and in the kīrtanas. When the Lord took sannyāsa he went with Him to Purī, with Nityānanda, Dāmodara Paṇḍita and Mukunda Datta; on the way, it was during his care that Nityānanda broke the Lord's daṇḍa.

## In Purī

He served the Lord in Purī, and every year went to Bengal to see Śacī Mātā and bring back her news and gifts.

**The pot of oil.** Once he had Śivānanda Sena prepare a pot of scented sandalwood oil in Bengal and brought it to Purī, so that the Lord might rub a little on His head and be cured of His bile and wind. The Lord would not take it: "A sannyāsī has no right to oil. Keep a man to rub Me every day! Was it for this that I became a sannyāsī? People on the road will smell the oil and call Me a married sannyāsī. Give it to Jagannātha, to burn in His lamps." Jagadānanda broke the pot before the Lord's eyes, went home, shut the door and lay there without eating for three days; the Lord went Himself, woke him and asked him to cook for Him, and ate with great pleasure (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 12.106–150).

**The pillow.** Seeing the Lord lie on dry banana bark, he made a soft pillow and quilt; the Lord refused them, and Svarūpa Dāmodara made a bed of dried banana leaves instead (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 13.4–23).

**To Vṛndāvana.** He begged the Lord's leave to go to Vṛndāvana, and the Lord gave it with instructions: stay with Sanātana and Rūpa, do not go alone into the forests, come back soon. In Vṛndāvana Sanātana came to eat with him wearing a saffron cloth on his head; thinking it was the Lord's, Jagadānanda was overjoyed — but hearing it came from the Māyāvādī sannyāsī Mukunda Sarasvatī, he grabbed the cooking pot to strike Sanātana. Sanātana was delighted: "This is the love for the Lord I wanted to see" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 13.24–76). He brought back gifts from Sanātana for the Lord.

See also *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 10.21, Antya 4.141–197 (with Sanātana), 12, 13.

## Works

Verse 271 of the *Padyāvalī* is his (*Viśvakoṣa*). The *Prema-vivarta*, a Bengali work on the Lord's pastimes, is also attributed to him.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Satyabhāmā** — "satyabhāmā-prakāśo 'pi jagadānanda-paṇḍitaḥ" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 51).

## Other persons of the same name

The Jagadānanda Ṭhākura of Śrīkhaṇḍa and Jofalāi (18th century, the poet) and the other Jagadānandas of the *Abhidhāna* are other persons.
