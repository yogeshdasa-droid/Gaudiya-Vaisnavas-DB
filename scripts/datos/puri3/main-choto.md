## In short

Choṭa ("junior") Haridāsa was a kīrtana singer of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Purī and a branch of the Caitanya tree: "Baḍa Haridāsa and Choṭa Haridāsa, two kīrtana singers, stayed with Mahāprabhu" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.147). He had a very sweet voice. For speaking with a woman to beg some rice, the Lord gave him up; he left his body in the Gaṅgā at Prayāga, and afterwards sang for the Lord in a spiritual body (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2).

## The rice of Mādhavī

**Bhagavān Ācārya** invited the Lord to his house, but had no fine rice; he sent Choṭa Haridāsa to beg some from **Mādhavī Devī**, the elderly sister of Śikhi Māhiti, a great Vaiṣṇavī — the "half" of the Lord's three and a half intimate devotees. The Lord ate with pleasure and asked where such rice came from. "From Mādhavī Devī." — "Who brought it?" — "Choṭa Haridāsa." Back at His house the Lord told Govinda: "From today Choṭa Haridāsa is not to come here."

When the devotees asked why, the Lord said: "I cannot see the face of a renunciant who talks with women. The senses are so strong that even a wooden woman steals the mind of a sage; little men make a show of renunciation like monkeys, and go about talking with women to please their senses" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2.117–120). Choṭa Haridāsa stopped eating. The devotees begged the Lord; Paramānanda Purī himself came to plead, and the Lord answered: "I will go to Ālālanātha with Govinda; you stay here," and Purī had to bring Him back. Svarūpa Dāmodara told Haridāsa to eat and wait: the Lord would surely be merciful one day. So for a year Haridāsa watched the Lord only from afar when He went to see Jagannātha.

## At Prayāga

At last one night, without telling anyone, Choṭa Haridāsa went to **Prayāga**, and at the meeting of the Gaṅgā and the Yamunā he gave up his life. Soon after, the devotees in Purī heard his sweet singing in the air, though no one could be seen: by the Lord's mercy he had a spiritual body and still sang for Him, unseen. When the devotees of Bengal came the next year and told of his death at Prayāga, Svarūpa and the others understood (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2.143–165). His story became a lesson for all renunciants.

## Not to be confused

He is not **Laghu Haridāsa**, who went with Rūpa Gosvāmī to see Gopāla in Vṛndāvana (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 18.52), nor Haridāsa Ṭhākura.
