## In short

Gajapati Mahārāja Pratāparudra Deva was the independent king of Orissa (reigned 1497–1541 CE; his capital was Cuttack) and a devotee of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, counted in the sub-branch of Gadādhara Paṇḍita: "Pratāparudra and Oḍhra Kṛṣṇānanda" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.135; *Abhidhāna*). The Lord refused to see him, because He was a sannyāsī and he a king; the king won Him with his humility and his service. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **King Indradyumna**, who began the worship of Lord Jagannātha (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 118).

## Family

He was the son of **Puruṣottama Deva** (1470–1497) and **Padmāvatī** (Rūpāmbikā), daughter of the king of Kāñcī: Lord Jagannātha Himself helped Puruṣottama defeat that king, and gave him the princess. Puruṣottama swept the road before the Ratha with a golden broom, and so did his son. Puruṣottama was the son of Kapilendra Deva (1435–1470), seventh in descent from Anaṅga Bhīmadeva, who built the present temple of Jagannātha.

Queens (according to his *Sarasvatī-vilāsa*): Padmā, Padmālayā, Ilā and Pratilā (or Mahilā); later Oriya biographers name more. Sons: **Puruṣottama Jānā** (the "baḍa-jānā", who nearly had Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka killed), Kaluyā Deva and Kakhāru Deva; a daughter, Tukkā. Puruṣottama Jānā, by an order of Śrī Rādhā in a dream, sent Her Deity from the temple of Jagannātha to Govindadeva in Vṛndāvana (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 6.63–113). The king, the queens and the princes were all devotees of the Lord (*Abhidhāna*): when the Lord danced before the Ratha, "the king watched from afar with his people, and the queens watched from the palace roofs" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 10.63).

His guru was **Kāśī Miśra**: whenever he was in Purī he came every day to his house, massaged his feet after his meal, and asked about the service of Jagannātha (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya; *Abhidhāna*). It was at his request that Kavi Karṇapūra wrote the *Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka*.

## The king and the sannyāsī

When the Lord first came to Purī the king was away at war with Vijayanagara. Hearing about Him from Sārvabhauma, he chose the house of Kāśī Miśra for the Lord to live in. But when Sārvabhauma spoke of the king, the Lord covered His ears: "Nārāyaṇa! For a sannyāsī, to see a king is as poison, like seeing a woman. Speak of this again and I will leave Purī." The king answered: "If He will not see me, I will not keep my life; without His mercy, my kingdom and my life are useless" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 11).

He gave **Rāmānanda Rāya** his salary to stay with the Lord, and from the palace roof he watched the devotees of Bengal arrive, while Sārvabhauma named them one by one. Nityānanda sent him a cloth of the Lord, which he worshipped as the Lord Himself. When Rāmānanda brought the young prince, dark and dressed in yellow, the Lord remembered Kṛṣṇa and embraced him; and the king, embracing his son, felt he had touched the Lord (Madhya 11–12).

**The Ratha-yātrā.** Taking off his royal dress, the king swept the road before the Ratha with a golden broom and sprinkled sandal-water; seeing this humility, the Lord was pleased. Dancing before the Ratha, the Lord fell, and the king held Him: "Fie, the touch of a king!" — but within, He was pleased. At the Jagannātha-vallabha garden, by Sārvabhauma's advice, the king came dressed as a plain Vaiṣṇava, massaged the Lord's feet and recited the *Gopī-gīta* of the *Bhāgavatam*. At the verse *tava kathāmṛtam* the Lord rose and embraced him: "You have given Me priceless jewels, and I have nothing to give you but this embrace" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 14).

**The dream.** The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* tells it differently: the king first saw the Lord from afar, rolling in the dust, covered with tears and saliva, and felt some repulsion. That night he dreamed of Jagannātha covered with dust and tears; when he went to touch His feet, Jagannātha said: "Do not touch Me — your body is anointed with sandal and camphor, and Mine is full of dust; you despised Me when I danced." And on the throne the king saw the Lord Himself, smiling. He fell to the ground and begged forgiveness (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.168–177).

When the Lord left for Vṛndāvana, the king came to Him at Cuttack and the Lord embraced him; at the Mahānadī the royal family watched from elephants as the Lord crossed in a new boat, and the king fainted. He ordered pillars to be raised where the Lord had rested and crossed, and said he would bathe there every day and die there (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 16).

## His end

He died, it is said, in 1534 CE, a year after the Lord's disappearance; the record's other text gives his reign as 1497–1541. When he heard of the Lord's disappearance he fell to the ground unconscious (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 3.218). It is said that, unable to bear the Lord's going to Vṛndāvana, he had a wooden Deity of Śrī Caitanya made; it was worshipped, with Jagannātha and Dadhivāmana, at the village of **Pratāpapura**, eleven miles from Baripada (Mayurbhanj), where his samādhi is said to have been, until the river took it away (*Gauḍīya* 24; *Abhidhāna*).

## Works

*Sarasvatī-vilāsa*, *Prauḍha-pratāpa-mārtaṇḍa*, *Kautuka-cintāmaṇi*, *Nirṇaya-saṅgraha*, and some songs; the *Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭaka* of Rāmānanda Rāya names him in its colophons (*Abhidhāna*).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**King Indradyumna**, who began the worship of Lord Jagannātha (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 118).
