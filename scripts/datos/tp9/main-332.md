## In short

**Giridhārī dāsa Bābājī**, born **Annadāprasāda Rāya**, was the elder brother of **Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya Bahādur**, the zamindar of the Tāḍāś estate (Pabna). Saved as a young man from becoming a Christian by **Gaurakiśora Śiromaṇi**, he took bheka in 1890 from **Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī of Govardhana** and lived in Vraja on mādhukarī; the Vaiṣṇavas called him **mahātmā**. He was found passed away in his kuṭīra on the **kṛṣṇa trayodaśī of Pauṣa, 1308 BS** (1901).

## Early life

When he was a child his father, **Gaṅgāprasāda**, brought the whole family to live in Vṛndāvana; there were no schools there then for householders' children, so he studied with private tutors. After his marriage his father died, and the service of the deities, the family and the estate fell on him. Religious from boyhood but never taught his own religion, he turned against Hinduism and decided to become a Christian; his pious mother averted it by putting him in the care of **Gaurakiśora Śiromaṇi**. In time he regained his balance and became a great devotee, and Śiromaṇi named him **Advaita dāsa**.

At thirty-six he lost his wife after the birth of a daughter, who soon followed her, and he did not marry again. After Śiromaṇi's passing he took veśa from **Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī of Govardhana** in **1890**, with the name **Giridhārī dāsa**, lived only on mādhukarī and took nothing from his family. From 1895 he lived under the shelter of his younger brother, who worshipped Śrī Rādhā-vinoda; their worldly bond became one of divine love.

## His words

Asked whether one should point out the faults of another devotee, he said no: "If he is aware of them he can correct himself; we all make mistakes we cannot help. Best is to pray at the feet of Girirāja and Paurṇamāsī for their mercy on him." And: "As the body needs food every day and medicine when it is ill, so līlā-smaraṇa and bhajana are the food of spiritual life, and tattva-jñāna the medicine that cleans the mind."

## Kṛṣṇadāsa must not leave Vraja

At **Rādhākuṇḍa** he became a close friend of a great Vaiṣṇava named **Kṛṣṇadāsa**. When Kṛṣṇadāsa said he wished to visit Bengal, Giridhārī rolled on the ground weeping: "It is my fault: our talks have stirred this wish. Leaving Vraja now would set back your bhajana. At least wait three days." For those three days he neither ate nor drank, chanted 25,000 names, offered 108 prostrations and went round Rādhākuṇḍa and Śyāmakuṇḍa four times a day, praying to Rādhārāṇī. On the third night Kṛṣṇadāsa's guru appeared to him in a dream: "For three days the mahātmā Giridhārī dāsa has fasted and prayed at Rādhārāṇī's feet." In the morning Kṛṣṇadāsa found him lying under the tulasī, praying in a weak voice, "O Vṛndādevī, Kṛṣṇadāsa must not leave your dhāma," and fell at his feet; he never went to Bengal.

## His departure

On the kṛṣṇa trayodaśī of Pauṣa 1308 BS word came that Giridhārī had not gone out for mādhukarī; his door was bolted from inside. He had been at the evening talks the night before, in good health. Rājarṣi Bahādur, Kāminī Kumāra Ghoṣa and others opened the door and found him lying where he slept, his right hand under his head, a dry kaupīna on and the wet one on the floor as if he had just bathed, eyes half closed and a smile on his face. From that look, one would think he had seen such a vision of the Lord's līlā that his heart could not bear its ecstasy.
