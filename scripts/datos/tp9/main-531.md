## In short

**Jagadīśa dāsa Bābājī Mahāśaya** of **Kāliya-daha** in Vṛndāvana, a disciple of **Siddha Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī of Kālnā**, was one of the most revered renunciants of Vraja at the end of the nineteenth century. He lived on mādhukarī, without salt, taught humility above all, and passed away at Kāliya-daha in **1322 BS (1915 AD)**, at the age of a hundred.

## From physician to bābājī

He was born in a high-caste brāhmaṇa family, a tall, handsome man of fair complexion, and became a physician with a good practice at **Kālnā**; a relative of his was a pleader of Burdwan. He took dīkṣā from **Siddha Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī**, who preached "the practice of Harināma and the cultivation of bhakti-bhāva" — Jagadīśa was drawn, perhaps, more by his character than by his līlā-kathā. In his last days Siddha Bābā called him and had him write down some names, and those names became Jagadīśa's guru-praṇālī. He later said that his guru had not preached rāgānugā bhakti, which he himself now followed: "As one perfects one's handwriting by writing again and again, so in līlā-smaraṇa one must follow the footsteps of experienced Vaiṣṇavas." He did not want smaraṇa learned by heart from books; the book should be kept at hand, to look at when needed.

## In Vraja

For a long time he lived with other Vaiṣṇavas near the old temple of Madana-mohana in Vṛndāvana, living only on mādhukarī. Once at Varṣāṇā **Jagadānanda Bābājī** and **Mādhava dāsa Bābājī**, disciples of Siddha Nityānanda dāsa Bābājī, asked him to stay there. "No, bābā, I must go back to Vṛndāvana, or I may fall seriously ill again." Jagadānanda did not see the meaning then; soon he himself fell ill, could no longer go for mādhukarī, went to Vṛndāvana, and died there loudly chanting the holy name.

Then Jagadīśa lived in a small kuṭīra at **Kāliya-daha**. He gave up salt; not wanting to go to a barber, yet thinking a Vaiṣṇava should not grow long hair, he cut his own hair and beard with a pair of scissors. Almost every day on his mādhukarī round he visited Nityānanda's gaddī at **Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa**, and was very close to its head, **Premānanda Gosvāmī**: when he felt dry in bhajana, Premānanda would tell him to put down his bag and roll in the dust of Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa by the Yamunā, and his eagerness would return. After Premānanda's passing he went with Mādhava dāsa to roll in the dust of his samādhi.

## Stories

- **The monkeys.** In his old age, on the disappearance day of Siddha Bhagavān dāsa, a devotee, Kinu Bābu, gave him forty rupees for Vaiṣṇava-sevā. He bought twenty rupees of molasses for mālpuā, left the bags in his kuṭīra and went to wash his feet; the monkeys came and ate it all. He was delighted, bowed down to them, and in the evening told the devotees: "The monkey Vaiṣṇavas of Vraja have eaten it all," and gave back the other twenty rupees for sevā.
- **The pond.** He once wished a great pond dug at Kāliya-daha for the Lord's pastimes; in a day a widow of Lālā Bābu's family offered 75,000 rupees, the King of Hetampur 25,000, and Rājarṣi Bahādur the rest. That night the plan kept him from his bhajana; he slipped away into the forest for four or five days, and on his return forbade the work: "Let it be done after my death; if it begins now I shall be ruined."
- **Rājarṣi Vanamālī Rāya.** "The śāstra says that one who has renounced everything should live in Vṛndāvana. You have not; why do you live here?" — and, after a silence: "You have been brought here to serve those who live in Vraja and do bhajana." From then the Rājarṣi spent freely in the service of the Vrajavāsīs.
- **A pilgrim** asked how to get prema-bhakti. "Can you come to live in Vraja?" — "No." — "Then spend two annas on Narottama Ṭhākura's *Prārthanā* and *Prema-bhakti-candrikā*, study them and follow them." To the letters that followed he answered once: "You will have prema-bhakti when you want nothing else."

## His teaching

He was always careful not to hurt anyone, and avoided going places lest he offend. To a visitor staying three days he said: "Do not offend even the ants and mosquitoes of Vraja." He preached the verse *tṛṇād api sunīcena*: "As long as you cannot live by this śloka you will not attain prema; you will be close to it exactly as much as you practise it" — and "to think oneself unworthy and wholly fallen is the source of prema and all perfection." When people found fault with others he told how Siddha Bhagavān dāsa answered a complaint that a disciple still ate fish: "You too ate fish once; like you, he too will give it up one day."

He passed away at Kāliya-daha in 1322 BS (1915), a hundred years old.
