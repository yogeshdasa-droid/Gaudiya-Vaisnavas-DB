## In short

**Manohara dāsa Bābājī** (born **Mahendra**, 1847–1947), a learned and humble bābājī, taught the Bhāgavatam and the Sandarbhas in Vraja and lived for many years at **Govinda-kuṇḍa** on mādhukarī; he wrote the *Vaidagdhī-vilāsa* and the *Nāma-ratna-mālā*. He was initiated by **Śrīpāda Nandakiśora Gosvāmī** of Advaita's family, took veśa from **Prāṇakṛṣṇa dāsa Bābājī** as **Ambikā dāsa**, and received his name Manohara dāsa from his śikṣā-guru **Śrī Rūpa dāsa Bābājī**.

## Youth

He was born in 1847 at **Mādhavapura** (Nadia), the son of Bholānātha Adhikārī and Pyārīsundarī. His mother died when he was a child. At six he dreamt that a saint took him into a forest and set it on fire on every side, and the dream gave him a distaste for worldly life. When he lost his father at thirteen his brother was annoyed by his lack of interest in the family, so he left and lived at his aunt's house at **Simuliyā**, where Nandakiśora Gosvāmī initiated him. When the ākhaḍā of Simuliyā had no mahānta, he took veśa from Prāṇakṛṣṇa dāsa Bābājī as Ambikā dāsa and looked after it for three years.

## Study and pilgrimage

Eager to learn, he studied for two years at a large ākhaḍā in **Navadvīpa** under Bipincandra Bhaṭṭācārya, and visited Rāmakeli, Gayeśapura, Kendubilva, Mayanāḍāl and other places; Śrī Rūpa dāsa Bābājī renewed his veśa and named him Manohara dāsa. He went with Paṇḍita Narottama dāsa Bābājī to Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī and listened to their discussions, had darśana of Siddha Bhagavān dāsa Bābā at Kālnā, and stayed at the ākhaḍā of Kambuliyā-ṭolā in Calcutta. In 1879–80, on his way to Purī, he stayed at the ākhaḍā of Kāntāpukur, visited Gopīvallabhapura and Remuṇā and the Gaṅgāmātā Maṭha, and studied the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* for some months at Remuṇā.

## Vraja

In 1881, though Śrī Rādhācaraṇa dāsa begged him to stay, he left for Vṛndāvana; on the way he fell very ill at a railway station until someone paid his fare. For five years he managed the temple founded by Śrī Gurucaraṇa dāsa, the former head of the Govinda temple, and studied the scriptures under Śrīpāda Gopīlāl Gosvāmī, the sevāit of Śrī Rādhāramaṇa; Siddha Balarāma Bābā of Jhāḍu-maṇḍala, Jagadīśa Bābā of Kāliya-daha and Nityānanda dāsa Bābā were then alive. Then he did bhajana at **Kusuma-sarovara**, where Śrī Caturbhuja Paṇḍita of Rādhākuṇḍa studied the Bhāgavatam with him. After five years he went on Vraja-parikramā with Rāmakṛṣṇa Paṇḍita Bābājī and others; a secluded place he chose had to be left for fear of the Muslims; he went to Kāmyavana, Nandagrāma, and to Bhādāvalī, where he studied the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* for a year. In **1893**, by a dream, he settled for good at **Govinda-kuṇḍa**.

There Gopāla dāsa, Rāmasvarūpa Miśra of Anor village and many others studied the Bhāgavatam with him. He begged only a little flour. When Rāmasvarūpa Miśra, a priest of the Gosvāmīs of the Vallabha sampradāya, took initiation from him, the village was in uproar; Maṇirāma Vaiśya and many others followed. He chanted constantly, remembering the aṣṭa-kālīya-līlā, slept and spoke little, and did bhajana under a banyan tree; bothered by monkeys and vultures, he had a cave built and lived in it.

## Two changes

For a year he suffered from chronic flatulence; when cured he was gentler, charitable, unable to see others suffer, and gave up regular mālā-japa and daily tilaka. In **1917** he built a temple and arranged the service of **Śrī Madana-mohana**; at the siṁhāsana-yātrā of 1918 he held a great festival (bread and a soup of neem leaves were offered, and Ananta dāsa and others cooked rice), and again in 1936. Later the illness came back and he changed again: restless, sorrowful, praying to be delivered; any talk of making disciples upset him. He offered daṇḍavats to everyone, blamed only himself, never let anyone touch his feet, and mixed the water from washing his feet with dust so that no one could drink it as caraṇāmṛta. He passed away on the **śukla trayodaśī of Śrāvaṇa, 1947**.

## His answer

"How can one overcome the obstacles in bhajana?" — "If one does bhajana with all one's heart and strength, the Lord, pleased with the effort, opens the gate. Obstacles diminish in the course of bhajana, not in one day. One must practise with eagerness and intensity, and with patience. To fix the mind is the hardest thing; it is not purified until it leaves its attachments, and without a pure mind there is no bhakti, and without bhakti no realisation of the Lord. By the strength of bhajana the material tendencies are wiped out little by little."
