## In short

**Gaura dāsa Bābājī** of **Nandagrāma** did bhajana around **1873** in the bhajana-kuṭīra of Sanātana Gosvāmī on the bank of **Pāvana-sarovara**, and was known as a realised soul. Every day he gathered flowers at Gājīpur near Prema-sarovara and made a garland for **Lālajī**, the Deity of Nandagrāma — and Kṛṣṇa Himself stopped him from leaving.

## "Who will offer Me flowers?"

After five or six years of offering flowers he grew bitter: "I have served Lālajī so long and still He has shown me no grace. Kṛṣṇa is rather hard-hearted; but the heart of Rādhikā, Vṛṣabhānu's daughter, is full of compassion — had I served Her so long, She would surely have been merciful. I shall go to Varṣāṇā at once." In the afternoon he set out across a field a mile south of Nandagrāma with his bundle on his back. At dusk he met a herd of cows coming home, and the handsome dark boy with them asked where he was going and urged him to go back to Nandagrāma. Gaura dāsa poured out his frustration and would not turn back; then the boy stretched out his arms and, whichever way the bābājī turned, blocked his path. "You naughty fellow! Why do you trouble me so?" — "Bābājī, if you go away, who will offer Me flowers?" Struck by the words, he asked who the boy was — and boy and cows vanished. Weeping and rolling on the ground he cried, "O Kṛṣṇa, why did You deceive me? I could not recognise You. Be merciful to me, the least worthy of all." He went back to Nandagrāma and wept all night, and the priest of Lālajī's temple was told: "Be watchful: Gaura dāsa must by no means stop offering flowers to Me."
