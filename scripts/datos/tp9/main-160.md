## In short

**Śrīpāda Brahmānanda Gosvāmī Prabhu** (later called **Śrī Paramānanda**), a fourth-generation descendant of **Nandakiśora**, founded the **Śrīpāṭa of Nityānanda Prabhu in Vṛndāvana**. Handsome, learned in the scriptures and a famous kīrtanīyā, he was a close friend of **Siddha Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī**. He passed away on the **śukla caturdaśī of Jyaiṣṭha, 1305 BS** (1898).

## The kīrtanīyā

He learned the art of kīrtana under an ascetic Vaiṣṇava, **Golokadāsa Bābājī**. When he sang with his cousin **Nṛsiṁhānanda**, the listeners felt that Caitanya and Nityānanda were present; when people heard that Brahmānanda was singing they left their work and ran to him, and great men and other kīrtanīyās forgot the time of day. When the kīrtana went on till midnight he would send word to his steward to prepare food for some forty Vaiṣṇavas.

Every year he held two ceremonies for his parents. Once a whole day of kīrtana was followed by nagara-saṅkīrtana the next day; the first day was led by the famous **Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī** with his brother **Gauradāsa Bābājī** (the father and uncle of Navadvīpa Vrajavāsī), whose kīrtana was then thought unequalled. Afterwards Brahmānanda took Kṛṣṇadāsa to his room, where a bed had been put beside his own, and when the tired singer fell asleep, quietly massaged his body; when Kṛṣṇadāsa woke and asked what he was doing, Brahmānanda put him back to sleep — he should think of tomorrow's nagara-saṅkīrtana, not of the massage.

## His character

He was dignified and blissful, without anger, without attraction to pleasure, and ate with love whatever he was given. Prabhupāda Premānanda once told his disciple Mādhava dāsajī that Brahmānanda had earned more than two lakh rupees and kept not a paisa for himself. One noon at **Śrīpāṭa Puruliyā**, as he was finishing his sādhana, he heard that his eldest son had died; while his family wept, he walked across the temple, took prasāda from the pūjārī and went on with his bhajana, leaving the rites to others — and no one ever heard him speak of his son again. When a devotee of his behaved unworthily he blamed himself for not guiding him well, while making sure the man was kept apart from the others, sending him to live elsewhere at his own expense if need be.

In his presence the second **Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa**, a disciple of Siddha Bābā Kṛṣṇadāsa of Govardhana, once recited Śrī Rādhā's snāna-śṛṅgāra-līlā for twenty-four hours without a break.
