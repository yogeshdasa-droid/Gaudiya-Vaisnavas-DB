## In short

**Govinda dāsa Bābājī** (born **Gauracaraṇa Cakravartī**), the mahānta of the **samādhi-maṭha of Haridāsa Ṭhākura in Purī**, was the devoted servant of **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa Deva**. By his guru's order he swept Rādhākuṇḍa for six years, and for twenty-two years he kept up the service of Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi, which was about to be sold. He passed away at Navadvīpa in **1930**.

## An orphan

He was born near **Dulalbāzār** in the Noakhali district (Bangladesh), the only child of his parents. His mother died early; his father took the boy with him everywhere, and soon he too died. Wandering without refuge, the boy kept truthfulness, simplicity and compassion, and the Adhikārī of the many Vaiṣṇavas of Dulalbāzār took him in and lovingly trained him in Vaiṣṇava ways. On a pilgrimage to Viśvanātha at **"Śrīkuṇḍa"** (so the text) he saw the thousand streams of a spring pour down twice as strongly when the holy name was sung, and set out with some mendicants to find its source, until hunger and cold in the mountains forced him back, chanting the name; after that he lost all attachment to home.

## Rādhāramaṇa's servant

He walked to Purī without a paisa and went on to other holy places, sleeping under trees and eating only what came unasked; after some months he returned to Purī, eating at the chatras and sleeping by the road. **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa** used to pass through the streets of Purī with his kīrtana party; one day, singing at the main gate of the Jagannātha temple, he drew Gauracaraṇa to him and initiated him. Later he told him to take veśa from the **Kartā Bābājī of Nārāyaṇa-chātā**; after much resistance Gauracaraṇa obeyed and became **Govinda dāsa**.

The Kartā Bābājī made him devote himself to the service of Rādhāramaṇa: he was to raise alms for the Vaiṣṇavas and eat only their remnants. When a great quantity of mahāprasāda came, the devotees would, for fun, pour the leftovers over his head and lick his body roaring "Haribol"; seeing his comic reaction and his soft rebukes they forgot their hunger. With **Navadvīpa dāsa** he was always alert to their guru's service, and he went through hard tests: he was told to offer daṇḍavat wherever he saw a latrine on his way to the temple, and, in a high fever, to take 108 dips in Narendra-sarovara — he did it joyfully. He knew his guru's wishes before they were spoken and cooked them in advance, and he danced in saṅkīrtana with Navadvīpa, Gokula, Rāmadāsa and Jayagopāla.

## Rādhākuṇḍa

One morning Rādhāramaṇa sent him a letter: "My dear Govinda, as soon as you read this, leave for Vṛndāvana without a second thought; stay at Rādhākuṇḍa, do jhāḍu-sevā (sweeping) and live on mādhukarī. I do not think we can meet now; as Lord Nitāicānd wills, we shall meet at the destined hour and place." Govinda only said: "Let his will be done; give him my daṇḍavats." With Nityasvarūpa Brahmacārī, Śyāmānanda dāsa and Nitāi dāsa he went to Vraja, went round Girirāja, and for six years swept Rādhākuṇḍa without a break, never going even near Varṣāṇā or Nandagrāma; at the end he was endowed with great spiritual wealth. When Rādhāramaṇa came to Vṛndāvana with his devotees, Govinda returned with them to Purī.

## Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi

Rādhāramaṇa suddenly passed away at Navadvīpa; Govinda's grief was beyond words, until a dream told him to go to Purī and serve **Haridāsa Ṭhākura**. The samādhi-temple, with its images of Nitāi-Gaura-Sītānātha in meditation, was very old; its service had fallen into disorder and the maṭha was about to be auctioned to a Christian mission to pay its debts. Govinda carried everything for the service with his own hands, offered the bhoga with deep love and gave the prasāda to the destitute, even to birds and animals, before taking any himself. For twenty-two years, never finding fault with anyone, he kept the service and gave shelter to the shelterless, and trained a devotee to take it over. People whom society hated and families ignored he drew close with love. **Giridhārī dāsa Bābājī** of the Haribol Kuṭīra took veśa from him there.

He loved the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* and the *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*. In his last days he lived near Śrī Rādhāramaṇa Bāg in Navadvīpa, where the Vaiṣṇava-sevā and sat-prasaṅga went on as before, and there he passed away in 1930.
