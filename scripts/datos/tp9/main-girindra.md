## In short

**Giridhārī dāsa Bābājī** (born **Girindra**) of the **Haribol Kuṭīra** in Navadvīpa was the disciple of the kīrtanīyā and munsiff **Vipina Candra Caṭṭopādhyāya**, and took veśa from **Govinda dāsa Bābājī** of the Haridāsa Ṭhākura Maṭha in Purī. He was born in **Māgha 1301 BS** (1894) and passed away at the samādhi of Haridāsa Ṭhākura in Purī on **20 Āśvina 1351 BS** (1944).

## Childhood

He was of the family of the famous **Makaranda Ghoṣa**, a well-to-do kulīna Kāyastha family of **Mahiskhola**, on the river Citrā in the Naḍāil subdivision of Jessore (Bangladesh); his father's younger brother, **Rādhāramaṇa-caraṇa dāsa**, brought this family great fame. His father was Baradākānta, his mother Śaśimukhī Devī, who had four sons: Mahendra, Girindra, Nagendra and Hemendra. Born on the ninth lunar day of Māgha 1301, it is said he came into the world entangled in his own intestines. Sickly from childhood, he had a natural reverence for brāhmaṇas and Vaiṣṇavas; once, near death from fever and dysentery, he was cured by eating a brāhmaṇa's remnants. He cared little for his studies but loved the Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata, dance and music, and the service of tulasī.

## Vipina Bābu

When he was in class six, **Vipina Candra Caṭṭopādhyāya** was posted as munsiff of Naḍāil. Drawn by Vipina's loud and sweet kīrtana every morning at daybreak, Girindra gave up school and became his devotee, against his guardians' wishes; when Vipina was transferred to Bankura after three years, Girindra persuaded him to take him along, leaving everything but a drinking glass. Vipina cared for him like a son, taught him renunciation by his own example and the path of bhajana of **Haribol Ṭhākura**, and after four years gave him dīkṣā in the eighteen-syllable mantra. Wherever Vipina was posted Girindra followed; every morning he went out with arms raised, loudly chanting "Haribol", walking more than eight miles a day, never asking a householder for alms, and cooked whatever was given freely. Once, for a grave fault, Vipina sent him away for fifteen days; Girindra wandered like a madman without food or sleep until he was close to death, and on the fifteenth day Vipina took him back — and from then on corrected him sternly, with whatever came to hand.

## Separation and veśa

After Vipina passed away (January–February 1923) Girindra, stricken with separation, wandered between Vṛndāvana, Purī and Navadvīpa without a fixed home, by the Ganges, on the ghāṭs or on a householder's veranda. His day: at three in the morning through the streets chanting "Haribol", then kīrtana, a bath in the Ganges, the maṅgala-ārati of Mahāprabhu, darśana of the deities, the household deity's service, bhikṣā, cooking and offering. At Purī, staying at the samādhi of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, he met **Govinda dāsa Bābājī**, the mahānta, veśa-disciple of Raghunātha dāsajī, and took veśa from him as **Giridhārī dāsa**. Then he settled in Navadvīpa, in a kuṭīra named **"Śrī Śrī Haribol"**, built on land given by Nanilal Bhakta with money raised by his devotees. He heard the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* every day and, between the readings, sharply pointed out the faults he had seen in the devotees during the day.

When his health failed he went back to Nīlācala on 10 Jyaiṣṭha 1351 (May–June 1944) and lived in the samādhi-temple of Haridāsa Ṭhākura; he passed away there on 20 Āśvina of the same year, remembering the name of Haridāsa Ṭhākura.
