## In short

**Dharma dāsa Bābājī** of **Kāmyavana**, an Oriyā of little learning, was the grand-disciple of **Siddha Bābā of Kāmyavana** (Siddha Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa) and served his Deity, **Śrī Rādhā-Madanagopāla**, at Vimalā-kuṇḍa. He never criticised anyone, and passed away about **1889 or 1890**, nearly a hundred years old.

## The service of Madanagopāla

He often quoted the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*: "One who chants the holy name with a heart free from malice receives the Lord's blessings and is liberated at once." He took charge of the Deity Siddha Bābā had served. Not strict about rituals, he was very careful about cleanliness: even in old age he would stand waist-deep in **Vimalā-kuṇḍa** to wash. He lived in the jagamohana of the temple and did the maṅgala-ārati all the year round — ringing the bell only after the ārati, so as not to wake **Mādhava dāsa Bābājī** and the others sleeping there.

The service was kept by bhikṣā, but his way of begging was his own: when something ran out he would stand in front of a shop, not looking at the shopkeeper, and simply say that such-and-such was out of stock for the service — and it would be delivered at once. To the Vrajavāsīs who came to bathe at Vimalā-kuṇḍa he gave twigs for their teeth, tilaka and beads, and kept a fire for them in winter. If he was given vegetables he took them; otherwise he picked wild greens and offered them to Madanagopāla with a few chapātīs, and in the early morning made little sweets of wheat flour fried in ghee with jaggery. He always made extra for unexpected guests; if there was not enough, he divided everything equally, and if someone was left out he went on mādhukarī for him.

## "Not our duty"

Once young Mādhava dāsa learned that a bābājī staying with them ate onions and told him to give them up or leave. Dharma dāsa rebuked him: "This place belongs to Siddha Bābājī Mahāśaya. Our duty is only to serve the visiting Vaiṣṇavas and guests; to advise others is not our business."

## In prison

Long before Mādhava dāsa came, a devotee died at Dharma dāsa's place and he gave the dead man's clothes to another Vaiṣṇava. The royal law required the government to be told before anything of the dead was touched, so he was summoned to the court, admitted it, and spent a few days in prison — "I have had the chance to visit the kingdom of Yama," he told Mādhava calmly. He was given no hard labour; flour and pulses came from the royal household, brāhmaṇa prisoners cooked for him, and others looked after his needs.
