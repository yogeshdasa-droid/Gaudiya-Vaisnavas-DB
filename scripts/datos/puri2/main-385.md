## In short

Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka was the second son of **Bhavānanda Rāya** and a brother of **Rāmānanda Rāya**, a branch of the Caitanya tree (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.133). He was a high officer of King Pratāparudra, the collector of Mālajāṭhyā Daṇḍapāṭa (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 9.18). Condemned to death for a debt to the king, he was saved by the mercy of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 9.13–152).

## On the cāṅga

Gopīnātha owed the royal treasury two lakhs of kāhanas of cowries. He offered to pay little by little, selling his goods, and to start with he brought ten or twelve fine horses to the palace. The king sent the prince **Puruṣottama Jānā**, who knew horses, to price them; the prince offered a very low price, and Gopīnātha, angry, mocked him: "My horses do not turn their necks and look up at the sky — so their price should not go down." The insulted prince told the king that Gopīnātha would not pay, and got leave to raise him on the **cāṅga**, a platform from which he would be thrown onto swords.

The devotees ran to the Lord. At first He said: "How can one blame the king? He only asks for what is his; Gopīnātha has spent the king's money." But at the devotees' prayer the news reached the king, who freed Gopīnātha, forgave the debt, gave him his post again and doubled his salary (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 9). Meanwhile his brother Vāṇīnātha, arrested with the family, went on chanting the holy name without fear (Antya 9.55). The Lord told the family not to misuse the king's money any more.
