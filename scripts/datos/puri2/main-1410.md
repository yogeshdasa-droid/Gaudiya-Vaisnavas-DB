## In short

Śikhi Māhiti, a kāyastha of Orissa, was the brother of **Murāri Māhiti** and **Mādhavī Devī** and the scribe of the temple of Jagannātha: he wrote the **Mādalā Pāñji**, its daily chronicle (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 10.42, Antya 2.106). He is one of the "three and a half" intimate devotees of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu: "In the world there are three and a half vessels: Svarūpa Gosāñi, Rāya Rāmānanda and Śikhi Māhiti — three — and his sister is the half" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 2.104–106). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Rāgalekhā**, a maidservant of Śrī Rādhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 189).

## The doubter who dreamed

The three lived in Purī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.136). When the Lord, after His sannyāsa, came to Sārvabhauma's house, the three went to see Him. Murāri and Mādhavī at once accepted Him as the Lord; Śikhi did not: "This sannyāsī who has come is a great soul in every way, but I cannot call him Bhagavān." His brother and sister were deeply hurt, and the quarrel grew until they would not even look at his face, fearing he would one day speak against the Lord.

Then one night a terrible weeping came from Śikhi's room. Murāri and Mādhavī ran in, thinking something had happened, and found the tears running down his cheeks — not tears of fear, but of prema. He had dreamed that the Lord merged into Jagannātha and came out of Him again, called him with raised arms and embraced him. The brothers and sister wept with their arms round each other's necks; Śikhi said: "You are the Lord's own people." The next day the three went to the Garuḍa-stambha in the temple and surrendered for ever at the Lord's feet (*Caitanya-carita-mahākāvya* 13.89–109; *Abhidhāna*).

## Family

His brother Murāri Māhiti and his sister Mādhavī Devī. Śikhi is said to have been the son of a brother of **Bhavānanda Rāya** (the record of Mādhavī Devī).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Rāgalekhā**, a maidservant of Śrī Rādhā; his sister Mādhavī was Kalākeli (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 189; *Abhidhāna*). The *Guru-bhakti-gītā* of Acyutānanda dāsa (Utkal University, 1975, vol. 3, ch. 49, p. 176) calls him the gopī Śaśikalā; so also the *Svarūpa-varṇana* (a manuscript attributed to Rūpa Gosvāmī, Utkal University Library 382) and the *Braja-vihāra* of Daśarathi dāsa (O.L. 49).
