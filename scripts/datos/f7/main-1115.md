## In short

**Premānanda Gosvāmī** (Premānanda Prabhupāda) of **Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa** in Vṛndāvana, the seat (gaddī) of Śrī Nityānanda's family, was the second of the three sons of **Pūrṇānanda Gosvāmī**, born by the blessing of Siddha Vaiṣṇava dāsa Bābājī of Kosi; he took dīkṣā from his elder brother **Nṛsiṁhānanda Prabhu**. After a wayward youth he became the embodiment of humility, with disciples from Bengal to Kabul; he passed away in **1896**, on the third day after the full moon.

## The change

When Nṛsiṁhānanda died soon after his marriage, leaving two daughters, Premānanda became head of the family while still young; wealth and power went to his head and he nearly ruined the family. A great Vaiṣṇava of the Madana-mohana temple took him aside: "Prabhu, how long will you go on like this? You are a descendant of Śrī Nitāicānd, sent to benefit the world." Ashamed — he had just lost a two-year-old daughter — he changed at once: he slept only in the outer room, dressed like any Vrajavāsī in a loincloth, a plain blanket in winter and a coarse cādar in summer, and called himself a servant and disciple of Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa; if distinguished people came he sent them to **Brahmānanda Gosvāmī**. He honoured his elder brother's wife as his guru's wife, "Baḍa Mā", went on his knees on his loincloth to open the cash box in her room, and stopped drinking if she asked him to. He ate from leaf plates and drank from clay cups, and let no disciple eat his remnants or bow to him. When his favourite disciple **Mādhava dāsa** complained that other disciples could take their guru's remnants and foot-water, he answered: "I am no scholar, but I know one verse of the Bhāgavatam: offer obeisances to all, even to animals, caṇḍālas and snakes. Are you outside those?"

## Servant of the Vaiṣṇavas

Whenever a Vaiṣṇava was ill he hurried there with all that was needed; when one died he performed the last rites; to any gathering of Vaiṣṇavas he secretly sent cooked food; he fed devotees lavishly at Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa. Keeping the temple's rule, he let no Vrajavāsī brāhmaṇa inside and took no cooked rice from them, but, as the Bihārī sādhus did, accepted bread from Vaiṣṇava women. Dressed like a poor man, he ran the zamindari with firm order; summoned once to court, he did not go, and by Nitāicānd's grace the government could not find him and the case was dropped. His disciples, men and women, were in Bihar, Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, the Punjab, the North-West, Sindh and even Kabul.

He gave veśa to Mādhava dāsa of Māldaha, and later, after testing him, a second dīkṣā by having him pick up a flower from the temple courtyard; so he initiated many disciples. He taught him etiquette: "Never hand prasāda from a higher place to someone below — and she was taking it for your guru's brother"; and thrift: "Bowing is enough; your service wastes your guru's wealth — you left three peppers on the floor," though the day before he had said, "Now there are a hundred thousand rupees in Śrī Nitāicānd's store." He kept his guru's yearly festival magnificently. Jagadīśa dāsa Bābājī of Kāliya-daha visited him almost daily, and when dry in bhajana was told by him to roll in the dust of Śṛṅgāra-vaṭa (record of Jagadīśa dāsa). Among his disciples the famous kīrtanīyā **Navadvīpa Vrajavāsī** was still alive when the record was written.

## Other Premānandas

The record also held **Premānanda Bhāratī** (Surendranātha Mukhopādhyāya, 1857–1914), who preached in America, and a **Premānanda, brother of Lord Nityānanda** (*Prema-vilāsa* 24); each now has a record of his own.
