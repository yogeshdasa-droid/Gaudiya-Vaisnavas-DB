## In short

Śrī Vīrabhadra Gosvāmī, also called **Vīracandra** and **Jagat-durlabha**, was the son of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu and Vasudhā Devī, and the disciple of Jāhnavā Mātā. "After Nityānanda, the greatest branch is Vīrabhadra Gosāñi, whose branches and sub-branches are countless. Though He was the Supreme Lord, He presented Himself as a great devotee… It is by His mercy that people all over the world can chant the names of Caitanya and Nityānanda" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.8–12). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Kṣīrodakaśāyī (Payodhiśāyī) Viṣṇu**, an expansion of Saṅkarṣaṇa; also Niśaṭha and Ulmuka (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 67). "Some call him Vīrabhadra, some Vīracandra" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 9.420).

## Birth

He was born of Vasudhā Devī at **Khaḍadaha** — on the **śukla caturdaśī of Agrahāyaṇa** according to the *Abhidhāna*; the record's texts give the kṛṣṇa navamī of Kārtika. His sister was **Gaṅgā Devī**. When Abhirāma Ṭhākura bowed to him he survived the obeisance that had killed the elder children, and Abhirāma accepted him as the second form of Śrī Gaurāṅga in his *Vīrabhadrāṣṭaka*. Abhirāma's flute once broke Vīrabhadra's boat; seeing Jāhnavā Mātā's four-armed form his mind changed and he took dīkṣā from her (*Prema-vilāsa* 24).

## Marriage and family

At Jāhnavā's wish he married **Śrīmatī** and **Nārāyaṇī**, the two daughters of **Yadunandana Ācārya** of Jhāmaṭapura near Rājabalahāṭ; Jāhnavā initiated the two girls, and Vīracandra initiated his father-in-law (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13). The *Abhidhāna* gives a daughter, **Bhuvanamohinī**, married to Pārvatīnātha, a Mukhuṭi of Phuliyā.

**Gopījana-vallabha, Rāmakṛṣṇa and Rāmacandra** were his three sons and disciples (Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī, *Anubhāṣya*; *Abhidhāna*): Rāmacandra, the youngest, lived at Khaḍadaha (descendants at Vṛndāvana, Navadvīpa, Khaḍadaha, Calcutta, Dhaka, Butni…); Rāmakṛṣṇa at Gaṇeśapura near Māldaha (descendants at Vṛndāvana, Gayeśpur, Sodpur, Kānāiḍāṅgā, Gorābājār, Māḍo…); Gopījana-vallabha at Latā near Mānakar, Bardhamān (descendants at Latādaha, Nūpura-vallabhapura, Purnia in Bankura, Kodalā, Moktārpur, Agartala, Jessore…). Another text holds that the three were only his disciples, since their gotras, titles and homes were different.

Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja never calls Vīrabhadra "Nityānanda's son" as he calls Advaita's sons "Advaita-nandana", and the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* does not name him; so some have argued he was only a disciple. Jayānanda and the *Bhakti-ratnākara* call him Nityānanda's son; he must have been born in Lord Caitanya's lifetime, since the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* names him.

## Pilgrimage to Vṛndāvana

With his mother's permission he set out from Khaḍadaha: at **Saptagrāma** Śrīnivāsa Datta, Uddhāraṇa's son, kept him two days; at **Śāntipura** Kṛṣṇa Miśra, Advaita's son, led him in kīrtana to the Advaita-bhavana; at **Ambikā-Kālnā** Hṛdaya Caitanya received him; in **Navadvīpa** Mahāprabhu's family; at **Śrīkhaṇḍa** Raghunandana and Kānāi Ṭhākura; at **Yājigrāma** Śrīnivāsa Ācārya worshipped him; then **Kaṇṭaka-nagara**, **Budhuri** (Govinda Kavirāja) and **Kheturī**, where Narottama danced with him in the courtyard of Śrī Gaurāṅga. On the way many sinners were changed by him. In Vṛndāvana Jīva Gosvāmī, Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja, Ananta Ācārya, Haridāsa Paṇḍita (priest of Govinda), Kṛṣṇadāsa Brahmacārī, Madhu Paṇḍita, Bhavānanda, Kāśīśvara, Govinda Gosvāmī and Yādavācārya came to meet him; with the permission of Jīva and Bhūgarbha he visited the twelve forests, Rādhā-kuṇḍa, Śyāma-kuṇḍa and Govardhana, and Kavirāja Gosvāmī accompanied him from Rādhā-kuṇḍa to Vṛndāvana (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13). At the installation of Śrī Rādhā-vinoda in the house of Govinda Cakravartī at Boro-kuli he danced in Narottama's kīrtana.

## Leader of the community

He organised the Vaiṣṇavas of Gauḍa and showed great respect to Śrīnivāsa and Narottama. A letter in the *Bhakti-ratnākara* shows his authority: when **Jayagopāla dāsa** of Kāṇḍarā, a learned kāyastha, proud of his learning, would name only his parama-guru and showed disrespect to prasāda, Vīracandra cut him off and asked all the devotees to do the same. Nityānanda's associates used to wear the cowherd dress with a cūḍā (crest); Vīracandra forbade the cūḍā, and those who refused became the **Cūḍādhārī** sampradāya. It is said he brought 1,200 **neḍā-neḍī** (shaven monks and nuns, perhaps Sahajiyā Buddhists) into Vaiṣṇavism.

At Ekacakrā (also called Vīracandrapura) he worshipped the Deity of Śrī Bāṅkima Rāya.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Kṣīrodakaśāyī (Payodhiśāyī) Viṣṇu**; Niśaṭha and Ulmuka (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 67).

## Other persons of the same name

Vīrabhadra, disciple of Śyāmānanda; Vīracandra Gosvāmī of Mādo (19th century); Vīrabhadra Gosvāmī who translated the *Bhāgavatam* (1858).
