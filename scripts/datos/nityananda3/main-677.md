## In short

Kānu Ṭhākura (Kānāi Ṭhākura), named **Śiśu Kṛṣṇadāsa** by Nityānanda Prabhu, was the son of **Puruṣottama dāsa Ṭhākura** and grandson of **Sadāśiva Kavirāja**, and was brought up by Jāhnavā Mātā at Khaḍadaha: "Kānu Ṭhākura, a very respectable gentleman, was such a great devotee that Kṛṣṇa always lived in his body" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.39–40).

## Life

He was born on the day of Ratha-yātrā (Āṣāḍha śukla dvitīyā) — in **1531 CE**, or 1450 / 1457 Śaka according to other texts. His mother, also named **Jāhnavā**, died twelve days after his birth (or just after); Nityānanda Prabhu came, took the infant to Khaḍadaha and gave him to Jāhnavā Mātā to bring up as her son, and he stayed there even after Vīrabhadra was born. Seeing his devotion Nityānanda named him Śiśu Kṛṣṇadāsa.

At five he went with Jāhnavā Mātā to Vṛndāvana (*Prema-vilāsa* 16); seeing his divine ecstasy and his expert flute-playing, Jīva Gosvāmī and the others called him **Kānu (Kānāi) Ṭhākura**. Dancing in kīrtana in the courtyard of Madana-mohana, the anklet of his right foot flew off and fell far away at **Bodhkhānā** (Jessore, now Bangladesh), his family's home; he said he would live where it fell, and later went there. The anklet is kept there. He was at the Kheturī festival with Jāhnavā (*Prema-vilāsa*). Late in life he left Bodhkhānā for a bhajana-kuṭīra at **Garbetā** (Midnapore), where he worshipped six or seven śālagrāmas and taught Vaiṣṇava philosophy (*Kānu-tattva-nirṇaya*, pp. 75–76).

Like his father he had many brāhmaṇa disciples; **Śrīrāma** was a famous one, and his disciples lived at Gaḍbetā near the Śilāvatī river. During the Maratha raids his descendants left Bodhkhānā with their Deity for **Bhājanghāṭ** (Nadia); when things settled, Harikṛṣṇa Gosvāmī, of his youngest son's line, went back to Bodhkhānā and installed Śrī Prāṇavallabha. At Bhājanghāṭ, the line of Vaṁśīvadana Gosvāmī (fourth from Kānāi) installed Śrī Śrī Rādhāvallabha (Nandarāma Gosvāmī), Rādhāmohana (Gopālavallabha) and Rādhā-Vṛndāvanacandra (Rādhāramaṇa Gosvāmī).

## Songs

A hymn to Nityānanda in the *Padakalpataru* (no. 2327, and perhaps 2321) is probably his; he seems to have signed "Kānudāsa" (six songs) and "Kānurāma dāsa" (seven songs).

## Puruṣottama dāsa or Nāgara Puruṣottama?

His family says his father was **Dāsa Puruṣottama** (Stoka-kṛṣṇa in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*), not Nāgara Puruṣottama.
