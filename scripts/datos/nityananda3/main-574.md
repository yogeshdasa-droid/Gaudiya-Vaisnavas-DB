## In short

Śrī Jāhnavā Mātā (Jāhnavā Devī, also called **Īśvarī**) was the younger wife and śakti of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu, "Nityānanda-svarūpiṇī", His other self. After the Lord's associates left this world she became the leader of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas: she presided over the great festival of **Kheturī**, twice went to Vṛndāvana, and gave dīkṣā to Vīrabhadra Gosvāmī. Her Śrīpāṭa is **Khaḍadaha**. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā she was **Revatī**, Balarāma's consort, and **Anaṅga-mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 65–66).

## Family

Her father was **Sūryadāsa Paṇḍita**, called Sarakhela because he kept the accounts of the king of Gauḍa, of **Śāligrāma** (Nadia); her grandfather Kaṁsāri Miśra and grandmother Kamalā Devī. Her uncles were Dāmodara, Jagannātha, **Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita**, Kṛṣṇadāsa and Nṛsiṁha-Caitanya. **Vasudhā** was her elder sister. "Śrī Vasudhā and Jāhnavā, the dear consorts of Nityānanda, are expansions of Vāruṇī and Revatī; their father Sūryadāsa, effulgent as the sun, was King Kakudmī. Some say Vasudhā, others Jāhnavī, is Anaṅga-mañjarī; both opinions are right" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 65–66).

## Marriage

When his daughters grew up, Sūryadāsa dreamt that he gave them both to Nityānanda Prabhu, whom he saw as Balarāma with the two girls at His sides as Revatī and Vāruṇī. He sent a brāhmaṇa friend to Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita in Navadvīpa, where Nityānanda was staying; Nityānanda agreed, and Advaita Ācārya urged that it be soon. **Kṛṣṇadāsa**, son of Rājā Hari Hoḍa of **Baḍagāchi**, a dear devotee of Nityānanda, took all the arrangements on himself. With Śrīvāsa, Advaita, Candraśekhara, Murāri Gupta and the other devotees singing kīrtana, Nityānanda came to Śāligrāma; Sūryadāsa fell weeping at His feet and gave Him his two daughters (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 12).

Nityānanda stayed some days at Śāligrāma and at Baḍagāchi, then brought His wives to Śacī Mātā in Navadvīpa, who took them on her lap; then to Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī at Śāntipura; then to Uddhāraṇa Datta at Saptagrāma, and finally to **Khaḍadaha**. Vasudhā had a son, **Vīracandra**, and a daughter, **Gaṅgā**; Jāhnavā had no children. She brought up **Kānu Ṭhākura** (Śiśu Kṛṣṇadāsa), whose mother had died at his birth, and adopted **Rāmacandra (Rāmāi Gosāi)**.

## Kheturī

After Nityānanda, Advaita, Śrīvāsa and the others had left this world, Śrīnivāsa Ācārya, Narottama Ṭhākura and Śyāmānanda held the great festival at **Kheturī**, at the house of Rājā Santoṣa Datta, on the Phālguna pūrṇimā, and Jāhnavā Mātā was especially invited. With her went her uncle Kṛṣṇa Miśra, Mīnaketana Rāmadāsa, Murāri Caitanya, Jñānadāsa, **Parameśvarī dāsa**, Balarāma dāsa, Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura and other followers of Nityānanda.

On the way she cooked for the Deities of Gaura-Nityānanda at **Ambikā-Kālnā**, where Hṛdaya Caitanya received her. In Navadvīpa she wept not to find Śacī Mātā, and at Śrīvāsa's house not to find Śrīvāsa and Mālinī; Śrīpati and Śrīnidhi took her in. At Śāntipura Advaita's sons Acyutānanda and Gopāla received her. Govinda Kavirāja received her at Teliyā-Budhuri, and Santoṣa Datta had boats and palanquins waiting at the Padmā.

At Kheturī six Deities were installed by Śrīnivāsa; on her order Narottama led the kīrtana, and Gaurāṅga Himself appeared in it with Nityānanda and His associates. On the third day Jāhnavā, after bathing at midday, cooked for the Deities with her own hands and served the prasāda herself (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 10).

## Vṛndāvana

From Kheturī she went through Prayāga and Kāśī to Mathurā. Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Bhūgarbha, Lokanātha, Kṛṣṇadāsa Brahmacārī, Kṛṣṇa Paṇḍita, Madhu Paṇḍita and Jīva Gosvāmī came to meet her, and Jīva introduced them all (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, as described by Parameśvarī dāsa). She saw Govinda, Gopīnātha and Madana-mohana, the forests, Govardhana, Rādhā-kuṇḍa and Śyāma-kuṇḍa, and the moods of those pastimes awoke in her (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 11.298 ff.). Once, as she came out of the Yamunā, a small dark hand caught the end of her cloth; Gopīnātha said: "I want to come and stay with you; one day when you bathe in the Gaṅgā I shall come to you."

On the way to Vṛndāvana she delivered a dacoit named **Kutubuddīn** (*Prema-vilāsa* 19). It is said that she had an image of herself made and placed at the left of Śrī Gopīnātha; Pyārījī was displeased, and the king of Jaipur settled the matter, so that Jāhnavā's image stayed at Gopīnātha's left (*Bhaktamāla* 3).

## Back in Bengal

She returned by Kheturī to **Budhuri**, where she had Śyāmadāsa Cakravartī (brother of Vaṁśīdāsa) give his daughter **Hemalatā** in marriage to **Baḍa Gaṅgādāsa**, and gave him the service of Śyāmasundara (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 11.362–396). At **Ekacakrā**, Nityānanda's birthplace, she wept hearing of Hāḍāi Paṇḍita and Padmāvatī, and spent the night in kīrtana in their empty house. Then Kaṇṭaka-nagara, where she wept at the place of the Lord's sannyāsa; Yājigrāma, where Śrīnivāsa Ācārya worshipped her; Navadvīpa, where she fainted at the Lord's house (old Īśāna was still there) and spent the night in kīrtana at Śrīvāsa-aṅgana; Ambikā, where she cooked again for Gaura-Nityānanda and dreamt of Gaurīdāsa; Saptagrāma; and home to Khaḍadaha by boat (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 11.397–786). Vīracandra and Gaṅgā bowed to her, and she and Vasudhā greeted each other.

One day, bathing in the Ganges at Khaḍadaha, she felt something touch her and lifted out **Śrī Gopīnātha**. This Deity is worshipped by the descendants of Gaṅgā Devī at **Jirāṭ** (Hooghly), at "Gopīnātha-talā".

At her order Vīracandra married **Śrīmatī** and **Nārāyaṇī**, the daughters of Yadunandana Ācārya of Jhāmaṭapura; she gave them dīkṣā (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13.249–257). She went to Vṛndāvana a second time (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13.268–280). Vīrabhadra, whose boat had been broken by Abhirāma's flute, took dīkṣā from her when he saw her four-armed form (*Prema-vilāsa* 24). Parameśvarī dāsa was her dear servant; at her order he installed Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha at Āṭapura.

## Dates

Her appearance is celebrated on the **śukla aṣṭamī of Vaiśākha**.

## Prayer of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura

In his *Kalyāṇa-kalpataru* Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura prays to her: "Fallen into this ocean of misery, I do not know how to reach its shore. Karma, jñāna, yoga and austerity have no power to save me… O Jāhnavā Devī, you are Nityānanda's śakti, the guru of kṛṣṇa-bhakti; you have delivered countless sinners — give me the shade of your feet and deliver me too."

## Other persons of the same name

- Jāhnavā, wife of Puruṣottama dāsa Ṭhākura and mother of Kānu Ṭhākura.
- Jāhnavī Devī, mother of Kāśīnātha Paṇḍita of Cātarā, who went to the Lord in Purī and brought her son back.
