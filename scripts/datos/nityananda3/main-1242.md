## In short

Rāmāi Gosāi (**Rāmacandra Gosvāmī**) was the adopted son and disciple of **Jāhnavā Mātā**, grandson of **Vaṁśīvadana Ṭhākura**, and so famous an ācārya that he was called "the second Vaṁśīvadana". He brought the Deities of **Kānāi-Balāi** (Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma) to Bengal and established them at **Bāghnāpāḍā**, near Ambikā-Kālnā. "I worship Rāmāi Gosāi, dear to Jāhnavā, who brought Kānāi-Balāi to Gauḍa; as Vīrabhadra is, so is Śrī Rāmāi — it is Jāhnavā Mātā's order, there is no doubt in it" (*Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*).

## Family

Vaṁśīvadana Ṭhākura had two sons, **Caitanya dāsa** and **Nityānanda dāsa**; Caitanya dāsa's sons were **Rāmacandra** and **Śacīnandana**.

## Life

Jāhnavā Mātā brought him up as her disciple and adopted son. He went with her to Vṛndāvana and lived there some years; another account says he went to Mathurā by Purī, Kāśī and Prayāga and saw Ādi-keśava at Kṛṣṇa's birthplace and the twelve forests. One morning, as he bathed at **Praskandana-tīrtha**, two Deities of Balarāma and Kṛṣṇa came floating into his arms (*Vaṁśī-śikṣā*). By Jāhnavā's order he brought them to Gauḍa.

Four miles from Ambikā-Kālnā, on the small river **Bālukā**, was a jungle full of tigers; Rāmacandra tamed (or delivered) a tiger there, made the place fit to live in, and it became known as **Bāghnāpāḍā**. There he installed **Kānāi-Balāi**. He lived sometimes at **Budhuri** and sometimes at **Rādhānagara**, near Bāghnāpāḍā. He was a lifelong brahmacārī, known for his devotion, honesty, strict sādhana and knowledge of the bhakti-śāstras; many learned and wealthy men took initiation from him. He initiated his younger brother **Śacīnandana** and gave him the service of Kānāi-Balāi; Śacīnandana's descendants, among them Vipina-vihārī Gosvāmī, live at Bāghnāpāḍā. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura explains the name Bāghnāpāḍā in his autobiography.

**The neḍā-neḍīs.** Vīracandra once tested his adoptive brother by sending over a thousand of his neḍā-neḍī disciples (shaven former Buddhist monks and nuns) to Bāghnāpāḍā in the middle of the night. They asked for hilsa fish and mangoes, though it was not the season. Rāmāi brought mangoes from the forest, someone brought hilsa from the Ganges; he cooked and offered everything, and the prasāda tasted like nectar, without a trace of fish.

## Works

Songs (padas); the *Kaḍacā-mañjarī* (or Kāñcā-mañjarī), *Sampuṭikā* and *Pāṣaṇḍa-dalana*. A *Kaḍacā* of Rāmāi Ṭhākura is also mentioned (*Abhidhāna*).

## Dates

Born in **1459 Śaka** (1456 according to another source); left this world in **1505 Śaka**, on the **kṛṣṇa tṛtīyā of Māgha**.

## Other persons of the same name

Rāmāi, Lord Caitanya's servant in Purī, brother of Nandāi; and Śrīrāma Paṇḍita (Rāmāi), Śrīvāsa's brother — other persons.
