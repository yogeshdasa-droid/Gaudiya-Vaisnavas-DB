## In short

Hāḍāi Paṇḍita (Hāḍāi Ojhā, Hāḍāi Ṭhākura), whose name was **Mukunda**, was the father of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu. He lived at **Ekacakrā** (Birbhum). His wife was **Padmāvatī Devī**. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Vasudeva**, Balarāma's father, and in Rāma-līlā **Daśaratha** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 40).

## Family

He was a brāhmaṇa of the line of **Nārāyaṇa Bhaṭṭa Caturvedī**, of the Śāṇḍilya gotra. The *Abhidhāna* gives the line in verse: Nārāyaṇa Bhaṭṭa, Ādivarāha, Vainateya, Subuddhi, Vibudheśa, Guha, Gaṅgāra, Suhāsa, Śakuni, Maheśvara (who became kulīna), Mahādeva, Tiku, Neṅgura; Neṅgura's son Mihira married into a vaṁśaja family, lost his kulīna status and was shunned; then Bhāskara, Puṣkara, Hṛṣṭidhara, Mālādhara, Vṛṣaketu, Candraketu, **Sundaramalla Bāḍurī**, and his son **Hāḍā Ojhā, called Mukunda**, whose son was Nityānanda, Balarāma Himself.

He was known in Bengal as Hāḍāi Ṭhākura or Hāḍāi Paṇḍita. A manuscript of the *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā* of Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa and Devakīnandana (1702) calls him Paramānanda, while the printed edition says **Mukunda Ojhā**; perhaps Hāḍāi was a nickname.

He and Padmāvatī had seven sons; Nityānanda was the eldest, and the others were Kṛṣṇānanda, Sarvānanda, Brahmānanda, Pūrṇānanda, Premānanda and Viśuddhānanda (*Prema-vilāsa* 24).

## Giving his son

When Nityānanda was twelve, a sannyāsī who had stayed at their house asked for the boy as alms. Hāḍāi gave Him, and Padmāvatī, devoted to her husband, did not object: "Whatever is your wish, my lord, is my word" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 3.93; see also Ādi 9.91, Madhya 3.71–96).

Jāhnavā Mātā later visited Ekacakrā and wept hearing of her husband's father and mother; she spent the night in kīrtana in their empty house.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vasudeva** and **Daśaratha** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 40).
