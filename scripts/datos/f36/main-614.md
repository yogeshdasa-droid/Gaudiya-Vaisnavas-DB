## In short

**Jiyaḍa Nṛsiṁha Barāṭ**, of a vaidya family of Burdwan, a wealthy man with a high post in the Judges' Court of Burdwan, practised **Gaura-bhajana** at home with his associates. **Rāmatanu Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa** (#1231) came to him and they spent days together in the ecstasy of bhajana; later he gave **Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī** of Navadvīpa dīkṣā in the **Gaura-mantra**. Both worshipped Gaura in **kānta-bhāva**.

## With Rāmatanu and Caitanya dāsa

When Rāmatanu Mukhopādhyāya Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa was preaching the glories of Lord Gaurāṅga, he heard of Nṛsiṁha's devotion and visited him at his house. Later Nṛsiṁha met Siddha Caitanya dāsa Bābājī of Navadvīpa and gave him the Gaura-mantra; both practised Gaura-upāsanā in kānta-bhāva. Bhāgavata-bhūṣaṇa accepted Lord Gaurāṅga as the supreme avatāra, but some difference of opinion with Jiyaḍa Nṛsiṁha later made him leave his company. (Rāmatanu's record adds that Nṛsiṁha, though close to him, also loved to do bhajana alone, and that by the preaching of the three, Bengal was flooded with love of God.)
