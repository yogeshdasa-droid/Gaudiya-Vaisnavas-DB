## In short

Śrīla **Locana dāsa Ṭhākura** (also called **Trilocana dāsa**), the author of the **Śrī Caitanya-maṅgala**, was a disciple of **Śrī Narahari Sarakāra Ṭhākura** of Śrīkhaṇḍa: "I am the servant of Ṭhākura Śrī Narahari, the master of my life; the desire of all my desires I place at his lotus feet — that this vile creature might describe something of the wonderful qualities of Śrī Gaurasundara" (*Caitanya-maṅgala*, Sūtra-khaṇḍa).

## Life

He was born in a family of Rāḍhī vaidyas at **Kogrāma**, in the Kāṭoyā subdivision of Burdwan, the only son of **Kamalākara dāsa** and **Sadānandī** (Arundhatī Devī), and much loved by them. He grew up and studied in his maternal grandfather's house, and was married very young. From childhood he met the devotees of Gaura and was attached to Him and indifferent to the world. In his youth he lived mostly at his guru's Śrīpāṭa, **Śrīkhaṇḍa**, where he learned kīrtana. According to the *Viśvakoṣa* he was born in 1445 Śaka and died in 1530 Śaka; the Digdarśanī's chronology gives 1523 and 1589.

## The Caitanya-maṅgala

He wrote it from the Sanskrit notes of **Murāri Gupta**: "Murāri Gupta, who lived in Nadīyā, composed many verses on the life of Śrī Gaurāṅga — the youthful pastimes he took part in himself, and those in Nīlācala after the sannyāsa, told by Dāmodara Paṇḍita. Hearing them I was drawn to compose this." Before beginning he offers his obeisances to **Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura**, "whose Caitanya-bhāgavata has enchanted the whole world." It was written between 1482 and 1488 Śaka.

He used the **pāñcālī** style of the village poets of Bengal (*Lakṣmīr pāñcālī*, *Śanir pāñcālī*, *Manasā-bhāsān*), five kinds of metre in one book, so that it could be sung. It has four parts: **Sūtra-khaṇḍa** (2 chapters: the invocation, and Nārada's talk with Uddhava on the reason of the Gaura-avatāra), **Ādi-khaṇḍa** (7: birth, childhood, studies, the marriages with Lakṣmīpriyā and Viṣṇupriyā, the journey to East Bengal and to Gayā), **Madhya-khaṇḍa** (16: the kīrtana in Navadvīpa, Advaita and Nityānanda, the sannyāsa at Kāṭoyā, the journey to Purī) and **Śeṣa-khaṇḍa** (3: the South Indian tour and the return to Gauḍa).

Some pastimes that Vṛndāvana dāsa only touches on, he tells in full — above all the Lord's last talk with **Viṣṇupriyā Devī** before the sannyāsa, when He consoled her, showed her His four-armed form and promised, "wherever I go, I shall remain with you." One verse of his book is taken to mean that the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* got that name from him and Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja.

## Other works

Many simple, sweet songs on Gaura and Nityānanda (his *dhāmālī* songs are still sung); the *Durlabha-sāra*; Bengali verse of the *Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭaka* and of the *Rāsa-pañcādhyāyī* (*Viśvakoṣa*).
