## In short

Śrī **Lakṣmīpriyā Devī** was the first wife of **Śrī Gaurasundara** (Nimāi Paṇḍita), the daughter of the brāhmaṇa **Vallabhācārya** of Navadvīpa, "equal to King Janaka". In Kṛṣṇa-līlā she is **Jānakī** (Sītā) and **Rukmiṇī**, an incarnation of Lakṣmī-devī (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 45-46). Vṛndāvana dāsa describes her qualities in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Ādi 14.45).

## The marriage

They first met by the Gaṅgā, where both had come to bathe: the Lord knew His eternal consort and smiled, and she offered Him prayers in her heart. The matchmaker **Vanamālī Ācārya** proposed the marriage to Śacī Devī, who answered, "My son has no father; let Him study and grow a little older." Meeting Nimāi on the way, Vanamālī told Him; the Lord said nothing, but at home asked His mother why she had not favoured the proposal — and Śacī understood. Vallabhācārya could give only five harītakī as dowry; Śacī wanted none.

The astrologers fixed the day; the adhivāsa was held with musicians and dancers, the friends of Jagannātha Miśra came, Śrīvāsa, Advaita and the other well-wishers rejoiced. On the wedding evening Lakṣmīpriyā, lifted on her seat, went seven times round the Lord, offered Him a garland, and in the exchange of glances flowers were showered on "Lakṣmī and Nārāyaṇa". The next day they came home in a palanquin, and the women on the way said they were Śiva and Pārvatī, Rāma and Sītā, Lakṣmī and Nārāyaṇa (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* Ādi 14).

## In Śacī's house

Śacī Devī saw her house filled with light and the fragrance of lotuses, and understood that her daughter-in-law was Lakṣmī herself; her poverty seemed to vanish. Lakṣmīpriyā cleaned the Deity room, drew the ālpanā, picked flowers and tulasī, cooked for the guests the Lord invited every day, and served Śacī with great affection.

## Her departure

When the Lord went to **East Bengal** to teach and earn, He asked her to serve His mother. In separation she gave up eating and sleeping; she left this world while He was away — bitten by a snake, some say (the Digdarśanī's chronology gives 1424 Śaka, 1502). The Lord, who knew everything, came home with His students and the wealth He had earned, consoled His mother, and took up His teaching again. Later Śacī arranged His marriage with Viṣṇupriyā.
