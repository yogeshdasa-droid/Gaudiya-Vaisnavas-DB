## In short

Śrīla **Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura**, the author of the **Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata**, is "the Vyāsa of Lord Caitanya's pastimes": "Vyāsadeva described the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; the Vyāsa of the pastimes of Lord Caitanya was Vṛndāvana dāsa" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.55). He was the son of **Nārāyaṇī Devī**, the niece of Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita, and the last disciple of **Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu**. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is Vedavyāsa, and the cowherd boy Kusumāpīḍa also entered him (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 109).

## His mother

When Mahāprabhu revealed Himself in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, Nārāyaṇī was a girl of four. The Lord told her, "Nārāyaṇī, call on Kṛṣṇa!", and she cried "Hari! Kṛṣṇa!" in ecstasy until the ground around her was wet with tears; she always received the remnants of His meals: "Nārāyaṇī eternally eats the remnants of Lord Caitanya; from her womb Śrīla Vṛndāvana dāsa was born" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 8.41). Vṛndāvana dāsa speaks often of his mother in his book, never of his father; Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī remarks that there is no need to know his paternal family, a devotee being known by his service.

## Birth and childhood

His father, the brāhmaṇa **Vaikuṇṭha dāsa**, died while Nārāyaṇī was carrying him. Left poor, she took shelter at **Māmagāchi** in the house of **Vāsudeva Datta**, where she was given the service of the Deity he had installed; there Vṛndāvana dāsa was born and studied (Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī, in the preface to the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, says he grew up in the ancestral home of Mālinī Devī). Jayakṛṣṇa dāsa says he was born at Kumārahaṭṭa and lived at Māmagāchi; with the poet Uddhava dāsa he says Nārāyaṇī was a child widow (B. B. Majumdar, *Caitanya-cariter Upādān* pp. 692-693). He was born about four years after the Lord took sannyāsa (about 1440 Śaka), and was not more than twenty when Mahāprabhu disappeared.

## Disciple of Nityānanda

He took dīkṣā from **Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu**, whose last disciple he was, and by His order wrote the Lord's pastimes. He went with **Jāhnavā Mātā** to the great festival of **Kheturī**. He worshipped in the mood of sakhya. His Śrīpāṭa, where his Deities of **Śrī Śrī Gaura-Nitāi** are still served, is at **Denuḍa** (reached by bus from Navadvīpa).

## The Caitanya-bhāgavata

So absorbed was he in Nityānanda's pastimes that the book grew large and he could not describe the Lord's last days; the devotees of Vṛndāvana, enchanted by it, asked Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja to complete what was left — the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*. Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja praises it again and again: "O fools, just read the Caitanya-maṅgala! … Hearing this book annihilates all misfortune; it gives the conclusions of devotional service to Kṛṣṇa … the Lord Himself speaks through him" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 8.33-40; Ādi 13.48).

**When it was written.** Rāmagati Nyāyaratna gives 1548 (1470 Śaka), which seems right: he was then about thirty, and when Kavi-karṇapūra called him Vedavyāsa in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (1498 Śaka) the book was already famous; others say 1495 or 1497 Śaka.

**Its name.** Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja calls it *Caitanya-maṅgala* throughout, even in the last chapter of the *Antya-līlā*; yet Locana dāsa (whose own *Caitanya-maṅgala* was written between 1482 and 1488 Śaka) and Kavi-karṇapūra already knew it as *Caitanya-bhāgavata*. Some scholars think it had that name from the start and was only called "maṅgala" as books glorifying a deity were; in the Atulakṛṣṇa Gosvāmī edition the first chapter ends "Vṛndāvana dāsa sings the Caitanya-maṅgala" (B. B. Majumdar).

Nṛsiṁha, the seventh descendant of Vāsudeva Datta, wrote the Sanskrit *Caitanya-mahābhāgavata* from it (manuscripts at the Baṅgīya Sāhitya Pariṣat, and one found by Haridāsa Gosvāmī of Navadvīpa with the Ṭhākura family of Dakṣiṇakhaṇḍa); there is also a Sanskrit translation (Pariṣat ms. 1691).

## Songs

Some of his padas are in the *Padakalpataru*; they show that he was a master of music. Songs that bear his name but are in no authentic collection and do not agree with the ācāryas are probably of another Vṛndāvana dāsa.
