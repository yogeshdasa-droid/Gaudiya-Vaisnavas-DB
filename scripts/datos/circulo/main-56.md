## In short

Śrī Anupama — born **Vallabha** — was the youngest brother of Śrī Sanātana and Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī and the father of **Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī**. Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu met him at Rāmakeli and gave him the name "Anupama" ("the incomparable"). With Sanātana and Rūpa he is one of the three best branches on the western side of the Caitanya tree (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.84). All his life he was a devotee of **Śrī Rāmacandra**, and he saw Mahāprabhu as Rāmacandra Himself. He left this world in 1436 Śaka (1514) on the bank of the Gaṅgā.

## Dates

- **Appearance:** not known. He was born in Bāklā-Candradvīpa, the youngest of the three brothers; his father, Kumāradeva, died shortly after his birth (*Abhidhāna*, entry Kumāradeva).
- **Disappearance:** 1436 Śaka (1514 AD), on the bank of the Gaṅgā, while travelling with Rūpa Gosvāmī from Vṛndāvana to Gauḍa (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.668–669).

## Family

The family descended from **Sarvajña**, a learned and wealthy brāhmaṇa of Karṇāṭa in the twelfth century Śaka. His son Aniruddha had two sons, Rūpeśvara and Harihara, who lost their kingdom and had to live in the hills. Rūpeśvara's son **Padmanābha** moved to **Naihāṭī** on the Gaṅgā in Bengal; he had five sons, and the youngest, **Mukunda**, was the father of **Kumāradeva**. Kumāradeva moved to **Bāklā-Candradvīpa** (in Jessore district, now Fateyabad), and there his three sons who became Vaiṣṇavas were born: **Sanātana, Rūpa and Vallabha**. The family were Bharadvāja-gotra Yajurvedī brāhmaṇas (*Abhidhāna*).

Their grandfather Mukunda held a high post in the government of Gauḍa and brought the boys to **Rāmakeli**, in the Māldah district, where they grew up. It was in Rāmakeli that Vallabha's son **Jīva** was born.

## In the service of Husain Shah

Like his brothers, Vallabha served in the Muslim government; for this the three brothers were called **Mallika** ("lord"). Anupama was the **head of the mint** of Husain Shah, the king of Gauḍa (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.665–669; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* 3.4.43; *Abhidhāna*).

## With Mahāprabhu

When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu visited Rāmakeli, He met Vallabha and gave him the name Anupama. The *Bhakti-ratnākara* says:

"Rūpa's younger brother, the wise Vallabha, was named 'Anupama' by Śrī Gaurasundara. He knew no one but Raghunātha and was always absorbed in His service; yet he knew that Caitanya Gosāñi was Śrī Raghunātha Himself, and he felt himself fortunate to have found the Lord" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.665–667).

When Rūpa Gosvāmī left the government service to join the Lord, Anupama went with him. They went first to **Mathurā**, where **Subuddhi Rāya**, who lived by selling dry wood, was delighted to meet them and showed them the twelve forests of Vṛndāvana. After a month there they went to look for Sanātana. Following the Gaṅgā they reached **Prayāga**, but Sanātana had come by another road, so they missed him; when Sanātana reached Mathurā, Subuddhi Rāya told him of their visit. Rūpa and Anupama met Mahāprabhu at Prayāga and at Vārāṇasī (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 19), heard from Him about Sanātana's travels, and then went back to Bengal to settle their affairs with the state, intending to go on to see the Lord in Jagannātha Purī.

## His faith in Śrī Rāma

Sanātana Gosvāmī later told Mahāprabhu in Purī the story of his brother's faith (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 4). From childhood Anupama had given his heart and life to **Śrī Raghunātha**. One day Sanātana and Rūpa told him: "Give up the worship of Raghunātha; let the three of us brothers worship Kṛṣṇa together." Out of respect for his elder brothers Anupama agreed at first, but that night his heart was in torment: whenever he tried to forget Raghunātha, a terrible pain arose in him. Unable to disobey his elder brothers and unable to leave Raghunātha, he wept all night. In the morning he fell at their feet and said that he could not give up the feet of Raghunātha. Seeing his heart, the Gosvāmīs praised him: "Your devotion is firm, you are a true sādhu" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 4.43).

Because he was mainly a devotee of Śrī Rāma, he could not take up the worship of Rādhā-Govinda that Mahāprabhu taught, though he directly accepted Mahāprabhu as Rāmacandra. This may be why his name is not given separately in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* or the *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*.

## Departure

"Śrī Rūpa took Vallabha with him to Gauḍa; Śrī Vallabha disappeared on the bank of the Gaṅgā, and some days later Rūpa went to Nīlācala" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.668–669). This was in **1436 Śaka**. He went to the abode where Śrī Rāmacandra eternally lives. In Jagannātha Purī, Rūpa Gosvāmī told Mahāprabhu what had happened.

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who gave him his name; he is counted as a branch of the Lord (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 10.84; *Abhidhāna*). According to Haridāsa Dāsa he belonged to the line coming down from Lord Caitanya.
- **Son:** Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

Not given in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (see above).
