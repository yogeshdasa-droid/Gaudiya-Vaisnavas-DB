## In short

Śrī Kumāradeva was the father of the three brothers **Śrī Sanātana, Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Vallabha (Anupama)**, and so the grandfather of Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī. A Bharadvāja-gotra Yajurvedī brāhmaṇa, very pure in conduct and devoted to his duties, he left Naihāṭī at his father's order and settled in **Bāklā-Candradvīpa**, where his three famous sons were born. He passed away shortly after the birth of Vallabha.

## Family

He was the only son of **Mukunda**, the youngest of the five sons of **Padmanābha**, who had come from Karṇāṭa to **Naihāṭī** on the Gaṅgā. The family of Padmanābha's sons and grandsons had grown large there (*Abhidhāna*, which refers to the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, 1.561–564).

## Life

- **To Candradvīpa:** at that time West Bengal, and especially the region of Navadvīpa, was suffering from the oppression of the Pirālīs (*Abhidhāna*, quoting the *Prema-vilāsa*). In **Bāklā** the Hindu kings of the line of **Danujamardana** were powerful, and there was no such oppression; moreover, King Danujamardana's grandfather had been a patron of Padmanābha. So the God-fearing Kumāradeva, following his father's order, left Naihāṭī and found shelter in **Bāklā-Candradvīpa** (in Jessore district, later called Fateyabad).
- **His sons:** there his three celebrated sons — **Śrī Sanātana, Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Vallabha** — were born. Of his many sons, these three took to the path of Vaiṣṇavism.
- **Death:** he passed away shortly after Vallabha was born. His father Mukunda was then still holding a high post in the government of Gauḍa; Mukunda brought his grandsons to **Rāmakeli** and brought them up there, and it was there that Śrī Jīva appeared (*Abhidhāna*).
