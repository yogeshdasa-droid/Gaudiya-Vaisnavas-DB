## In short

Śrīla Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī, the author of the **Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta**, was born in a vaidya (physician) family at **Jhāmaṭapura**, near Kāṭoyā in the Barddhamān district. Orphaned of his father at six, he grew up in his aunt's house, renounced the world young, and on the order of **Lord Nityānanda in a dream** went to Vṛndāvana, where he lived to the end of his life at the feet of the Gosvāmīs, with **Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī** as his guru. Besides the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* he wrote the *Govinda-līlāmṛta* and the *Sāraṅga-raṅgadā* commentary on the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*. He left this world at Rādhākuṇḍa on Āśvina Śukla Dvādaśī. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Ratnarekhā** (some say Kastūrī Mañjarī).

## Dates

- **Appearance:** **1428 Śaka (1506 AD)**, at Jhāmaṭapura (*Abhidhāna*, which also gives 1418 Śaka with a question mark). Other texts of the record give **1496 AD** and **1482 Śaka (1560 AD)**.
- **Disappearance:** on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of Āśvina (**Āśvina Śukla Dvādaśī**), after the disappearance of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī; the year is not known (another text: about 1615). His samādhi is at **Rādhākuṇḍa**.

## Family and early life

His father was **Bhagīratha**, a physician, and his mother **Sunandā**; his younger brother **Śyāmadāsa** was also a physician. The family belonged to Nadīyā and lived at **Jhāmaṭapura**, near Kāṭoyā and Naihāṭī. His father died when Kṛṣṇadāsa was six, and the two brothers were brought up in the house of their paternal aunt. From childhood he showed a strong inclination to renunciation; when he came of age he handed all the property over to his brother and gave himself up to the holy name. More about his early life is found in a book called *Ānanda-ratnāvalī*. His family line seems to have died out.

## Mīnaketana Rāmadāsa and the dream of Nityānanda

In the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 5) he tells why he left home:

"Lord Nityānanda had a servant named Śrī Mīnaketana Rāmadāsa, a reservoir of love. At my house there was saṅkīrtana day and night, and he came, having been invited. Absorbed in ecstatic love he sat in my courtyard, and all the Vaiṣṇavas bowed at his feet. In his joy he sometimes climbed on the shoulders of someone offering obeisances, and sometimes struck others with his flute or slapped them gently. Whoever saw his eyes shed tears, for a constant shower of tears flowed from them; sometimes his body was covered with eruptions of ecstasy like kadamba flowers, one limb stunned while another trembled; and when he shouted the name 'Nityānanda!', everyone was astonished.

"A respectable brāhmaṇa named **Śrī Guṇārṇava Miśra** was serving the Deity, and he did not stand up to honour Mīnaketana. Seeing this, Rāmadāsa became angry: 'Here I find a second Romaharṣaṇa-sūta, who did not stand up to honour Lord Balarāma!' Then he danced and sang to his heart's content; but the brāhmaṇa did not become angry, for he was serving Lord Kṛṣṇa.

"At the end of the festival Rāmadāsa had some controversy with my brother, who had firm faith in Lord Caitanya but only a dim glimmer of faith in Lord Nityānanda. I rebuked my brother: 'These two brothers are like one body; They are identical. If you do not believe in Lord Nityānanda, you will fall down. To believe in one and disrespect the other is the logic of accepting half a hen. It would be better to be an atheist by slighting both than a hypocrite by believing in one and slighting the other.' Then Rāmadāsa broke his flute in anger and went away, and at that time my brother fell down."

That night Lord Nityānanda, pleased with the way Kṛṣṇadāsa had defended His dear devotee, appeared to him in a dream: *āre āre kṛṣṇadāsa, nā karaha bhaya / vṛndāvane yāha, tāṅhā sarva labhya haya* — "O my dear Kṛṣṇadāsa, do not be afraid. Go to Vṛndāvana; there you will attain everything" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 5.195). Receiving this mercy, he set out joyfully for Vṛndāvana, where he spent the rest of his life.

## In Vṛndāvana

He accepted the Gosvāmīs **Śrī Rūpa, Śrī Sanātana, Śrī Jīva, Śrī Raghunātha dāsa, Śrī Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa and Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa** as his instructing spiritual masters, and Lord Nityānanda Himself as the one who had given him shelter. According to the *Prema-vilāsa* (18), **Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī** was his guru, and he lived with him at Rādhākuṇḍa. His mastery of the Sanskrit scriptures shows in all his works. From **Śrī Lokanātha Gosvāmī** and Śrī Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa he begged permission to write the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; Lokanātha asked not to be mentioned in it, and that is why the book hardly speaks of him. In the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, Sanātana Gosvāmī bows to "Śrī Kāśīśvara, Śrī Lokanātha and Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa, dear to Vṛndāvana".

## The stolen books

When the Gosvāmīs' manuscripts sent to Gauḍa were stolen on the way, the news reached Vraja. The *Prema-vilāsa* (13) says that on hearing it Kavirāja Gosvāmī jumped into **Rādhākuṇḍa**; the devotees pulled him out; he looked once at Dāsa Gosvāmī's face, clasped his feet and, eyes closed, gave up his life. The *Karṇānanda* (7), however, says that he did jump into Rādhākuṇḍa but did not die then: by the order of Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana he remained some days longer, anxiously awaiting the news that the books had been recovered (*Abhidhāna*). He passed away after the disappearance of Dāsa Gosvāmī, on Āśvina Śukla Dvādaśī, and his samādhi is at Rādhākuṇḍa.

## At Jhāmaṭapura today

At Jhāmaṭapura the Deities of **Gaura-Nityānanda** installed by him (the *Abhidhāna* says a Deity of Mahāprabhu) are still worshipped, together with his **pādukās** and his **bhajana-sthāna**.

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** **Śrīla Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī** (*Prema-vilāsa* 18); śikṣā from the six Gosvāmīs; ordered by Lord Nityānanda.
- **Disciples:** **Mukunda dāsa Gosvāmī**, author of the *Artha-ratnālpa-dīpikā* commentary on the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* ("Gosāñi Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa, who lives at Rādhākuṇḍa … his disciple Śrī Mukunda"); **Viṣṇudāsa**, author of the *Svātma-pramodinī* commentary on the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* (*Abhidhāna*). The record also names **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura**, who learned from him in Vraja.

## Works

- **Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — "the life and soul of the Vaiṣṇavas".
- **Śrī Govinda-līlāmṛta** — the eightfold daily pastimes of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa.
- **Sāraṅga-raṅgadā** — commentary on Bilvamaṅgala's *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*.
- Attributed to him: **Svarūpa-varṇana**, also called *Svarūpa-nirṇaya*, published in the *Nityānanda-dāyinī Patrikā* (Pāṭbāḍī manuscript B 144 according to the *Abhidhāna*; B 194 in another text).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Ratnarekhā** (*Abhidhāna*); some say **Kastūrī Mañjarī**.
