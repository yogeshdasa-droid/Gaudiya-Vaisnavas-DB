## In short

Śrīla Lokanātha Gosvāmī — "Lokanātha Dāsa Gosvāmī", "Lokanātha Prabhu" — was a personal associate of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and the eldest of the Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana. Born in **Tālakhaḍi** (Jessore, East Bengal), he left home young; the Lord sent him to Vṛndāvana even before taking sannyāsa, and with **Bhūgarbha Gosvāmī** he was among the very first of Gaura's devotees to settle in Vraja. There **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vinoda** came to him, and he carried the Deity in a cloth bag around his neck, living under the trees. He was the dīkṣā-guru of **Śrīla Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura**, his only disciple, and asked Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja not to write about him in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* — which is why so little is known of his life. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he is **Mañjulālī sakhī** (*Abhidhāna*), also called **Līlā Mañjarī**.

## Dates

- **Appearance:** about **1405 Śaka (1483 AD)**, in the house of Padmanābha Cakravartī and Sītā Devī at Tālakhaḍi (*Abhidhāna*, entry Padmanābha Cakravartī).
- **Left home:** in the month of Agrahāyaṇa, **1431 Śaka (1509 AD)**, when he came to Mahāprabhu in Navadvīpa (*Abhidhāna*).
- **Disappearance:** **1510 Śaka (1588 AD)** (*Abhidhāna*), on the eighth day of the dark fortnight — Āṣāḍha Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī according to B. V. Tīrtha, Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Aṣṭamī according to other texts of the record (one text says the eighth day of the *bright* fortnight of Śrāvaṇa). He left this world in old age at **Khadiravana** (the village of Khāyarā).

## Family

His father, **Padmanābha Cakravartī** (also called Paramānanda), was a kulīna Rāḍhī brāhmaṇa of the Bharadvāja gotra, living at **Tālakhaḍi** in Jessore district. Padmanābha took shelter of **Śrī Advaita Ācārya** at Śāntipura, near Phuliyā, received dīkṣā from him and was always intoxicated with the taste of the Bhāgavatam; after his initiation he returned to Tālakhaḍi and from time to time came to Śāntipura and Navadvīpa to cultivate devotion. His wife, **Śrī Sītā Devī**, was also a great devotee (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.296; *Abhidhāna*).

Padmanābha had four sons: **Bhavanātha, Pūrṇānanda or Pragalbha, Lokanātha and Raghunātha** (*Abhidhāna*). The texts of the record name only the younger brother, **Pragalbha Bhaṭṭācārya**, whose descendants still live at Tālakhaḍi; among the descendants of Lokanātha's brother the *Abhidhāna* names Nīlāmbara Mukhopādhyāya, Ṛṣivara Mukhopādhyāya and Mahāmahopādhyāya Phaṇibhūṣaṇa Tarkavāgīśa. According to the *Abhidhāna* (quoting the *Sādhana-dīpikā*), **Bhūgarbha Gosvāmī** was Lokanātha's paternal uncle; the texts of the record call him his dearest friend. One text says that before Tālakhaḍi the family lived at Kāñcanapāḍā.

Tālakhaḍi can be reached from Jessore station by way of Sonākhālī and Khejurā, and from there on foot, or by boat in the rainy season.

## With Mahāprabhu in Navadvīpa

From childhood Lokanātha had no interest in worldly life. Leaving his parents and his home for ever, he went to **Māyāpura in Navadvīpa** to see the lotus feet of Śrī Gaurasundara. The Lord embraced him with great affection and soon told him to go to Vṛndāvana quickly. But Lokanātha understood from certain signs that the Lord would leave home and take sannyāsa within a few days, and he was in great anxiety. The Lord, knowing his heart, consoled him: "We shall meet again in Vṛndāvana."

"While he wept and wept at the feet of Śrī Caitanya, Lokanātha felt himself embraced by the Lord. As he took leave, the Lord gave him a confidential message that consoled him greatly. Then he offered his very self at the feet of Mahāprabhu, offered praṇāma to all the devotees, and went on his way" (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, 1st Taraṅga).

## To Vraja, and the search for the Lord

Lokanātha set out for Vṛndāvana taking with him **Bhūgarbha Gosvāmī**, the disciple of Gadādhara Paṇḍita. On foot they went by way of **Rājmahal, Tājpur, Pūrṇiyā, Ayodhyā and Lucknow** and reached Gokula, in Vraja. Of all Gaura's devotees, only **Subuddhi Miśra** had gone to Vraja before them. At the Lord's command the two of them first went to Vṛndāvana and **brought to light the lost places of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes** (*Prema-vilāsa* 7; *Abhidhāna*).

He never saw the Lord in this world again. Mahāprabhu took sannyāsa and went to Nīlācala, and from there on His tour of South India. "Hearing this, Lokanātha went south; from the south the Lord came to Vṛndāvana; hearing this, Lokanātha went to Vraja; the Lord left Vṛndāvana for Prayāga; Lokanātha came to Vraja and was overwhelmed" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 1.310–312). Having wandered all over India without seeing Him, he resolved to leave for Prayāga the next morning; but that night the Lord came to him in a dream and stopped him: "O Lokanātha, stay in Vṛndāvana. I have not broken My promise: I have already come to Vṛndāvana and live here in another form. In this way you will always keep My company."

So Lokanātha stayed in Vraja, alone and unknown, until he met the Lord's dear followers — **Śrī Rūpa, Śrī Sanātana, Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Śrī Bhūgarbha** and others. They loved one another as if they had one mind and one heart. Lokanātha was the **eldest of the Gosvāmīs** and was always absorbed in prema. When Rūpa Gosvāmī stayed at Mathurā and went to see Śrī Gopāla, he was accompanied by Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Raghunātha dāsa, Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa and Lokanātha (*saṅge gopāla-bhaṭṭa, dāsa-raghunātha, raghunātha bhaṭṭa-gosāñi, āra lokanātha*, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 18.52–53). In the opening of the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, Sanātana Gosvāmī offers his respects to him:

*vṛndāvana-priyān vande śrī-govinda-padāśritān / śrīmat-kāśīśvaraṁ lokanāthaṁ śrī-kṛṣṇadāsakam*

"I offer my obeisances to Śrī Kāśīśvara, Śrī Lokanātha and Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa, who are dear to Vṛndāvana and have taken shelter at the feet of Śrī Govinda."

## Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vinoda

Lokanātha wandered through the twelve forests of Vṛndāvana in ecstasy. Near **Chatravana** there is a village called **Umarāo**, and there, on the bank of **Kiśorī-kuṇḍa**, he stayed for some time. He longed to serve the Lord in His Deity form, and Kṛṣṇa, who is in everyone's heart, fulfilled his wish: someone placed a Deity in his hands, saying, "Worship this Deity; His name is Rādhā-Vinoda," and at once disappeared. Lokanātha looked around and, seeing no one, began to wonder how the Deity had come. Then Rādhā-Vinoda smiled and said: "Who would bring Me here? I have come by Myself. I live in the forest of this village of Umarāo; this Kiśorī-kuṇḍa you see is My home. Now quickly give Me something to eat."

Beside himself with joy, Lokanātha cooked an offering, served the Deity fine rice on a fresh banana leaf, made Him a bed of flowers and laid Him down to rest, fanning Him with leaves and meditating on massaging His feet.

Lokanātha had no house. When the cowherds of the village wanted to build him a hut, he refused, and stayed under the trees. He made a cloth bag for Rādhā-Vinoda and always kept it hanging round his neck; the Deity was like a jewel on a necklace, and the bag was His temple. So he lived until the end, the crest-jewel of renunciation; only by great insistence did the other Gosvāmīs persuade him to stay near them (*Abhidhāna*). Narottama Ṭhākura's praṇāma-mantra for him remembers this service:

*śrīmad-rādhā-vinodaika-sevā-sampat-samanvitam / padmanābhātmajaṁ śrīmal-lokanātha-prabhuṁ bhaje*

"I worship Śrīla Lokanātha Prabhu, the son of Śrī Padmanābha, whose one wealth is the service of Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vinoda."

His Deities Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Vinoda are now worshipped in **Jaipur**. One text of the record says that he established the **Gokulānanda** temple in Vṛndāvana.

## Narottama, his only disciple

Śrīla **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura** chose Lokanātha as his guru, and after long service and prayer received dīkṣā from him; there is no record of any other disciple. Before daybreak, in secret, Narottama would go to the place where his guru passed stool and urine and clean it carefully; seeing his humility, Lokanātha accepted him. Narottama prays to him:

"O Lokanātha, my Lord! Keep me at your feet and cast your merciful glance on me; by your mercy my heart's desire will be fulfilled. Where one meets Śrī Caitanya, there one meets Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa. If you are not merciful, who will be? Fulfil my desire this once; in the three worlds I have no one but you. Give me a place at your feet, where day and night I may sing the qualities and pastimes of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa. Without you Narottama's desire cannot be fulfilled."

## Silence in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta

When **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī** asked Lokanātha (and Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa) for permission to write the *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Lokanātha gave his blessings but forbade him to write anything about him. Out of respect for this order the book barely mentions him, and so there is no way to know his life in detail (*Abhidhāna*).

## Last years

When Mahāprabhu and His dear followers beginning with Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana disappeared, the separation was almost unbearable for Lokanātha; he stayed alive only by the Lord's wish, to fulfil His desire. In his old age he lived at **Khadiravana**, in the village of Khāyarā, and there he entered the eternal pastimes. His **samādhi** is beside the large lake called **Śrī Yugala-kuṇḍa**; there is also a samādhi of his at the **Gokulānanda temple** in Vṛndāvana (*Abhidhāna*; "puṣpa-samādhi", says another text).

## Guru and disciples

- **Guru:** **Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — he is counted among the Lord's disciples (*Prema-vilāsa* 20; *Abhidhāna*). One text of the record calls him a disciple of **Advaita Ācārya** (his father's guru); the record had **Sanātana Gosvāmī** as his guru.
- **Disciple:** Śrīla **Narottama Dāsa Ṭhākura**, his only disciple.

## Works

On the order of Advaita Prabhu he wrote a commentary (*ṭīkā* / *ṭippanī*) on the **tenth canto of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** (H. P. Śāstrī; *Abhidhāna*: «Bhāgavata-ṭippanī» by Śrī Lokanātha).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Mañjulālī sakhī** (*Abhidhāna*). The texts of the record say **Līlā Mañjarī** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 187); the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (107) also names a Lokanātha among the four Kumāras (Sanātana).
