## In short

**Hita Harivaṁśa** (1473–1551), a brāhmaṇa from Gauḍa, founded the **Rādhāvallabha sampradāya** and in 1508 installed **Śrī Rādhāvallabhajī** in Vṛndāvana. The Prema-vilāsa (18) says he was first a disciple of **Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī**, who disowned him for chewing Rādhā's betel prasāda on a Harivāsara.

## Life

His father **Vyāsa Miśra** served the government of the Emperor of Delhi and was stationed at Bādgrām near Mathurā; his mother was **Tārā-devī**. At eleven he married **Kṛṣṇadāsī** and **Manoharadāsī**, the two daughters of Dvija Anantarāma of Cāthavāl. In Kārttika (October-November) 1508 he installed **Śrī Rādhāvallabhajī** at Purāṇa Śahar; he built an altar called **Rāsa-maṇḍala** at Govinda-ghāṭ and a garden at Nikuñja-vana. His noted disciples were Naravāhan, Naval, Chabile, Gāhā, Nāhar and Suvitan. He died in Āśvina (September-October) 1551.

## His works and his sampradāya

In Hindi he wrote the **Caurāsījī** and **Mahāvāṇī**, in Sanskrit the **Karmānanda** and the **Rādhā-rasa-sudhā-nidhi**. Both the Prema-vilāsa and the Bhaktamāla tell something of the Rādhāvallabhī sampradāya: its members worship a stone plaque bearing Rādhā's name; for them Kṛṣṇa is an *anukūla nāyaka*, a lover always devoted to one beloved, and Rādhikā a *svakīyā nāyikā*, upholding the story of Kṛṣṇa's marriage to Rādhikā at Bhāṇḍīravana told in the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa's Kṛṣṇa-janma-khaṇḍa (canto 15).
