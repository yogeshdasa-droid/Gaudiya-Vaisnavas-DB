## In short

**Vidyāpati** (born 1374, according to the Digdarśanī), court poet of **King Śivasiṁha** of **Mithilā**, wrote the Vaiṣṇava songs that **Lord Caitanya** relished deeply in the Gambhīrā. Some think he was a Bengali living in Mithilā. Many of his songs name his patron Śivasiṁha and Queen **Lachimā-devī**. **Caṇḍīdāsa** (#1813) was his contemporary.

## The poet

It is said that he loved the queen profoundly, and that on seeing her he composed poems spontaneously. The greatness of his writing is shown by Lord Caitanya's deep relish of his songs in the Gambhīrā.

His works: the **Padāvalī**, **Puruṣa-parīkṣā**, **Kīrtilatā**, **Likhanāvalī**, **Śaiva-sarvasva-sāra**, **Gaṅgā-vākyāvalī**, **Vibhāga-sāra**, **Gayā-pattana**, the **Gorakṣa-vijaya nāṭaka** and **Durgā-bhakti-taraṅgiṇī**. The manuscript of the *Gorakṣa-vijaya*, kept in Nepal, tells how Matsyendranātha, a victim of attachment to women, was rescued by his disciple Gorakṣanātha; its songs are in Vrajabuli and the rest in Sanskrit and Prakrit. Vidyāpati wrote this musical drama at King Śivasiṁha's order for the festival of Śiva Bhairaveśvara at Mithilā, some time before 1416 (Bhaktamāla 14.6; Viśva-bhāratī Patrikā 12.4).
