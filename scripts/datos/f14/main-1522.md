## In short

**Śyāmadāsa Bābā** was a Vaiṣṇava who came floating down the Gaṅgā on a timber raft to the bank at **Guhavaṭī** (Murshidabad), with a Deity of **Gopāla** he had recovered from the bottom of the river, in the days when **Rājīvalocana Rāya** was Dewan of Rāṇī Svarṇamayī of Kāśimbāzār. The people of the place built him a temple and an ākhaḍā; he always fed his guests, yet never took more than he needed.

## The Dewan's gift

Wrapped in a torn blanket, with a wicker basket for a mādhukarī bowl, he went to the Dewan's house nearby; Rājīva Bābu, seeing his radiant face, gave him rice and vegetables, and he cooked, offered them to Gopāla and took prasāda. Two days later four Vaiṣṇavas came to see him. Asking them to wait, he went to Rājīva's house: "Rājīva, you are fortunate — four Vaiṣṇava guests have come to my place." The Dewan, famous for his charity, at once offered ten rupees; the Bābā took only two, what he needed for his guests. Seeing his renunciation and hospitality, the learned and good people of the place built a temple for his Deity and an ākhaḍā for him, and in time the temple filled with other Deities too — Śrī Sītā-Rāma, Śrī Gopāla-govinda, Śrī Nitāi-Gaura.

## Tulasī leaves

One day of heavy rain he had nothing to offer the Deities, so he offered only Tulasī leaves and gave them to the devotees; shortly afterwards **Viṣṇucaraṇa Sen**, father of the late zamindār of Berhampur, sent a huge quantity of food. Such things were common in his life.

## The gong

After he had left this world a devotee of Murshidabad who did not know of it went to Vṛndāvana and met him on the road. "When do you go home?" the Bābā asked. "Soon." "I shan't be going for some time. There is no gong in the temple; I'll buy one, and you please give it to the priest." The devotee carried the gong back and told the priest of the meeting — and the shocked sevāit told him of the Bābā's death and showed him his samādhi. That gong is still worshipped there daily.
