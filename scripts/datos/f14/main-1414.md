## In short

**Śiśir Kumār Ghoṣ** (1839–1911), of a respected zamindār family of **Māgurā** (Jessore), founder of the **Amṛta Bazar Patrikā**, turned from the path of jñāna to become an ardent devotee of Śrī Gaurāṅga. His Bengali **Amiya Nimāi-carita** made many devotees of Gaurāṅga, and his English **Lord Gaurāṅga** helped many Westerners understand Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu.

## Finding Gaurāṅga

A kāyastha, son of **Harinārāyaṇa Ghoṣ** and **Amṛtamayī**, he had great respect for his mother, and in her memory established the Amṛta Bazar market and a charitable school in his village. At first he followed jñāna-mārga, but his brother **Hemanta Bābu** wrote to him that he was Śrī Caitanya's servant and that the Lord would make him do His work by any means. Perplexed, Śiśir asked how he could know Śrī Caitanya, and his brother told him to read the *Prārthanā* and the *Prema-bhakti-candrikā*. Reading in the *Prārthanā* «gaurāṅga bolite habe pulaka śarīra» — your body will thrill when you say «Gaurāṅga» — and feeling nothing, he asked why; his brother answered that he might feel nothing, but others surely did. Śiśir went looking for such a person and at last found a brāhmaṇa in the Hooghly district. From then on he became a great Bhāgavata and studied every book on Śrī Caitanya he could find.

## A changed life

His whole life changed: almost mad with love for Gaurāṅga, the well-known public man now presented himself as the most humble and ordinary person, walking barefoot in the mornings in simple clothes, chanting the name of Śrī Gaurāṅga. He began collecting manuscripts and, to preach Gaura-bhajana, compiling the *Amiya Nimāi-carita*; the first book he completed was the *Kālācāṅd-gītā*.

At a wedding in Hooghly he saw a painting of **Śrī Śrī Nitāi-Gaura and Sītānātha** and, overwhelmed, insisted like a child on having it; the owner gave it to him, and he carried it home and gave it to his mother to worship. According to his intimate disciple he could not stand before it, for whenever he looked at it he fainted in ecstasy. The painting is still kept on the sixth floor of the Amṛta Bazar Patrikā building — the paper he founded, first called *Ānanda-bazar Viṣṇupriyā* — and is printed in the edition of Murāri Gupta's *Kaḍacā*.
