## In short

**Gaṅgāgovinda Siṁha**, founder of the royal family of **Kāndi** (Murshidabad) and Dewan to Lord Hastings, was a great Vaiṣṇava devoted to Lord Caitanya. Inspired by **Siddha Ṭoṭārāma**, he left his estate to his grandson **Lālā Bābu** and went to **Navadvīpa** with some hundreds of Vaiṣṇavas, where he led the search for the site of Lord Gaurāṅga's house and in 1792 built a great temple there.

## Gaurāṅga's house

So amazed and inspired was he by the virtuous character of Siddha Ṭoṭārāma — the Ṭoṭā Rāmadāsa Bābājī (#1553) whose insult by a rogue he, passing in a boat, had reported to the king — that he made over his whole property to his grandson and left for Navadvīpa. There he pioneered the work of finding where Lord Gaurāṅga's ancestral house had stood: many residents who had seen it were still alive, and from what they said, checked against authentic evidence, the true place was identified, in the neighbourhood of Navadvīpa called **Rāmacandrapura**.

## The temple

On the first of Agrahāyaṇa 1199 BS (1792) he built a temple more than sixty feet high and began the worship of **Govinda, Gopīnātha, Kṛṣṇa** and **Madana-mohana**. It still stood in 1819, but by 1821 the Gaṅgā had washed it away.

He also built a ṭola for students, giving them money and clothes at the start of every month; he served the paṇḍitas of Navadvīpa and arranged meals for Vaiṣṇava sādhus and pilgrims (*Navadvīpa-mahimā*, pp. 407-408).
