## In short

Śrīla A.C. Bhaktivedānta Svāmī Prabhupāda — **Abhaya Caraṇa De**, initiated **Abhaya Caraṇāravinda** — was the disciple of **[[Śrīla Bhakti Siddhānta Sarasvatī#ficha-124|Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī]]** who, at sixty-nine, took his guru's order to preach in English to the West, founded the **International Society for Krishna Consciousness** (ISKCON, New York, July 1966) and in twelve years spread the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra and Mahāprabhu's teachings all over the world.

## Family

- Born in **Calcutta** in **1896**, on **Nandotsava**, the day after Janmāṣṭamī. Father: **Gour Mohan De**; mother: **Rajanī**; both devotees of Kṛṣṇa. An astrologer foretold that at seventy he would cross the ocean and open 108 temples.
- As a child he held his own Ratha-yātrā and worshipped Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa Deities his father gave him. He studied at the **Scottish Church College**, Calcutta, married in **1918**, and after his B.A. examination (1920) refused his degree as a follower of Gandhi. In 1921 Kārtikcandra Bose made him assistant manager of his chemical laboratory.

## Life

- **1922**: a friend took him to Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī at Ultadanga, Calcutta, who told the two young men at once to preach Lord Caitanya's message in English all over the world; «in my heart I accepted him as my spiritual master».
- **1923**: he moved to Allahabad (Prayāga) with his family and opened a pharmacy. **1932**, in Prayāga: harināma and dīkṣā with the sacred thread from Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī («he likes to hear; I have marked him»), named Abhaya Caraṇāravinda; he wrote for *The Harmonist*. **1934** in Bombay; **1935** at Rādhā-kuṇḍa his guru told him: «If ever you get money, print books.» In late 1936 his guru wrote that he could become «a very good English preacher».
- After his guru's departure he befriended **[[Śrīla Bhakti Prajñāna Keśava Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1798|Vinodabihārī Brahmacārī]]** (Keśava Mahārāja), who stayed months in his house in Allahabad; with him and Nṛsiṁhānanda Brahmacārī he founded the **Śrī Gauḍīya Vedānta Samiti** (Calcutta, Akṣaya Tṛtīyā 1940). In Calcutta he gave part of his house to **[[Śrīla Bhakti Rakṣaka Śrīdhara Deva Gosvāmī#ficha-1802|Śrīdhara Mahārāja]]**, a lifelong friend. In **1944** he began *Back to Godhead*; his godbrothers gave him the title **«Bhaktivedānta»**.
- **1953**: the League of Devotees in Jhansi; **1954**: he left his business and family. In **1955** he came to the Keśavajī Gauḍīya Maṭha, Mathurā, where **[[Śrīla Bhaktivedānta Nārāyaṇa Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1809|Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja]]** took him in; Keśava Mahārāja made him director of the *Gauḍīya Patrikā* and the Hindi *Bhāgavata Patrikā*, and he wrote, translated, cooked and played mṛdaṅga at the evening ārati.
- **Sannyāsa**: **17 September 1959**, Viśvarūpa-mahotsava, at the Keśavajī Gauḍīya Maṭha, Mathurā, from **Bhakti Prajñāna Keśava Gosvāmī Mahārāja**, who gave him the name *Svāmī*; Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja performed the fire sacrifice and **[[Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī Mahārāja#ficha-1839|Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī]]** sang the mahā-mantra; Sanātana Prabhu (Bhaktivedānta Muni Mahārāja) took sannyāsa with him.
- He lived at the **Rādhā-Dāmodara** temple in Vṛndāvana and in Delhi (Chippiwada), translating and printing the First Canto of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam in three volumes.
- **13 August 1965**: he sailed from Calcutta on the *Jaladuta*, free of charge thanks to Mrs Morarji; after two heart attacks on board he reached Boston on **17 September 1965**. In New York, from **26 Second Avenue** (June 1966), he registered **ISKCON** in **July 1966**; centres followed in San Francisco (Ratha-yātrā 1967), Montreal, Boston, Los Angeles, Buffalo; New Vṛndāvana (1968); London (1969, Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa Temple record, Bhaktivedānta Manor 1973); Europe, Russia, Africa, Australia.
- In **1967** he visited his ailing sannyāsa guru in Calcutta for the last time. In India from **1970**: life membership, the centres of Bombay, Vṛndāvana (Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir) and **Māyāpura** (land bought 1971); in 1971 he opened a temple at Rajahmundry for **[[Bhakti Vaibhava Purī Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1583|Bhakti Vaibhava Purī]]**, and in 1973 stayed at his Visakhapatnam āśrama. He founded the Bhaktivedānta Book Trust (1970), the Governing Body Commission (1970–71), gurukulas (Dallas; Vṛndāvana 1976) and the Bhaktivedānta Institute (1976).
- In twelve years he produced some 60 large volumes (Bhagavad-gītā As It Is, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the seventeen-volume Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Nectar of Devotion, Kṛṣṇa…), translated into over 70 languages; opened more than 108 temples, wrote over 8,000 letters, travelled round the world 14 times and initiated more than 5,000 disciples.
- Before his departure he asked Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja to forgive his strong words about his godbrothers, to care for his disciples and to place him in samādhi; he also asked forgiveness of **[[Śrīla Bhakti Hṛdaya Bon Mahārāja#ficha-1795|Bon Mahārāja]]**. He left this world on **14 November 1977** at the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir, Vṛndāvana; Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja put him into samādhi with his own hands.
- Appearance: **Bhādra Kṛṣṇa Navamī** (Nandotsava); departure: **Kārtika Śukla Caturthī**; sannyāsa day: **Bhādra Pūrṇimā** (Gauḍīya calendar).
