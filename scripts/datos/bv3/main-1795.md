## In short

Śrīla Bhakti Hṛdaya Bon (Vana) Mahārāja — **Nandasuno Brahmacārī** — was one of the foremost preachers of **[[Śrīla Bhakti Siddhānta Sarasvatī#ficha-124|Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī]]**, the first of them to preach in England and Germany, and later a great ascetic of Vṛndāvana, founder of the **Institute of Oriental Philosophy** and of the bhajana-kuṭīra on the Madana-mohana Ghera. **[[Śrīla Bhaktivedānta Nārāyaṇa Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1809|Bhaktivedānta Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja]]** called him his śikṣā-guru.

## Family

- Born **Narendranātha Mukherjī** in **1901** in East Bengal; son of **Brahmarṣi Rajanīkānta**, a Vedic scholar and devotee. A lifelong celibate.

## Life

- **1918**: dīkṣā from Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī as Nandasuno Brahmacārī. With his godbrother Hayagrīva Brahmacārī (later [[Śrīla Bhakti Dayita Mādhava Gosvāmī#ficha-1794|Bhakti Dayita Mādhava]]) he silenced the principal of a Sanskrit college in Kāśī who mocked their guru for making non-brāhmaṇas brāhmaṇas (Bhāg. 7.11.35).
- **1924**, aged 23: the third to receive sannyāsa from Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī. He gave thousands of lectures in India, founded maṭhas and organised theistic exhibitions in Calcutta and Dacca.
- In the 1930s his guru sent him to England and Germany; he brought back two Germans, Dr E. G. Schulze (later Sadānanda) and Baron Koeth, for initiation.
- After his guru's departure, troubled by the division of the Gauḍīya Maṭha, he turned to austerity. In **1941** he lived on a boat at the Śivālaya Ghāṭa in Vārāṇasī, offering a thousand bel-leaves daily to Śiva, protector of Vṛndāvana, until he got his permission to live in Vraja. He then walked the Himalayan places of pilgrimage with four vows (no speaking, sleeping on the ground, only milk, fruits and nuts, the Name at every step) and wrote of it in *Vaikuṇṭher Pathe* («On the Way to Vaikuṇṭha»). He had, his disciples say, a vision of his guru there.
- **1943–46**: in an underground cell of his bhajana-kuṭīra on the Madana-mohana Ghera, Vṛndāvana, serving Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Govinda in near silence. He took bābājī veśa from **[[Advaita Dāsa Bābājī Mahāśaya (Śrī Vṛndāvan)#ficha-17|Advaita dāsa Bābājī]]** of Govardhana as Vṛndāvana dāsa Bābājī, and wrote the songs *Viraha-vedanā*; Advaita dāsa Bābājī confirmed what he had written of his spiritual form.
- **1946**: inspired to found a Vaiṣṇava university, he took up sannyāsa again; the **Vaiṣṇava Theological University** became the **Institute of Oriental Philosophy** (affiliated with Agra University). He also built the **Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Primary School** at Nandagrāma and cared for Sanātana Gosvāmī's bhajana-kuṭīra there, where Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī left his body.
- Godbrothers such as **[[Bhakti Pramoda Purī Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1799|Bhakti Pramoda Purī]]**, **[[Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī Mahārāja#ficha-1839|Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī]]** and **[[Bhakti Kumuda Santa Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-810|Bhakti Kumuda Santa]]** honoured him as śikṣā-guru. Bhaktivedānta Svāmī, at the end of his life, asked his forgiveness; Bon Mahārāja said there was no offence.
- Late in life he was elected ācārya of the **Śrī Caitanya Maṭha** of Māyāpura (with the Yogapīṭha and the samādhis of Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī and Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī), initiated many disciples in 1978–80, and founded the **Rādhā-Govinda Trust** with temples at Hingalganj, South Calcutta and Nandagrāma. His sannyāsī disciples include Kṛṣṇānanda, Rasānanda, Rasikānanda, Mohanānanda, Sudhānanda, Śyāmānanda, Mādhavānanda, **Gopānanda** (later ācārya of the bhajana-kuṭīra), Keśavānanda and Śatānanda Bon Mahārāja.
- He left this world at **9.04 pm on 7 July 1982** in Vṛndāvana, chanting in a semi-coma during continuous kīrtana; his samādhi is at the bhajana-kuṭīra on the Madana-mohana Ghera.
- Works: an English translation of the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *The Gītā as a Chaitanyite Reads It*, *Śrī Caitanya*, *Viraha-vedanā*, *Vaikuṇṭher Pathe* and other books in English, German, Hindi and Bengali.
- Appearance: **Caitra Śukla Pañcamī**; departure: **Śrāvaṇa Kṛṣṇa Dvitīyā** (Gauḍīya calendar).

## Other versions

- One text says he became ācārya of the Śrī Caitanya Maṭha after Bhakti Vilāsa Tīrtha Mahārāja (1976); another, after Bhakti Kusuma Śramaṇa Mahārāja.
