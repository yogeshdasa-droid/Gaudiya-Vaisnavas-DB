## In short

Śrīla Bhakti Pradīpa Tīrtha Mahārāja (Jagadīśa Vasu, d. 1953) received dīkṣā from **[[Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura#ficha-128|Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura]]** and was the **first sannyāsī disciple** of **[[Śrīla Bhakti Siddhānta Sarasvatī#ficha-124|Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī]]** (1920). He preached in East Bengal and Orissa and in Europe (1933), and from 1941 was president of the **Gauḍīya Mission**. His younger brother was [[Bhakti Prasād Purī#ficha-123|Bhakti Prasāda Purī (Ananta Vāsudeva)]].

## Life

- Born at Sandvīpa-Hātiyā (Noākhālī, now Bangladesh), son of Rajanīkānta Vasu, a government employee, and Bidhumukhī; both parents were initiated by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura (Rajanīkānta later took the bābājī vow from Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī). As a boy he was called **Jagadīśa**. After Calcutta University he was a teacher, married, in Calcutta.
- On 25 March 1910 he walked from Dhubulia to Māyāpura with Vaikuṇṭhanātha Ghoṣāla for Gaura Pūrṇimā and met Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura; Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī preached to him for hours and sent him to Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī at Kuliyā, who accepted his watermelon, had him lead kīrtana and sent him back. He took **dīkṣā from Bhaktivinoda** at Godruma, and the sacred thread under Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī. He served Bhaktivinoda at Godruma and was with him on the evening of his departure (23 June 1914).
- Titles «Bhakti Pradīpa» and «Vidyāvinoda Bhakti-śāstrī Sampradāya-vaibhavācārya». When his wife died he took sannyāsa in **Kārtika 1920** — Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's first sannyāsī — and preached from East Bengal to Orissa.
- On 18 March 1933 his guru gave a farewell address for him, Bhakti Hṛdaya Bon and Saṁvidānanda before they sailed for Europe; there he wrote articles and translated a life of Śrī Caitanya and the Gītā into English.
- He was present at his guru's departure (31 December 1936 / 1 January 1937) and gave the opening address when his brother was made ācārya at Māyāpura (Gaura Pūrṇimā 1937). In 1941 he was elected president of the Gauḍīya Mission.
- At 82 he retired to the Puruṣottama Maṭha in Purī. On **Agrahāyaṇa Pūrṇimā** (Bengali 1360, December 1953) he did his usual sādhana, dressed in new cloth, sat with his japa and listened to the Caitanya-bhāgavata; at the verse on the Lord's saṅkīrtana he put down his beads, folded his hands, said «O Gaura, O Nitāi, O Gadādhara» and left his body.

## Other versions

- His departure is dated 1953 (Bengali 1360, Agrahāyaṇa pūrṇimā) or «1954» in another text; one text gives «1877–1953».
