## In short

**Ananta**, called **Śiśu Ananta**, the youngest of the Oṛiyā **Pañcasakhās**, was born at **Balipatna** (Purī district) in the twenty-fifth year of King Puruṣottamadeva (1486), according to Acyutānanda's *Udaya-kāhānī*. He founded the **Śiśu-sampradāya**. In Īśvara dāsa's Oṛiyā *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Lord Caitanya had **Nityānanda** initiate him.

## «Śiśu»

Īśvara dāsa tells how Ananta and Jagannātha dāsa once visited Lakṣmī and Nārāyaṇa in Vaikuṇṭha. Jagannātha took the form of a sakhī and drew the Divine Couple's attention; Ananta became Padmana (Pradyumna, Lakṣmī's infant son), and the Deities caressed him as their child; searching for him, Jagannātha dāsa realized in meditation that the child near Them was Ananta (*Caitanya-bhāgavata* 64, pāda 126-132, p. 403). From then on «Śiśu» was added to his name, and many later poets took the title: Śiśu Arjuna dāsa (*Rāmābhibhā*, *Kalpalatā*), Śiśu Balarāma dāsa (*Rāsa*), Śiśu Śaṅkara dāsa (*Uṣābhihara*), Śiśu Pratāparāya (*Śaśisena*), Śiśu Dayānidhi dāsa (*Aśvamedha-yāga*).

## With Lord Caitanya

According to Īśvara dāsa, Ananta was an associate of Kṛṣṇa in Dvāpara — born then as **Dāma** — and the Lord told him He would come in Kali as Caitanya and that Ananta should take birth then too; after the end of the Yādavas, Balarāma-Saṅkarṣaṇa lived in Bindu-sarovara as the serpent Ananta. At fifteen a sage told him: "Nityānanda, the embodiment of Saṅkarṣaṇa, has been born in Gauḍa; Caitanya will come to Nīlācala with His companions, and you will serve His feet. You are a part of Nityānanda — that is why you bear the name Ananta." On His way to Purī the Lord visited **Koṇārka**; Ananta met Him there and fell at His feet, and the Lord told Nityānanda to initiate him — and Nityānanda, joyfully, gave him the mahā-mantra in his ear.

All the Pañcasakhās came to Purī, near Baḍadaṇḍa, in Caitra to join the saṅkīrtana; on the Lord's way to Vṛndāvana, at Cuttack, the king sent for them and the Lord embraced them all. Returning from Gauḍa, Mahāprabhu asked about Ananta's writings, and he said he had written the *Bhaviṣya Purāṇa*. In Sudarśana dāsa's *Caurāsī Ājñā*, Śiśu Ananta shows his powers before the other four sakhās, Lord Caitanya and Pratāparudra, plucking a ripe jackfruit and sharing it with everyone.

## His works

Only the *Udebākhāṇa* and some small poems have been published. Manuscripts in the State Museum of Orissa: *Garuḍa-Ananta-saṁvāda*, *Agata-cumbaka-mālikā*, *Hetu-udaya-bhāgavata*, *Udebākhara*, *Chatabākhara*, *Ṭhikā-bākhara stotra*, a *cautiśā* and bhajanas.
