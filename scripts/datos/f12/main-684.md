## In short

**Karmetī** («Karametivai Mātā»), an exceptionally devout brāhmaṇa woman, was the daughter of **Paraśurāma Paṇḍita**, priest of a royal family of the village of **Khajal** (the record says «South India»). Unable to live with a husband who was no Vaiṣṇava, she fled to **Vṛndāvana** — hiding three days in the carcass of a camel from those sent after her — and lived in ecstatic meditation by **Brahma-kuṇḍa**, where the king had a hut built for her.

## Leaving home

Both the king and her father were exalted devotees, and as a child she grew deeply attached to Vaiṣṇava ways. She married reluctantly to honour her father's wish; finding her husband attached to wealth and no Vaiṣṇava, she refused to live with him and meditated on the Lord's feet in a solitary place, where people wondered at her ecstasies — laughing like a madwoman, bursting into tears, crying out. Forced back to her husband's house, by then wholly averse to the world, she jumped from an upstairs window in the dead of night and ran, not knowing even which way Vṛndāvana lay.

## The camel

In the morning Paraśurāma told the king, who sent people in all directions. Crossing open country, she saw the searchers behind her and nowhere to hide — until she came on the carcass of a camel half eaten by dogs and jackals. In spite of the stench she hid inside its belly, and they passed by. For three days she stayed there without food, remembering the Lord; then she came out, bathed in the river and, with great difficulty, reached Vṛndāvana, where she sat in meditation in the forest by Brahma-kuṇḍa.

## Her father in Vṛndāvana

Her grief-stricken father searched far and wide and at last came to Vṛndāvana. Climbing a tall tree, he saw her deep in the forest. Her face was radiant and her body shone from her austerity; she sat unconscious, tears flowing. His heart melted; he no longer dared think of her as his daughter and fell flat before her. When at last she opened her eyes, she silently offered him respect and sat mute, as if they were strangers. He begged her to come home and serve the Lord there; she plainly refused, told him to forget her and remember only Kṛṣṇa's feet — and, speaking, fell unconscious in prema. He went home alone, weeping, and told the king, who came himself to Vṛndāvana and, against her strong resistance, had a hut built for her. Its remains can still be seen.
