## In short

**Ṭoṭā Rāmadāsa Bābājī** — born **Rāmadāsa Miśra**, a Drāviḍa brāhmaṇa — came to Navadvīpa to study Nyāya, left it for bhajana in Vṛndāvana and, by Mahāprabhu's order, came back to **Navadvīpa** to look after the service of **Mahāprabhu's Deity**. He became the head of the Vaiṣṇavas of Navadvīpa; **Mahārāja Kṛṣṇacandra** gave him the title «Ṭoṭā» and tax-free land, where the **Baḍa Ākhaḍā** stands (deed of 1202 BS, 1785).

## From Nyāya to bhajana

Before finishing his studies he was drawn to renunciation and went to Śrī Vṛndāvana, where he did bhajana for a long time at the **ṭhaurā south of the Śrī Gopīnātha temple**, until Śrīman Mahāprabhu ordered him to Navadvīpa to see to His sevā. At that time the service of Mahāprabhu's Deity was in disorder: the Gosvāmīs were poor, there was no permanent temple, the Deity was carried in turn to the sevakas' houses, and at times had to be hidden from evil men.

## Under the peepal trees

Rāmadāsa fixed his seat under the ten aśvattha trees near the Gaṅgā. With tilaka, tulasī beads and a beggar's dress, he was taken for a madman and mocked, and bore the abuse calmly. Once he put some questions of Nyāya to a mocker, who carried them to his teacher; the teacher, guessing a great scholar behind them, came and discussed with him. Another day two logicians argued while bathing in the Gaṅgā from morning till evening; Rāmadāsa came with his begging bowl and settled the matter at once — and his fame spread far and wide.

## «Ṭoṭā»

Once, while he was deep in meditation, a rogue hung a string of shoes round his neck. **Dewan Gaṅgāgovinda Siṁha**, passing in a boat, was shocked and asked him who had done it, but Rāmadāsa, absorbed, did not answer. The Dewan went straight to **Mahārāja Kṛṣṇacandra** of Navadvīpa, who came at once to beg forgiveness for the offence done in his kingdom. They discussed the systems of Indian philosophy at length, and the king, deeply impressed, gave him the title «**Ṭoṭā**».

## Baḍa Ākhaḍā and Mahāprabhu's temple

Rāmadāsa served **Śrī Giridhārī**, keeping the Deity with him under the trees; after many discussions the king gave six acres of tax-free land for an āśrama, and the house built there became famous as the **Baḍa Ākhaḍā**, still run by the descendants of his disciples. Through his efforts the present compound of **Mahāprabhu's Deity** was chosen and improved, the old temple rebuilt and the daily pūjā arranged. A Bengali deed of **1785 (1202 BS)** says the land granted to Ṭoṭā Rāmadāsa Bābājī for the service of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu is free of tax.

His disappearance day is **the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Pauṣa** (the record: «the tenth day of the full moon»).
