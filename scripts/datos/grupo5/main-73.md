## In short

Śrīla **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa**, the **Gauḍīya Vedāntācārya**, wrote the **Govinda-bhāṣya** on the *Vedānta-sūtra*, by Govindajī's order, when at **Galtā** near Jaipur he defended the Gauḍīya line as a true sampradāya. A scholar of Orissa first in the Mādhva (Tattvavādī) line, he became a Gauḍīya after studying the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* with **Rādhādāmodara**, his dīkṣā-guru, and read the *Bhāgavatam* with **Viśvanātha Cakravartī**. As a renunciant he was called **Ekānti Govinda dāsa**.

## Life

He was born in a village near **Remuṇā** in the Balasore district of Orissa, in the 18th century (the record: c. 1700). In a learned place he studied grammar, alaṅkāra and nyāya, then went to **Mysore** to study the Vedas, where he became a disciple of the Mādhva sampradāya; he took sannyāsa, defeated the paṇḍitas of Purī, and lived in the Tattvavādī maṭha there. Then he studied the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* with **Rādhādāmodara** of Kānyakubja, a grand-disciple of Rasikānanda, was overwhelmed by the depth of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism and became his disciple. He is said to have studied bhakti-śāstra with **Pītāmbara dāsa** and the *Bhāgavatam* with Viśvanātha Cakravartī (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). He installed **Śrī Śyāmasundara** in Vṛndāvana.

## Galtā and the Govinda-bhāṣya

When news came that the Bengali priests of the Jaipur temples had been put out as "of no sampradāya", Viśvanātha, in his old age, sent him with **Kṛṣṇadeva Sārvabhauma** to Jaipur. At Galtā he defeated the opponents, re-established the Gauḍīyas, and installed **Śrī Vijaya-gopāla**, still in the temple there; by Govinda's mercy and order he wrote the **Govinda-bhāṣya** (*Abhidhāna*). The record dates the assembly between 1706 and 1718.

## Works

*Govinda-bhāṣya*; *Siddhānta-ratna*; *Prameya-ratnāvalī*; *Vedānta-syamantaka* (inspired by Rādhādāmodara, as its last verse says); *Siddhānta-darpaṇa*; *Aiśvarya-kādambinī*; *Sāhitya-kaumudī*; *Vaiṣṇavānandinī* on the *Bhāgavatam*; commentaries on the *Gītā*, the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha*, the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, the *Stava-mālā* and others.

## Disciples

**Uddhava dāsa** and **Nanda Miśra**, his two chief disciples (*Abhidhāna*).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** c. 1700 (date unknown).
**Disappearance:** Jyaiṣṭha śukla daśamī (Gauḍīya calendar); 1768 (the record).
