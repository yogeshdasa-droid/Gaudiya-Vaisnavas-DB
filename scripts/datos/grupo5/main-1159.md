## In short

Śrī **Rādhāmohana Ṭhākura** of **Mālihāṭī** (Burdwan), a descendant of **Śrīnivāsa Ācārya**, was a great paṇḍita and padakartā of the 18th century. He compiled the **Padāmṛta-samudra**, a great collection of songs with his own commentary (*Mahābhāva-anusāriṇī*); 182 songs of his are in the *Padakalpataru*. He converted **Mahārāja Nandakumāra**, a śākta, to Vaiṣṇavism, and took part in the debate on **svakīyā and parakīyā** at the court of Murshid Quli Khan (1125 BS) (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*).

## Life

He was born at Mālihāṭī in **1104 BS** (c. 1697), in the family of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya (son of **Jagadānanda Ṭhākura**). He defeated in debate the court paṇḍitas, among them **Nayanānanda Tarkālaṅkāra** of Vaidyapura, and brought the king to Vaiṣṇava dīkṣā. His disciples included the padakartā **Uddhava dāsa** (Kṛṣṇakānta Majumdār) and **Vaiṣṇava dāsa** (Gokulānanda Sena), the compiler of the *Padakalpataru*.

## Departure

On **Caitra śukla navamī** he bathed, put on tilaka and garlands, and passed away in the tulasī garden during harināma-saṅkīrtana. It is said that his dear disciples **Kālindī dāsa** and **Parāṇa dāsa**, coming back from Vṛndāvana where they had repaired the old kuñja of Śrī Īśvarī, saw him on the way. His wife left her body seven days after him (*Abhidhāna*).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** 1104 BS, at Mālihāṭī. **Disappearance:** Caitra śukla navamī (*Abhidhāna*).
