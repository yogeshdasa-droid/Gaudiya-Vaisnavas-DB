## In short

Puruṣottama dāsa Ṭhākura, son of **Sadāśiva Kavirāja**, was one of the **twelve gopālas** and a disciple of Śrī Nityānanda: "The twenty-third and twenty-fourth prominent devotees of Nityānanda were Sadāśiva Kavirāja and his son Puruṣottama dāsa, the tenth gopāla. From birth he was merged in the service of Nityānanda's feet, and always played like a child with Kṛṣṇa" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.38–39). "He had no consciousness of his body; Nityānanda always lived in his heart" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.741–742). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Stoka-kṛṣṇa** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 130; *Bhaktamāla* 3).

## Family

His father **Sadāśiva Kavirāja** was a vaidya (physician by caste) and a great devotee; the *Candraprabhā* of Bharata Mallika (p. 74) says Sadāśiva had two sons, the elder being Puruṣottama, "known as Ṭhākura, like an associate of Viṣṇu".

His wife, **Jāhnavā**, died just after giving birth to their son **Kānu**. Nityānanda Prabhu came to the house and took the child to Khaḍadaha; seeing his devotion He named him **Śiśu Kṛṣṇadāsa**. Kānu was born on the day of Ratha-yātrā (Āṣāḍha śukla dvitīyā) in 1450 Śaka, or 1457 Śaka according to another text. At five Jāhnavā Mātā took him to Vṛndāvana, where Jīva Gosvāmī and the others called him **Kānāi Ṭhākura**; he was at the Kheturī festival with Jāhnavā (*Prema-vilāsa*). "Kānu Ṭhākura, a very respectable gentleman, was such a great devotee that Kṛṣṇa always lived in his body" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.40). The Śrīpāṭa is called the "Vasu-Jāhnavā Pāṭa", perhaps because Jāhnavā adopted Kānu.

The family later settled at **Bodhkhānā** (Jessore, Bangladesh) and **Bhājanghāṭ** (Nadia); the poet **Kṛṣṇakamala Gosvāmī** (*Rāi-unmādinī*, *Vicitra-vilāsa*) was of this line (*Abhidhāna*).

## Disciples

His three chief disciples were kulīna brāhmaṇas: **Mādhavācārya**, who married Nityānanda's daughter Gaṅgā Devī; **Yādavācārya**; and **Devakīnandana dāsa**, author of the *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā* and of the Sanskrit *Vaiṣṇavābhidhāna*. Like Kānu after him, he accepted many disciples from brāhmaṇa families.

## Works

Songs (padāvalī), praised by the *Abhidhāna* as worth studying and relishing.

## His Deities

He lived at times at **Sukhasāgara**, near Cākdaha and Simurāli stations. His Deities were first at Beleḍāṅgā; when that temple was destroyed they went to Sukhasāgara; when Sukhasāgara went into the Ganges they were taken with Jāhnavā Mātā's Deity to Sāhebaḍāṅgā-Berigrāma, and now they are at **Cāndūḍe-grāma**, a mile from Pālapāḍā (Bhaktivedānta Svāmī).

## Dates

The Gauḍīya calendar keeps his appearance on the **śukla tṛtīyā of Phālguna** (Govinda) and his disappearance on the **kṛṣṇa pañcamī of Phālguna** (as «Puruṣottama Ṭhākura»).

## Puruṣottama dāsa and Nāgara Puruṣottama

The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya 6.474) names Sadāśiva's son Puruṣottama dāsa, and Bhaktivedānta Svāmī calls him Nāgara Puruṣottama. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* counts them as two different gopālas: Puruṣottama dāsa (Stoka-kṛṣṇa) and Nāgara Puruṣottama (Dāma).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Stoka-kṛṣṇa** — "the one who was Stoka-kṛṣṇa is Dāsa Puruṣottama" (*Bhaktamāla* 3; *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 130).
