## In short

Puruṣottama Nāgara was the **eldest son of Īśāna Nāgara**, the servant of Śrī Advaita Ācārya and author of the *Advaita-prakāśa*. He lived at **Teotha-Bāṅkpāl**, in the Dhaka district, on the eastern bank of the Padmā.

## The floating stone

At the south-western edge of the village, below Lihānpur, the Bāiś-kodāliyā and the Padmā meet in the **Huḍāsāgara**, where Puruṣottama did his daily worship. One day, after bathing, he was absorbed in his prayers half in the water when a rich man's boats came up the river, towed from the bank; the men pulling the rope did not stop for the poor Vaiṣṇava, but their feet would not move. The man in the boat, to test the Vaiṣṇava's power, had the boatmen throw in a huge stone, three cubits by two and a half: "Let us see whether this stone floats by the Vaiṣṇava's wish." Puruṣottama roared, and the stone floated to him. He touched it, bowed to it, carried it home on his head alone and kept it beside the throne of his Deity of Jagannātha, worshipping it every day.

Later, when his descendants tried to saw the stone to divide it among themselves, blood came out of it, and they stopped: some took Jagannātha, some the stone and the other Deities. The stone is still worshipped at **Vāmandī** village (*Advaita-prakāśa*, introduction; *Abhidhāna*).

## Other persons of the same name

He is not Nāgara Puruṣottama, Dāma gopāla, one of the twelve gopālas of Nityānanda (his own record) — the two were mixed in one record until 05-11-2026.
