## In short

Kāla Kṛṣṇadāsa, also called **Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa**, was one of the **twelve gopālas** of Śrī Nityānanda: "Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa was a first-class Vaiṣṇava; he knew nothing but Nityānanda" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.37). "Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa is famous in the three worlds; by remembering him one attains Gauracandra" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.740). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Lavaṅga** sakhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 132). His Śrīpāṭa is **Ākāihāṭ**, near Katwa (Bardhamān), on the old royal road between Navadvīpa and Katwa. He is also counted in Mahāprabhu's branch.

## Life

He was born at Ākāihāṭ in a pure kulīna brāhmaṇa family (*Abhidhāna*).

**With Mahāprabhu in the South.** The *Abhidhāna* identifies him with the simple brāhmaṇa Kṛṣṇadāsa whom Nityānanda and Sārvabhauma gave the Lord as His servant for the journey to South India: "This is a simple brāhmaṇa named Kṛṣṇadāsa; take him with You" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 7.39). In the Malabar country the Bhaṭṭathāris, a sect of sannyāsīs with women and wine, tempted him away with women and wealth; the Lord went to their camp and brought him back, and they fled when they tried to attack Him. Back in Purī the Lord told Sārvabhauma what had happened and let him go: "Now he may go wherever he likes; I have no more responsibility for him." Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa fell at His feet weeping; he was then sent to Navadvīpa with the news of the Lord's return for Śacī Mātā and the devotees (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 9–10). — One of the record's texts says, on the contrary, that he is **not** the Kṛṣṇadāsa who went to the South.

He went with the devotees of Ākāihāṭ to Kaṇṭaka-nagara (Katwa) for the disappearance festival of Dāsa Gadādhara (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 10.409). "At Ākāihāṭ lives Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa, known before as Lavaṅga sakhā" (*Pāṭa-paryaṭana*).

**Ākāihāṭ.** His samādhi is there, and **Śrī Rādhāvallabha** is still served, with a festival on his disappearance day. West of the samādhi is a pond called **Nūpura-kuṇḍa**: when Raghunandana Ṭhākura was dancing at Baḍa-ḍāṅgā, his anklet fell there; the anklet is still worshipped with the Deity. His descendants live in different holy places in Bengal.

**Sonātalā.** According to the tradition of Sonātalā (old name Bhāduṭi-Mathurāpura) in the Pabna district, Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa went preaching the holy name from Katwa to Sonātalā and made an āśrama there. This tradition calls him the son of Kālidāsa Ṭhākura, a vārendra brāhmaṇa of the Bharadvāja gotra; he married and had a son, Mohana dāsa, whom he left at Sonātalā with his property while he went with his wife to Vṛndāvana. There a second son was born, Gaurāṅga dāsa (also called Vṛndāvana dāsa), whom he later sent back to Sonātalā with a Deity like Śrī Govindajī, **Śrī Kālācāṅd**. The ruins of the āśrama, temple and bathing ghāṭa can still be seen; his disappearance festival is held there on the kṛṣṇa dvādaśī of Agrahāyaṇa (*Abhidhāna*).

## Disappearance

On a **kṛṣṇa dvādaśī**: of **Agrahāyaṇa** in the Gauḍīya calendar (and at Sonātalā); of **Caitra** at Ākāihāṭ, according to the *Abhidhāna* and the record's texts.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Lavaṅga** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 132).
