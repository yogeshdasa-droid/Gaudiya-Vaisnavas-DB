## In short

Parameśvara dāsa, usually called **Parameśvarī dāsa**, was one of the **twelve gopālas** and a very dear servant of Śrī Jāhnavā Mātā: "Parameśvara dāsa, said to be the fifth gopāla, completely surrendered to the feet of Nityānanda. Whoever remembers him easily gets love of Kṛṣṇa" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.29). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Arjuna** sakhā: "nāmnārjunaḥ sakhā prāg yo dāsaḥ parameśvaraḥ" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 132). His Śrīpāṭa is **Āṭapura** (Taḍā-Āṭapura, formerly Viśākhala) in the Hooghly district.

## Life

The *Abhidhāna* calls him a brāhmaṇa and a song-writer (padakartā); another text says he was born in a family of physicians (vaidyas). His Śrīpāṭa was at **Ketugrāma** (or Kāugrāma); from there he went to live at **Khaḍadaha**. The *Pāṭa-paryaṭana* places his home at Sāṅcaḍā.

"Parameśvara is the life of Nityānanda; Nityānanda sports in his body. Kṛṣṇadāsa and Parameśvara, both in the mood of cowherd boys, are always shouting 'hai! hai!'" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.740).

When Narottama Ṭhākura came to Khaḍadaha, Parameśvara eagerly told him all about the road to Purī (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 8.219). He went with Jāhnavā Mātā to the Kheturī festival and to Vṛndāvana; on the way back he stayed some days at **Garalgāchā**. He accompanied the Deity of Śrī Rādhā that Jāhnavā sent to Vṛndāvana for Śrī Govinda.

**Āṭapura.** By Jāhnavā's order — "Go quickly to Taḍā-Āṭapura" — he established the service of **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha** there, and she herself was present at the installation (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 13.244–246). It is said that he planted two twigs he used for cleaning his teeth, which grew into two great **bakula trees**; they still stand in front of the temple, and his **samādhi** is between them. Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Govinda are still served there.

**The fox.** One day at **Ākanā-Māheśa**, near Śrīrāmpur, Kamalākara Pippalāi's Śrīpāṭa, there was a nāma-saṅkīrtana in the temple of Lord Jagannātha, and Parameśvara was dancing, mad with love. Some pāṣaṇḍīs threw a dead jackal into the kīrtana. Parameśvara was not angry; feeling for the poor animal he brought it back to life with his glance, and it trotted away while the offenders fled. "I carefully worship Parameśvara dāsa, who made a jackal chant the holy name in the saṅkīrtana" (*Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*).

## Disappearance

On the **pūrṇimā of Vaiśākha**. On that day the khunti (a ladle-like emblem carried on a staff before the kīrtana party) that he used in saṅkīrtana is set beside his samādhi.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Arjuna** sakhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 132).

## Other persons of the same name

Parameśvara Modaka, the sweet-seller of Navadvīpa who loved Nimāi as a child and came to see Him in Purī with "Mukunda's mother" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 12.54–60), is another person.
