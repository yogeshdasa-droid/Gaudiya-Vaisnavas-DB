## In short

**Gopāla Cakravartī**, a handsome and learned young man, was chief tax collector for **Hiraṇya** and **Govardhana Majumdār** of Saptagrāma, charged with depositing twelve lakhs of coins in the Emperor's treasury. When **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** said in their court that liberation comes from the mere dawning of the holy name, he could not bear it and insulted him openly; within three days he was struck by leprosy (Caitanya-caritāmṛta Antya 3.190-210). Some say he was the same as **Cāpala Gopāla**.

## The offence

Balarāma Ācārya, the Majumdārs' priest, brought Haridāsa Ṭhākura to their court for a recital of nāma-kīrtana. Gopāla could not tolerate Haridāsa's teaching that liberation is attained simply by the first glimpse of the awakening of the holy name, and, very angry, insulted him before the assembly. Balarāma Ācārya at once rebuked him: "You have insulted Haridāsa Ṭhākura; you have put yourself in great danger, and nothing auspicious will come to you." Govardhana dismissed him from service.

Within three days Gopāla was attacked by leprosy: his high nose and his beautiful fingers and toes, once like golden campaka buds, melted and withered away.
