## In short

Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita was one of the **twelve gopālas** of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu: "Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita had all the signs of a great soul; Nityānanda Prabhu was always in his heart" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.733). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Vasudāma** sakhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 127). His Śrīpāṭa is **Śītala-grāma** near Maṅgalkoṭ, Bardhamān district, where his samādhi is.

## Birth and family

Accounts of his birthplace differ. The usual view is Śītala-grāma itself. Others say his family home was **Jāḍagrāma** in Chittagong, his father **Śrīpati Bandyopādhyāya** and his mother **Kālindī Devī**. According to the Bengali periodical *Śrī Gaurāṅga-mādhurī*, he was born at **Siyān-Muluk** near Bolpur (Birbhum) to **Ādideva Vācaspati** and **Dayāmayī Devī** (*Abhidhāna*). As a child he bowed down full length to Tulasī three times a day.

He married **Haripriyā** when young, but very soon left home on the pretext of pilgrimage. His wealthy father had given him much money for the journey; on seeing Mahāprabhu he gave it all to the Lord and took a begging pot in his hand: "I worship the renounced Paṇḍita Dhanañjaya, who gave everything to the Lord and took a pot in his hand" (*Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*). On the road he received from Narahari Ṭhākura a mantra made of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya's names (*Śrī Kṛṣṇa-caitanya-sahasranāma* 44–46; *Abhidhāna*).

His younger brother **Sañjaya Paṇḍita**, perhaps also his disciple, settled at **Jalandi** (near Bolpur); his descendants still serve Śrī Rādhā-Govinda there. Sañjaya's son was Śrī Rāma Kānāi Ṭhākura (Śrīpāṭa Muluk-grāma). Dhanañjaya himself left no descendants.

## Life

He was in Navadvīpa during Mahāprabhu's saṅkīrtana pastimes, sang in His kīrtana party, and on His order travelled widely preaching; *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 11.31) counts him among the intimate servants of Nityānanda. After seeing the Lord he went back to Śītala, where many dacoits and pāṣaṇḍīs became devotees by his mercy. On his way to Vṛndāvana he stayed some days at **Sāṅcaḍā-Pāṅcaḍā** (six miles south of Memari station), which is therefore also called "Dhanañjayera Pāṭa"; he left the worship there with a disciple. On his return he established the service of Deities at **Jalandi** and came back to Śītala.

## Śītala-grāma

Śītala is about a mile north-east of Kaicara station on the narrow line from Bardhamān to Katwa. To the left of the entrance a Tulasī altar is called "Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita's samādhi". The Deities are **Śrī Gaura-Nitāi, Śrī Gopīnātha** and the śālagrāma **Śrī Dāmodara**; they are served by the descendants of his disciples (or of Sañjaya). The temple was a thatched house with mud walls; the Mullicks, zamindars of Bājāravana Kābāsī, built a larger one that later fell into ruin (Bhaktivedānta Svāmī, *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Ādi 11.31, purport).

## Disciples

Śrī Jīva Kṛṣṇa, who established a Deity of Śrī Śyāmasundara, served in the house of Śrī Gopāla Caudhurī. **Cūḍāmaṇi dāsa**, author of the *Bhuvana-maṅgala* (*Gaurāṅga-vijaya*), wrote his life of the Lord by Nityānanda's order in a dream, from what he heard from Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita and Gadādhara dāsa (*Abhidhāna*).

## Disappearance

On the **śukla aṣṭamī of Kārtika**. (Bhaktivedānta Svāmī wrote that it was observed every year in January.)

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vasudāma** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 127).

## Other persons of the same name

Dhanañjaya Vidyānidhi (or Vidyā-vācaspati), Śrīnivāsa Ācārya's teacher, is another person.
