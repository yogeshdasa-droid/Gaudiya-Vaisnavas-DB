## In short

Kamalākara Pippalāi, called Kamalākānta Pippalāi in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, was one of the **twelve gopālas** and an associate of Śrī Nityānanda: "Kamalākara Pippalāi is said to have been the third gopāla. His behaviour and his love of Godhead were uncommon, and he is celebrated all over the world" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.24). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Mahābala** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 128). His Śrīpāṭa is **Māheśa**, on the Ganges a krośa south of Śrīrāmpur (Hooghly), with its famous Deity of **Lord Jagannātha**.

## Birth and family

According to the family tradition of his descendants, the Adhikārīs of Māheśa (as told by Prasāda dāsa Adhikārī, fourteenth in his line), he was born in **1414 Śaka (1492 CE; Bengali year 899)** at **Khālijuli**, a village near the Sundarbans, in a śuddha-śrotriya rāḍhī brāhmaṇa family of the Vātsya gotra. His father was a wealthy zamindar; his younger brother was **Nidhipati**. The *Pāṭa-paryaṭana* places his birth at Ākanā-Māheśa.

He married late in life. Accounts of his children differ:
- The family says his son was **Caturbhuja** and his daughter **Rādhārāṇī**, who married **Kāmadeva Paṇḍita** of Khaḍadaha; Nidhipati's daughter **Ramā Devī** married Yogeśvara Paṇḍita.
- The *Nityānanda-vaṁśa-vistāra* names his daughter **Vidyunmālā**, married to Sudhāmaya Caṭṭopādhyāya of Māheśa; their daughter **Nārāyaṇī** married Prabhu Vīrabhadra.
- The *Vaiṣṇavācāra-darpaṇa* names his son-in-law Yadunandana.

Caturbhuja's sons were Nārāyaṇa and Jagannātha; Nārāyaṇa's son Jagadānanda, whose son was Rājīvalocana. His descendants have the title Adhikārī.

## Life

"Paṇḍita Kamalākānta was extremely intense; Nityānanda gave him the village of Saptagrāma" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 5.729); Jayānanda's *Caitanya-maṅgala* says Nityānanda gave him **Pāṇihāṭi**. He was at the Daṇḍa-mahotsava at Pāṇihāṭi (1439 Śaka, 1517 CE), at the great festival of Kheturī (1504 Śaka, 1582 CE) and at the disappearance festival of Dāsa Gadādhara at Katwa (*Pāṭa-paryaṭana*, *Vaiṣṇavācāra-darpaṇa*).

**Jagannātha of Māheśa.** The *Vaiṣṇavācāra-darpaṇa* calls him the founder of the Jagannātha temple at Māheśa: "He who was Mahābala gopāla in Vṛndāvana is Kamalākara Pippalāi here … on the western bank of the Ganges he stayed at Māheśa, made a form of Jagannātha and served Him." The family says that **Dhruvānanda Brahmacārī** installed the Deity and handed the service to Kamalākara, who was called there in a dream. His brother came looking for him and, unable to bring him home, brought the whole family, with their priest Caṇḍīvara Ṭhākura, from Khālijuli to Māheśa, which was then jungle and became a beautiful village. Later the Nawab of Dhaka gave the Deity 1185 bighās (Bengali year 1060; the land became Jagannāthapura); the present temple was built by Nayanacāṅd Mallik of Pāthuriyāghāṭā, Calcutta; the old temple on the river bank was lost when the Ganges moved.

## Disappearance

On the **śukla trayodaśī of Caitra**. The family says he passed away in **1485 Śaka** (Bengali 970) at the age of 71, but not where or how; there is no samādhi at Māheśa. The *Vaiṣṇavācāra-darpaṇa* says that after giving his daughter in marriage he went to **Vṛndāvana** and left his body there. The *Abhidhāna* notes that the family's account does not agree with the books in everything and says nothing of his meeting with Nityānanda.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Mahābala** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 128; *Pāṭa-paryaṭana*).

## Other persons of the same name

Kamalākara dāsa of Gaurāṅgapura, disciple of Abhirāma Gosvāmī, and Kamalākānta (Dvija Kamalākānta) are other persons.
