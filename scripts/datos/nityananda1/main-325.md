## In short

Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita was one of the **twelve gopālas** and one of the most important branches of Śrī Nityānanda: "Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita, the emblem of the most elevated devotional service, had the greatest power to receive and give love of Kṛṣṇa. Making Lord Caitanya and Lord Nityānanda the Lords of his life, he gave up everything — even his caste and family — for the service of Nityānanda" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.26–27). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Subala** sakhā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 128). His Śrīpāṭa is **Ambikā-Kālnā** (Bardhamān), where Gaura and Nityānanda stayed with him as Deities.

## Family

His father was **Kaṁsāri Miśra**, a kulīna brāhmaṇa of Śāligrāma (Nadia), and his mother **Kamalā Devī**. There were six brothers: Dāmodara, Jagannātha, **Sūryadāsa Sarakhela**, Gaurīdāsa, **Kṛṣṇadāsa** (Dīna Kṛṣṇadāsa, the poet) and Nṛsiṁha-Caitanya. The two daughters of his elder brother Sūryadāsa, **Vasudhā** and **Jāhnavā**, married Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu. His wife was **Vimalā Devī**; their sons were **Balarāma** ("big Balarāma") and **Raghunātha** (*Abhidhāna*). Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura adds that Raghunātha's sons were Maheśa Paṇḍita and Govinda, and that Gaurīdāsa's daughter was Annapūrṇā; some descendants still live at Śāligrāma.

He was patronised by King Kṛṣṇadāsa, son of Hari Hoḍa (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura).

## From Śāligrāma to Ambikā

He first lived at Śāligrāma, near Muḍāgāchā station in Nadia; with the permission of his elder brother he moved to the bank of the Ganges at **Ambikā**, in the Kālnā area, across the river from Śāntipura (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 7.330–331).

**The oar.** Once Mahāprabhu and Nityānanda came to his house from Harinadī (or Śāntipura) in a small boat, rowing it themselves, and sat under a tamarind tree outside. Mahāprabhu gave him the oar: "Take this oar; with it carry the living beings across the river of material existence" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 7.336). The tamarind tree, the oar, and a palm-leaf *Bhagavad-gītā* said to be in the Lord's own hand (not accepted by all) are still at the temple (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 7.361).

**The Deities.** When the Lord was about to take sannyāsa He came to bid Gaurīdāsa farewell. Gaurīdāsa fell at His feet weeping: "Never leave my house! Stay here at Ambikā, or I shall die." The Lord promised to stay in a Deity form; then the two brothers stood before him with two Deities exactly like Them. "Whichever two you recognise as Us will stay with you and beg you to feed Them." Gaurīdāsa cooked for all four, garlanded them and gave them tāmbūla; two stayed, and two went to Nīlācala. This is told in a song by his brother, Dīna Kṛṣṇadāsa. Another account says the Lord made the two Deities from a nearby nīm tree. Through his unflinching devotion the Deities were seen actually eating his offerings.

One day the Deities reminded him: "Gaurīdāsa, you were Our friend Subala — don't you remember our play on the banks of the Yamunā?" and appeared to him as Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma dressed as cowherd boys, with horn, stick and flute, peacock feather and forest garlands. When he grew old and still cooked many preparations every day, They refused to eat out of concern for his labour; he promised to cook only a little śāk on a banana leaf, and They laughed and ate. Once, when he wished to decorate Them, They appeared before him already wearing ornaments he had never seen.

## Hṛdaya Caitanya

His dearest disciple was **Hṛdayānanda**. Once, with the Lord's appearance day three days away and Gaurīdāsa still away, Hṛdayānanda sent out the invitations himself. Gaurīdāsa, pleased within, feigned anger and sent him away. Sitting under a tree by the Ganges, Hṛdayānanda received a large donation from a passing merchant, and on his guru's order held a festival there; Gaura and Nityānanda Themselves left the altar to dance in his kīrtana. When the pūjārī, **Baḍa Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita**, found the altar empty, Gaurīdāsa went to the river with a stick; the two Lords vanished into Hṛdayānanda's heart. Weeping, Gaurīdāsa embraced him: "From today your name is **Hṛdaya Caitanya**." He then gave him the service of the Deities. Hṛdaya Caitanya was the guru of Śyāmānanda Prabhu (*Suvala-maṅgala* of Naṭavara dāsa); Gaurīdāsa's granddaughter married Hṛdaya Caitanya's son, and the gosvāmīs of Ambikā descend from that line and worship in sakhya-rasa.

Narahari Cakravartī tells his life in the seventh taraṅga of the *Bhakti-ratnākara*.

## Disappearance

In the bright fortnight of **Śrāvaṇa** — the Gauḍīya calendar keeps the **dvādaśī**; the texts give the ekādaśī or the trayodaśī. He left this world in **Vṛndāvana** (*Abhidhāna*); his samādhi is at **Dhīra-samīra**, on the bank of the Yamunā, with his Deities Śrī Śrī Śyāma-rāya.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Subala** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 128).

## Other persons of the same name

Gaurīdāsa Kīrtanīyā (Nityānanda's branch) and Gaurīdāsa, disciple of Śyāmānanda, are other persons.
