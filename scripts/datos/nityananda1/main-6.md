## In short

Abhirāma Ṭhākura, also called **Rāmadāsa**, Rāma or Abhirāma Gopāla, was one of the **twelve gopālas** of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu and a fiery preacher of His mission in Bengal. "Rāmadāsa was full of sakhya-prema; he once picked up a log that sixteen men could not lift and used it as a flute" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 11.16). His Śrīpāṭa is **Khānākula Kṛṣṇanagara** (Hooghly district). In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Śrīdāmā**, Kṛṣṇa's cowherd friend (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 126); in Rāma-līlā he was Bharata (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*).

## Śrīdāmā comes to Navadvīpa

It is said that after Kṛṣṇa's pastimes Śrīdāmā never took birth again but went on wandering in the same form. In the unpublished *Aiśvaryāmṛta-kāvya* of Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura (109–111), Nityānanda Prabhu brought Śrīdāmā, in his subtle body, out of a mountain cave where he had stayed since the Dvāpara-yuga, told him of the pastimes of Gaura and took him to Navadvīpa. A popular version of the story: Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma and the cowherd boys were playing hide-and-seek when Kṛṣṇa decided to go to Nadia, and Śrīdāmā was forgotten in his hiding place. Balarāma, as Nityānanda, came for him; Śrīdāmā would not believe that so small a person was Balarāma until He caught him within ten paces, and, still hurt at being left behind, he sent an expansion of himself, Rāmadāsa, who agreed to go.

The *Bhakti-ratnākara* (4.105–108) says simply that Abhirāma appeared in a brāhmaṇa family to deliver the fallen souls, was learned in all the scriptures, very charming, and expert in dancing, singing and music.

## With Nityānanda in Bengal

When Śrī Gaurasundara ordered Nityānanda Prabhu to preach in Gauḍa-deśa (Bengal), He sent Rāmadāsa, Gadādhara dāsa and others with Him. By Nityānanda's order Abhirāma became a great ācārya; he was always in ecstasy and very kind to the fallen, and the atheists and pāṣaṇḍīs trembled at the sight of him: "The power of Abhirāma Gosvāmī is fierce; seeing him the unconquerable pāṣaṇḍīs always tremble" (*Pāṭa-paryaṭana*). He delivered great numbers of them. By the wish of Nityānanda Prabhu he married **Mālinī Devī**, the learned daughter of a scholarly brāhmaṇa, herself "of indescribable power" (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 4.105–108).

**The flute.** Overwhelmed one day by sakhya-rasa and wanting a cowherd boy's flute, he lifted a huge log and held it like a flute. *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Ādi 11.16) says sixteen men could not lift it; the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (126) says thirty-two; the *Bhakti-ratnākara* (4.123) says a hundred.

**His obeisances.** He was so powerful that any stone that was not a śālagrāma or a consecrated Deity cracked when he bowed down to it. When he offered obeisances to the newborn sons of Nityānanda Prabhu, seven of them left this world one after another; Vīrabhadra (Vīracandra) Gosvāmī survived his praṇāma, and Abhirāma joyfully accepted him as the second form of Śrī Gaurāṅga, as he wrote in his *Śrī Vīrabhadrāṣṭaka*: "so 'yaṁ prasīdatu hariḥ kila vīrabhadraḥ". So too with Gaṅgā Devī, Nityānanda's daughter by Vasudhā: "This time we have not been deceived!" In his *Gaṅgā-stotra* (6) he says: "I, Śrīdāmā sakhā, am moving all over the earth to find that object which can withstand my obeisances. Only when I bowed to you twelve times, O Mother Gaṅgā, and you remained unharmed and smiling, did I understand your extraordinary spiritual wealth."

**Jaya-maṅgala.** He carried a whip (or cane) named **Jaya-maṅgala**; whoever it touched received prema. When young Śrīnivāsa Ācārya came to see him he struck him three times, and Mālinī stopped him: "Ṭhākura, don't beat him anymore! He is just a boy." Śrīnivāsa was drowned in Kṛṣṇa-prema and became very dear to him, though it is doubtful that he was his initiated disciple. The whip is still kept at the Kṛṣṇanagara temple.

**Other meetings.** He came once to Śrīkhaṇḍa to meet Raghunandana Ṭhākura; Mukunda dāsa, knowing his reputation, said Raghunandana was not at home. Raghunandana ran after him and caught up with him at Baḍa-ḍāṅgā, where they danced together in ecstasy; Raghunandana's anklet fell at Ākāihāṭ, where it is still worshipped with Kāliya Kṛṣṇadāsa's Deity. He tested the boy Gopāla Guru in Purī in the same way, and by the Lord's mercy the boy was unharmed.

## Khānākula Kṛṣṇanagara

He lived at Kṛṣṇanagara on the banks (kūla) of the Khānā, the Dvārakeśvara River, in the Hooghly district — hence Khānākula Kṛṣṇanagara. Before his temple stands the **Siddha-bakula**, the bakula tree under which he first sat on arriving and liked to sit (Siddha-bakula-kuñja). In a dream Śrī **Gopīnātha** told him: "I am within the earth here; take Me out and worship Me." He dug there and found the Deity; the pit became a lake of sweet water, **Rāma-kuṇḍa**, where whoever bathes is freed from birth and death (*Bhakti-ratnākara* 4.118). The temple also has Vrajavallabha, Gopāla and a śālagrāma-śīlā. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī visited it on his Gaura-maṇḍala parikramā and was received with great honour.

The **Bali Maṭha** in Purī is said to have been founded by him. The descendants of his disciples live in the Hooghly and Bankura districts, at Kṛṣṇanagara, Āmtā, Viṣṇupura and Koṭalpura.

## Disappearance

On the **kṛṣṇa saptamī of Caitra**, with a great festival every year at Khānākula Kṛṣṇanagara. The *Abhidhāna* gives "gauṇa Vaiśākha kṛṣṇa saptamī", which is the same day counted from the full moon; one text says simply "Vaiśākha".

## Works

*Śrī Vīrabhadrāṣṭaka* and *Śrī Gaṅgā-stotra* (a hundred verses in praise of Gaṅgā Devī, it is said). Two unpublished works about him are known: *Abhirāma-paṭala* and *Abhirāma-vandana*; also the *Abhirāma-līlāmṛta*.

## Abhirāma and Rāmadāsa

Almost all Vaiṣṇava books take Abhirāma and Rāmadāsa as one person. The late Jagadbandhu Bhadra says they are not: according to the *Abhirāma-līlāmṛta*, when Lord Gaurāṅga asked that Abhirāma be brought from Vṛndāvana, Abhirāma did not come himself but empowered and manifested the form of Rāmadāsa, who went to Navadvīpa; so Rāmadāsa, his svarūpa, belongs to Nityānanda's branch and Abhirāma himself to Caitanya's (*Gaura-pada-taraṅgiṇī*, p. 21; *Abhidhāna*).

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Śrīdāmā** — "pūrā śrīdāmā-nāmāsīd abhirāmo 'dhunā mahān / dvātriṁśatā janair eva vāhyaṁ kāṣṭham uvāha saḥ": "he who was Śrīdāmā is now the great Abhirāma; he carried a log that only thirty-two men could lift" (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 126). In Rāma-līlā: Bharata (*Abhidhāna*).

## Other persons of the same name

Abhirāma dāsa, the author of the *Pāṭa-paryaṭana* and of the *Abhirāma-ṭhākurera-śākhā-nirṇaya* (and, by another account, of *Govinda-vijaya* and *Kṛṣṇa-maṅgala*), is another person. Disciples of Abhirāma: Sundarānanda Paṇḍita of Bhāṅgāmoḍā, Kamalākara dāsa.
