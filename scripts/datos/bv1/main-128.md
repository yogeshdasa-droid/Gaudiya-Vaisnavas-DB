## In short

Śrīla Saccidānanda Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura (Kedāranātha Datta, 1838–1914) brought back the pure teaching of Śrī Caitanya after a century of darkness, when sahajiyā and caste-bound sects had made Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism despised by educated people. While working as a magistrate of the British government he wrote about a hundred books in Sanskrit, Bengali, English and other languages, published the journal Sajjana-toṣaṇī, began the Nāma-haṭṭa, and found the birthplace of Śrī Caitanya at Māyāpura. He is called the **Seventh Gosvāmī**. His śikṣā guru was **[[Śrī Jagannātha dās Bābājī#ficha-1828|Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī]]**, his dīkṣā guru **[[Vipinavihari Gosvāmī#ficha-1696|Vipina-vihārī Gosvāmī]]**; his son was **[[Śrīla Bhakti Siddhānta Sarasvatī#ficha-124|Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī]]**.

## Family

- Born on 2 September 1838 (18 Bhādra 1245), a Sunday, at **Bīranagara (Ulā)**, Nadia, in the mansion of his mother's father, the rich zamindar Īśvaracandra Mustauphi. In the Gauḍīya calendar his appearance is kept on **Bhādra Śukla Trayodaśī**.
- Father: **Ānandacandra Datta**; mother: **Jaganmohinī Devī**. His father's father, Rājavallabha Datta of the Datta family of Hāṭkholā (Calcutta), lived as an ascetic at Choṭi-Govindapura in Orissa; at his death in 1858–59 he blessed Kedāranātha that he would become a great and famous Vaiṣṇava.
- His father died when he was eleven; his two elder brothers died young, and the family became poor.
- First wife (1850): the five-year-old daughter of Madhusūdana Mitra of Rāṇāghāṭa; after her death, **Bhāgavatī Devī** of Jakpur.
- Children, among them Annadā Prasāda (born 1860 at Bhadrak), Kamalā Prasāda, **Bimala Prasāda** (born 1874 in Purī, later Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī), Varadā Prasāda, Virajā Prasāda and Lalitā Prasāda.

## Life

- **Studies.** Village school, then Calcutta with his uncle, the poet Kāśīprasāda Ghosh, editor of the Hindu Intelligencer; a favourite student of Īśvaracandra Vidyāsāgara and friend of Dvijendranātha Tagore. He wrote in English and Bengali (The Poriade, 1857–58) and, for a time, was close to the Brāhmo Samāja.
- **Teacher and magistrate.** Founded an English school at Kendrāpāḍā, taught at Cuttack, Bhadrak and Medinīpura, wrote «The Maths of Orissa». From 1866 deputy magistrate and collector in many places (Chāprā, Dinājpur, Purī, Nadail, Serampore, Kṛṣṇanagara…).
- **Turning to Śrī Caitanya.** At Dinājpur he at last obtained the Caitanya-caritāmṛta and the Bhāgavatam, and gave up the Brāhmo Samāja; he wrote Caitanya-gītā.
- **Purī (from 1868).** In charge of the Jagannātha temple for five years; arrested the false avatāra Bisakisan, who swore to drive out the British and tried to harm his family by yogic power. Studied the Ṣaṭ-sandarbha, Govinda-bhāṣya and Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, founded the Bhāgavata Saṁsad, wrote Datta-kaustubha and the Kṛṣṇa-saṁhitā. Siddha Raghunātha dāsa Bābājī, who had avoided him because he wore no tilaka or kaṇṭhī, begged his pardon («Lord Caitanya has not yet sent me a guru»). Svarūpa dāsa Bābājī guided him. Bimala Prasāda was born there.
- **Vṛndāvana (1880).** Met Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī, who became his guide for life. From 1881 published the monthly **Sajjana-toṣaṇī**; set up the Caitanya Press and printed many Vaiṣṇava books.
- **Dīkṣā.** Lord Caitanya told him in a dream that Vipina-vihārī Gosvāmī of Bāghnāpāḍā, of the family of Vaṁśīvadana, would be his guru; soon a letter came from him, and he gave him dīkṣā.
- **Māyāpura (1887–88).** Posted at Kṛṣṇanagara, he looked for the Lord's birthplace every weekend from Navadvīpa town. From the roof he twice saw a light across the Gaṅgā; there he found a palm tree and the ruins of Ballāla Sena, and with the Bhakti-ratnākara, the Caitanya-bhāgavata and the old village names (Meyāpura) he identified **Māyāpura**. Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī confirmed it, dancing at the spot. He wrote the Navadvīpa-dhāma-māhātmya.
- **Preaching.** Built his house at **Godruma** (Svānanda-sukhada-kuñja), started the **Nāma-haṭṭa** and preaching tours (1891–92), founded the Prapannāśrama at Āmalājoḍā. Sisir Kumar Ghosh called him «the Seventh Gosvāmī».
- **Last years.** In 1902 built «Bhakti-kuṭī» in Purī near the samādhi of Haridāsa Ṭhākura; later lived in renunciation at Godruma. He left the world while meditating on Gaura-Gadādhara, on the disappearance day of **Gadādhara Paṇḍita**: Āṣāḍha Amāvasyā, 23 June 1914.

## Works

About a hundred books, among them: Jaiva-dharma, Śrī Caitanya-śikṣāmṛta, Harināma-cintāmaṇi, Bhajana-rahasya, Śaraṇāgati, Gītāvalī, Gītamālā, Kalyāṇa-kalpataru, Datta-kaustubha, Kṛṣṇa-saṁhitā, Tattva-viveka, Āmnāya-sūtra, Daśa-mūla-śikṣā, Prema-pradīpa, Navadvīpa-dhāma-māhātmya, Bhakti-tattva-viveka; the Rasika-rañjana commentary on the Gītā, the Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya of the Caitanya-caritāmṛta, commentaries on the Śikṣāṣṭaka and the Upadeśāmṛta; in English «The Bhāgavata: Its Philosophy, Its Ethics and Its Theology» and «Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu: His Life and Precepts»; his autobiography, the Svalikhita-jīvanī. Also songs in the spirit of Narottama, Govinda dāsa and Jñāna dāsa.

## Other versions

- One text of this record calls his father «Rāja Kṛṣṇānanda Datta» (that is the name of Narottama Ṭhākura's father) and makes Ānandacandra a «godfather»; another says he was born «to Īśvaracandra and Jaganmohinī» (Īśvaracandra was his mother's father). Here: Ānandacandra Datta, as in his autobiography.
- Bimala Prasāda's birth: 1873 (one text) or 6 February 1874 (Bhaktisiddhānta's record).
- One text gives his death as «1916 (1914?)»: it was 1914.
