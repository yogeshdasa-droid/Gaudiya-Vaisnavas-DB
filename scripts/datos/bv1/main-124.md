## In short

Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Gosvāmī Ṭhākura Prabhupāda (Bimala Prasāda Datta, 1874–1937), son of **[[Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura#ficha-128|Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura]]** and disciple of **[[Gaura Kiśora dāsa bābājī#ficha-1819|Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī]]**, founded the **Gauḍīya Maṭha** — sixty-four maṭhas in India and Burma, and a centre in London — and sent sannyāsīs to preach Śrī Caitanya's teaching all over the world. He fought the sahajiyās and the smārtas who said that only a born brāhmaṇa could be a guru, and used the printing press as a «great mṛdaṅga» (bṛhad-mṛdaṅga). His disciples spread the movement across the world.

## Birth and childhood

- Born on 6 February 1874 in **Jagannātha Purī**, in the house «Nārāyaṇa-chātā» near the temple, where his father was the magistrate in charge of the temple; in the Gauḍīya calendar, **Phālguna Kṛṣṇa Pañcamī** (Māgha kṛṣṇa pañcamī counting the month from new moon). One of the ten children of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura and Bhāgavatī Devī, born after his father prayed for a son to help his preaching. He came with the umbilical cord across his chest like a sacred thread.
- At six months, when the Ratha-yātrā chariot stopped for three days before their house, his mother put him on the chariot; he touched Lord Jagannātha's feet and a garland of the Lord fell on him.
- As a small boy he vowed never to eat mangoes again after eating one not yet offered. At seven he knew the Gītā by heart and learned proof-reading and printing for the Sajjana-toṣaṇī. In 1881 a Deity of Kūrmadeva was found when the Bhakti-bhavana was built in Calcutta; his father initiated him and gave him Its service.

## Studies and work

- Serampore High School (invented a script, «Bicanto»), mathematics and astronomy with Maheśacandra Cūḍāmaṇi; in 1892 the Sanskrit College of Calcutta, the Vedas with Pṛthvīdhara Śarmā.
- In 1897 opened the Sārasvata Catuṣpāṭhī and published the astronomical journals Jyotirvid and Bṛhaspati. For his Sūrya-siddhānta he received the title **Siddhānta Sarasvatī**. Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī asked him to prepare the Vaiṣṇava calendar: the beginning of the Navadvīpa Pañjikā.
- From 1895 worked for the kings of Tripurā (the history Rājaratnākara, teaching the princes, inspecting the palace); disgusted with the intrigues, he retired and later gave up his pension too.

## Dīkṣā and bhajana

- After a pilgrimage with his father in 1898, his wish for renunciation grew. On his father's advice he went to **Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī**, who twice refused him («I forgot to ask Mahāprabhu»; «learning is nothing beside devotion»). He answered that Rāmānuja was sent back eighteen times by Goṣṭhīpūrṇa, and he would also wait. Gaura Kiśora gave him dīkṣā at Godruma in 1900 and told him to preach the Absolute Truth and leave everything else.
- From 1905 he chanted a billion names (śata-koṭi nāma) in a grass hut near the Lord's birthplace in Māyāpura, eating once a day, sitting under an umbrella when the rain came through.
- In 1911, for his sick father, he wrote «Brāhmaṇa and Vaiṣṇava» and defeated the smārta paṇḍitas at Midnapore. In 1912 he spoke at the Vaiṣṇava conference of the Mahārāja of Kāsimbāzār but took no food there because of the sahajiyās.

## Sannyāsa and the Gauḍīya Maṭha

- After his father's departure (1914) and his guru's (1915) he took over the Sajjana-toṣaṇī and set up the Bhāgavata Press at Kṛṣṇanagara. In **1918**, at Māyāpura, before a picture of Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī, he accepted **tridaṇḍa-sannyāsa** himself and became Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Gosvāmī Mahārāja; Śrī Caitanya Maṭha at Māyāpura became the centre of his mission.
- Between 1918 and 1937 he founded **sixty-four maṭhas**: Navadvīpa, Māyāpura, Calcutta (Bāghbāzār), Dhaka, Mymensingh, Chittagong, Midnapore, Remuṇā, Purī, Ālālanātha, Madras, Kovur, Delhi, Patna, Gayā, Lucknow, Vārāṇasī, Haridvāra, Prayāga, Mathurā, Vṛndāvana, Kurukṣetra and others, also Rangoon and London. He set footprints of Mahāprabhu (Gaura-pāda-pīṭha) at Mandāra hill and in South India, and gave tridaṇḍi-sannyāsa to some twenty-five educated men.
- Papers: Sajjana-toṣaṇī, The Harmonist (English), Gauḍīya (Bengali weekly), Bhāgavata (Hindi), **Nadīyā Prakāśa** (a Bengali daily), Kīrtana (Assamese), Paramārthī (Oriya); many books.
- In 1933 he sent sannyāsīs to Europe.

## Departure

His last instruction: «Preach the message of Rūpa and Raghunātha with the utmost enthusiasm. Our goal is to become a speck of dust at the feet of the followers of Rūpa Gosvāmī. Stay united under the āśraya-vigraha… never give up the worship of Hari, even amid hundreds of dangers, insults and persecutions.» He left the world in the early hours of **1 January 1937** in Calcutta, on **Pauṣa Kṛṣṇa Caturthī** (Gauḍīya calendar); his samādhi is at Śrī Caitanya Maṭha, Māyāpura.

## Disciples

Among many: [[Śrīla Bhakti Rakṣaka Śrīdhara Deva Gosvāmī#ficha-1802|Bhakti Rakṣaka Śrīdhara]], [[Śrīla Bhakti Prajñana Keśava Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1798|Bhakti Prajñāna Keśava]], [[Śrīla Bhakti Dayita Mādhava Gosvāmī#ficha-1794|Bhakti Dayita Mādhava]], [[Bhakti Pramod Purī#ficha-1799|Bhakti Pramoda Purī]], [[Bhakti Hridoy Bon#ficha-1795|Bhakti Hṛdaya Bon]], [[Bhakti Vilās Tīrtha#ficha-1811|Bhakti Vilāsa Tīrtha]], [[Vaibhava Purī Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1583|Bhakti Vaibhava Purī]], [[Saranga Gosvāmī Mahārāja#ficha-1377|Bhakti Sāraṅga Gosvāmī]], [[Bhaktikevala Audulomi#ficha-127|Bhakti Kevala Auḍulomi]], [[Vichar Yajavara#ficha-1667|Bhakti Vicāra Yāyāvara]], [[Kṛṣṇa dāsa bābājī#ficha-1839|Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī]], Ananta Vāsudeva, Sundarānanda Vidyāvinoda; and [[Śrīla Bhaktivedānta Svāmī Prabhupāda#ficha-125|A. C. Bhaktivedānta Svāmī Prabhupāda]] (dīkṣā 1933), who took the movement to the whole world.

## Other versions

- One text dates his father's departure «1916 (1914?)»: it was 1914.
- One text gives his birth as Friday, 3 February 1874; the others, 6 February 1874.
- One of the texts in this record notes that, after the split of the Gauḍīya Maṭha, each party's biographies leave out disciples of the other side.
