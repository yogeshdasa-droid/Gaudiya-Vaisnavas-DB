## In short

Śrī **Madhvācārya** — **Pūrṇaprajña**, **Ānanda Tīrtha** — founder of the **Tattvavāda** (Dvaita) school and of the **Brahma-sampradāya** in which the Gauḍīya guru-praṇālī counts Mādhavendra Purī and Lord Caitanya. He refuted Śaṅkara's Māyāvāda, wrote commentaries on the *Brahma-sūtra*, the *Gītā*, the Upaniṣads and the *Bhāgavatam*, and installed **Śrī Kṛṣṇa** (Bāla-kṛṣṇa) at **Uḍupī**, served to this day by the eight maṭhas he founded. He is held to be **Vāyu**, who came as Hanumān and Bhīma.

## Birth and sannyāsa

He was born near Uḍupī (South Kanara), at Pājaka-kṣetra, in a Śivaḷḷi brāhmaṇa family; father **Madhyageha Bhaṭṭa**, mother **Vedavatī**; his name as a child was **Vāsudeva**. The record gives 1040 Śaka; the biographies (*Madhva-vijaya* of Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita; the Śrī Kūrma inscriptions of his disciple Narahari Tīrtha) give **1238–1317**, seventy-nine years. At twelve (some texts: sixteen) he took sannyāsa from **Acyutaprekṣa Tīrtha** and was named Pūrṇaprajña; when he defeated the scholars he was called Ānanda Tīrtha.

## Life and works

He went twice to **Badarikāśrama** and met **Vyāsadeva**, who instructed him; he wrote the *Gītā-bhāṣya*, the *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya*, the *Anu-bhāṣya* and *Anuvyākhyāna*, the bhāṣyas on the Upaniṣads and the *Ṛg-veda*, the *Gītā-tātparya-nirṇaya*, the *Bhāgavata-tātparya*, the *Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya*, the *Dvādaśa-stotra*, the *Kṛṣṇāmṛta-mahārṇava*, *Pramāṇa-lakṣaṇa*, *Tattva-viveka* and others — thirty-seven works in all (*Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava Abhidhāna*). His teaching: two categories, the independent (Viṣṇu, the Lord, faultless and full of all good qualities) and the dependent; five eternal differences; bhakti the means. The deity of **Kṛṣṇa** came to him from a ship wrecked off the coast, in a block of gopī-candana; he installed Him at Uḍupī.

## The eight maṭhas

The original head of the Uttarādi maṭha was **Padmanābha Tīrtha**. The eight maṭhas of Uḍupī and their first heads: Palimār (Hṛṣīkeśa Tīrtha), Admār (Narahari), Kṛṣṇāpur (Janārdana), Puttige (Upendra), Śirur (Vāmana), Sode (Viṣṇu), Kāṇiyūr (Śrīrāma), Pejāvar (Adhokṣaja); each worships its own deity (Rāmacandra, Kṛṣṇa, Caturbhuja, Kāliya-mardana Kṛṣṇa, Viṭṭhala and others). The Kṛṣṇa of Uḍupī has nine services a day. His followers later divided into the **Dāsa-kūṭa** (bhajanānandī) and the **Vyāsa-kūṭa** (goṣṭhy-ānandī) (*Abhidhāna*).

## His line

His disciples Padmanābha Tīrtha, Narahari Tīrtha, Mādhava Tīrtha, Akṣobhya Tīrtha, then **Jayatīrtha** (the Ṭīkācārya) and the line down to **Vyāsatīrtha** and **Lakṣmīpati**, Mādhavendra Purī's guru. Śrīla Prabhupāda and Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa accept this succession; historians discuss the link. Lord Caitanya visited the Tattvavādīs at Uḍupī on His tour of the South (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta* Madhya 9.245–277).

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** Āśvina śukla daśamī (Vijayā-daśamī) (Gauḍīya calendar); 1238.
**Disappearance:** Māgha śukla navamī (Gauḍīya calendar); 1317.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Vāyu**, who appeared as Hanumān and as Bhīma.
