## In short

Śrī Advaita Ācārya is one of the **Pañca-tattva**, the combined form of **Mahā-Viṣṇu** and **Sadāśiva**; it was his calling, with Gaṅgā water and tulasī, that brought Śrī Kṛṣṇa down as Śrī Caitanya: "All glories to Advaita Prabhu, the Lord Himself, who made Kṛṣṇa descend and delivered the whole world" (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 8.4). He is "the trunk of the tree of bhakti" (Ādi 9.21). He was a disciple of **Mādhavendra Purī**. His homes were **Śāntipura** and Navadvīpa. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā he was **Sadāśiva**, who also danced in Vraja as a cowherd boy (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 76–80). "Because he is non-different from Hari he is called Advaita; because he teaches bhakti, Ācārya."

## Birth and family

He was born on the **śukla saptamī of Māgha, 1355 Śaka** (1433/1434 CE), in a vārendra brāhmaṇa family at **Lāuḍ** in Śrīhaṭṭa (Sylhet); the *Prema-vilāsa* (24) says at Śāntipura. His name was **Kamalākṣa** (Kamalākānta), also called Maṅgala, and the title **Vedapañcānana**. His father was **Kuvera Paṇḍita** (Kuvera Tarkapañcānana), minister of King Divyasiṁha of Lāuḍ, and his mother **Nābhā Devī**. They had already six sons and a daughter; the sons went on pilgrimage and four died, and the grieving parents moved from Lāuḍ to Śāntipura; then Advaita was born to them in their old age (*Prema-vilāsa* 24). His grandfather was Narasiṁha Nāḍiyāla.

He married two sisters, **Sītā** and **Śrī**, daughters of **Nṛsiṁha Bhāḍuḍī**, at **Phuliyā**; according to the *Prema-vilāsa*, Hiraṇya and Govardhana Majumdār (the father and uncle of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī) bore the cost of the wedding. Sītā's sons were **Acyutānanda** (born 1425 or 1426 Śaka), **Kṛṣṇa Miśra**, **Gopāla**, **Balarāma**, **Svarūpa** and **Jagadīśa**; Śrī's son was **Śyāmadāsa** (*Prema-vilāsa* 24; the *Advaita-prakāśa* differs in places).

## Before the Lord came

He studied the Vedas and śāstras under **Śānta Ācārya** at Phullavatī near Śāntipura, who gave him the title "Ācārya". On pilgrimage in Mithilā he heard an old brāhmaṇa singing of Kṛṣṇa under a banyan tree and stood stunned "like a deer struck by an arrow": it was the poet **Vidyāpati** (*Advaita-vilāsa*); the *Abhidhāna* shows from the dates that the meeting is possible. After his parents' death he offered śrāddha at Gayā, travelled to the holy places, met **Mādhavendra Purī** and took dīkṣā from him, and found the Deity of **Madana-gopāla** in a kuñja in Vṛndāvana (*Advaita-prakāśa*; *Prema-vilāsa* 24 tells of his meeting with Vijaya Purī and receiving Madana-mohana). He set up a school (ṭol) in Navadvīpa.

Seeing the world without devotion, he explained every scripture in the light of kṛṣṇa-bhakti and worshipped a śālagrāma of Kṛṣṇa with Gaṅgā water and tulasī, roaring for Kṛṣṇa to come; his calls pierced the universe and reached Vaikuṇṭha (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 2.79–105). The place by the Gaṅgā near Śāntipura where he did this is **Bābalā**, with a temple in memory of it. According to the *Advaita-prakāśa* he gave Śacī and Jagannātha Miśra the four-syllable Gaura-gopāla mantra.

## With the Lord

When Nimāi was born, Advaita, then 52, danced with Haridāsa Ṭhākura at Śāntipura and sent Sītā with gifts. Viśvarūpa came to his house daily to discuss śāstra, and little Nimāi came to call his brother home; when Viśvarūpa took sannyāsa Advaita wept (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi 7). Īśvara Purī was introduced at his house; he met Haridāsa Ṭhākura.

When the Lord revealed Himself, Advaita worshipped His feet with tulasī, sandal and arghya and sang His glories; asked for a boon, he said: "Distribute love of Kṛṣṇa even to women, śūdras and fools" (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya 6.116–167). He once went to Śāntipura to test the Lord and Rāmāi brought him back. He quarrelled lovingly with Nityānanda; he took the dust of the Lord's feet while the Lord was unconscious and was scolded for it. Grieved to be worshipped by the Lord as a devotee, he went to Śāntipura and explained the *Yoga-vāsiṣṭha* (Madhya 19.13–160). He saw the Lord's universal form (Madhya 24). Śacī's offence towards him was forgiven when she took the dust of his feet (Madhya 22).

When the Lord took sannyāsa, Advaita was overwhelmed; the Lord came to his house at Śāntipura and ate Sītā's cooking. Every year Advaita led the Bengali devotees to Purī for the Ratha-yātrā, cooked for the Lord with his own hands, danced in the kīrtanas and the water-sports after the cleaning of Guṇḍicā (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 14.88–92). He chanted the Nṛsiṁha mantra when his son Gopāla fainted at Guṇḍicā (Ādi 12.23). He comforted his disciple Kamalākānta Viśvāsa when the Lord punished him (Ādi 12). He gave Haridāsa Ṭhākura the śrāddha plate, and for this was put out of the brāhmaṇa community of Śāntipura, until Haridāsa showed his power (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 3.213–220; *Prema-vilāsa* 24). He sent the Lord a riddle (tarajā) through Jagadānanda (Antya 19.16–21). The *Prema-vilāsa* (1) tells of the Lord's sorrow when Advaita preached jñāna a second time.

## Last years

He lived on for many years after the Lord, and left this world in **1480 Śaka (1558/1559 CE) at the age of 125** (*Advaita-vilāsa*; *Advaita-prakāśa* 22). His last teaching: "Preach the qualities and religion of Śrī Caitanya as much as you can — this is my heart" (*Advaita-prakāśa* 22). He gave the service of his Deity to Kṛṣṇa Miśra.

The Nṛsiṁha-śilā and the Deities of **Madana-gopāla** that he worshipped are at **Madana-gopāla-pāḍā** in Śāntipura.

## Works and prayers

His praise of the Lord's limbs (pratyaṅga-varṇana-stotra) is famous. Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya wrote the *Advaita-dvādaśa-nāma-stotra*, the *Advaitāṣṭaka* and the *Advaitāṣṭottara-śata-nāma*. His life is told in the *Advaita-prakāśa* (Īśāna Nāgara, 1568), the Sanskrit *Bālya-līlā-sūtra* (Lāuḍiyā Kṛṣṇadāsa), the *Advaita-maṅgala*, the *Advaita-vilāsa* and the *Sītā-caritra*. His dhyāna, mantra and gāyatrī are in Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī's *Paddhati*.

## Appearance and disappearance

**Appearance:** the **śukla saptamī of Māgha** (Advaita Saptamī, kept with a fast till noon).
**Disappearance:** 1480 Śaka, at 125.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Sadāśiva** and **Mahā-Viṣṇu** together (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 76–80).
