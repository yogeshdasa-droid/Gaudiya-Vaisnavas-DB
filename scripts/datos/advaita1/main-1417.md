## In short

Śrī Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī, the wife of Śrī Advaita Ācārya, is worshipped like Śacī Mātā as a mother of the world. She was always absorbed in parental love (vātsalya) for Śrī Gaurasundara, and it was she who named Him **Nimāi**. In Kṛṣṇa-līlā she is **Yogamāyā** (Paurṇamāsī), and her sister **Śrī**, Advaita's other wife, is Yogamāyā's manifestation (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 86: "yogamāyā bhagavatī gṛhiṇī tasya sāmpratam / sītā-rūpeṇāvatīrṇā śrī-nāmnī tat-prakāśataḥ").

## Family and marriage

She and her sister Śrī were the daughters of **Nṛsiṁha Bhāḍuḍī**; their mother died leaving the two girls, and Nṛsiṁha was told to give both to Advaita. They were married at **Phuliyā**; according to the *Prema-vilāsa*, Hiraṇya and Govardhana Majumdār (father and uncle of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī) bore the cost. After the wedding Advaita lived at Śāntipura (*Abhidhāna*; *Bhakti-ratnākara* 12.1785).

Her sons were **Acyutānanda**, **Kṛṣṇa Miśra** (Kṛṣṇadāsa), **Gopāla**, **Balarāma**, **Svarūpa** and **Jagadīśa** (*Prema-vilāsa* 24 names five, without Svarūpa; the texts often name only the first three). When Śrī's own newborn son died, Sītā, with Advaita's permission, gave her second son Kṛṣṇadāsa to Śrī to bring up (*Advaita-prakāśa* 11).

## Nimāi

When Nimāi was born, Advaita sent her from Śāntipura to Māyāpura. She came in a covered palanquin with maidservants, bringing gold bangles, armlets, necklaces and anklets, tiger nails set in gold, silk saris and a silk garment for the child, gold and silver coins, and a basket of auspicious things — fresh grass, paddy, gorocana, turmeric, kuṅkuma, sandalwood. Seeing the child she understood that, but for His colour, He was Kṛṣṇa of Gokula Himself; her heart melted, and she blessed Him with grass and paddy on His head: "May You live long." Out of fear of witches and ghosts she named Him **Nimāi**, after the bitter nīm tree that evil spirits avoid (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 13.111–120). At the birthday ceremony Śacī and Jagannātha let her be the first to give the child new clothes.

Advaita and Sītā then spent much of their time at their Māyāpura house; she came every day to Śacī and taught her how to raise the child. When Viśvarūpa took sannyāsa she and Advaita consoled the parents and looked after Nimāi. When the Lord came back from Gayā and revealed Himself, Advaita brought her from Śāntipura, and they were the first to worship His feet.

Before His sannyāsa the Lord and Nityānanda held a great kīrtana festival at Advaita's house at Śāntipura, and the Lord asked her to invite the Vaiṣṇavas, as Parameśvarī dāsa sings in the *Padakalpataru*. When He left for sannyāsa, she lay on the ground with Śacī for four days as if dead; when the Lord came back to Śāntipura instead of going to Vṛndāvana, she cooked for Him after His four days of fasting.

## In Purī

Every year she went to Purī with Advaita and Acyutānanda and cooked the Lord's favourite dishes; He ate them as if charmed by a mantra, and honoured her as He honoured His own mother.

## Appearance

The Gauḍīya calendar keeps her appearance on the **śukla pañcamī of Bhādra**.

## In Kṛṣṇa-līlā

**Yogamāyā** (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* 86). The *Gaura-pārṣada-caritāvalī* says Paurṇamāsī, the mother of Sāndīpani Muni, grandmother of Madhumaṅgala and Nāndīmukhī, who advised Nanda and Yaśodā at Kṛṣṇa's birth; the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, however, makes Paurṇamāsī Govinda Ācārya.
