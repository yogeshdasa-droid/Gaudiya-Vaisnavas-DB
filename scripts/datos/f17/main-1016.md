## In short

**Nāthamuni** (born 908), also called **Raṅganātha Muni** and «Yogīndra», was the first great ācārya of the **Śrī Vaiṣṇava** (Viśiṣṭādvaita) tradition, the link between the Ālvārs and the ācāryas. He gathered the scattered Tamil hymns of the **Ālvārs** into the four volumes of the **Prabandha**, the «Tamil Veda», and was the grandfather of **Yāmunācārya**, the precursor of Rāmānuja.

## The Tamil Veda

The son of a learned South Indian brāhmaṇa, he was the first important teacher to bring together the Vedic Sanskrit texts and the ancient Tamil hymns of the Ālvārs — saints famed as incarnations of Viṣṇu's paraphernalia, His disc, conch, garland and so on. Travelling widely in Tamil Nadu, he collected all the hymns he could find, composed over the four preceding centuries, and arranged them in four volumes. These hymns, the **Prabandha**, are still sung before Viṣṇu in the temple of **Śrī Raṅgam**, the chief seat of the Śrī Vaiṣṇavas; at the end of his life Nāthamuni was the temple's commander.

## Family and sannyāsa

His son **Īśvaramuni** died young while his wife was pregnant; her child, Nāthamuni's grandson, became famous as **Yāmunācārya**. After his son's death Nāthamuni took sannyāsa. For his learning he was called «Muni», and for his perfection in yoga «Yogīndra».

## His works

His systematic theology is set out in the **Nyāya-tattva**, a treatise on logic, and his view of bhakti and mystic yoga in the **Yoga-rahasya**; both are quoted in many later works.
