## In short

**Nṛsiṁhavallabha Mitra Ṭhākura** was born, as a boon, to **Kālīcaraṇa Mitra** of **Rājurā** near Kāndrā (west of Kāṭoyā), who had no children and took shelter of **Śrī Maṅgala Ṭhākura**, a disciple of Gadādhara Paṇḍita. At sixteen he took dīkṣā from Maṅgala Ṭhākura and installed **Mahāprabhu**'s Deity at Mayanāḍāl. A gifted kīrtana singer, he gave his name to the **Manoharaśāhī** style.

## Kīrtana

The tunes he sang so pleased the people of the Manoharaśāhī paragaṇā that they became known as «Manoharaśāhī». Whatever he begged he offered to the Deity of Śrī Gaura.

## The pulses

One day a Muslim offered some pulses, and Nṛsiṁha's son refused them; but later he was told in a dream to accept the offering, and since then, once a year, the bhoga is cooked with only that kind of pulse. Nṛsiṁhavallabha's son was **Hare Kṛṣṇa Siddhapuruṣa**, and many well-known kīrtana singers and mṛdaṅga players came from this family.
